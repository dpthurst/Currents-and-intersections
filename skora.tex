\section{Skora's theorem: \texorpdfstring{$\protect\mathbb{R}$-trees}{Rtrees} dual to measured laminations}
\label{sec:skora}


As a final application of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, we give a new
characterization of small actions of surface groups on
$\mathbb{R}$-trees, and in turn we reprove a classical theorem of
Skora~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}.

\subsection{Finitely generated groups acting on real trees} \hspace{1cm} \\

\textbf{Real trees} We start by recalling some basic notions of real trees.
An \emph{$\mathbb{R}$-tree} is a non-empty metric space $T$ such that for
all $x,y \in T$, the intersection of all connected subsets containing
both $x$ and $y$ is isometric to the interval $[0, d(x, y)]$-  This
intersection is denoted
$[x, y]$, and called an \emph{arc} connecting $x$ and $y$. In particular, note that arcs are non-empty, any two points are connected by a unique arc. When an arc is a singleton, we say it is \emph{degenerate}, otherwise we say it is \emph{non-degenerate}.
A \emph{ray} in a real tree is the image of an isometric embedding of the ray $[0,\infty)$. If $p$ is the image of $0$ under this embedding,
the ray \emph{emanates} from $p$. The \emph{space of ends} of a metric space $T$ is the limit of the inverse system $\pi_0(T-B)$ where
$B$ ranges over the closed and bounded subsets of $T$. Let $p$ a be a fixed point of $T$. For every point of the limit there is a ray emanating from $p$,
and a cofinal sequence of components of the complement of closed and bounded sets that represent the limit point, such that every
point in the sequence meets the ray, and this ray is uniquely determined. Hence, there is a one-to-one correspondence with rays of $T$ emanating from $p$ and ends
of $T$. Given two distinct ends of $T$, the corresponding rays meet in an interval with $p$ as endpoint. The union of these two rays minus
the interior of their intersection is a line in $T$. Conversely, given any line in $T$, the two oppositely oriented rays emanating from any point
in the line determine two distinct ends of the tree, and these ends do not depend on the choice of point. We will say this line \emph{joins} the two ends.
By the definition of tree, there is only one line connecting any pair of ends.


\textbf{Actions on real trees} If $\Gamma$ is a finitely generated group, a $\Gamma$-tree is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree together with a group action $\Gamma \acts T$ by isometries.
There are two types of isometries: those with fixed points, called \emph{elliptic}, and those without fixed points, called \emph{hyperbolic}.
Given $g\in \Gamma$, let $\| g\|\coloneqq\inf_{x \in T} d(x,gx)$, and let $T_g \coloneqq \{ x\in T : d(x,gx)=\| g\| \}$ be the \emph{characteristic set} of $g$ which is a closed, non-empty subtree of $T$ invariant under the action of $g$.
If $g$ is elliptic, then $\| g \| =0$. The converse is also true;
see~\cite[Section~1.3]{CM87:GroupActions}.
If $g$ is hyperbolic, then $\| g \| >0$, $T_g$ is isometric to the real line and $g$ translates along $T_g$ by distance $\| g\|$. If $g$ is hyperbolic, we will call $T_g$ the \emph{axis} of $g$.

We say that $\Gamma \acts T$ is \emph{small} if the stabilizer of any (non-degenerate) arc is (virtually) cyclic.
We say that $\Gamma \acts T$ is \emph{minimal} if there is no proper non-trivial subtree of $T$ that is $\Gamma$-invariant.

An action of a finitely generated group $\Gamma$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree with non-trivial translation length $\| \cdot \|$ is one of three types~\cite[Page~583]{CM87:GroupActions}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fixed end: $\| \cdot \|$ trivial on $[G,G]$ (equivalently $G$ fixes an end of $T$ ~\cite[Theorem~2.2, Corollary~2.3]{CM87:GroupActions}).
\item Dihedral: $\| \cdot \|$ non-trivial on $[G,G]$, but $\| [g,h] \|=0$ for all $g,h$ hyperbolic in $T$ (equivalently $G$ fixes a line of $T$ inverting orientation~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{CM87:GroupActions}).
\item Irreducible:  $\| [g,h] \| \neq 0$ for some $g,h$ hyperbolic in $T$.
\end{enumerate}

Consider two hyperbolic elements $g, h \in \Gamma$ with axes in the $\mathbb{R}$-tree given respectively by $T_g,T_h$.
If $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, we say $T_g$ and $T_h$ \emph{intersect}.

Let $\Delta \coloneqq T_g \cap T_h$. If $\Delta$ is a non-degenerate interval (i.e., non-empty and not a singleton) we distinguish two types of overlap:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $g$ and $h$ translate in the same direction along $\Delta$, we say
that $g$ and $h$ \emph{overlap coherently};
\item if $g$ and $h$ translate in opposite
directions along $\Delta$, we say that $g$ and $h$ \emph{overlap incoherently}.
\end{itemize}
Following~\cite[Section~3.2]{CM87:GroupActions}, we say that $g,h$ form a \emph{good pair} if 
\begin{itemize} 
\item they are both hyperbolic;
\item their axes in the tree intersect and their overlap is not a singleton;
\item they overlap coherently; and
\item the length of $T_g \cap T_h$ is strictly less than $\min \{ \| g \|, \| h \| \}$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[{\cite[Remark~3.3]{CM87:GroupActions}}]
If $g,h$ are hyperbolic and $T_g,T_h$ meets in an arc of positive but finite length, then there exist integers $m>0$ and $n$ so that $g^m$ and $h^n$
form a good pair of isometries. Here the sign of $n$ is chosen so that the overlap is coherent, and the magnitudes of $m,n$ are chosen
to ensure $m \| g \|$ and $n \|h\|$ are both greater than the length  of $T_g \cap T_h$.
\label{rmk:powers_goodpair}
\end{remark}

Another way to characterize irreducibility is in terms of the existence of a pair of hyperbolic elements with bounded overlap, as shown in~\cite[Theorem~2.7]{CM87:GroupActions}
\begin{lemma}
A finitely generated group $\Gamma$  acting on a real tree $T$ is irreducible if and only if there exist hyperbolic elements $g,h$ in $T$ so that $T_g \cap T_h$ is bounded.
\label{lem:char_irred_noninter}
\end{lemma}

A useful criterion for irreducibility in terms of pairs of hyperbolic elements is as follows (see~\cite[Lemma~2.1]{CM87:GroupActions}).

\begin{lemma}
    If every pair of hyperbolic elements $(a,b)$ satisfies $T_a \cap T_b \neq \emptyset$, then the action is reducible.
    \label{lem:CM_red_criterion}
\end{lemma}

Finally, good pairs provide pairs of non-intersecting hyperbolic elements.

\begin{lemma}
  For any action of a finitely-generated group on an $\bbR$-tree, if $g,h$ form a good pair, then $gh^{-1}$ and $h^{-1}g$ are hyperbolic isometries of $T$ and $T_{gh^{-1}}, T_{h^{-1}g}$ do not intersect.
    \label{lem:oppgoodpair}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
   This is contained in the proof of~\cite[Lemma~3.4]{CM87:GroupActions}.
\end{proof}

We will consider the following classical results about the relative position of axes in a real tree. Figure~\ref{fig:trees_disjoint} and Figure~\ref{fig:trees_intersection} correspond to Fig.~2 and Fig.~3, respectively, in~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}. Similar less detailed diagrams appear in~\cite{CM87:GroupActions}.

\begin{lemma}
Let $g,h$ be hyperbolic isometries with axes $T_g$ and $T_h$.
We have the following cases.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Intersecting axes)
$T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ if and only if $\| g \| + \| h \| =\max \{ \| gh \| , \|gh^{-1}\| \}$, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:trees_intersection}.
Moreover,
\begin{itemize}
\item $T_g \cap T_h = \{ p \}$ iff $\|gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\| = \|  g \| + \|h \|$;
\item $g,h$ overlap coherently iff 
$\| g \| + \| h \| = \| gh \| >\|gh^{-1}\|$. In this case, $T_g \cap T_h = T_{gh} \cap T_{hg}$;
\item $g,h$ overlap incoherently iff 
$\| g \| + \| h \| = \| gh^{-1} \| >\|gh\|$, and in this case $T_g \cap T_h = T_{gH} \cap T_{Hg}$.
\end{itemize}
\item (Parallel axes) $T_g \cap T_h =\emptyset$ if and only if
\[
\|gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\| = \|  g \| + \|h \| + 2d(T_g,T_h),
\]
as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:trees_disjoint}.
Then, $T_{gh}, T_h$ overlap in a segment of positive length and, if $T_g \cap T_h =\emptyset$, then  $T_{gh}$ and $T_{hg}$ overlap incoherently on a segment of length $d(T_g, T_h)$.
\end{enumerate}
\label{lem:foundation_trees}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof of the translation length equalities and inequalities of (1) and (2), as well as the equality $T_g \cap T_h = T_{gh} \cap T_{hg}$ in case (1) is in~\cite[Sections~1.5, 1.8]{CM87:GroupActions}.   
The remark of part (2) about $T_{gh}, T_h$ overlapping in a segment of positive length can be found in the proof of~\cite[Theorem~2.7]{CM87:GroupActions}.
The statement about the axes $T_{gh},T_{hg}$ overlapping incoherently in the parallel axes case is~\cite[Remark~3.5]{CM87:GroupActions}.
\end{proof}

We will also use the following, found in~\cite[Lemma~6.2]{CM87:GroupActions}

\begin{lemma}
    If $g,h$ are hyperbolic and satisfy $\|g\| + \|h\| = \|gh\|$, then for every positive integers $m,n$, we have
    \[
    \|g^m h^n\| = m \| g \| + n \|h \|.
    \]
    \label{lem:powers_intersect}
\end{lemma}

Finally, we will need analogous statements for pairs $g,h \in \Gamma$ where one or both of the elements are allowed to be elliptic. These have been analyzed in~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}, we state them here in the form that will be most convenient for us.

\begin{lemma}
Suppose $g$ is elliptic and $h$ is hyperbolic. Let $T_g$ denote the set of fixed points of $g$ and $T_h$ the axis of $h$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, then $I\coloneqq T_{h} \cap T_{ghg^{-1}}$ is a non-empty interval and contains $T_g \cap T_h$.
Moreover:
\begin{itemize}
\item $T_{h}$, $T_{ghg^{-1}}$ overlap in a point or coherently if and only if $\|gh\|=\|h\|$.
\item $T_{h}$, $T_{ghg^{-1}}$ overlap incoherently  if and only if $\|h\|>\|gh\|$.
\end{itemize}
\item $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$ if and only if $\|gh\|>\|h\|$. In this case $\|gh\| = \|gh^{-1}\|= \|h \| + 2d(T_g, T_h)$.
\end{enumerate}
\label{lem:pau_ell_hyp}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The statement is a condensed version of \cite[Proposition~1.7]{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}, where we have omitted the specifics about relative positions of axes. The equality of lengths in part (2) is stated explicitly in~\cite[Section~1.5]{CM87:GroupActions}. 
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Proposition~1.8]{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}}]
If $g,h$ are both elliptic, and $T_g, T_h$ denote their sets of fixed points, we have $\|gh\|>0$ if and only if $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$. Moreover, if $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$, the shortest connecting arc between $T_g$ and $T_h$ is contained in the axis of $gh$.
\label{lem:pau_two_ell}
\end{lemma}

In general, if the translation lengths of two $\Gamma \acts T$ actions are equal, tha does not necessarily imply the trees are equivariantly isometric, but it is true if the action is minimal irreducible or dihedral by~\cite[Theorem~3.7]{CM87:GroupActions}.
In fact, if the length is non-zero, and the action is assumed irreducible, by \cite[Proposition~3.1]{CM87:GroupActions} there is a unique minimal invariant subtree (i.e., a subtree invariant under $\Gamma$ that does not contain any proper non-degenerate invariant subtree). The length function for the restriction to this minimal invariant subtree is equal to the length function of $\Gamma  \acts T$. Hence, without loss of generality, one can always restrict to the action on the minimal invariant subtree without changing the length functional. In what follows, we will always consider minimal actions.

\subsection{Surface group actions on real trees}

In this section, we assume $\Gamma = \pi_1(S)$.

It is standard that a measured lamination $\lambda$ defines a (dual) minimal irreducible $\Gamma$-action on $\mathbb{R}$-tree (see~\cite{MO84:Valuations}).
It follows from the construction that the translation length on $T_\lambda$ of $g \in \pi_1(S)$, $\| g \|$, is equal to $i(\lambda,[g])$, where $[g]$ denotes the free homotopy class of a closed curve associated to $g$.
Given a minimal action of a surface group $\Gamma$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T$, if there is a measured lamination $\lambda$ and $\Gamma$-equivariant isometry $\phi \colon T_\lambda \to T$, we say that $T$ is \emph{dual to} $\lambda$.

If $\Sigma$ is a hyperbolic structure on $S$, let $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ be the hyperbolic axes corresponding to these elements. 

Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths} first showed that a minimal action of a surface group is dual to a measured lamination if and only if 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item it is small,
    \item it \emph{preserves axis intersection}: for any pair $g,h \in \Gamma$ acting as hyperbolic isometries on $T$, we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ cross.
\end{enumerate}

Morgan-Otal asked in~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths}: if a minimal action preserves axis intersection, does it follow it is small?
Without further assumptions, the axis intersection being preserved alone does not imply in general that the action is small, as the example of a dihedral action of a surface group acting on $\mathbb{R}$ shows (this was noted by Skora in~\cite[Section~4]{Sko90:GeometricAction}).
However, in~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}, Skora showed that a minimal action $\Gamma \acts T$ of a surface group $\Gamma$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axis intersection.

The main result of this section will be showing that Skora's theorem~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction} follows readily from our main Theorems~\ref{thm:intersect} and~\ref{item:lam-char} and classical considerations from actions on real trees in~\cite{CM87:GroupActions} and~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}.

In~\cite{Sk96:Splittings},  Skora showed that $\Gamma \acts T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if the action is small. In fact, Skora showed this in the more general setting where $S$ has cusps provided that then parabolic elements in $\Gamma$ are elliptic in $T$.
It follows from this that the action being small is equivalent to it being irreducible and preserving axis intersection. 

\begin{theorem}
Let $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ be an irreducible action that preserves axis intersection. Then it follows that $\| \cdot \|$ satisfies max-smoothing.
\label{prop:small}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:small}]
We consider several cases of the max-smoothing equality, depending on if $g,h$ acts hyperbolically or elliptically, and also depending on the relative position of $\ora{g},\ora{h}$. We first deal with the case where $g,h$ are both hyperbolic, and then reduce the other cases to this one.

\begin{case}
\label{case:hyperbolic}
Suppose $g,h$ act hyperbolically on $T$.
\begin{subcase}
Suppose that $\ora{g}$ and $\ora{h}$ cross.
\end{subcase}
Then $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, and disconnected max-smoothing follows from~Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (1).
\begin{subcase}
Suppose that $\ora{g}$ and $\ora{h}$ are parallel. 
\end{subcase}
There are three subcases to consider.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=20pt]
    \item $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset, \{ p \}$. Then $\|gh\|=\|gh^{-1}\| \geq \|g\|+\|h\|$ by~Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (2).
    \item $g,h$ overlap coherently.
    Then, $\|gh\|=\|g\|+\|h\|>\|gh^{-1}\|$, by~Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (1).
    \item  $g,h$ overlap incoherently. 
    We show this case cannot occur.
    If the overlap is bounded, according to Remark~\ref{rmk:powers_goodpair} there exists $m>0,n>0$ so that $g^m$ and $h^{-n}$ form a good pair, and hence, Lemma~\ref{lem:oppgoodpair}, we have $T_{g^mh^n}$ and $T_{h^ng^m}$ do not intersect. On the other hand, since $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ are parallel, it follows by~Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation} that $\ora{g^mh^n}$ and $\ora{h^ng^m}$ cross, which by preservation of axis implies $T_{g^mh^n}$ and $T_{h^ng^m}$  intersect, a contradiction.
    Suppose now the overlap is unbounded. Here is where the assumption of irreducibility will be invoked.
    Consider the subset 
\[
\mathcal{I} \coloneqq 
\Bigl\{ (a,b) \;\Big|\;
a,b \in \pi_1(S),
\text{ parallel},
\text{ hyperbolic in } T,
\text{ overlap incoherently}
\Bigr\}.
\]
    We claim there exists a pair $(a,b) \in \mathcal{I}$ so that the overlap is bounded. Suppose not, that is, suppose all pairs in $\mathcal{I}$ have unbounded overlap. 
    First, we claim that since the action is irreducible there exist a crossing hyperbolic pair $(a,b)$ so that $T_a \cap T_b$ is bounded. Suppose not, i.e., say that for every crossing hyperbolic pair $(a,b)$, $T_a \cap T_b$ is unbounded.
    We show that for \emph{all} hyperbolic pairs $(a,b)$, $T_a \cap T_b \neq \emptyset$, which implies the action is reducible by Lemma~\ref{lem:CM_red_criterion}. Since the action preserves axis, a crossing hyperbolic pair $(a,b)$ always satisfies $T_a \cap T_b \neq \emptyset$.
    Suppose $(a,b)$ is parallel and assume, for the sake of contradiction, that $T_a \cap T_b =\emptyset$. Then $(ab,ba)$ are a crossing pair with incoherent overlap length given by $d(T_a, T_b)$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (2), a contradiction.
    If $(a,b)$ is antiparallel and $T_a \cap T_b = T_a \cap T_B = \emptyset$, repeat the same argument with the parallel pair $(a,B)$. 
    
    We have thus shown there exists a crossing hyperbolic pair $(a,b)$ with $T_a \cap T_b$ bounded. Since the action preserves axis, $T_a \cap T_b \neq \emptyset$. If $T_a \cap T_b=\{ * \}$, we replace $a$ by $ab$, to get the pair of crossing $(ab,b)$, with non-degenerate overlap.
    We can further assume that the overlap is coherent up to exchanging the orientation of $b$, and take powers, by Remark~\ref{rmk:powers_goodpair}, to ensure it is a good pair.
     Hence, without loss of generality, we can assume that $(a,b)$ is crossing and a good pair. Thus, by Lemma~\ref{lem:oppgoodpair}, we have that $aB$ is hyperbolic and $(aB,Ba)$ are parallel.
     Hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (2), $aB^2a, Ba^2B$ are hyperbolic and overlap incoherently with bounded overlap, a contradiction.
    \label{subcase:bad_case}
    \end{enumerate}
        \end{case}


\begin{case} Suppose $g$ is elliptic and $h$ hyperbolic.
\begin{subcase}
Suppose $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ cross.
\end{subcase} Then $\ora{ghg^{-1}}$ and $\ora{h}$ are parallel, and $ghg^{-1}$ and $h$ act hyperbolically on $T$.
By Subcase~\ref{subcase:bad_case}, $T_{ghg^{-1}}$ and $T_{h}$ cannot intersect incoherently, and hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_ell_hyp}, $\| gh \| \geq \|h\|$. We claim that $\| gh \|= \|h\|$. Indeed,  if $\| gh \| > \| h \|$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_ell_hyp} (2) this is equivalent to $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$, with 
\[
  \|gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\|=\|h\| + 2d(T_g,T_h).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}, it follows that $gh, h^{-1}g$ overlap, and since $\|(gh)(h^{-1}g)\| = \|g^2\| =0$, it follows the overlap is incoherent. 
Hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}
\begin{equation}
  \|(gh)(g^{-1}h)\|=\|(gh)(h^{-1}g)^{-1}\| = \| gh\| +\| h^{-1}g\|=2\|gh\|.
\label{eq:intermediate}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, since $T_g \cap T_h=\emptyset$, it follows that $T_h \cap T_{ghg^{-1}}=\emptyset$, thus
\[
  \|(ghg^{-1})h)\|=\|(ghg^{-1})h^{-1})\| = \| (gh)(hg)^{-1}\|.
\]
Hence, combining this with Equation~\ref{eq:intermediate}, we get $\| (gh)(hg)^{-1}\|=2\|gh\|$, and thus $gh,hg$ overlap incoherently. But since $gh,hg$ are hyperbolic and parallel, this is a contradiction by Subcase~\ref{case:hyperbolic}~\ref{subcase:bad_case}.
\begin{subcase} Suppose $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ are parallel. \end{subcase} Then $gh, h$ are parallel by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}. Since $\|(gh)h^{-1}\| = \|g\|=0$, it follows $gh$, $h$ overlap incoherently, by Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}, and thus, again, by Subcase~\ref{case:hyperbolic}~\ref{subcase:bad_case}, it is a contradiction.
\end{case}
\begin{case}
Suppose both $g,h$ act elliptically.
\begin{subcase}
Suppose $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ cross.
\end{subcase}
Then we claim that $\|gh\|=0$, which, by~Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_two_ell} is equivalent to $T_g \cap T_h= \emptyset$. Suppose $\|gh\|>0$
Since $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ cross, $\ora{gh},\ora{hg}$ are parallel.
On the other hand, by~Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_two_ell}, $gh$ and $hg$ overlap on the interval $[x,y]$, the shortest arc connecting $x \in T_g$ to $y \in T_h$, in opposite orientations.
This is a contradiction by Subcase~\ref{case:hyperbolic}~\ref{subcase:bad_case}.
\begin{subcase}
Suppose $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ are parallel. \end{subcase} Then it suffices to show that $\| gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\|$. Let $S=[x,y]$ denote the segment connecting $T_g$ and $T_h$.
Let $[x,z]=g S \cap S$, which is a segment fixed by $g$.
We have
\[
\|hg\| = d(z, hg z) = d(z,h(z)) = d(z,hg^{-1}(z))=\|hg^{-1}\|,
\]
where the last inequality is because $T_g=T_{g^{-1}}$.
\end{case}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
One could hope to bypass cases 2 and 3 by approximating by actions consisting solely of hyperbolic isometries (case 1), but this is would require some extra work.
Indeed, note that if an action $\Gamma \acts T$ satisfies that all elements
act hyperbolically, it follows the action is free.
Observe that, in particular, 
minimal free actions $\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma, T)$ are small and, in fact, ``very small'', in the sense of Cohen-Lustig~\cite{CL95:Verysmall}, which roughly means that the stabilizers of tripods must be trivial.
Culler-Morgan~\cite{CM87:GroupActions} proved that the subspace of minimal small actions of any finitely generated group is closed in the space of all actions, and Cohen-Lustig~\cite{CL95:Verysmall} proved that the space of very small actions $\operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T)$ is a closed subspace of small actions $\operatorname{S}(\Gamma, T)$, and the closure of free actions is contained in the space of very small actions:
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma, T)) \subset \operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T) \subset \operatorname{S}(\Gamma, T).
\]
For free groups, by a result of Bestvina-Feighn~\cite{BF11:OuterLimits} it is known that $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma, T)) = \operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T)$, and it is also known that $\operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T) \subsetneq \operatorname{S}(\Gamma, T)$.
For surface groups, both inclusions above are equalities, a fact that follows \emph{a posteriori} once one knows all small actions are dual to measured laminations: filling measured laminations (those intersecting all simple closed curves) are dense in the space of measured laminations, and their dual tree actions are free.
It then follows that very small actions are equal to small actions in this case. This can also be seen directly by definition, since stabilizers of tripods correspond to stabilizers of ideal triangle complementary regions of the lamination, which are the M\"obius transformation corresponding to the identity.
\end{remark}

We follow-up with some consequences of this proof.

\begin{proposition}
  Let $\mathcal{I}$ denote the set of parallel hyperbolic pairs of isometries with incoherent overlap.
 $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ preserves axis intersection if and only if either $\mathcal{I}$ is empty or all $(g,h) \in \mathcal{I}$ have unbounded overlap.
 \label{prop:preserves_axis}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:small} shows that if $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ preserves axis intersection, or all $(g,h) \in \mathcal{I}$ have unbounded incoherent overlap.
The converse is also true: if $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ does not preserve axis intersection, it means there exists a pair of hyperbolic elements $(g,h)$ so that $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$. This implies, by Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (2) that $T_{gh},T_{hg}$ overlap incoherently with overlap of length $d(T_g,T_h)$, and since $(gh,hg)$ are parallel and hyperbolic, we found a pair of parallel hyperbolic isometries with bounded incoherent overlap, which proves the other implication.
\end{proof}

This allows us to give another characterization of small surface actions on real trees.

\begin{theorem}
$\pi_1(S) \acts T$ is irreducible and preserves axis intersection if and only if no pair of parallel hyperbolic elements $(g,h)$ have incoherent overlap.
\label{thm:axis_incoherent_characterization}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
One implication follows from the proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:small}.
In the other direction, it follows by Proposition~\ref{prop:preserves_axis} that $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ preserves axis intersection. Finally, it cannot be reducible since otherwise it would have hyperbolic pairs with unbounded incoherent overlap.
\end{proof}

We conclude this section showing that reducible actions satisfy disconnected smoothing but never satisfy connected smoothing.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\Gamma=\pi_1(S)$ be a surface group acting on a real tree $T$. Let $\| \cdot \|$ denote the translation length of this action.
If  $(\Gamma, T)$ is reducible, then:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\| \cdot \| \colon \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ does not satisfy connected smoothing but it satisfies disconnected smoothing.
    \item $-\| \cdot \| \colon \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ does not satisfy disconnected smoothing but it satisfies connected smoothing.
\end{itemize}
\label{lem:red_not_smoothing}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We start with a reducible action and show it cannot satisfy smoothing.
If the action is reducible, every pair $(g,h)$ of elements acting hyperbolically on $T$ has axes overlapping in a ray. By~\cite[Proposition~1.6~(3)]{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}, either $g,h$ overlap coherently, in which case
\begin{equation}
\|gh \|=\| g \| + \| h \| \mbox{ and } \|gh^{-1} \| = |\|g \|- \| h \| |,
\label{eq:reducible1}
\end{equation}
or $g,h$ overlap incoherently, in which case
\begin{equation}
\|gh^{-1} \| =\| g\| + \| h \| \mbox{ and } \| gh \| = | \| g \| - \| h \||.
\label{eq:reducible2}
\end{equation}
Note that Equations~\ref{eq:reducible1}~and~\ref{eq:reducible2} hold for any pair of hyperbolic isometries $(g,h)$. In particular, they hold if $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ cross.
Hence, $\| \cdot \|$ always satisfies disconnected smoothing. Similarly, they also hold if $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ are parallel. Thus, $-\| \cdot \|$ always satisfies connected smoothing.

Now, take a pair $a,x \in \Gamma$, so that $(a,x)$ $R$-cross.
WLOG, assume $\| a \|<\| x \|$.

\begin{claim}
Without loss of generality, we can furthermore assume that $a$ and $x$ overlap coherently.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Claim]
If $a,x$ overlap incoherently, then note $(xa^{-1}, ax)$ also $R$-cross, by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}. 
Then $\| ax \| =\| x\|- \|a \|$ and $\| xa^{-1} \| =\| a \| + \| x \|$. Since $\| (xa^{-1})(ax) \| =\| x^2 \|$ is equal to $\| ax \| + \| xa^{-1} \|= 2\| x \|$, $ax$ and $xa^{-1}$ overlap coherently, and they do it in a subray of the overlap between $a$ and $x$.
\end{proof}
If $(a,x)$ R-cross, we use Equation~\ref{eq:cross-split} in Proposition~\ref{prop:join-split-geom}, with $m=-n$, $a=b$, and $n$ large enough, and choosing some admissible $r$ for the inequalities there, by smoothing,
\begin{equation}
 \| ax^nax^{-n} \| \geq \| ax^{n+r} \| + \| ax^{-n-r} \|.
 \label{eq:unoriented1}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, by a similar argument as in the proof of the above Claim, $ax^n, ax^{-n}$ overlap incoherently, so, we have that the left hand side is
\begin{equation}
\|ax^nax^{-n} \| = \| ax^n \| - \|ax^{-n} \| = \| a \| + n\| x \| - (n\| x \|-\|a\|) = 2\|a\|
\label{eq:lhs1}
\end{equation}
where we used that Equation~\ref{eq:reducible2} applies to the pair ($ax^n,ax^{-n}$) overlapping incoherently in the first equality, and Equation~\ref{eq:reducible1} for the coherent pair $(a,x^n)$ and Equation~\ref{eq:reducible2} for the incoherent pair $(a,x^{-n})$ in the second equality.
A similar computation, yields the following result for the right hand side:
\begin{equation}
\|ax^{n+r}\|=\|a\|+(n+r)\|x\| + (n+r)\|x\|-\|a\| = 2(n+r)\|x\|.
\label{eq:rhs1}
\end{equation}
Combining Equation~\ref{eq:unoriented1} with Equations~\ref{eq:lhs1} and~\ref{eq:rhs1} we get $\|a \| \geq (n+r)\|x\|$, which contradicts $\|a\| < \|x\|$.
Hence, $\| \cdot \|$ (and, similarly, $-\| \cdot \|$) cannot satisfy smoothing. The claims then follow in each case.
\end{proof}

Hence, by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} a reducible action cannot be dual to a geodesic current, since smoothing (both connected and disconnected) is a necessary condition. Another example of curve functional satisfying disconnected smoothing but not connected smoothing in the setting of the linear extension of extremal length can be seen in Example~\ref{ex:linEL}.
