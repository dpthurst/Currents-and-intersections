\section{Skora's theorem: \texorpdfstring{$\protect\mathbb{R}$-trees}{Rtrees} dual to measured laminations}
\label{sec:skora}


As a final application of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, we give a new
characterization of small actions of surface groups on
$\mathbb{R}$-trees, and in turn we reprove a classical theorem of
Skora~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}.

\subsection{Finitely generated groups acting on real trees} \hspace{1cm} \\

\textbf{Real trees} We start by recalling some basic notions of real trees.
An \emph{$\mathbb{R}$-tree} is a non-empty metric space $T$ such that for
all $x,y \in T$, the intersection of all connected subsets containing
both $x$ and $y$ is isometric to the interval $[0, d(x, y)]$.  This
intersection is denoted
$[x, y]$, and called an \emph{arc} connecting $x$ and $y$.
In particular, note that arcs are non-empty, any two points are connected by a unique arc. When an arc is a singleton, we say it is \emph{degenerate}, otherwise we say it is \emph{non-degenerate}.
An equivalent way to characterize real trees is as geodesic metric space $X$ satisfying the \emph{$0$-hyperbolic Gromov point condition}: for any four-tuple of points $x,y,z,w \in X$, we have
\[
d(x,z) + d(y,w) = \max \{ d(x,y) + d(z,w), d(x,z) + d(y,w) \}.
\]
A \emph{ray} in a real tree is the image of an isometric embedding of the ray $[0,\infty)$. If $p$ is the image of $0$ under this embedding,
the ray \emph{emanates} from $p$. The \emph{space of ends} of a metric space $T$ is the limit of the inverse system $\pi_0(T-B)$ where
$B$ ranges over the closed and bounded subsets of $T$. Let $p$ a be a fixed point of $T$. For every point of the limit there is a ray emanating from $p$,
and a cofinal sequence of components of the complement of closed and bounded sets that represent the limit point, such that every
point in the sequence meets the ray, and this ray is uniquely determined. Hence, there is a one-to-one correspondence with rays of $T$ emanating from $p$ and ends
of $T$. Given two distinct ends of $T$, the corresponding rays meet in an interval with $p$ as endpoint. The union of these two rays minus
the interior of their intersection is a line in $T$. Conversely, given any line in $T$, the two oppositely oriented rays emanating from any point
in the line determine two distinct ends of the tree, and these ends do not depend on the choice of point. We will say this line \emph{joins} the two ends.
By the definition of tree, there is only one line connecting any pair of ends.


\textbf{Actions on real trees} If $\Gamma$ is a finitely generated group, a $\Gamma$-tree is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree together with a group action $\Gamma \acts T$ by isometries.
There are two types of isometries: those with fixed points, called \emph{elliptic}, and those without fixed points, called \emph{hyperbolic}.
Given $g\in \Gamma$, let $\| g\|\coloneqq\inf_{x \in T} d(x,gx)$, and let $T_g \coloneqq \{ x\in T : d(x,gx)=\| g\| \}$ be the \emph{characteristic set} of $g$ which is a closed, non-empty subtree of $T$ invariant under the action of $g$.
If $g$ is elliptic, then $\| g \| =0$. The converse is also true;
see~\cite[Section~1.3]{CM87:GroupActions}. In this case, $T_g$ is the
set of fixed points of~$g$.
If $g$ is hyperbolic, then $\| g \| >0$, $T_g$ is isometric to the
real line and $g$ translates along $T_g$ by distance $\| g\|$; in this
case we call $T_g$ the \emph{axis} of $g$.

The translation lengths of any action $\Gamma \acts T$ can be characterized by the following relation, reminiscent of the $0$-hyperbolic condition. 
Even though we will not use it in the proofs, it is helpful to keep it in mind.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely generated group and $f \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{R}$ be a conjugacy class invariant function.
Then $f$ is the translation length function of
an action $\Gamma \acts T$ on a real tree if and only if for any pair $g,h \in \Gamma$, the maximum among the following three quantities appears (at least) twice:
\begin{equation}
\|g\|+\|h\|, \quad \|gh\|, \quad \|gH\|.
\label{eq:maxcondition_tree}
\end{equation}
\label{fact:max_twice}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The fact that the translation length of an action $\Gamma \acts T$
satisfies the condition in \eqref{eq:maxcondition_tree} follows from
more precise relations developed
in~\cite{CM87:GroupActions,Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}, which we will write
as a sequence of lemmas below. Specifically, for hyperbolic pairs
$(g,h)$, it follows from~Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}; if $g$ is
elliptic and $h$ hyperbolic, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_ell_hyp}; and, if
both are elliptic, by~Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_two_ell}.

The other implication follows by a deep result of Parry~\cite{Par91:Translation}. It is straightforward to show that the condition in Equation~\ref{eq:maxcondition_tree} implies the Culler-Morgan axioms assumed in the main characterization theorem of Parry, and, hence, it also
characterizes translation lengths of actions on real trees among all conjugacy class invariant functionals.
\end{proof}

We say that $\Gamma \acts T$ is \emph{small} if the stabilizer of any (non-degenerate) arc is (virtually) cyclic.
We say that $\Gamma \acts T$ is \emph{minimal} if there is no proper non-trivial subtree of $T$ that is $\Gamma$-invariant.

An action of a finitely generated group $\Gamma$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree with non-trivial translation length $\| \cdot \|$ is one of three types~\cite[Page~583]{CM87:GroupActions}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fixed end: $\| \cdot \|$ trivial on $[G,G]$ (equivalently $\Gamma$ fixes an end of $T$ ~\cite[Theorem~2.2, Corollary~2.3]{CM87:GroupActions}).
\item Dihedral: $\| \cdot \|$ non-trivial on $[G,G]$, but $\| [g,h] \|=0$ for all $g,h$ hyperbolic in $T$ (equivalently $\Gamma$ fixes a line of $T$ inverting orientation~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{CM87:GroupActions}).
\item Irreducible:  $\| [g,h] \| \neq 0$ for some $g,h$ hyperbolic in $T$.
\end{enumerate}

Consider two hyperbolic elements $g, h \in \Gamma$ with axes in the $\mathbb{R}$-tree given respectively by $T_g,T_h$.
If $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, we say $T_g$ and $T_h$ \emph{intersect}.

Let $\Delta \coloneqq T_g \cap T_h$. If $\Delta$ is a non-degenerate interval (i.e., non-empty and not a singleton) we distinguish two types of overlap:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $g$ and $h$ translate in the same direction along $\Delta$, we say
that $g$ and $h$ \emph{overlap coherently};
\item if $g$ and $h$ translate in opposite
directions along $\Delta$, we say that $g$ and $h$ \emph{overlap incoherently}.
\end{itemize}
Following~\cite[Section~3.2]{CM87:GroupActions}, we say that $g,h$ form a \emph{good pair} if 
\begin{itemize} 
\item they are both hyperbolic;
\item their axes in the tree intersect and their overlap is a non-degenerate arc;
\item they overlap coherently; and
\item the length of $T_g \cap T_h$ is strictly less than $\min \{ \| g \|, \| h \| \}$.
\end{itemize}

The following appears in~\cite[Remark~3.3]{CM87:GroupActions}.

\begin{lemma}
If $g,h$ are hyperbolic and $T_g,T_h$ meets in an arc of positive but finite length, then there exist integers $m>0$ and $n$ so that $g^m$ and $h^n$
form a good pair of isometries. Here the sign of $n$ is chosen so that the overlap is coherent, and the magnitudes of $m,n$ are chosen
to ensure $m \| g \|$ and $n \|h\|$ are both greater than the length  of $T_g \cap T_h$.
\label{lem:powers_goodpair}
\end{lemma}

Another way to characterize irreducibility is in terms of the existence of a pair of hyperbolic elements with bounded overlap, as shown in~\cite[Theorem~2.7]{CM87:GroupActions}
\begin{lemma}
A finitely generated group $\Gamma$  acting on a real tree $T$ is irreducible if and only if there exist hyperbolic elements $g,h$ in $T$ so that $T_g \cap T_h$ is bounded.
\label{lem:char_irred_noninter}
\end{lemma}

A useful criterion for irreducibility in terms of the existence of a hyperbolic element with non-empty intersection with all other hyperbolic elements is the following (see~\cite[Lemma~2.1]{CM87:GroupActions}).

\begin{lemma}
    If there exists a hyperbolic element $g \in \Gamma$ so that for all hyperbolic $h \in \Gamma$ we have  $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, then the action is reducible.
    \label{lem:CM_red_criterion}
\end{lemma}

Finally, good pairs provide pairs of non-intersecting hyperbolic elements.

We will consider the following classical results about the relative position of axes in a real tree. Figure~\ref{fig:trees_disjoint} and Figure~\ref{fig:trees_intersection} correspond to Fig.~2 and Fig.~3, respectively, in~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}. Similar less detailed diagrams appear in~\cite{CM87:GroupActions}.

\begin{lemma}
Let $g,h$ be hyperbolic isometries with axes $T_g$ and $T_h$.
We have the following cases.
\begin{enumerate}[(\arabic*)]
\item \label{item:tree-intersect}(Intersecting axes)
$T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ if and only if $\| g \| + \| h \| =\max \{ \| gh \| , \|gh^{-1}\| \}$, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:trees_intersection}.
Moreover,
\begin{itemize}
\item $T_g \cap T_h = \{ p \}$ iff $\|gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\| = \|  g \| + \|h \|$;
\item $g,h$ overlap coherently iff 
$\| g \| + \| h \| = \| gh \| >\|gh^{-1}\|$. In this case, $T_g \cap T_h = T_{gh} \cap T_{hg}$;
\item $g,h$ overlap incoherently iff 
$\| g \| + \| h \| = \| gh^{-1} \| >\|gh\|$. In this case $T_g \cap T_h = T_{gH} \cap T_{Hg}$.
\end{itemize}
\item \label{item:tree-parallel}(Parallel axes) $T_g \cap T_h =\emptyset$ if and only if
\[
\|gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\| = \|  g \| + \|h \| + 2d(T_g,T_h),
\]
as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:trees_disjoint}.
Then, $T_{gh}, T_h$ overlap in a segment of positive length and, if $T_g \cap T_h =\emptyset$, then  $T_{gh}$ and $T_{hg}$ overlap incoherently on a segment of length $d(T_g, T_h)$.
\end{enumerate}
\label{lem:foundation_trees}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof of the translation length equalities and inequalities of (1) and (2), as well as the equality $T_g \cap T_h = T_{gh} \cap T_{hg}$ in case (1) is in~\cite[Sections~1.5, 1.8]{CM87:GroupActions}.

\end{proof}

We will also use the following, found in~\cite[Lemma~6.2]{CM87:GroupActions}

\begin{lemma}
    If $g,h$ are hyperbolic and satisfy $\|g\| + \|h\| = \|gh\|$, then for every positive integers $m,n$, we have
    \[
    \|g^m h^n\| = m \| g \| + n \|h \|.
    \]
    \label{lem:powers_intersect}
\end{lemma}

Finally, we will need analogous statements for pairs $g,h \in \Gamma$ where one or both of the elements are allowed to be elliptic. These have been analyzed in~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}. We state them here in the form that will be most convenient for us. This makes Fact~\ref{fact:max_twice}---that the maximum among $\|gh\|,\|gH\|$ and $\|g\|+\|h\|$ appears at least twice---more apparent.
\begin{lemma}
Suppose $g$ is elliptic and $h$ is hyperbolic.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, then $I\coloneqq T_{h} \cap T_{ghg^{-1}}$ is a non-empty interval and contains $T_g \cap T_h$.
  Moreover:
\begin{itemize}
\item $T_h$ and $T_{ghg^{-1}}$ overlap in a point or coherently iff
\[
\norm{gh} = \norm{h} > \norm{gH}.
\]
\item $T_h$ and $T_{ghg^{-1}}$ overlap incoherently iff
\[
\norm{gH} = \|h\| > \|gh\|
\]
\end{itemize}

\item\label{item:ell-hyp-disjoint} $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$ iff $\|gh\|>\|h\|$.  
In this case
\[
\|gh\| = \|gh^{-1}\| = \|h\| + 2\,d(T_g, T_h).
\]

\end{enumerate}
\label{lem:pau_ell_hyp}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The statement is a condensed version of \cite[Proposition~1.7]{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}, where we have omitted the specifics about relative positions of axes. The equality of lengths in part (2) is stated explicitly in~\cite[Section~1.5]{CM87:GroupActions}. 
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Proposition~1.8]{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}}]
  If $g,h$ are both elliptic, we have $\|gh\|>0$ if and only if
  $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$. Moreover, if $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$,
  the shortest connecting arc between $T_g$ and $T_h$ is contained in
  $T_{gh}$.
\label{lem:pau_two_ell}
\end{lemma}

Finally, the following result, proven in~\cite[Fait, page 854]{Gui05:Coeur} will be useful.

\begin{lemma}
Let $g,h$ be two hyperbolic elements of $\Gamma$ so that
$T_{ghg^{-1}}$ and $T_h$ do not share a ray, and let $I$ be a non-degenerate arc contained
in $T_h$. Then for large enough $k \geq 0$, $h^k g h^k$ is hyperbolic
and its axis contains $I$, translating along the same direction as $h$.
\label{lem:make_coherent}
\end{lemma}

In general, if the translation lengths of two $\Gamma \acts T$ actions are equal, that does not necessarily imply the trees are equivariantly isometric, but it is true if the action is minimal irreducible or dihedral by~\cite[Theorem~3.7]{CM87:GroupActions}.
In fact, if the length is non-zero, and the action is assumed irreducible, by \cite[Proposition~3.1]{CM87:GroupActions} there is a unique minimal invariant subtree. The length function for the restriction to this minimal invariant subtree is equal to the length function of $\Gamma  \acts T$. Hence, without loss of generality, one can always restrict to the action on the minimal invariant subtree without changing the length functional. In what follows, we will always consider minimal actions.

\subsection{Surface group actions on real trees}

In this section, we assume $\Gamma = \pi_1(S)$.

It is standard that a measured lamination $\lambda$ defines a (dual) minimal irreducible $\Gamma$-action on $\mathbb{R}$-tree (see~\cite{MO84:Valuations}).
It follows from the construction that the translation length on $T_\lambda$ of $g \in \pi_1(S)$, $\| g \|$, is equal to $i(\lambda,[g])$, where $[g]$ denotes the free homotopy class of a closed curve associated to $g$.
Given a minimal action of a surface group $\Gamma$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T$, if there is a measured lamination $\lambda$ and $\Gamma$-equivariant isometry $\phi \colon T_\lambda \to T$, we say that $T$ is \emph{dual to} $\lambda$.

If $\Sigma$ is a hyperbolic structure on $S$, let $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ be the hyperbolic axes corresponding to these elements. 

Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths} first showed that a minimal action of a surface group is dual to a measured lamination if and only if 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item it is small,
    \item it \emph{preserves axis intersection}: for any pair $g,h \in \Gamma$ acting as hyperbolic isometries on $T$, we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ cross.
\end{enumerate}

Morgan-Otal asked in~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths}: if a minimal action preserves axis intersection, does it follow it is small?
Without further assumptions, the axis intersection being preserved alone does not imply in general that the action is small, as the example of a dihedral action of a surface group acting on $\mathbb{R}$ shows (this was noted by Skora in~\cite[Section~4]{Sko90:GeometricAction}).
However, in~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}, Skora showed that a minimal action $\Gamma \acts T$ of a surface group $\Gamma$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axis intersection.

The main result of this section will be showing that Skora's theorem~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction} follows readily from our main Theorems~\ref{thm:intersect} and \ref{mainthm:apps}~\ref{item:lam-char} and classical considerations from actions on real trees in~\cite{CM87:GroupActions} and~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}.

In~\cite{Sk96:Splittings},  Skora showed that $\Gamma \acts T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if the action is small. In fact, Skora showed this in the more general setting where $S$ has cusps provided that then parabolic elements in $\Gamma$ are elliptic in $T$.
It follows from this that the action being small is equivalent to it being irreducible and preserving axis intersection.


\begin{lemma}
Suppose $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ preserves axis intersection. If $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ is irreducible, then for all hyperbolic elements $g \in \pi_1(S)$, there exist a hyperbolic element $h \in \pi_1(S)$ R-crossing $g$ so that $T_g \cap T_h$ is bounded.
\label{lem:if_unbounded_then_reducible}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    We will prove the contra-positive: if there exists a hyperbolic
    element $g$ so that for every hyperbolic element $h$ R-crossing
    $g$, the intersection $T_g \cap T_h$ is unbounded, we will deduce
    that the action must be reducible. (Note $T_g \cap T_h$ cannot be
    empty since the action on $T$ is axis-preserving.)
    
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:CM_red_criterion}, it suffices to show that for any hyperbolic element $h$ so that $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ are parallel (or anti-parallel), $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$.

    For the sake of contradiction, suppose $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ are parallel and $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$. By density of axes of hyperbolic elements, let $k \in \pi_1(S)$ so that $\ora{k}$ R-crosses $\ora{g}$ and $\ora{h}$, as in Figure~\ref{fig:k_crossing}. Since $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ preserves axis, $T_k$ intersects both $T_g$ and $T_h$. Thus, by hypothesis, $T_g \cap T_k$ is unbounded.
      
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:measures_parallelogram}, for $n$ large enough,
    $\ora{h^nk^n}$ R-crosses $\ora{g}$. See
    Figure~\ref{fig:tree_k_crossing} for an illustration. In
    particular, since the action preserves axis, $T_{h^nk^n}$ and
    $T_g$ must intersect. But this forces $T_g \cap T_h$ to intersect,
    as desired. 
    
    If $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ were anti-parallel, we could proceed similarly with the parallel pair $(\ora{g},\ora{H})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:parallel-incoherent}
  Let $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ be an irreducible action that preserves axis
  intersection. Then, for $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$, it cannot happen that
  $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ are parallel, and $T_g,T_h$ intersect incoherently.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    If the overlap is bounded, according to
    Lemma~\ref{lem:powers_goodpair} there exists $m>0,n>0$ so that
    $g^m$ and $h^{-n}$ form a good pair, and hence, by
    Lemma~\ref{lem:oppgoodpair}, we have $T_{g^mh^n}$ and $T_{h^ng^m}$
    do not intersect. On the other hand, since $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ are
    parallel, it follows by~Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation} that
    $\ora{g^mh^n}$ and $\ora{h^ng^m}$ cross, which by preservation of
    axis implies $T_{g^mh^n}$ and $T_{h^ng^m}$  intersect, a
    contradiction.

    Suppose now the overlap is unbounded and incoherent. We will use
    the assumption of irreducibility to construct another pair with
    bounded overlap. Suppose, without essential loss of generality,
    that $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ are R-parallel,
    and that $T_g \cap T_h$ includes the attracting end of $T_g$. By
    Lemma~\ref{lem:if_unbounded_then_reducible}, there exists
    $c_0 \in \pi_1(S)$ acting hyperbolically on~$T$ so that
    $T_{c_0} \cap T_g$ is empty or bounded, and so that
    $(\ora{c_0},\ora{g})$ R-cross.
    % (For other configurations of
    % $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ and $T_g,T_h$, we may want
    % $(\ora{c_0},\ora{g})$ to L-cross instead.)
    By
    Lemma~\ref{lem:make_coherent}, if we set $c_1=g^kc_0 g^k$ for
    $k \geq 0$ large enough, the overlap between $T_{c_1}$ and $T_g$
    is coherent and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod}, $\ora{c_1}$ still
    R-crosses $\ora{g}$. Letting $c_2=g^n c_1 g^{-n}$ for $n$ large
    enough, we can ensure two things. On the one hand, the endpoints of
    $\ora{c_2}$ are near $g^+$ on the boundary of the disk and thus
    $(\ora{c_2},\ora{h})$ are
    parallel. On the other hand, the same conjugation also insures
    that the (still bounded and coherent) intersection between
    $T_{c_2}$ and $T_g$ is near the attracting end of $T_g$ and thus
    $T_{c_2}$ has bounded, incoherent intersection with $T_h$. Thus
    $c_2$ and $h$ form a pair that is parallel in the disk and
    intersect incoherently on the tree, a contradiction.
   See Figure~\ref{fig:tree_bounded_incoherent} for a diagram of the
   configuration.
   \end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:small}]
We consider several cases of the max-smoothing equality, depending on if $g,h$ acts hyperbolically or elliptically on the tree, and also depending on the relative position of $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ in the disk. We first deal with the case where $g,h$ are both hyperbolic, and then reduce the other cases to this one.

\begin{case}
\label{case:hyperbolic}
In the action on~$T$, $g,h$ are hyperbolic.
\end{case}
\begin{subcase}
$\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ cross.
\end{subcase}
Then $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, and disconnected max-smoothing follows from~Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}\ref{item:tree-intersect}.
\begin{subcase}
$\ora{g},\ora{h}$ are parallel and $T_g \cap T_h$ is empty or a single point.
\end{subcase}
Then $\|gh\|=\|gh^{-1}\| \geq \|g\|+\|h\|$
by~Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}\ref{item:tree-parallel}.
\begin{subcase}
$\ora{g},\ora{h}$ are parallel and $T_g,T_h$ overlap coherently.
\end{subcase}
    Then $\|gh\|=\|g\|+\|h\|>\|gh^{-1}\|$, by
    Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}\ref{item:tree-intersect}.
    \begin{subcase}
      $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ are parallel and $T_g,T_h$ overlap incoherently.
    \end{subcase}
    This cannot happen by Lemma~\ref{lem:parallel-incoherent}.

\begin{case}
  In the action on~$T$, $g$ is elliptic and $h$ is hyperbolic.
\end{case}
\begin{subcase}
$\ora{g},\ora{h}$ cross.
\end{subcase}
\DPTtodo{Read this case again}
Then $\ora{ghg^{-1}}$ and $\ora{h}$ are parallel, and $ghg^{-1}$ and $h$ act hyperbolically on $T$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:parallel-incoherent}, $T_{ghg^{-1}}$ and $T_{h}$
cannot intersect incoherently. If they intersect coherently or in a
point then, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_ell_hyp}, $\| gh \| = \|h\|$, and we
are done. The only remaining option is that $T_{ghg^{-1}}$ and $T_{h}$ do not intersect. Then by
Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (2), we get
\begin{equation}
\| (gh)(hg)^{-1}\| = \| (ghg^{-1})h^{-1} \| = \| (ghg^{-1})h \| >2\|h\|>0.
\label{eq:intermediate0}
\end{equation}
Also, since $\| (gh)(g^{-1}h)^{-1}\| = \|(gh)(h^{-1}g)\| = \|g^2\| =0$, it follows $gh, g^{-1}h$ overlap coherently.
Hence, by Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}
\begin{equation}
  \|(gh)(g^{-1}h)\| = \| gh\| +\| gh^{-1}\|=2\|gh\|,
\label{eq:intermediate}
\end{equation}
where we used $\|gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\|$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_ell_hyp}\ref{item:ell-hyp-disjoint} since $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$, in the last equality.
Combining  Equation~\ref{eq:intermediate0} and Equation~\ref{eq:intermediate}, it follows that $gh,hg$ overlap incoherently. But since $gh,hg$ are hyperbolic and parallel, this is a contradiction by Lemma~\ref{lem:parallel-incoherent}.
\begin{subcase} $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ are parallel. \end{subcase}
Then $(\ora{gh}, \ora{h})$ are parallel by
Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}. Since
$\|(gh)h^{-1}\| = \|g\|=0$, it follows $gh$, $h$ overlap incoherently, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees}, and thus, again, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:parallel-incoherent}, it is a contradiction.
\begin{case}
  In the action on~$T$, $g,h$ are both elliptic.
\end{case}
\begin{subcase}
$\ora{g},\ora{h}$ cross.
\end{subcase}
Then we claim that $\|gh\|=0$, which, by~Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_two_ell} is equivalent to $T_g \cap T_h= \emptyset$. Suppose $\|gh\|>0$.
Since $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ cross, $\ora{gh},\ora{hg}$ are parallel by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}.
On the other hand, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pau_two_ell}, $T_{gh}$ and
$T_{hg}$ overlap on the shortest arc connecting
$T_g$ to $T_h$, and are incoherent on that arc.
This is a contradiction by Lemma~\ref{lem:parallel-incoherent}.
\begin{subcase}
  $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ are parallel. \end{subcase}
Then it suffices to show that $\| gh\| = \| gh^{-1}\|$. Let $S=[x,y]$ denote the segment connecting $T_g$ and $T_h$.
Let $[x,z]=g S \cap S$, which is a segment fixed by $g$.
We have
\[
\|hg\| = d(z, hg z) = d(z,h(z)) = d(z,hg^{-1}(z))=\|hg^{-1}\|,
\]
where the last inequality is because $T_g=T_{g^{-1}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
One could hope to bypass cases 2 and 3 by approximating by actions consisting solely of hyperbolic isometries (case 1), but this is would require some extra work.
Indeed, note that if an action $\Gamma \acts T$ satisfies that all elements
act hyperbolically, it follows the action is free.
Observe that, in particular, 
minimal free actions $\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma, T)$ are small and, in fact, ``very small'', in the sense of Cohen-Lustig~\cite{CL95:Verysmall}, which roughly means that the stabilizers of tripods must be trivial.
Culler-Morgan~\cite{CM87:GroupActions} proved that the subspace of minimal small actions of any finitely generated group is closed in the space of all actions, and Cohen-Lustig~\cite{CL95:Verysmall} proved that the space of very small actions $\operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T)$ is a closed subspace of small actions $\operatorname{S}(\Gamma, T)$, and the closure of free actions is contained in the space of very small actions:
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma, T)) \subset \operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T) \subset \operatorname{S}(\Gamma, T).
\]
For free groups, by a result of Bestvina-Feighn~\cite{BF11:OuterLimits} it is known that $\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma, T)) = \operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T)$, and it is also known that $\operatorname{VS}(\Gamma,T) \subsetneq \operatorname{S}(\Gamma, T)$.
For surface groups, both inclusions above are equalities, a fact that follows \emph{a posteriori} once one knows all small actions are dual to measured laminations: filling measured laminations (those intersecting all simple closed curves) are dense in the space of measured laminations, and their dual tree actions are free.
It then follows that very small actions are equal to small actions in this case. This can also be seen directly by definition, since stabilizers of tripods correspond to stabilizers of ideal triangle complementary regions of the lamination, which are the M\"obius transformations corresponding to the identity.
\end{remark}

We follow-up with some consequences of this proof.

\begin{proposition}
  Let $\mathcal{I}$ denote the set of parallel hyperbolic pairs of isometries with incoherent overlap.
 $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ preserves axis intersection if and only if either $\mathcal{I}$ is empty or all $(g,h) \in \mathcal{I}$ have unbounded overlap.
 \label{prop:preserves_axis}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:small} shows that if $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ preserves axis intersection, then either $\mathcal{I}$ is empty or all $(g,h) \in \mathcal{I}$ have unbounded incoherent overlap.
The converse is also true: if $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ does not preserve axis intersection, it means there exists a pair of hyperbolic elements $(g,h)$ so that $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$. This implies, by Lemma~\ref{lem:foundation_trees} (2) that $T_{gh},T_{hg}$ overlap incoherently with overlap of length $d(T_g,T_h)$, and since $(gh,hg)$ are parallel and hyperbolic, we found a pair of parallel hyperbolic isometries with bounded incoherent overlap, which proves the other implication.
\end{proof}

We now show that reducible actions satisfy disconnected smoothing but never satisfy connected smoothing, and that any reducible action has a parallel hyperbolic pair with incoherent overlap.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\Gamma=\pi_1(S)$ be a surface group acting on a real tree $T$ with translation length $\| \cdot \|$.
If  $(\Gamma, T)$ is reducible, then:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\| \cdot \| \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ does not satisfy connected smoothing but it satisfies disconnected smoothing.
    \item $-\| \cdot \| \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ does not satisfy disconnected smoothing but it satisfies connected smoothing.
    \item There exist a pair of hyperbolic parallel elements $(g,h)$ with incoherent overlap.
\end{itemize}
\label{lem:red_not_smoothing}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By~\cite[Proposition~1.6~(3)]{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}, either $g,h$ overlap coherently, in which case
\begin{equation}
\|gh \|=\| g \| + \| h \| \mbox{ and } \|gh^{-1} \| = |\|g \|- \| h \| |,
\label{eq:reducible1}
\end{equation}
or $g,h$ overlap incoherently, in which case
\begin{equation}
\|gh^{-1} \| =\| g\| + \| h \| \mbox{ and } \| gh \| = | \| g \| - \| h \||.
\label{eq:reducible2}
\end{equation}
Note that Equations~\ref{eq:reducible1}~and~\ref{eq:reducible2} hold for any pair of hyperbolic isometries $(g,h)$. In particular, they hold if $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ cross.
Hence, $\| \cdot \|$ always satisfies disconnected smoothing. Similarly, they also hold if $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ are parallel. Thus, $-\| \cdot \|$ always satisfies connected smoothing.

\begin{claim} Suppose $a,x \in \Gamma$ are hyperbolic elements so that $\| a \|>\| x \|$ and $(a,x)$ overlap coherently. We claim that the pair $(ax,xa^{-1})$ has incoherent overlap.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Indeed, on the one hand, by Equations~\ref{eq:reducible1} and \ref{eq:reducible2} 
\begin{align*}
\|ax\|&=\|a\|+\|x\| \\
\|xa^{-1}\| &= \|a\|-\|x\|
\end{align*}
so adding these equations, we get
\[
\|ax\| + \|xa^{-1}\| = 2\|a\|.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\|(xa^{-1})(ax)\| = 2\|x\|.
\]
Thus
\[
\|(xa^{-1})(ax)\| <  \|xa^{-1}\| + \| ax\|
\]
which means that $(ax,xa^{-1})$ overlap incoherently, by Equation~\ref{eq:reducible2}.
\end{proof}
Let $\Gamma'$ denote the (normal) subgroup of $\Gamma$ generated by elements acting hyperbolically on $T$.
If $\| \cdot \|$ is non-trivial, $\Gamma'$ is infinite, and we can find a pair $(g,h) \in \Gamma'$ of R-parallel elements. Indeed, take an arbitrary pair $(g,h)$ in $\Gamma'$. If it is R-parallel, we are done. If it is R-crossing, consider $(gh,hg)$, which is R-parallel by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}. If it is R-anti-parallel, consider $(gH,Hg)$ which is R-crossing, and proceed in the same way. From the hyperbolic R-parallel pair $(g,h)$, use Example~\ref{ex:ab_parallel_aB_crossing} to obtain a hyperbolic an R-parallel pair $(a,x)$, with $\|a\|>\|x\|$ so that $(ax^{-1},x^{-1}a)$ is R-crossing. By Lemma~\ref{lem:a_parallel_Ba}, it follows that $(ax,x^{-1}a)$ is R-parallel.
Applying the Claim above, we have found a pair $(g,h)$, with $g\coloneqq ax, h\coloneqq x^{-1}a$, of parallel hyperbolic elements with incoherent overlap. This proves the third item. To finish the proof of the first and second items, note 
this tells us $\| gh \| < \|gh^{-1}\|=\|g\| + \|h\|$, which violates (both oriented and unoriented) connected smoothing.
\end{proof}

Putting this all together we can give another characterization of small surface actions on real trees.

\begin{theorem}
$\pi_1(S) \acts T$ is irreducible and preserves axis intersection if and only if no pair of parallel hyperbolic elements $(g,h)$ have incoherent overlap.
\label{thm:axis_incoherent_characterization}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
One implication follows from the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:small}.
In the other direction, it follows by
Proposition~\ref{prop:preserves_axis} that $\pi_1(S) \acts T$
preserves axis intersection. Finally, it cannot be reducible since
otherwise it would have hyperbolic pairs with (unbounded) incoherent
overlap, by Lemma~\ref{lem:red_not_smoothing}.
\end{proof}

Now we prove Theorem~\ref{mainthm:skora}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{mainthm:skora}]
(A) is equivalent to (C) is proven by the first part of Theorem~\ref{mainthm:apps}.
(B) implies (C) is Theorem~\ref{thm:small}.
(C) implies (B) follows by work of Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths}.
Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:axis_incoherent_characterization} implies (C) is equivalent to (D).
\end{proof}
