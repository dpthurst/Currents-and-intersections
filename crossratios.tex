\section{Cross ratios}
\label{sec:crossratios}

In this section we will see that there is a one-to-one correspondence between geodesic currents and periods of cross-ratios. Precisely, we will prove that cross-ratios induce curve functionals (periods) satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.

There are many definitions of cross ratios in the literature (Ledrappier, Hamenstadt, Otal, etc.) and, for some of those, people have showed they can be upgraded to geodesic currents.
Typically the authors consider them to be continuous or even Holder continuous, but we will not assume this here.
In this section we show that cross-ratios induce curve functionals (periods) satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, and thus induce geodesic currents.
This recovers a result of~\cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}.

A \emph{generalized cross-ratio} is a function $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]$ on $\partial S^{(4)}$ satisfying:

\begin{enumerate}
\item (flip) $[a,b,c,d]=[c,d,a,b]$ for any ccw tuple $(a,b,c,d)$.
\item (additivity) If $a,b,c,d,e$ appear ccw, $[a,b,d,e]=[a,b,c,e]+[a,c,d,e]$
\item $\Gamma$-invariance.
\item (positive) For any ccw tuple $a,b,c,d$, we have $[a,b,c,d] \geq 0$.
\end{enumerate}

Generalized cross-ratios were studied in Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}.
In this setting, there are two natural cross-ratios associated to a geodesic current.
\[
[a,b,c,d]^+ \coloneqq \mu([d,a) \times [b,c))
\]
and
\[
[a,b,c,d]^- \coloneqq \mu((d,a] \times (b,c])
\]
It is easily checked that these satisfy the properties of generalized cross-ratios,
and these cross-ratios are different in general if $\mu$ has atoms (see~\cite[Example~3.4]{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, when $\mu$ is a closed curve).

Given a generalized cross-ratio $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]$, let the period function of a curve $c$ on $\Sigma$ represented by a group element $g$, be defined as
\[
\ell(g) \coloneqq [g^-, x , g \cdot x, g^+ ]
\]

Given a geodesic current $\mu$, the two (possibly distinct) cross-ratios $[]^{\pm}$ it induces have the same period. Thus, in general, cross-ratios are not uniquely determined by their periods. However, if the cross-ratios are assumed to be continuous, cross ratios are uniquely determined by their period (cite Ledrappier).



From the properties of a generalized cross-ratio, it is easy to see that 

\begin{lemma}
    For every $g \in \Gamma$, we have
    \[
    \ell(g)=\ell(g^{-1}).
    \]
    \label{lem:crinv}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The geodesic $\gamma$ with endpoints $g^-, g^+$ divides $\partial \Gamma$ into two open intervals: one on the left in the forward direction of $\gamma$, the other on the right.
Let $x \in \partial \Gamma$ be on the left interval, and $y \in \partial \Gamma$ on the right interval.
We then have
\begin{align*}
\ell(g^{-1}) &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^-, g^+, g^{n} y]}{2n} \\
  &= \lim_{n \to \infty}
     \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^-, g^{-n}x, g^{n} y]}{2n} +  \\
  &      \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^{-n}x, g^{n}x, g^{n} y]}{2n} + \\
  &      \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^{n}x, g^+, g^{n} y]}{2n} = \ell(g) \\
\end{align*}
The first and last terms have limit zero as $n \to \infty$, by $\Gamma$-invariance, and the middle term is symmetric under
switching $g$ and $g^{-1}$.
\end{proof}
There $\ell$ induces thus a function on curves that can be extended linearly to multi-curves. In this section we prove that this function satisfies the smoothing property.
We note that this can be inferred a posteriori from \cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, where they construct a geodesic current for every generalized cross-ratio. It follows from this that $\ell$ must satisfy smoothing. However, the proof we give here uses only the definition of generalized cross-ratio and thus subsumes the work in \cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios} under the scope of our theorem. Moreover, the proof also provides a framework on what cases to consider if one desires to check the smoothing property by working in the universal cover, as opposed to doing surgeries on the surface.

We divide the proof into several cases, depending on the type of smoothing (connected vs disconnected) and the relative position of certain axes of the resolutions.

\subsection{Disconnected smoothing}



\begin{lemma}
Suppose we have curves $[a]$ and $[b]$ so that $b$ right crosses $a$, and therefore $[a] \cup [b] \searrow [ab]$ and  $[a] \cup [b] \searrow [aB]$.
Then
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(b) \geq \ell(ab)
\]
and
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(B) \geq \ell(aB).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} 
The second inequality follows from the first by noting that if $b$ right crosses $a$, then $a$ right crosses $B$, and thus the first inequality applied to this pair becomes
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(B) \geq \ell(Ba)
\]
which is the same as the second inequality noting that $\ell(B)=\ell(b)$, by Lemma\ref{lem:crinv} and $\ell(Ba)=\ell(aB)$, by $\Gamma$-invariance.

The proof of the first inequality will start by considering the fundamental domain of $ab$ given by $(B \cdot Ab^+, bA^+) \times (ab^+, ab^-)$, depicted in green in Figure\ref{fig:d1}. At each step, we will apply an isometry given by either $a$ or $b$ (say, for example, $a$) and then partition the resulting box of geodesics into two subboxes, let's call them I and II.
Box I will be contained in either the fundamental domain of $a$, depicted in blue in Figure\ref{fig:d1} or a fundamental domain for $b$ depicted in red in that same Figure.
We will apply the inverse of the isometry $a$, denoted by $A$, to Box II, and continue the process: apply a different isometry, say $b$ or $B$, partition so that one box is in a fundamental domain of $a$ or $b$, and apply the inverse isometry to the next.
After a finite number of such steps we will end up having partitioned the fundamental domain of $ba$ into a union of disjoint boxes $(\mathfrak{a}_i)_{i=1}^k$, (resp. boxes $(\mathfrak{b}_j)_{j=1}^{\ell}$) contained without overlap in $\mathfrak{a}$, the fundamental domain of $a$, (resp. $\mathfrak{b}$, the fundamental domain of $b$) and whose total cross-ratio value is equal to $\ell(ab)$.
Since we are assuming the cross ratio is positive, and $\cup_{i=1}^k \mathfrak{a}_i \subset \mathfrak{a}$, $\cup_{j=1}^{\ell} \mathfrak{b}_j \subset \mathfrak{b}$, we obtain the desired inequality.
In what follows we illustrate the sequence of operations performed, at each step labeling the endpoints of the box of geodesics we are operating on, and highlighting it in bold purple.
At most one isometry among $a$, $b$ or its inverses is applied to each partitioned box at the end of the process, so we can assume we are in the free group.

\subsection{Connected smoothing}
\label{subsec:connected_smoothing}

\begin{lemma}
Suppose $C$ represents a curve with a self-intersection decomposing as $ab$, so that one of its smoothings is the multi-curve $[a] \cup [b]$, and the other resolution in $[aB]$.
Then, we have
\[
\ell(ab) \geq \ell(aB)
\]
and
\[
\ell(ab) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}



Now that we know which possible configurations of lifts can arise in the universal cover, we proceed to check both inequalities in the Lemma.

The first inequality involves the relative position of $aB, Ba$ with respect to the other axes, so we must proceed case by case. We start by analyzing case 1 first.

We will compare a fundamental domain of $BA$ to that of $Ab$.
Figure \ref{fig:c7} shows these two fundamental domains, with the fundamental domain of $Ab$ partitioned into three subsets $I$, $II$ and $III$. Figure \ref{fig:c71} singles out the first one of these regions, region $I$, corresponding to $(Ab^+, Ab^-) \times (B \cdot a^+, ab^-)$. 
Noting that $Ab^+ = Ba^-$ and $Ab^- = Ba^+$,
this set of geodesics is translated by $b$ to $(aB^-, aB^+) \times (a^+, ba^-)$, represented in Figure \ref{fig:c721}, which is contained in the fundamental domain of $BA$.
Region $II$ is already contained in the fundamental domain of $BA$, and it does not intersect the $b$ translate of region $I$ considered above.
We focus on region $III$, to finish, singled out in Figure \ref{fig:c72}, given by $(Ba^-, Ba^+) \times (ab^+, a^+)$. Applying $B$ to it, we obtain $ (B \cdot Ba^-, B \cdot Ba^+) \times (B \cdot ab^+, B \cdot a^+)$. Since $ba^- < Ba^-$, we have $ab^- < B \cdot Ba^-$, and thus $(B \cdot ab^+, B \cdot a^+) \subset (ab^-, ab^+)$. Also, $(B \cdot ab^+, B \cdot a^+) \subset (a^+, B \cdot a^+)$. Thus, the $B$ translate of region $III$ is contained in the fundamental domain of $BA$. The last observation is that the $B$ translate of region $III$ does not intersect either the $b$ translate of region $I$ or region $II$. This concludes the proof.

Following that figure, note that $B \cdot a^- = BA \cdot a^-$, thus $(a^-, B \cdot a^-) \subset (ab^+, ab^-)$.
    Also, $ab \cdot Ba^- = a \cdot aB^-$. Observe then that since $ba^+ > aB^-$ in ccw order, we have that $a \cdot aB^- > ab^+$ in ccw order.
    We thus have $(Ba^+, Ba^-) \subset (Ba^-, a \cdot aB)$, and therefore
    $\ell(Ba) \leq \ell(ab)$. 

For the second inequality, we observe (compare Figure~\ref{fig:c5}) that the fundamental domain for $ba$ given by
$[ba^+, ba^-) \times [A \cdot b^+, b^+)$ 
contains the non-intersecting boxes $[a^+, a^-) \times [A \cdot b^+, b^+)$ and $[b^-, b^+) \times [ba^+, ab^+)$, which are a fundamental domains for $a$ and $B$, respectively. Thus,
using positivity of cross-ratio and flip-invariance, we get
\[
\ell(ba) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(B) = \ell(a) + \ell(b)
\]
Note this computation works for both cases of connected smoothing, since we have not used the position of the axes $aB$ and $Ba$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
There is a one-to-one correspondence between periods of generalized cross-ratios and geodesic currents.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the above, every cross-ratio period $\ell$ satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, and thus furnishes a geodesic current $\ell(C)=i(\mu,C)$, which is uniquely determined since geodesic currents are uniquely determined by its intersection numbers (\cite[Theoreme~2]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}).
On the other hand, a geodesic current $\mu$ induces two cross-ratios $\pm$. Their periods $\ell^{\pm}$ satisfy $\ell^{\pm}(C)=i(\mu,C)$.
\end{proof}
