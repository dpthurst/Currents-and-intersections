\section{Cross ratios}

\label{sec:crossratios}

Given a generalized cross-ratio $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]$, let the period function of a curve $c$ on $\Sigma$ represented by a group element $g$, be defined as
\[
\ell(g) \coloneqq [g^-, x , g \cdot x, g^+ ]
\]


\begin{lemma}
Given two $C_1$ and $C_2$ represented by group elements $a$ and $b$ with an essential crossing, we prove that

\[
\ell(a) + \ell(b) \geq \ell(ab).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}



We can write $\ell(ab)=[(ab)^-, b^+, ab \cdot b^+, (ab)^+]$.
We furthermore note that $ab \cdot b^+-=a \cdot b^+$, and write

\begin{align*}
[(ab)^-, b^+, ab \cdot b^+, (ab)^+] &= \\
& [a \cdot b^+, (ab)^+, (ab)^-, b^+] = \\
[a \cdot b^+, (ab)^+, (bA)^-, b^+] + \\
[a \cdot b^+, (bA)^-, (ab)^-, b^+]
\end{align*}
where in the first equality we used flip relation, and the additivity relation in the second equality, which is justified since $(bA)^-$ is located in the interval $((ab)^+, (ab)^-)$ in the natural cyclic order.
We shall prove that both factors are smaller than $\ell(a)$ now.
For the first one, apply $A$ and use $\pi_1(\Sigma)$-invariance, to get

\[
[a \cdot b^+, (ab)^+, (bA)^-, b^+] = [b^+, A\cdot (ab)^+, a^+, A \cdot b^+]
\]
and use the fact that 

\[
[(Ab)^-, B \cdot a^-, a^-, (ab)^+] 
\]
which, by monotonicity is smaller than
\[
[b^-, B \cdot a^-, a^-, b^+].
\]
As for the other summand, we have, acting by $b$ and applying $\Gamma$-invariance of the cross-ratio,

\begin{align*}
=[b\cdot a^-, (bA)^-, (ba)^-, a^-]&= \\
& [ (ba)^-, a^-, b\cdot a^-, (bA)^-] \leq \\
&[(ba)^-, a^-, a^+, (bA)^-] = \\
&[a^-,(ba)^- , (bA)^-, a^+] \leq \\
&=[a^-,(ba)^- , ab^-, a^+] 
\end{align*}
where in the first equality we applied $\Gamma$-invariance and the north-south dynamics, in the first inequality we went from $b\cdot a^-$ to $a^+$, moving the third coordinate counter-clockwise, and thus increasing the value of the cross-ratio, in the second equality we applied two peripheral swaps,  

\end{proof}
