\section{Cross-ratios}
\label{sec:crossratios}

In this section we will see that there is a one-to-one correspondence between geodesic currents and periods of cross-ratios. Precisely, we will prove that cross-ratios induce functions on $\pi_1(S)$ (periods) satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, hence subsuming work of Martone-Zhang~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} and Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios} under our framework.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{cross-ratio} on $S$ is a function of type
\[
[\cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot] \colon (\partial_{\infty} S)^{(4)} \to \mathbb{R}
\]
where $(\partial_{\infty} S)^{(4)}$ denotes the space of ccw oriented 4-tuples in $(\partial_{\infty} S)^{4}$ such that $\{ a,d\} \cap \{b,c\} = \emptyset$, satisfying the following four properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{flip-invariance} $[a,b,c,d]=[c,d,a, b].$
\item \emph{additivity} For any ccw oriented $a,b,c,d,e$, we have
$[a,b, d, e]=[a,b,c,e] + [a,c,d, e]$.
\item \emph{$\pi_1(S)$-invariance} For any ccw oriented $a,b,c,d$ and every $g \in \pi_1(S)$, $[g \cdot a, g \cdot b, g \cdot c, g \cdot d] = [a,b,c,d]$.
\item \emph{positivity} For every ccw oriented $a,b,c,d$, we have
\[
[a,b,c,d] \geq 0.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\label{def:general_crossratio}
\end{definition}

There are many definitions of cross ratios in the literature (Ledrappier~\cite{L95:BordDesVar}, Hamenst\"adt~\cite{Ham99:Cocycles}, Otal~\cite{Otal92:Symplectique}, Martone-Zhang~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed}, Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}). 
In all of these references (except the last one) the authors consider the cross-ratios to be continuous or even H\"older continuous, but we will not assume this here. Furthermore, in all of the above definitions, flip-invariance is assumed.
For an even more general definition of cross-ratio where flip-invariance is not assumed, see Labourie's~\cite[Definition~3.1]{L07:Crossratio}. We will always assume our cross-ratios are flip-invariant.

In this section we show that cross-ratios induce curve functionals (periods) satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, and thus induce geodesic currents.

As alluded above, cross-ratios as in our definition above have been recently considered in Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}.
In this setting, there are two natural cross-ratios associated to a geodesic current.
\[
[a,b,c,d]^+ \coloneqq \mu([d,a) \times [b,c))
\]
and
\[
[a,b,c,d]^- \coloneqq \mu((d,a] \times (b,c]).
\]
It is easily checked that these satisfy the properties of generalized cross-ratios,
and these cross-ratios are different in general if $\mu$ has atoms (see~\cite[Example~3.4]{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}, when $\mu$ is a closed curve).

Given a generalized cross-ratio $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]$, let the \emph{period} function of a curve $C$ on $\Sigma$ represented by a group element $g$, be defined as
\[
\ell(g) \coloneqq [g^+, x , g \cdot x, g^- ].
\]
The $\pi_1(S)$-invariance and additivity of the cross-ratio show this is well-defined.

Given a geodesic current $\mu$, the two (possibly distinct) cross-ratios $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]^{\pm}$ induce the same period. Thus, in general, cross-ratios are not uniquely determined by their periods. However, if the cross-ratios are assumed to be H\"older continuous, this is indeed the case~\cite[Theorem 1.a]{L95:BordDesVar}.
In fact, if $\mu$ has no atoms, the two cross-ratios $[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]^{\pm}$ are easily seen to be equal.
Hence, it will follow that, for non-atomic geodesic currents, there is a one-to-one correspondence between cross-ratios and geodesic currents.

From the properties of a generalized cross-ratio, it is easy to see that $\ell$ is symmetric.

\begin{lemma}
    For every $g \in \pi_1(S)$, we have
    \[
    \ell(g)=\ell(g^{-1}).
    \]
    \label{lem:crinv}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The geodesic $\gamma$ with endpoints $g^-, g^+$ divides $\partial_{\infty}S$ into two open intervals: one on the left in the forward direction of $\gamma$, the other on the right.
Let $x \in  \partial_{\infty}S$ be on the left interval, and $y \in  \partial_{\infty}S$ on the right interval.
We then have
\begin{align*}
\ell(g^{-1}) &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^-, g^+, g^{n} y]}{2n} \\
  &= \lim_{n \to \infty}
     \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^-, g^{-n}x, g^{n} y]}{2n} +  \\
  &      \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^{-n}x, g^{n}x, g^{n} y]}{2n} + \\
  &      \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^{n}x, g^+, g^{n} y]}{2n} = \ell(g) \\
\end{align*}
The first and last terms have limit zero as $n \to \infty$, by $\pi_1(S)$-invariance, and the middle term is symmetric under
switching $g$ and $g^{-1}$.
\end{proof}


It is similarly easy to check $\ell$ satisfies stability.

\begin{lemma}
For every $g \in \pi_1(S)$, and every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ we have
\[
\ell(g^n) = n\ell(g).
\]
\label{lem:crossratio_stability}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    It follows readily by additivity and $\pi_1(S)$-invariance of the cross-ratio.
\end{proof}

Now we will prove that $\ell$ satisfies the smoothing hypotheses in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}.

We divide the proof into two cases, depending on the type of smoothing (connected vs disconnected).
Our goal is to check that the smoothing conditions in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} are satisfied.

\subsection{Disconnected smoothing}

We will verify Equation~\ref{eq:disconn_oriented} in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}.

\begin{lemma}
Let $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ so that $(a,b)$ are $R$-crossing.
Then
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(b) \geq \ell(ab).
\]
\label{lem:crossratio_disconnected_smoothing}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} 
 The strategy of the proof will go as follows. Let $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}, \mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}$ be fundamental domains of $ab$, $a$ and $b$ respectively, fixed once and for all.
We will partition $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}$ into subboxes, and apply isometries $a$, $b$ or their inverses in order to fit each subbox in either  $\mathfrak{a}$ or $\mathfrak{b}$ without overlaps. Let us label them by $\mathfrak{a}_i$ and $\mathfrak{b}_j$ according to if we fit them in $\mathfrak{a}$ or in $\mathfrak{b}$.
By additivity and invariance of the cross-ratio, since $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b} = \cup_{i=1}^{\ell} \overline{\mathfrak{a}_i} \cup \cup_{j=1}^{k} \overline{\mathfrak{b}_j}$, where the overline notation denotes a (possible) translate of the subbox, we will have $\ell(ab)$ is the sum of the cross-ratios of the boxes $\mathfrak{a}_i$ and $\mathfrak{b}_j$.
Furthermore, we have
$\cup_{i=1}^k \mathfrak{a}_i \subset \mathfrak{a}$, $\cup_{j=1}^{\ell} \mathfrak{b}_j \subset \mathfrak{b}$.
So, by positivity of the cross-ratio, it follows that
\[
\ell(ab) \leq \ell(a) + \ell(b),
\]
as desired.
Let us now implement this strategy.
Consider the fundamental domain of $ab$ given by $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b} \coloneqq [B \cdot Ab^+, bA^+) \times (ab^+, ab^-)$, depicted in green in Figure~\ref{fig:d1}.

\subsection{Connected smoothing}

Here we verify that Equation~\ref{eq:conn_oriented} in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} is satisfied.


\begin{lemma}
Let $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$ with $(a,b)$ $R$-parallel..
Then, we have
\[
\ell(ab) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b).
\]
\label{lem:crossratio_connected_smoothing}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}


    \begin{figure}[ht!]
     \centering
\resizebox{80mm}{!}{\svgfig{c5}}
    \caption{Checking $\ell(ab) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b)$}\label{fig:c5}
\end{figure} 

We observe (compare Figure~\ref{fig:c5}) that the fundamental domain for $ba$ given by
$[ba^+, ba^-) \times [A \cdot b^+, b^+)$ 
contains the non-intersecting boxes $[a^+, a^-) \times [A \cdot b^+, b^+)$ and $[b^-, b^+) \times [ba^+, ab^+)$, which are a fundamental domains for $a$ and $B$, respectively. Thus,
using positivity of cross-ratio, additivity and flip-invariance, we get
\[
\ell(ba) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(B) = \ell(a) + \ell(b).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
There is a one-to-one correspondence between periods of generalized cross-ratios and geodesic currents.
Moreover, periods uniquely determine cross-ratios arising from non-atomic geodesic currents.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the Lemmas~\ref{lem:crinv},~\ref{lem:crossratio_stability}, ~\ref{lem:crossratio_disconnected_smoothing} and ~\ref{lem:crossratio_connected_smoothing} above, every cross-ratio period $\ell$ satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, and thus there exists a unique dual geodesic current $\mu_{\ell}$ such that $\ell(C)=i(\mu_{\ell},C)$ for every closed curve.
On the other hand, a geodesic current $\mu$ induces two cross-ratios $[\cdot]^\pm$ with the same periods $\ell^{\pm}$ satisfying $\ell^{\pm}(C)=i(\mu,C)$.
Finally, if $\mu$ has no atoms, the two cross ratios are equal, and hence the claim follows.
\end{proof}
