\section{Cross ratios}

\label{sec:crossratios}

A \emph{generalized cross-ratio} is a function $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]$ on $\partial S^{(4)}$ satisfying:

\begin{enumerate}
\item (flip) $[a,b,c,d]=[c,d,a,b]$ for any ccw tuple $(a,b,c,d)$.
\item (additivity) If $a,b,c,d,e$ appear ccw, $[a,b,d,e]=[a,b,c,e]+[a,c,d,e]$
\item $\Gamma$-invariance.
\end{enumerate}

Given a generalized cross-ratio $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]$, let the period function of a curve $c$ on $\Sigma$ represented by a group element $g$, be defined as
\[
\ell(g) \coloneqq [g^-, x , g \cdot x, g^+ ]
\]
From the properties of a generalized cross-ratio, it is easy to see that $\ell(g)=\ell(G)$. \DMGtodo[inline]{insert proof here? we have it.}

$\ell$ induces thus a function on curves that can be extended linearly to multi-curves. In this section we prove that this function satisfies the smoothing property.
We note that this can be inferred a posteriori from \cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, where they construct a geodesic current for every generalized cross-ratio. It follows from this that $\ell$ must satisfy smoothing. However, the proof we give here uses only the definition of generalized cross-ratio and thus subsumes the work in \cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios} under the scope of our theorem. Moreover, the proof also provides a framework on what cases to consider if one desires to check the smoothing property by working in the universal cover, as opposed to doing surgeries on the surface.
THis will also be helpful in establishing the generalized max-smoothing property for measured laminations.

We divide the proof into several cases, depending on the type of smoothing (connected vs disconnected) and the relative position of certain axes of the resolutions.

\subsection{Disconnected smoothing}



\begin{lemma}
Suppose we have curves $[a]$ and $[b]$ so that $b$ right crosses $a$, and therefore $[a] \cup [b] \searrow [ab]$ and  $[a] \cup [b] \searrow [aB]$.
Then
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(b) \geq \ell(ab)
\]
and
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(B) \geq \ell(aB).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} 
We will only prove the first inequality, since the second one follows from the first by noting that if $b$ right crosses $a$, then $a$ right crosses $B$, and thus the first inequality applied to this pair becomes
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(B) \geq \ell(Ba)
\]
which is the same as the second inequality noting that $\ell(B)=\ell(b)$ and $\ell(Ba)=\ell(aB)$.

The proof of the first inequality consists on partitioning the fundamental domain of $Ab$ given by $(Ab^+, Ab^-) \times ((ba)^-, ba \cdot (Ab)^+)$ (which will compute $\ell(ba)$ via 
$[(ba)^-, (Ab)^+, (ba) \cdot (Ab)^+, (ba)^+]$)
into boxes $(1,I), (1,II), (2,I), (2,II), (3,I)$ and $(3, II)$ and showing, by splitting these boxes into sub-boxes and applying $b$ and $b^{-1}$ conveniently, that each one of these sub-boxes can be contained without intersections in either the fundamental domain of $a$ given by $(a^+, a^-) \times ((Ab)^+, a \cdot (Ab)^+)$ (computing $\ell(a)$ via 
$[a^-, (Ab)^+, a \cdot (Ab)^+, a^+]$) or the fundamental domain of $b$ given by $(b^+, b^-) \times ((bA)^+, b \cdot (bA)^+)$ (computing $\ell(b)$ with the cross-ratio $[b^-, (bA)^+, b \cdot (bA)^+, b^+]$). 

We also note that $(ba)(Ab)(ba)^{-1}=b(bA)b^{-1}$, which will play a key role in the pictures below.
In the pictures, the bold font in the caption indicates that the result is final, and will not be perturbed further.

\subsection{Connected smoothing}

\begin{lemma}
Suppose $C$ represents a curve with a self-intersection decomposing as $ab$, so that one of its smoothings is the multi-curve $[a] \cup [b]$, and the other resolution in $[aB]$.
Then, we have
\[
\ell(ab) \geq \ell(aB)
\]
and
\[
\ell(ab) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

If $C$ represents a curve with a self-intersection decomposing as $ab$, we can consider intersecting axes $\ora{ab}$ and $\ora{ba}$ in the universal cover so that their intersection is a lift of the self-intersection of the curve $ab$.

One of the resolutions of $[ab]$ is the multi-curve $[a] \cup [b]$.
The lifts of $[a]$ and $[b]$ to the universal cover correspond to axes $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ which are parallel. \DMGtodo[inline]{does this need justification? This is essentially saying that we only need to consider the case $a$ and $b$ parallel, and we do not need to worry about $a$ and $b$ being anti-parallel.}
It remains to determine the position of the lifts $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$.

To that end, consider the common perpendicular line of $a$ and $b$. Let $R_1$ be the reflection along this line.
Let $R_2, R_3, R_4, R_5$ be reflections so that $a=R_1 R_2 = R_4 R_1$, and $b=R_1 R_3 = R_5 R_1$. Note that $R_i$ are reflections along pairwise non-intersecting reflection lines, since $aB$ and $ab$ are hyperbolic.
It then follows that 
$ab=R_4 R_3$ and $ba=R_5 R_2$. Furthermore, $Ba=R_3 R_2$ and $aB=R_4 R_5$.
We have the following possible four configurations of non-intersecting reflection lines.

In the figures, we also draw their corresponding translation axes for each isometry.
We observe that case 2 can be ruled out, since the NS dynamics of $b$ should translate $\ora{Ba}$ to $\ora{aB}$, and thus these two axes should stay on the same side of $\ora{b}$, which does not happen in that configuration.
We note there is another case, analogous to case 2, where the reflection lines below $R_1$ are exchanged by the ones above it, which can be similarly ruled out.

We observe that in case 3, $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$ could cross.

Now that we know which possible configurations of lifts can arise in the universal cover, we proceed to check both inequalities in the Lemma.

The first inequality involves the relative position of $aB, Ba$ with respect to the other axes, so we must proceed case by case. We start by analyzing case 1 first.

We will compare a fundamental domain of $BA$ to that of $Ab$.
Figure \ref{fig:c7} shows these two fundamental domains, with the fundamental domain of $Ab$ partitioned into three subsets $I$, $II$ and $III$. Figure \ref{fig:c71} singles out the first one of these regions, region $I$, corresponding to $(Ab^+, Ab^-) \times (B \cdot a^+, ab^-)$. 
Noting that $Ab^+ = Ba^-$ and $Ab^- = Ba^+$,
this set of geodesics is translated by $b$ to $(aB^-, aB^+) \times (a^+, ba^-)$, represented in Figure \ref{fig:c721}, which is contained in the fundamental domain of $BA$.
Region $II$ is already contained in the fundamental domain of $BA$, and it does not intersect the $b$ translate of region $I$ considered above.
We focus on region $III$, to finish, singled out in Figure \ref{fig:c72}, given by $(Ba^-, Ba^+) \times (ab^+, a^+)$. Applying $B$ to it, we obtain $ (B \cdot Ba^-, B \cdot Ba^+) \times (B \cdot ab^+, B \cdot a^+)$. Since $ba^- < Ba^-$, we have $ab^- < B \cdot Ba^-$, and thus $(B \cdot ab^+, B \cdot a^+) \subset (ab^-, ab^+)$. Also, $(B \cdot ab^+, B \cdot a^+) \subset (a^+, B \cdot a^+)$. Thus, the $B$ translate of region $III$ is contained in the fundamental domain of $BA$. The last observation is that the $B$ translate of region $III$ does not intersect either the $b$ translate of region $I$ or region $II$. This concludes the proof.

Following that figure, note that $B \cdot a^- = BA \cdot a^-$, thus $(a^-, B \cdot a^-) \subset (ab^+, ab^-)$.
    Also, $ab \cdot Ba^- = a \cdot aB^-$. Observe then that since $ba^+ > aB^-$ in ccw order, we have that $a \cdot aB^- > ab^+$ in ccw order.
    We thus have $(Ba^+, Ba^-) \subset (Ba^-, a \cdot aB)$, and therefore
    $\ell(Ba) \leq \ell(ab)$. 



For the second inequality, compare Figure \ref{fig:c2}.

\begin{figure}[h!]
     \centering
\resizebox{80mm}{!}{\svgfig{c2}}
    \caption{Checking $\ell(ab) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b)$}\label{fig:c2}

We can write, using the flip relation and the additive relation of the cross-ratio
\begin{align*}
\ell(ba)&=[ba^-, a^-, b \cdot a^-, ba^+] = [b \cdot a^-, ba^+,ba^-, a^-] =\\
&[b \cdot a^-, ba^+,b^+, a^-] +  [b \cdot a^-, b^+,b^-, a^-] + \\
&[b \cdot a^-, b^-,ab^-, a^-] + [b \cdot a^-, ab^-,ba^-, a^-].
\end{align*}
Therefore, by using the addivity property repeatedly, we get
\begin{align*}
\ell(ba) &\geq  [b \cdot a^-, b^+,b^-, a^-] + [b \cdot a^-, ab^-,ba^-, a^-] =\\
&[b \cdot a^-, b^+,b^-, a^-] +[ba^-, a^-, b \cdot a^-, ab^-]\\
&[b \cdot a^-, b^+,b^-, a^-] +[ba^-, a^-, a^+, ab^-] +  [ba^-, a^+, b \cdot a^-, ab^-] \geq \\
& [b \cdot a^-, b^+,b^-, a^-] +[ba^-, a^-, a^+, ab^-] = \\
& [b \cdot a^-, b^+,b^-, a^-] +[ba^-, A^+, A^-, ab^-] = \\
& \ell(b) + \ell(A) = \ell(b) + \ell(a)
\end{align*}
where in the last equation we used the fact that $\ell(a)=\ell(A)$.
Note this computation works for case 1 and case 3, since we have not used the position of the axes $aB$ and $Ba$.
\end{proof}
