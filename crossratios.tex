\section{Cross-ratios}
\label{sec:crossratios}

In this section we will see that every generalized cross-ratio induces a geodesic current. Precisely, we will prove that generalized cross-ratios induce functions on $\pi_1(S)$ (periods) satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, and therefore induce a geodesic current $\mu$ dual to the period. This subsumes work of Martone-Zhang~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} and Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios} under our framework.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{generalized cross-ratio} on $S$ is a function of type
\[
[\cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot] \colon (\partial_{\infty} S)^{(4)} \to \mathbb{R}
\]
where $(\partial_{\infty} S)^{(4)}$ denotes the space of ccw oriented 4-tuples in $(\partial_{\infty} S)^{4}$ such that $\{ a,d\} \cap \{b,c\} = \emptyset$, satisfying the following four properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{flip-invariance:} $[a,b,c,d]=[c,d,a, b].$
\item \emph{additivity:} For any ccw oriented $a,b,c,d,e$, we have
$[a,b, d, e]=[a,b,c,e] + [a,c,d, e]$.
\item \emph{$\pi_1(S)$-invariance:} For any ccw oriented $a,b,c,d$ and every $g \in \pi_1(S)$, $[g \cdot a, g \cdot b, g \cdot c, g \cdot d] = [a,b,c,d]$.
\item \emph{positivity:} For every ccw oriented $a,b,c,d$, we have
\[
[a,b,c,d] \geq 0.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\label{def:general_crossratio}
\end{definition}

There are many definitions of cross ratios in the literature (Ledrappier~\cite{L95:BordDesVar}, Hamenst\"adt~\cite{Ham99:Cocycles}, Otal~\cite{Otal92:Symplectique}, Martone-Zhang~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed}, Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}). 
In all of these references (except the last one) the authors consider the cross-ratios to be continuous or even H\"older continuous, but we will not assume this here. Furthermore, in all of the above definitions, flip-invariance is assumed.
For an even more general definition of cross-ratio where flip-invariance is not assumed, see Labourie's~\cite[Definition~3.1]{L07:Crossratio}. We will always assume our cross-ratios are flip-invariant.

As alluded above, generalized cross-ratios as in our definition above have been recently considered in Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}.
In this setting, there are two natural generalized cross-ratios associated to a geodesic current.
\[
[a,b,c,d]^+ \coloneqq \mu([d,a) \times [b,c))
\]
and
\[
[a,b,c,d]^- \coloneqq \mu((d,a] \times (b,c]).
\]
It is easily checked that these satisfy the properties of generalized cross-ratios,
and these cross-ratios are different in general if $\mu$ has atoms (see~\cite[Example~3.4]{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}, when $\mu$ is a closed curve).
If $\mu$ has no atoms, the two generalized cross-ratios $[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]^{\pm}$ are equal, since the measure of the boundary of any box of geodesics is zero.

Given a generalized cross-ratio $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]$, let the \emph{period} function of a curve $C=[g]$ be
\[
\ell(g) \coloneqq [g^+, x , g \cdot x, g^- ],
\]
where $x$ is an arbitrary point in $(g^-,g^+)$.
The $\pi_1(S)$-invariance and additivity of the cross-ratio show this is well-defined.

Given a geodesic current $\mu$, the two (possibly distinct) generalized cross-ratios $[ \cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot ]^{\pm}$ induce the same period. Thus, in general, generalizd cross-ratios are not uniquely determined by their periods. However, if the cross-ratios are assumed to be H\"older continuous, this is indeed the case~\cite[Theorem 1.a]{L95:BordDesVar}.

Since the two generalized cross-ratios are equal for non-atomic geodesic current, it follows there is a one-to-one correspondence between generalized cross-ratios and geodesic currents in this case.

\begin{lemma}
For every $g \in \pi_1(S)$, and every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ we have
\[
\ell(g^n) = n\ell(g).
\]
\label{lem:crossratio_stability}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    This follows readily by additivity and $\pi_1(S)$-invariance of the cross-ratio.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    For every $g \in \pi_1(S)$, we have
    \[
    \ell(g)=\ell(g^{-1}).
    \]
    \label{lem:crinv}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The geodesic $\ora{g}$ with endpoints $g^-, g^+$ divides $\partial_{\infty}S$ into two open intervals:
one on the left in the forward direction of $\gamma$, the other on the right.

Let $y \in  \partial_{\infty}S$ be on the left interval, and $x \in  \partial_{\infty}S$ on the right interval.
We then have
\begin{align*}
\ell(g^{-1}) &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^-, g^+, g^{n} y]}{2n} \\
  &= \lim_{n \to \infty}
     \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^-, g^{-n}x, g^{n} y]}{2n} +
     \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^{-n}x, g^{n}x, g^{n} y]}{2n} +
     \frac{[g^{-n}y, g^{n}x, g^+, g^{n} y]}{2n}\\
  &= \ell(g).
\end{align*}
To see the last equality, note that by $\pi_1(S)$-invariance, the
numerator of the first term is $[y,g^-,x,g^{2n}y]$ which tends to
$[y,g^-,x,g^+]$, and thus the entire first term has limit zero
as $n \to \infty$. Similarly for the last term, and the middle term is
symmetric under switching $g$ and $g^{-1}$.
\end{proof}


It is similarly easy to check $\ell$ satisfies stability.

Now we will prove that $\ell$ satisfies the smoothing hypotheses in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}.

We divide the proof into two cases, depending on the type of smoothing (connected vs disconnected).
Our goal is to check that the smoothing conditions in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} are satisfied.


\subsection{Disconnected smoothing}

We will verify Equation~\ref{eq:disconn_oriented} in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}.
In order to prove this, we will show that connected smoothing of $\ell$, together with the extra control given by the cross-ratio, imply disconnected smoothing.

We will use the following intermediate classical lemma.
\begin{lemma}
Given any sequence of real numbers $a_{n}$ satisfying that there exist a uniform constant $K>0$ so that for all integers $m,n \geq 0$
\[
a_{m+n} \leq a_m + a_n + K
\]
(respectively,
\[
a_{m+n} \geq a_m + a_n + K),
\]
we have
\[
\lim_n \frac{a_n}{n} = \inf_n \frac{a_n}{n} \leq a_1
\]
(respectively,
\[
\lim_n \frac{a_n}{n} = \sup_n \frac{a_n}{n} \geq a_1
\]
\label{lem:fekete}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
Let $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ so that $(b,a)$ are $R$-crossing.
Then
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(b) \geq \ell(ab).
\]
\label{lem:crossratio_disconnected_smoothing}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The strategy of the proof will be in two steps. 
\begin{itemize} \item Step 1. We will show that $\lim_n \frac{\ell(a^nb^n)}{n} = \ell(a)+\ell(b)$.
In order to prove this, we will show $\ell(a^nb^n)-\ell(a^n)-\ell(b^n)$ is bounded above and below by constants independent of $n$.
This will involve breaking each fundamental domain of $a^nb^n, a^n, b^n$ into boxes, cancelling matching ones and showing that the non-cancelling ones are contained in bigger but bounded boxes which
are independent of $n$.
\item Step 2. We will show that the sequence $a_n \coloneqq \ell(a^n b^n)$ is quasi-super-additive, which will imply
\[
\lim_n \frac{\ell(a^nb^n)}{n} \geq \ell(ab)
\]
by Fekete's lemma~\ref{lem:fekete}. Combining both, the result will follow.
\end{itemize}
Let's proceed with Step 1.
In the following, compare Fig~\ref{fig:rcross_cr}. Consider the fundamental domain $F(ab)$ of $ab$ given by $F(ab)=(ab^+, ab^-) \times [Ab^+, a \cdot bA^+)$.
We will partition $F(ab)$ into four subboxes, $F(ab)=R_1 \cup R_2 \cup R_3 \cup R_4$, given by:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R_1(a,b) = (a^+, ab^-) \times [bA^+, a \cdot bA^+)$,
\item $R_2(a,b) = (ab^+, b^-) \times [Ab^+, bA^+)$,
\item $R_3(a,b) = (ab^+, a^+] \times [bA^+, a \cdot bA^+)$,
\item $R_4(a,b)=[b^-, ab^-) \times [Ab^+, bA^+)$.
\end{enumerate}
Note that $R_1$ is contained in the fundamental domain of $a$, $F(a)$, given by $(a^+, a^-) \times [bA^+, a \cdot bA^+)$, and $R_2$ is contained in the fundamental domain of $b$, $F(b)$, given by $(b^+, b^-) \times [Ab^+, bA^+)$.
In fact, $F(a) = R_5 \cup R_1$, where $R_5(a,b) \coloneqq [ab^-,a^-) \times [bA^+, a \cdot bA^+)$.
Similarly, $F(b) = R_2 \cup R_6$, where $R_6 \coloneqq [b^+,ab^+) \times [Ab^+,bA^+)$.


Now we will take powers of $a$ and $b$. Given a box $R \subset G(\Sigma)$, with vertices $(x,y,z,w)$ oriented ccw, whenever we write $[R]$ we mean the cross ratio $[x,y,z,w]$. By additivity of the cross-ratio, we have the following
\begin{equation}
[F(a^nb^n)] = [R_1] + [R_2]+[R_3] + [R_4] = [F(a^n)]-[R_5] + [F(b^n)] - [R_6] + [R_3] + [R_4].
\label{eq:difference}
\end{equation}


We claim that there exist a positive constant $C=C(a,b)$ independent of $n \in \mathbb{N}$ so that
$-C < [R_6(a^n, b^n)]-[R_3(a^n,b^n)] < C, [R_4(a^n,b^n)]<C, [R_5(a^n,b^n)]<C$.
The second two boxes can each one be contained in a bigger bounded box independent of $n$.
Indeed, $R_4(a^n,b^n) \subset (b^-, ab^-) \times (a^-, b^+)$. This is because, by Lemma~\ref{lem:anbn_vs_bkak_parallel}, $(a^nb^n, ab)$ are R-parallel for $n>1$, and hence $(a^nb^n)^- \in (b^-, ab^-)$.
By the same Lemma, $(Ab, A^nb^n)$ are R-parallel, and $(b^nA^n, bA)$ are R-parallel, and thus $((A^nb^n)^-, (b^nA^n)) \subset (a^-, b^+)$.
Therefore, the axes are disposed as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rcross_cr_powers}.

By a similar argument, we also have $R_5(a^n, b^n) \subset (b^-, a^-) \times (aB^+, a^+)$.
We now deal with the term $[R_6(a^n, b^n)]-[R_3(a^n,b^n)]$, by decomposing the boxes one step further.
For the following, compare Fig~\ref{fig:rcross_cr3}.
First, we define $R_3'(a,b) = A \cdot R_3 = [a^+, ba^+) \times [Ab^+, bA^+)$.
Note that we can partition $R_3'(a,b)= R_7(a,b) \cup R_8(a,b)$, where:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R_7(a,b) = (ab^+, a^+) \times [Ab^+, bA^+)$
\item $R_8(a,b) = (ba^+,a^+] \times [Ab^+, bA^+)$
\end{enumerate}
Similarly, we can partition $R_6(a,b) = R_9(a,b)\cup R_8(a,b)$, where $R_9(a,b)=(b^+, ba^+]\times [Ab^+, bA^+)$ and $R_8(a,b)$ the same as above.
Now, a similar argument as for $R_4(a^n,b^n)$, shows that $R_7(a^n,b^n) \subset (ab^+, a^-) \times (a^-, b^+)$. 
Finally, define $R_9'(a,b)=B \cdot R_9(a,b) = (b^+, ab^+) \times [B \cdot Ab^+, Ab^+)$, and note
$R_9'(a^n, b^n) \subset (b^+, a^+) \times (b^-, Ab^+)$, by a similar argument as for $R_4(a^n,b^n)$.
This shows that all the terms are bounded.

As a consequence, dividing Equation~\ref{eq:difference} by $n$ and letting $n$ go to infinity, we get
\begin{equation}
\lim_n \frac{\ell(a^n b^n)}{n} = \ell(a) + \ell(b).
\label{eq:limit_anbn}
\end{equation}



Now, as Step 2, we will show that there exists $K=K(a,b)>0$ so that for all $m,n>0$,
\[
\ell(a^m b^m) + \ell(a^nb^n) \leq \ell(a^{m+n}b^{m+n}) + K.
\]

    \begin{figure}[ht!]
     \centering
\resizebox{80mm}{!}{\svgfignormal{rcross_cr_sa}}
    \caption{Checking $\ell(a^m b^m) + \ell(a^nb^n) \leq \ell(a^{m+n}b^{m+n}) + K$}\label{fig:rcross_cr_sa}
\end{figure}

    \begin{figure}[ht!]
     \centering
\resizebox{80mm}{!}{\svgfignormal{rcross_cr_sa3}}
    \caption{Checking $\ell(a^m b^m) + \ell(a^nb^n) \leq \ell(a^{m+n}b^{m+n}) + K$}\label{fig:rcross_cr_sa3}
\end{figure}

Consider the fundamental domains for $a^{m+n} b^{m+n}$, $a^m b^m$, $a^n b^n$ given, respectively, by
\begin{align*}
    F(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})=((a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^+,(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^-) \times [b^+, a^{m+n} \cdot b^+) \\
    F(a^{m} b^{m})=((a^{m} b^{m})^+, (a^{m} b^{m})^-) \times [b^+, a^m \cdot b^+) \\  
    F(a^{n} b^{n})=((a^{n} b^{n})^+, (a^{n} b^{n})^-) \times [b^+, a^n \cdot b^+) \\ 
\end{align*}
depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:rcross_cr_sa}, where we consider $m \geq n$.
Applying $A^n$ to the fundamental domain of $a^{m+n} b^{m+n}$ we get a fundamental domain for $A^n \cdot a^{m+n} b^{m+n}$ given by
\[
F'(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})=(A^n \cdot (a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^+,A^n \cdot (a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^-) \times [A^n \cdot b^+, a^m \cdot b^+).
\]
We have
\[
F(a^{m} b^{m}) \subset F'(a^{m+n} b^{m+n}).
\]
On the other hand, consider the new fundamental domain for $A^n \cdot a^nb^n$ given by
\[
F'(a^nb^n) = A^n \cdot F(a^nb^n) = ((b^n a^n)^+, (b^n a^n)^-) \times [A^n \cdot b^+, b^+).
\]

This can be partitioned into
\begin{align*}
    B_1=[(b^n a^n)^+, A^n \cdot(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^+]\times [A^n \cdot b^+, b^+) \\
    B_2=(A^n \cdot(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^+, A^n \cdot (a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^-) \times [A^n \cdot b^+, b^+) \\  
    B_3=[A^n \cdot (a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^-, (b^n a^n)^-] \times [A^n \cdot b^+, b^+). \\ 
\end{align*}
Observe that $B_2 \subset F'(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})$, and $B_2 \cap F(a^m b^m) =\emptyset$.
Furthermore, $B_1$ can in turn be partitioned into two subboxes,
\begin{align*}
    B_1'&=[(b^n a^n)^+, A^n \cdot(a^{m} b^{m})^+)\times [A^n \cdot b^+, b^+) \\
    B_1''&=(A^n \cdot(a^{m} b^{m})^+, A^n \cdot(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^+]\times [A^n \cdot b^+, b^+).
\end{align*}
First, note that
\[
a^n \cdot  B_1'=[(a^n b^n)^+, (a^{m} b^{m})^+]\times [b^+, a^n \cdot b^+) \\
\]
and so 
$a^n \cdot  B_1' \subset F'(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})$, with $a^n \cdot  B_1' \cap B_2 = \emptyset$ and  $a^n \cdot  B_1' \cap F(a^m b^m) =\emptyset$, that is, $a^n \cdot  B_1'$ can also be contained in the fundamental domain of $A^n \cdot a^{m+n} b^{m+n}$ without intersecting the previous boxes we had already translated to it.
Thus, by additivity and positivity of cross-ratio, we have 
\begin{equation}
[a^n \cdot B_1'] + [B_2] + [F(a^m b^m)] = [B_1'] + [B_2] + [F(a^m b^m)] \leq [F'(a^{m+n}b^{m+n})].
\label{eq:first_inequality}
\end{equation}

Second, we claim that
$B_1''$ and $B_3$ have bounded cross-ratio independently of $n,m$.
Indeed, note that
\begin{align*}
    B_1'' = [(a^{m-n} b^{m}a^n)^+, (a^{m} b^{m}a^n)^+]\times [ b^+, a^n \cdot b^+) \\
    \subset (ba^+, a^+) \times [B^n A^n \cdot [a^-, b^+),
\end{align*}
where the last inclusion is justified since $((a^{m-n} b^{m}a^n)^+, (a^{m} b^{m}a^n)^+) \subset (ba^+, a^+)$ because $m-n>0$.
To conclude, we show that $B_3$ also has bounded cross-ratio independently of $n,m$.
\begin{align*}
    a^n \cdot B_3 = [(a^{m+n} b^{m+n})^-, (a^n b^n)^-] \times [b^+, a^n b^+) \subset (a^-, ab^-) \times [b^+, a^+).
\end{align*}
All in all, there exist a constant $K=K(a,b)>0$ so that
\begin{equation}
[F'(a^n b^n)] = [B_1] + [B_2] + [B_3] = [B_1'] + [B_1''] + [B_2] + [B_3]\leq K + [B_2] + [B_1'].
\label{eq:quasi_superadditive}
\end{equation}
Then, combining Equation~\ref{eq:first_inequality} with Equation~\ref{eq:quasi_superadditive}, we get
\[
[F'(a^n b^n)] + [F(a^m b^m)] \leq [F'(a^{m+n}b^{m+n})] + K.
\]
 Finally, the $\Gamma$-invariance of cross-ratio, and the definition of period yield
\[
\ell(a^n b^n) + \ell(a^m b^m) \leq \ell(a^{m+n}b^{m+n}) + K
\]
i.e., the sequence $a_{n} \coloneqq \ell(a^{n}b^{n})$ is $K$-super-additive, and so by Fekete's lemma~\ref{lem:fekete}, we get
\[
\lim_n \frac{\ell(a^n b^n)}{n} = \lim_n \frac{a_n}{n} \geq a_1 = \ell(ab).
\]
Combining this with Equation~\ref{eq:limit_anbn}, we get
\[
\ell(a) + \ell(b) \geq \ell(ab),
\]
as desired.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Connected smoothing}

Here we verify that Equation~\ref{eq:conn_oriented} in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} is satisfied.

\begin{lemma}
Let $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$ with $(a,b)$ $R$-parallel..
Then, we have
\[
\ell(ab) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b).
\]
\label{lem:crossratio_connected_smoothing}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

Suppose $(a,b)$ are R-parallel. We first claim that $(b^m a^m, a^nb^n)$ R-cross for every $m,n \geq 1$.
This follows from induction on $m$ with base case $m=1$ given by Lemma~\ref{lem:anbn_vs_bkak}.

\[
\ell(a^n b^n) + \ell(b^m a^m) \geq \ell((b^m a^m)(a^n b^n)) = \ell(a^{n+m}b^{n+m}),

i.e. the sequence $a_n \coloneqq \ell(a^n b^n)$ is sub-additive. By Fekete's lemma~\ref{lem:fekete},

\ell(a^nb^n) \geq \ell(a^n) + \ell(b^n).

Indeed, take $n_0$ large enough so that for all $n \geq n_0$, we have $\ora{a}$ and $B^n \cdot \ora{a}$ do not cross, and so that $\ora{b}$ and $a^n \cdot \ora{b}$ do not cross, as sketched in Figure~\ref{fig:c8}.

\lim_n \frac{\ell(a^nb^n)}{n} \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b),

\ell(ab) \geq \ell(a) + \ell(b),
\]
as desired.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between periods of generalized cross-ratios and geodesic currents.
Moreover, periods uniquely determine cross-ratios arising from non-atomic geodesic currents.

\begin{proof}
By the Lemmas~\ref{lem:crinv}, \ref{lem:crossratio_stability}, \ref{lem:crossratio_disconnected_smoothing} and~\ref{lem:crossratio_connected_smoothing} above, every period $\ell$ induced by a generalized cross-ratio satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, and thus there exists a unique dual geodesic current $\mu_{\ell}$ such that $\ell(C)=i(\mu_{\ell},C)$ for every closed curve.
On the other hand, a geodesic current $\mu$ induces two cross-ratios $[\cdot]^\pm$ with the same periods $\ell^{\pm}$ satisfying $\ell^{\pm}(C)=i(\mu,C)$.
