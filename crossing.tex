\section{Crossings and Smoothings}
\label{sec:smoothing}



\subsection{Left vs right actions of \texorpdfstring{$\pi_1(S)$}{π1(S)}}
\label{sec:actions}

To fix conventions, we briefly recall elementary facts about actions of $\pi_1(S,\ast)$ on the universal
cover~$\wt S$ of a surface (or indeed any connected length space). We
follow the convention that path composition works in the natural
order, where a product $\gamma_1 \cdot \gamma_2$ of paths in~$S$ is
defined if $\gamma_1(1) = \gamma_2(0)$.
If $a \in \pi_1(S,\ast)$, we let $[a]$ denote the free homotopy class of $a$.
In particular, we note that $[g a g^{-1}]=[a]$. We will follow the
group theory convention of denoting inverses by a capital letter, and will use
boldface to distinguish points in $\wt S$.

\begin{definition}[Right action]
For a covering map $p \colon \wt S \to S$ of~$S$ and base-point
$\ast \in S$, we have a \emph{right} action of $\pi_1(S,\ast)$ on
the fiber $p^{-1}(\ast)$, defined by, for $\bm{x} \in p^{-1}(\ast)$,
$\bm{x}\cdot[\gamma] = \wt \gamma(1)$, where $\wt\gamma$ is the
unique lift of~$\gamma$ with $\wt\gamma(0) = \bm{x}$.
\end{definition}
We observe
the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item This right action does not depend on a choice of basepoint
  on~$\wt S$, and works for any covering space.%
  \footnote{One succinct statement of the classification theorem for
    covering spaces is that coverings of $S$ are in bijection with
    sets with a right action of $\pi_1(S)$. If we restrict to
    connected covering spaces, they
    correspond to transitive actions of $\pi_1(S)$, which
    in turn correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups.}
\item In the induced length metric on~$\wt S$, the distance between
  $\bm{x}$ and $\bm{x}\cdot [\gamma]$ is bounded by the length of~$\gamma$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Left action]
When $\wt S$ is the universal cover of~$S$ (or, more generally, any
normal cover of~$S$), there is a \emph{left} action of $\pi_1(S,\ast)$
on all of $\wt S$ by deck transformations. If you take
the standard model of $\wt S$, where one lifts $\ast \in S$ to a basepoint  $\bm{\wt \ast} \in \wt S$, and points
$[\gamma] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast}$ correspond
to homotopy classes of paths~$\gamma$
in~$S$ with $\gamma(0) = \ast$ (with homotopies fixing the endpoints), then this
action is defined by
$[\gamma_1] \cdot ([\gamma] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast}) \coloneqq
[\gamma_1 \cdot \gamma_2] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast}$.

\end{definition}
Here we make the following observations.
\begin{itemize}
\item This left action is an isometry of all of~$\wt S$.
\item The left action depends on a lift of
  $\ast \in S$ to a basepoint  $\bm{\wt \ast} \in \wt S$, for instance
  since the left and right actions are
  related by $[\gamma] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast} = \bm{\wt \ast} \cdot [\gamma]$.
\item The distance between $\bm{x}$ and $[\gamma] \cdot \bm{x}$
  may be unbounded, although the translation length of the left action
  of $[\gamma]$ on
  $\wt S$ is bounded by the length of $\gamma$.
\item This left action extends to an action on the circle at infinity $\partial_{\infty} S$.
\end{itemize}

We will frequently draw pictures of group elements $g\in\pi_1(S,\ast)$
acting on~$\bbH^2$. In these pictures, the arrows will be paths that
are lifts of~$g$, and thus correspond to the \emph{right} action on the
fiber.

For any non-trivial $x \in \pi_1(S,*)$, there is a corresponding
oriented closed curve $[x]$ on $S$, corresponding to the conjugacy
class of~$x$. We also need to specify different lifts of $[x]$ to
$\wt S$. This is conveniently specified by $x$ itself: $x$ determines
a sequence of points $\{[x^k] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast} \mid k \in \bbZ\}$, which is a
quasi-geodesic in $\wt S$. Choosing a hyperbolic structure $\Sigma$ on $S$, which is equivalent to choosing a discrete faithful representation $\rho \colon \pi_1(S) \to \Isom^+(\mathbb{H}^2)$, this quasi-geodesic determines an oriented hyperbolic geodesic
denoted $\ora{x}$ with endpoints $x^+ = \lim_{k \to \infty} [x^k] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast}$ 
and $x^- = \lim_{k \to -\infty} [x^k] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast}$. Note that $x$ acts on $\wt S$ on the left isometrically as a hyperbolic isometry fixing $\ora{x}$ set-wise. This action
extends continuously to $\overline{\wt S} \coloneqq \wt S \cup \partial_{\infty} S$ and has north-south (NS) dynamics, with unique
attracting point $x^+$ and repelling point $x^-$: for any point
$\bm{p} \in \overline{\wt S}$ other
than $x^-$, we have $\lim _{k \to \infty} x^k \cdot \bm{p} = x^+$.

The other lifts of $[x]$ to $\wt S$ are translates $q \cdot \ora{x}$
of $\ora{x}$ by a
group element~$q$; we can either think of this as the geodesic
$\ora{qxQ}$ passing within a bounded distance of the points $[qx^kQ] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast}$; 
or we can think of it as the geodesic corresponding to the coset
$q\langle x\rangle$, i.e., passing within a bounded distance of the
points $[qx^k] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast}$ in the coset. Note these two sequences of
points are within a
bounded distance of each other, since the right action of $\pi_1$
moves points a bounded distance.

With these preliminaries, we can see that two different lifts of
$[x]$,
\begin{align*}
  \ora{\ell_1} &= q_1 \cdot \ora{x} & \ora{\ell_2} &= q_2 \cdot \ora{x},
\end{align*}
are related by the isometry $q_2 q_1^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:line-translate}
  \ora{\ell_2} = q_2 q_1^{-1} \cdot \ora{\ell_1},
\end{equation}
while the geometric relation between the two lifts, meaning up to the left
$\pi_1(S,*)$ action, is captured instead by the double coset
\begin{equation}\label{eq:double_coset}
  \langle x \rangle q_1^{-1} q_2 \langle x \rangle.
\end{equation}
Note that the order of the product is reversed relative to
\eqref{eq:line-translate}. This double coset is what is relevant for,
e.g., determining if $\ora{\ell_1}$ and $\ora{\ell_2}$ intersect.

We can express many of our constructions as working with either
elements of $\pi_1(S)$ (acting by isometries on the universal cover)
or with general hyperbolic elements of the group $\Isom^+(\bbH^2)$.
(Every element of $\pi_1(S)$ necessarily acts hyperbolically on
$\wt S$.) When there is a choice, we prefer to state lemmas in terms
of $\Isom^+(\bbH^2)$, since in principle it is more general (as we do
not need to assume the group elements generate a discrete subgroup).


\subsection{Crossings and smoothings}
\label{subsec:crossings}

Loosely speaking, an essential crossing is a crossing of a
multi-curve that can't be homotoped away.


\begin{definition}\label{def:essential-crossing}
  A representative~$\gamma$ of a multi-curve~$C$ is \emph{minimal} if
  it has only transverse self-intersections and a minimum number of
  them.
    Let $\gamma$ be a concrete multi-curve on~$S$ representing a multi-curve $C$. A \emph{crossing} of $\gamma$ is a pair $(p,q)$ of
  points $p,q \in X(\gamma)$ so that
  $x \coloneqq \gamma(p) = \gamma(q) \in S$.
  A crossing that appears in some minimal representative is said
  to be an \emph{essential crossing}.
\end{definition}

Given an essential crossing of a multi-curve, we can do an operation
of ``smoothing'' in two different ways, each reducing the original
multi-curve to a different one.
We follow~\cite{MGT25:SmoothingsErratum} closely.


\begin{definition}[smoothings]
  \label{def:smoothing}
  Let $(p,q) \in X(\gamma)$ be an essential crossing of~$\gamma$
  on~$S$. To make a \emph{smoothing}~$\gamma'$ of $(p,q)$, cut
  $X(\gamma)$ at $p$ and~$q$ and reglue the resulting four endpoints
  in one of the two other possible ways, getting a new $1$-manifold
  $X(\gamma')$. The map $\gamma'$ agrees with~$\gamma$; this is
  well-defined since $\gamma(p) = \gamma(q)$. In pictures we will
  homotop $\gamma'$ slightly to round out the resulting
  corners, and we will write $\gamma \reducesto \gamma'$.

\end{definition}

``Some'' in the definition of essential crossing can be replaced by ``every'':  if there is a crossing $x$ in some minimal representative
  $\gamma$ of~$C$, then for any other minimal representative $\gamma'$
  there is a corresponding crossing~$x'$ giving the same
  smoothings.



\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Lemma~2.6]{MGT21:Smoothings}}]\label{lem:essential-cross}
  If $\gamma$ and $\gamma'$ are homotopic concrete
  multi-curves, with $\gamma$ minimal and $(p,q) \in X(\gamma)$ is an (essential)
  crossing of~$\gamma$,
  then there is a crossing $(p',q') \in X(\gamma')$ so
  that the smoothings of $(p,q)$ and of $(p',q')$ are homotopic.
\end{lemma}

As a result of Lemma~\ref{lem:essential-cross}, we can speak of
essential crossings for concrete curves $\gamma$ that are not minimal:
a crossing is essential if its two smoothings also arise from a
crossing in some minimal diagram.

We will also distinguish between different types of smoothings.
\begin{definition}\label{def:smoothing_types}
  If $\gamma$ is oriented, then the \emph{oriented smoothing}
  is the smoothing that respects the orientation on $X(\gamma)$:
  \begin{equation*}
    \mfig{curves-3} \reducesto \mfig{curves-4}
  \end{equation*}
  The \emph{unoriented smoothing} is the one that does not respect the
  orientation.
  In addition, we call a smoothing \emph{connected} or \emph{disconnected} if the
  original curve is connected or disconnected. We thus have four types
  of smoothings (using group notation that we will expand on
  later):
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Oriented connected smoothing: $[ab] \reducesto [a][b]$ (result
    disconnected)
  \item Unoriented connected smoothing: $[ab] \reducesto [aB]$ (result
    connected)
  \item Oriented disconnected smoothing: $[a][b] \reducesto [ab]$
    (result connected)
  \item Oriented disconnected smoothing: $[a][b] \reducesto [aB]$
    (result connected)
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Triality of crossings: topological, geometric and algebraic}

To give a more workable and algebraic criterion for essential
crossings, we
adopt a group-theoretic point of view. For $a,b \in \pi_1(S,*)$, write
$[a]$ for the free homotopy class of curves represented by $a$;
algebraically this is the union of the conjugacy classes of~$a$ and
of~$A$. Write $[a][b]$ for the multi-curve $[a] \cup [b]$. Say that a
factorization $[ab]$ \emph{reduces} if some (equiv.\ every) taut
representative of $[ab]$ has a crossing so that one smoothing gives
$[a][b]$ and the other smoothing gives $[aB]$. Similarly say that
$[a][b]$ \emph{reduces} if some/every taut representative has a crossing
whose smoothings give $[ab]$ and $[aB]$. (This is an abuse of
notation, since the reduction is not invariant under individual
conjugation of $a$ and $b$.)

For $a,b \in \pi_1(S,*)$, let $a * b$ be a 4-valent graph mapped into
$S$ obtained by taking the union of the paths $a,b$ joined at the
basepoint~$*$, and let $[a * b]$
be its free homotopy class.
Free homotopy means in particular invariance under simultaneous
conjugation: $[a * b] = [caC * cbC]$. A \emph{minimal} representative of $[a * b]$
is one with a minimum number of transverse crossings.

Finally, if we fix a hyperbolic structure on~$S$, for a non-trivial
element $a \in \pi_1(S,*)$ let $\ora{a}$ be the geodesic in
$\wt{S}$, determined as the unique geodesic that stays a bounded distance from
$a^k \cdot \wt{*}$, with endpoints $a^+$ and $a^-$ (see Section~\ref{sec:actions}).

On the one hand, again by Section~\ref{sec:actions}, any non-trivial element
$a \in \pi_1(S)$ acts on $\wt S = \bbH^2$ on the left by an isometry
of $\bbH^2$ that is hyperbolic (in the sense of having two distinct
fixed points on $\partial_{\infty}\bbH^2$). On the other hand, the notions we
will consider will only depend on the cyclic order on the circle at
infinity of endpoints of geodesics, and in particular do not depend on
the metric.
In principle one should invoke a representation $\rho \colon \pi_1(S)
\to \Isom^+(\bbH^2)$, but since nothing depends on this representation
we will suppress it.

\begin{definition}\label{def:crossing}
  Depending on the (counterclockwise) cyclic order of the endpoints,
  we say that the pair $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ are
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{R-parallel} if they appear in order $a^+,a^-,b^-,b^+$;
  \item \emph{R-anti-parallel} if they appear in order
    $a^+,a^-,b^+,b^-$; and
  \item \emph{R-cross} or \emph{cross to the right} if they appear in
    order $a^+,b^-,a^-,b^+$.
  \end{itemize}
  (See Figure~\ref{fig:cases-a-b}.) In each case we say that they are
  L-parallel/L-anti-parallel/L-cross if they appear in the reverse
  cyclic order, and are parallel/anti-parallel/cross if they appear in
  either the above order or its reverse.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
  Non-trivial group elements $a,b\in \pi_1$ are \emph{common powers}
  if there are non-zero $k,\ell$ so that $a^k = b^\ell$. Since $\pi_1$
  has no torsion, we may assume that $k,\ell$ are relatively prime and
  then we see this is equivalent to the existence of a $c$ so that
  $a = c^\ell$ and $b = c^k$.
\end{definition}


\begin{proposition}[{\cite[Proposition~3]{MGT25:SmoothingsErratum}}]\label{prop:crossings-triality}
  Let $a,b \in \pi_1(S,*)$ be non-trivial elements that are not
  common powers. Then in the following three trialities the same
  possibility holds in each case.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item (Reduction) One of the following factorizations reduces
    to the other two:\\
    \begin{enumerate*}[itemjoin=\qquad]
    \item $[ab]$;
    \item $[aB]$; or
    \item $[a][b]$.
    \end{enumerate*}
  \item\label{item:triality-geom}
    (Geometric) For every taut embedding of $a * b$, around the
    4-valent vertex we see in cyclic
    order, up to orientation reversal:\\
    \begin{enumerate*}[itemjoin=\qquad]
    \item $\mfig{curves-30}$;
    \item $\mfig{curves-31}$; or
    \item $\mfig{curves-32}$.
    \end{enumerate*}
  \item\label{item:triality-alg} (Algebraic) The geodesics $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ are:\\
    \begin{enumerate*}[itemjoin=\qquad]
    \item parallel;
    \item anti-parallel; or
    \item cross.
    \end{enumerate*}
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Note that triality~\eqref{item:triality-geom} is about the local
behavior near the vertex, while triality~\eqref{item:triality-alg} is
similar but
about the asymptotic behavior near $\infty$.

We extract the following two lemmas that follow immediately from
Proposition~\ref{prop:crossings-triality}, and will use them
extensively without explicit reference.

\begin{lemma}
Let $a,b \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$. If $\ora{a}$ crosses $\ora{b}$, then
\[
  [a][b] \reducesto [ab]\qquad\text{and}\qquad[a][b] \reducesto [aB].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
Let $a,b \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$. If $\ora{a}$ is parallel to $\ora{b}$, then
\[
  [ab] \reducesto [a][b]\qquad\text{and}\qquad[ab] \reducesto [aB].
\]
\end{lemma}

If we swap $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$, the type of L/R crossing/parallelism
can change, as recorded below for future reference.

\begin{lemma} Let $a,b\in \Isom^+(\bbH^2)$ be hyperbolic elements.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ R-cross, then $(\ora{B},\ora{A})$
    R-cross and $(\ora{b},\ora{a})$ L-cross.
  \item If $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ are R-parallel, then
    $(\ora{B},\ora{A})$ are R-parallel, $(\ora{b},\ora{a})$ are
    L-parallel, and $(\ora{a},\ora{B})$ are
    R-anti-parallel.
  \item If $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ are R-anti-parallel, then
    $(\ora{b},\ora{a})$ are R-anti-parallel, $(\ora{A},\ora{B})$ are
    L-anti-parallel,
    and $(\ora{a},\ora{B})$
    are R-parallel.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Immediate from the definitions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
  Let $a,x \in \Isom^+(\bbH^2)$ be hyperbolic elements that do not share any endpoints.
  Then $\ora{a}$ and~$\ora{x}$ R-cross (resp.\ L-cross) iff
  $(\ora{x},a \cdot \ora{x})$ are R-parallel (resp.\ L-parallel).
  More generally, if
  $a \in \Isom(\bbH^2)$ is a hyperbolic element with attracting
  endpoint $a^+$ in an interval $(x,y)$ iff
  $a \cdot (x,y) \subsetneq (x,y)$.
 \label{lem:rhs}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The statement that $(\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{x})$ are R-parallel is (by
  definition) equivalent to $a\cdot(x^-,x^+) \subsetneq (x^-,x^+)$,
  which in turn is equivalent to $a^+ \in (x^-,x^+)$ and
  $a^- \notin (x^-,x^+)$, which is turn is equivalent to
  $(\ora{a},\ora{x})$ R-crossing. The last claim follows similarly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
  Let $a,b,x \in \pi_1(S)$ be group elements so that $(\ora{a},\ora{x})$ and $(\ora{b},\ora{x})$ R-cross. Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item All four geodesics $\ora{xa}$, $\ora{Xa}$, $\ora{ax}$, and
    $\ora{aX}$ R-cross $\ora{x}$.
  \item $(\ora{ab},\ora{x})$ R-cross.
  \item More generally, for any $n,k \in \bbZ$, $(\ora{a x^n},\ora{x})$
    and $(\ora{ax^nbx^k},\ora{x})$ R-cross.
  \end{enumerate}
    \label{lem:rhs_prod}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  For the first claim, consider the action of the respective group
  element on the interval $(x^-,x^+)$: we have
  $x^{\pm1} \cdot (x^-,x^+) = (x^-,x^+)$ and
  $a \cdot (x^-,x^+) \subsetneq (x^-,x^+)$, so four of $ax^{\pm1}$ and
  $x^{\pm1}a$ map this interval strictly inside itself. It follows
  that all these elements are hyperbolic and R-cross $\ora{x}$.

  For the second claim, we similarly have that both $a$ and $b$ map
  $(x^-,x^+)$ strictly inside itself, so their product does as well.

  The last claim is an immediate consequence of the first two parts.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Position of axes \texorpdfstring{$\protect\ora{aB}$}{aB} and \texorpdfstring{$\protect\ora{Ba}$}{Ba} in parallel  case}
\label{subsec:parallel_aB}
In order to check the smoothing property in more algebraic settings, we will need to know the relative position of the axes $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$. This will be useful in particular when curve functionals arise from translation lengths of actions on real trees, as in Section~\ref{sec:skora}.
Observe that the relative of position of $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$ is completely determined  when $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ cross, by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}(I). However, when $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ are parallel (or anti-parallel), several cases arise. In this section we study these cases.

If $C$ represents a curve with a self-intersection decomposing as $ab$, we can consider intersecting axes $\ora{ab}$ and $\ora{ba}$ in the universal cover so that their intersection is a lift of the self-intersection of the curve $ab$.

One of the resolutions of $[ab]$ is the multi-curve $[a] \cup [b]$.
The lifts of $[a]$ and $[b]$ to the universal cover correspond to axes $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ which are parallel.
It remains to determine the position of the lifts $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$.

To that end, consider the common perpendicular line of $a$ and $b$. We will denote this line by $R_1$, and will use the same notation for the reflection along $R_1$.
Let $R_2, R_3, R_4, R_5$ be perpendicular lines and corresponding reflections so that $a=R_1 R_2 = R_4 R_1$, and $b=R_1 R_3 = R_5 R_1$. Note that $R_i$ are reflections along pairwise non-intersecting reflection lines, since $aB$ and $ab$ are hyperbolic.
It then follows that

\subsection{Fundamental smoothing lemmas}
\label{sec:fund-smoothing}


As part of our construction, we will use large powers of some fixed
elements. We collect some propositions guaranteeing essential
crossings in this context.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:join-split-geom}
Let $a,b,c,d,x \in \pi_1(S)$ be curves with $\ora{a}$, $\ora{b}$,
$\ora{c}$, $\ora{d}$
crossing $\ora{x}$ to the right. Then there is $s \ge 0$ (depending on $a,b$)
  so that, for all
 $n, m, r \in \mathbb{Z}$ with either $s < r < m-n-s$ or $m-n+s < r <
 -s$, we have
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:cross-join} [a x^n] [b x^m] &\reducesto [ax^{n+r}bx^{m-r}]\\
    \label{eq:cross-split} [a x^nb x^m] &\reducesto [ax^{n+r}][bx^{m-r}].
  \end{align}


  There exists $s' \ge 0$ (depending on $a,x$) so that for all
  $|n-m| > s'$,

  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:cross-cancel}[ax^n][ax^m] &\reducesto [x^{|n-m|}].
  \end{align}

 If $x$ is simple and $a=b$, we may take $s=s'=0$. In particular, for
 all $n\in\bbZ$,
 \begin{align}
    [a x^{n+1}] [a x^{n-1}] &\reducesto 2[ax^{n}].\label{eq:cross-simple}
  \end{align}
\end{proposition}


Note that for the allowed values of $r$, Eqs.\ \eqref{eq:cross-join} and
\eqref{eq:cross-split} move the exponents $n,m$ closer to the diagonal
$m=n$.

We will make heavy use of the \emph{band model} centered on
$\ora{x}$, meaning the conformal map from $\bbH^2$ to a horizontal
strip $(-\infty,\infty) \times [-v,v]$ mapping $x^-$ to infinity along
the negative $x$-axis and $x^+$ to infinity on the positive $x$-axis.
We choose the \emph{normalized} band model, picking $v$ so that the
action of $x$ is horizontal translation by $z \mapsto z+1$.

For any geodesic $\ora{y}$ that is parallel to
$\ora{x}$, there is a \emph{window} $w(\ora{y})$ cut out by the
endpoints on the band model, the interval between $y^\pm$ that
misses~$x^\pm$. Observe that $w(x^k\cdot\ora{y})$ is the horizontal
translate by $k$ of $w(\ora{y})$. See Figure~\ref{fig:abcrossings} for
examples.

\begin{lemma}
  Given $a,b, x \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ so that $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ cross $\ora{x}$ to
  the right and $g_1 = x^k ax^\ell$ with $k,\ell \in \bbZ$, we have
  $g_1^+ \in w(x^k a\cdot\ora{x})$ and
  $g_1^- \in w(x^{-\ell}A\cdot\ora{x})$. Similarly for
  $g_2 = x^k a x^\ell b x^n$ with $k,\ell,n\in\bbZ$, we have
  $g_2^+ \in w(x^ka \cdot \ora{x})$ and $g_2^- \in w(x^{-n} B
  \cdot\ora{x})$.
\label{lem:windows}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For the first part, it suffices to show that $\ora{g_1}$ crosses
  $x^ka \cdot \ora{x}$ and $x^{-\ell A} \cdot \ora{x}$ to the right,
  or (by translation) that $\ora{x^{\ell+k}a}$ and $\ora{ax^{k+\ell}}$
  cross $\ora{x}$ to the right. This follows from
  Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod}.

  For the second part, we similarly use Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod} to
  see that $\ora{g_2}$ crosses $x^ka \cdot \ora{x}$ and $x^{-n}B \cdot
  \ora{x}$ to the right.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:join-split-geom}]
  In each case, we will show that we can choose lifts of the curves to
  the normalized band model for which a
  relevant crossing is essential. We start with Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-join}.
  Since each window is a
  finite size, there is a finite range $[s_1,s_2]$ so that $w(x^rb\cdot\ora{x})$
  is to the left of (resp.\ to the right of) $w(a\cdot\ora{x})$ if $r < s_1$
  (resp.\ $r > s_2$). Similarly there
  is a range $[s_1',s_2']$ so that $w(A\cdot\ora{x})$ is disjoint from
  $w(x^rB\cdot\ora{x})$ if $r \notin [s_1',s_2']$, and determining the
  ordering of the windows. Set
  $s \coloneqq \max\{s_2,s_2',-s_1,-s_1'\}$.

  Now consider
  $g_1 = ax^n$ and $g_2 = x^rbx^{m-r}$, with $s < r < m-n-s$. We have
  \[
    r-m < -n-s \le -n+s_1',
  \]
  so $g_1^- \in w(x^{r-m}B\cdot\ora{x})$ is to the left of $g_2^- \in
  w(x^{-n}A\cdot\ora{x})$, and
  \[
    s_2 \le s < r,
  \]
  so $g_1^+ \in w(x^rb\cdot\ora{x})$ is to the right of $g_2^+ \in
  w(a\cdot\ora{x})$ (using Lemma~\ref{lem:windows}). Thus
  $\ora{x^rbx^{m-r}}$ crosses $\ora{ax^n}$ to the left. Similarly if
  $m-n+s < r < -s$, then $\ora{x^rbx^{m-r}}$ crosses $\ora{ax^n}$ to the
  right. In either case,
  \[
    [ax^n][bx^m] = [ax^n][x^r bx^{m-r}] \reducesto [ax^{n+r}bx^{m-r}]
  \]
  by oriented disconnected smoothing.
  For Equation~\eqref{eq:cross-split}, write $[x^r ax^n bx^{m-r}]=[gh]$ with
  $g=x^r ax^n$ and $h=bx^{m-r}$, 
  so $g^+ \in w(x^ra\cdot\ora{x})$ and $g^- \in w(x^{-n}A \cdot \ora{x})$, and $h^+ \in w(b \cdot \ora{x})$, $h^- \in w(x^{r-m} B \cdot \ora{x})$.
  Hence, if $s < r < m-n-s$, we have $s_1 < r$
  so $w(b\cdot\ora{x})$ is to the left of $w(x^r a\cdot\ora{x})$,
  while $r-m < -n+s_1'$, so $w(x^{r-m}B \cdot\ora{x})$ is to the left
  of $w(x^{-n}A\cdot\ora{x})$, and hence $\ora{h}$ and $\ora{g}$ are $R$-parallel, giving Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-split} by oriented connected smoothing.
  
  We similarly get parallelism if $m-n+s < r < -s$.

  To see Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-cancel}, suppose without loss of generality
  that $n < m$. Let $s_0 = \lceil \wid(w(a\cdot\ora{x})) \rceil$
  (with width measured in the normalized band model),
  and
  let $s' = 2s_0 - 1$. Since $m-n \ge 2s_0$, we can pick $r$ so that
  $s_0 \le r \le m-n-s_0$. By the above argument, $\ora{ax^n}$ and
  $\ora{x^rax^{m-r}}$ R-cross (since, e.g., $(ax^n)^+ \in w(a\cdot\ora{x})$ and
  $(x^rax^{m-r})^+ \in w(x^r a\cdot\ora{x})$, which are disjoint), so we get the
  desired smoothing in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-cancel} by using unoriented smoothing.

  In the cases when $x$ is simple, two windows are disjoint, nested,
  or identical; this implies that $s_1 = s_2$ and $s_1' = s_2'$.
  If in
  addition $a=b$, we also see that $s_1=s_1'=0=s$. This gives Eqs.\
  \eqref{eq:cross-join} and \eqref{eq:cross-split}, and the special
  case Eq.\ \eqref{eq:cross-simple}.

  To see that with $x$ simple we get Eq.\ \eqref{eq:cross-cancel} with
  $s'=0$ (i.e., $[ax^n][ax^{n+1}] \reducesto [x]$), we look slightly deeper
  in the words. The analysis above shows $(ax^n)^- \in
  w(x^{-n}A\cdot\ora{x})$, which is to the right of
  $(ax^{n+1})^- \in w(x^{-n-1}A\cdot\ora{x})$. By
  looking at the band model centered on $a \cdot \ora{x}$ (or
  equivalently by conjugating by $a$), we see that
  $(ax^n)^+ \in w(ax^na\cdot\ora{x}) \subset w(a\cdot\ora{x})$. Similarly
  $(ax^{n+1})^+ \in w(ax^{n+1}a\cdot\ora{x}) \subset
  w(a\cdot\ora{x})$. We therefore have that $(ax^n)^+$ is to the left
  of $(ax^{n+1})^+$, implying that $\ora{ax^n}$ crosses $\ora{ax^{n+1}}$ as
  desired.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Note the main place we use unoriented smoothings in this paper is in the
  proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-cancel}, but it is necessary. See
  Sec.~\ref{sec:asymmetric}.
\end{remark}

We also need some lemmas about smoothings where we have high powers of
two different elements. We work with three elements $a,x,y\in\pi_1(S)$
so that $(\ora{y},\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{y})$ are R-parallel; this
implies, for instance, that $(a\cdot\ora{y},a\cdot\ora{x})$ are also
R-parallel.
We again work in the normalized band model
centered on~$\ora{x}$, starting with another lemma about endpoints.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ayx-window}
  For any triple $a,x,y\in\pi_1(S)$ so that
  $(\ora{y},\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{y})$ are R-parallel, and for
  $\ora{g}=\ora{x^k ay^n x^\ell}$ with $k,\ell,n\in\bbZ$, we have
  $g^+ \in w(x^k a \cdot\ora{y})$ and
  $g^- \in w(x^{-\ell}\cdot\ora{y})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Consider $g$ acting on $x^{-\ell} \cdot (y^-,y^+)$, the \emph{complement}
  of $w(x^{-\ell} \cdot \ora{y})$. We have
  $x^k ay^n \cdot(y^-,y^+) = x^k a\cdot (y^-,y^+) = w(x^k a \cdot
  \ora{y}) \subset (x^-,x^+) \subset x^{-\ell} \cdot (y^-,y^+)$,
  showing the claims about~$g^\pm$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fundamental-ayx}
  Let $a,x,y\in\pi_1(S)$ be chosen so that
  $(\ora{y},\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{y})$ are R-parallel. Then there exists
  $s \ge 0$ so that for all $r > s$ and $m,n \in \bbZ$,
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:crossuniform_n}
       [a y^m x^{n+r}][a y^m x^{n-r}] &\reducesto [(a y^m x^n)^2]\\
    \label{eq:crossuniform_m}
       [a y^{m+r} x^n][a y^{m-r} x^n] &\reducesto [(a y^m x^n)^2].
  \end{align}
  Furthermore, there is $s' > 0$ so that, for $n,m \ge s'$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:cancel-ayx}
    [a y^m x^n][a] \reducesto [y^m][x^n].
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We first prove Eq.~(\ref{eq:crossuniform_n}). We will have a number
  of windows $w(x^m \cdot \ora{y})$ on the top, all of equal width
  in the normalized band model, and $w(x^m a \cdot \ora{y})$ on the
  bottom, also of equal widths (but not necessarily equal to widths on
  the top). Take
  \[
    s_0 \coloneqq \lceil\max(\wid(w(\ora{y})),
    \wid(w(a\cdot\ora{y})))\rceil.
  \]
  For $r > s_0$, consider the elements $g_1 = x^n ay^m x^r$ and $g_2 =
  x^{n-r} a y^m$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ayx-window}, we have
  \begin{align*}
    g_1^+ &\in w(x^n a\cdot\ora{y}) & g_1^- &\in w(x^{-r}\cdot\ora{y})\\
    g_2^+ &\in w(x^{n-r} a \cdot\ora{y}) & g_2^- &\in w(\ora{y}).
  \end{align*}
  Since $r>s_0$, the endpoints appear in the cyclic order
  $(x^-,g_2^+,g_1^+,x^+,g_2^-,g_1^-)$ on $\partial_\infty S$. In
  particular $[g_1][g_2] \reducesto [g_1 g_2]$ by oriented disconnected smoothing, as desired.
  
  To see Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossuniform_n}, apply
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossuniform_m} with the change of variables
  $a' = a$, $y' = x$, and $x' = ayA$.
  This gives the desired result for some $s_1 \ge 0$. For the full
  statement, take $s \coloneqq\max(s_0,s_1)$.

  For Eq.~\eqref{eq:cancel-ayx}, we consider $g = a y ^m x^n$ and
  compare its endpoints to those of $a$. By
  Lemma~\ref{lem:ayx-window}, we have
  $g^- \in w(x^{-n} \cdot \ora{y})$, which for large enough $n$ is to
  the left of $a^- \in w(\ora{y})$. For $g^+$,
  we again have to look deeper at the endpoints: since
  $g \cdot (x^+,x^-) = a y^m \dot (x^+, x^-) = w(a y^m \cdot
  \ora{x})$, we have
  $g^+ \in w(a y^m \cdot \ora{x}) \subset w(a \cdot \ora{y})$.
  Similarly $a^+ \in w(a \cdot \ora{x}) \subset w(a \cdot \ora{y})$.
  Consider the bands model centered on $a \cdot \ora{y}$ shows that,
  for $m$ large enough, $g^+$ is to the right of $a^+$, and thus
  $\ora{g}$ crosses $\ora{a}$ to the right. We therefore have
  \[
    [a x^n y^m][a] = [g][a] \reducesto [x^n y^m]
  \]
  by oriented disconnected smoothing.
  Finally, by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}, since $\ora{x^n}$
  and $\ora{y^m}$ are R-parallel, the geodesics $\ora{y^m x^n}$ and
  $\ora{x^n y^m}$ cross so $[x^n y^m] \reducesto [x^n][y^m]$, by oriented connected smoothing.
\end{proof}

In practice we will apply Proposition~\ref{prop:fundamental-ayx} with
four group elements, in a configuration that is easy to find.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fund-axby}
  Let $a,b,x,y \in \pi_1(S)$ be chosen so that $(B\cdot \ora{x},
  \ora{y}, a\cdot\ora{x})$ are R-parallel.
  Then there exists
  $s \ge 0$ so that for all $r > s$ and $m,n \in \bbZ$,
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:axby-cross-n}
       [a x^{n+r} by^m][ax^{n-r} by^m] &\reducesto [(ax^n by^m)^2]\\
    \label{eq:axby-cross-m}
       [ax^n by^{m+r}][ax^n by^{m-r}] &\reducesto [(ax^n by^m)^2].
  \end{align}
  Furthermore, there is $s' > 0$ so that, for $n,m \ge s'$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:cancel-axby}
    [a x^n b y^m][ab] \reducesto [x^n][y^m].
  \end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:fundamental-ayx} with $y' = y$,
  $x' = axA$, and $a' = ba$. (Observe that the conditions imply that
  $(\ora{y}, a \cdot \ora{x}, ba \cdot \ora{y})$ are R-parallel.)
\end{proof}
