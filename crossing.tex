\subsubsection{Left vs right actions of \texorpdfstring{$\pi_1(S)$}{π1(S)}}
\label{sec:actions}

\DMGtodo[inline]{left vs right actions}

To fix conventions, we briefly recall elementary facts about actions of $\pi_1(S,\ast)$ on the universal
cover~$\wt S$ of a surface (or indeed any connected length space). We
follow the convention that path composition works in the natural
order, where a product $\gamma_1 \cdot \gamma_2$ of paths in~$S$ is
defined if $\gamma_1(1) = \gamma_2(0)$.
If $a \in \pi_1(S,\ast)$, we let $[a]$ denote the free homotopy class of $a$.
In particular, we note that $[g a g^{-1}]=[a]$.

\begin{definition}[Right action]
For a covering map $p \colon \wt S \to S$ of~$S$ and base-point
$\ast \in S$, we have a \emph{right} action of $\pi_1(S,\ast)$ on
the fiber $p^{-1}(\ast)$, defined by, for $\bm{x} \in p^{-1}(\ast)$,
$\bm{x}\cdot[\gamma] = \wt \gamma(1)$, where $\wt\gamma$ is the
unique lift of~$\gamma$ with $\wt\gamma(0) = \bm{x}$.
\end{definition}
(The bold font denotes points in $\wt{S}$.)
We observe
the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item This right action does not depend on a choice of basepoint
  on~$\wt S$, and works for any covering space.%
  \footnote{One succinct statement of the classification theorem for
    covering spaces is that coverings of $S$ are in bijections with
    sets with a right action of $\pi_1(S)$.}
\item In the induced length metric on~$\wt S$, the distance between
  $\bm{x}$ and $\bm{x}\cdot [\gamma]$ is bounded by the length of~$\gamma$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Left action]
When $\wt S$ is the universal cover of~$S$ (or, more generally, any
normal cover of~$S$), there is a \emph{left} action of $\pi_1(S,\ast)$
on all of $\wt S$ by deck transformations. If you take
the standard model of $\wt S$ where points
$\bm{P}_{[\gamma]}$ correspond
to homotopy classes of paths~$\gamma$
in~$S$ with $\gamma(0) = \ast$ (with homotopies fixing the endpoints), then this
action is defined by
$[\gamma_1] \cdot \bm{P}_{[\gamma_2]} \coloneqq
\bm{P}_{[\gamma_1\cdot\gamma_2]}$.

\DPTtodo{Change this bold-face convention}
We will also denote the point in the cover corresponding to a group
element by bold-face: $\bm{P}_{[\gamma]} = \bm{[\gamma]}$.
\end{definition}
Here we make the following observations.
\begin{itemize}
\item This left action is by an isometry of all of~$\wt S$.
\item The left action depends on a lift of
  $\ast \in S$ to a basepoint  $\bm{\wt \ast} \in \wt S$, for instance
  since the left and right actions are
  related by $[\gamma] \cdot \bm{\wt \ast} = \bm{\wt \ast} \cdot [\gamma]$.
\item The distance between $\bm{x}$ and $[\gamma] \cdot \bm{x}$
  may be unbounded, although the translation length of $[\gamma]$ on
  $\wt S$ is bounded by the length of $\gamma$ (by the first bullet
  point).
\item This left action extends to an action on the circle at infinity $\partial\wt S$.
\end{itemize}

We will frequently draw pictures of group elements $g\in\pi_1(S,\ast)$
acting on~$\bbH^2$. In these pictures, the arrows will be paths that
are lifts of~$g$, and thus correspond to the \emph{right} action on the
fiber.

For any non-trivial $x \in \pi_1(S,*)$, there is a corresponding
oriented closed curve $[x]$ on $S$, corresponding to the conjugacy
class of~$x$. We also need to specify different lifts of $[x]$ to
$\wt S$. This is conveniently specified by $x$ itself: $x$ determines
a sequence of points $\{\bm{x^k} \mid k \in \bbZ\}$, which is a
quasi-geodesic in $\wt S$, and thus determines an oriented geodesic
denoted $\ora{x}$ with endpoints $x^+ = \lim_{k \to \infty} \bm{x^k}$
and $x^- = \lim_{k \to -\infty} \bm{x^k}$. Note that $x$ acting on the
left on $\overline{\wt S}$ has north-south (NS) dynamics, with unique
attracting point $x^+$ and repelling point $x^-$: for any point
$\bm{p} \in \wt S \cup \partial \wt S$ other
than $x^-$, we have $\lim _{k \to \infty} x^k \cdot \bm{p} = x^+$.

The other lifts of $[x]$ to $\wt S$ are translates $q \cdot \ora{x}$
of $\ora{x}$ by a
group element~$q$; we can either think of this as the geodesic
$\ora{qxQ}$ passing within a bounded distance of the points $\bm{qx^kQ}$;%
\footnote{We follow the group theory convention of denoting inverses
  by a capital letter.}
or we can think of it as the geodesic corresponding to the coset
$q\langle x\rangle$, i.e., passing within a bounded distance of the
points $\bm{qx^k}$. (Note these two sequences of points are within a
bounded distance of each other, since the right action of $\pi_1$
moves points a bounded distance.)

In this way, we can see that two different lifts
\begin{align*}
  \ell_1 &= q_1 \cdot \ora{x} & \ell_2 &= q_2 \cdot \ora{x}
\end{align*}
are related by the isometry $q_2 q_1^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:line-translate}
  \ell_2 = q_2 q_1^{-1} \cdot \ell_1,
\end{equation}
but the geometric relation between the two lifts (up to the left
$\pi_1(S,*)$ action) is captured instead by the double coset
\begin{equation}\label{eq:double_coset}
  \langle x \rangle q_1^{-1} q_2 \langle x \rangle.
\end{equation}
Note that the order of the product is, confusingly, reversed relative to
\eqref{eq:line-translate}.

We can express many of our constructions as working with either
elements of $\pi_1(S)$ (acting by isometries on the universal cover)
or with general hyperbolic elements of the group $\Isom^+(\bbH^2)$.
(Every element of $\pi_1(S)$ necessarily acts hyperbolically on
$\wt S$.) When there is a choice, we prefer to state lemmas in terms
of $\Isom^+(\bbH^2)$, since in principle it is more general (as we do
not need to assume the group elements generate a discrete subgroup).

\subsection{Crossings}
\label{subsec:essential}

\DMGtodo[inline]{definition of minimal/essential crossing}
\begin{definition}
  A representative~$\gamma$ of a multi-curve~$C$ is \emph{minimal} if
  it has only transverse self-intersections and a minimum number of
  them. A crossing that appears in some minimal representative is said
  to be \emph{essential}, and induces a reduction relation
  $C \reducesto C_1$ and $C \reducesto C_2$ for the two different ways
  of smoothing the crossing.
\end{definition}

``Some'' above can be replaced by ``every'':
\begin{lemma}
  If there is a crossing $x$ in some minimal representative
  $\gamma$ of~$C$, then for any other minimal representative $\gamma'$
  there is a corresponding crossing~$x'$ giving the same
  smoothings.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  By results of Haas and Scott \cite{HS94:ShorteningCurves}, any two
  minimal representatives of a curve are related by Reidemeister III
  (RIII) moves and by interchanging two components that are powers of
  the same curve. Both of these operations induce a natural bijection
  between crossings that preserves the curves that result from
  smoothing.
\end{proof}

We will distinguish two types of smoothings.

A \emph{disconnected smoothing} is a smoothing of a crossing of two distinct curves.
A \emph{connected smoothing} is a smoothing of a self-crossing of two distinct curves.

\subsubsection{Right-handed crossings}
\label{subsec:rhcrossings}

\DMGtodo[inline]{right handed crossings}
In order to work better with group elements and crossings between
oriented geodesics, we pick out one type of essential crossing.

\begin{definition}[Right handed crossing]
  \label{def:righthandedcrossing}
  For non-trivial $x,a \in \pi_1(S)$ that do not share a common power,
  the corresponding geodesics $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{x}$ have a
  \emph{right-handed crossing} or \emph{R-cross} if the endpoints
  $a^-, x^-, a^+, x^+$ appear in that
  (counter-clockwise) cyclic order on $\partial \wt S$. (We also apply
  rules of English grammar, for instance talking about left-handed
  crossings if they have the opposite cyclic order.)
\end{definition}

Note that, if we draw the loops $[x]$ and $[a]$ as based at the
basepoint $* \in S$, the loops intersect at $*$ by definition. If that
intersection is essential (and $x$ and $a$ do not share a power),
there are two possible cyclic orders for the endpoints, so either
$\ora{a}$ crosses $\ora{x}$ to the right or $\ora{x}$ crosses
$\ora{a}$ to the right. In the first case (as in the Definition) the picture looks
intuitively like

Since a right-handed crossing is essential, we have the following.
\begin{lemma}
Let $a,x \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$. If $\ora{a}$ crosses $\ora{x}$ to the
right (or to the left), then
\[
  [a][x] \reducesto [ax]\qquad\text{and}\qquad[a][x] \reducesto [aX].
\]
\end{lemma}

Observe that whether a crossing is ``right-handed'' is invariant under
simultaneous conjugation of $a$ and $x$, but does depend on
more than the conjugacy classes of $a$ and~$x$. (We need to specify
which crossing we are considering.)

We also have the following.

\begin{lemma}
Let $a,x \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$. If $\ora{a}$ is right parallel (or left parallel) to $\ora{x}$, then
\[
  [ax] \reducesto [a][x]\qquad\text{and}\qquad[ax] \reducesto [aX].
\]
\end{lemma}

Following this, we will distinguish between different types of smoothings. The nomenclature below is chosen with regards to  the connectedness of the multi-curve in the pre-smoothing, and, in turn, each corresponding case will have two subcases depending on orientation.
Indeed, if $(a,b)$ is a pair of crossing or parallel distinct elements, then the curves $[ab]$ and $[aB]$ are connected.

\begin{definition}
We will coin the following types of smoothing.
\begin{enumerate}
\item disconnected smoothing: For any pair of crossing elements $(a,b)$, 
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sm_disconn_oriented} \mbox{oriented} \quad [a] \cup [b] &\searrow [ab]\\
    \label{eq:sm_disconn_unoriented} \mbox{unoriented} \quad [a] \cup [b] &\searrow [aB]
  \end{align}
\item connected smoothing: For any pair of parallel elements $(a,b)$, 
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sm_conn_oriented} \mbox{oriented} \quad [ab] &\searrow [a] \cup [b] \\
    \label{eq:sm_conn_unoriented} \mbox{unoriented} \quad [ab] &\searrow [aB].
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Observe that requiring the above condition for $R$-crossing (resp. $R$-parallel) is the same as requiring them for $L$-crossing (resp. $L$-parallel), by the symmetry of the conditions, conjugacy invariance, and the fact that if $(a,b)$ are $R$-crossing, then $(b,a)$ are $L$-crossing, and if $(a,b)$ are $R$-parallel, then $(b,a)$ are $L$-parallel.

We also make the following trivial observation, using
Convention~\ref{conv:circle-intervals}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hyperbolic}
  The geodesics $(\ora{a}, \ora{x})$ R-cross iff
  $a \cdot (x^-, x^+) \subsetneq (x^-, x^+)$. More generally, if
  $a \in \Isom(\bbH^2)$ is a hyperbolic element with attracting
  endpoint $a^+$ in an interval $(x,y)$ iff
  $a \cdot (x,y) \subsetneq (x,y)$.
\end{lemma}

\subsubsection{Parallelism and crossing of hyperbolic axes}

We now turn to some elementary lemmas about crossing. For this
purpose, we also distinguish the various other types of relations
between two geodesics $\ora{a}, \ora{b}$.

We will switch to using elements of $\Isom^+(\bbH^2)$. On the one
hand, if we fix any hyperbolic metric on~$S$, any non-trivial element
$a \in \pi_1(X)$ acts on $\wt S = \bbH^2$ on the left by an isometry
of $\bbH^2$ that is hyperbolic (in the sense of having two distinct
fixed points on $\partial\bbH^2$). On the other hand, the notions we
will consider will only depend on the cyclic order on the circle at
infinity of endpoints of geodesics, and in particular do not depend on
the metric.
In principle one should invoke a representation $\rho \colon \pi_1(S)
\to \Isom^+(\bbH^2)$, but since nothing depends on this representation
we will suppress it.

\begin{definition}
  Let $a,b$
  be hyperbolic elements in $\Isom^+(\mathbb{H}^2)$, and let
  $\ora{a}, \ora{b}$ be the oriented geodesics that are the
  corresponding axes.
\begin{itemize}
    \item As before, the pair $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ \emph{cross to the
        right} or \emph{R-cross} if $a^-, b^-, a^+,b^+$ appear in this
      ccw order on the boundary at infinity. See
      Figure~\ref{fig:cross}.
    \item The pair $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ is \emph{R-parallel} if $b^-,b^+, a^+, a^-$ appear in this ccw order on the boundary at infinity. See Figure~\ref{fig:parallel}.
   \item  The pair $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ is \emph{R-anti-parallel} if $b^+,b^-, a^+, a^-$ appear in this counter-clockwise order on the boundary at infinity. See Figure~\ref{fig:anti}.
   \end{itemize}
   We can similarly define L-cross, L-parallel, and L-anti-parallel
   (reversing the orientation on the circle).
   We will also say a triple $(\ora{a},\ora{b},\ora{c})$ is R-parallel
   if $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ and $(\ora{b},\ora{c})$ are.
   \label{def:crossing_parallel}
\end{definition}

If we swap $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$, the type of crossing/parallelism
can change, as explained below.

\begin{lemma} Let $a,b\in \Isom^+(\bbH^2)$ be hyperbolic elements.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ R-cross, then $(\ora{A},\ora{B})$
    R-cross and $(\ora{b},\ora{a})$ L-cross.
  \item If $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ are R-parallel, then
    $(\ora{B},\ora{A})$ are R-parallel, $(\ora{b},\ora{a})$ are
    L-parallel, and $(\ora{a},\ora{B})$ are
    R-anti-parallel.
  \item If $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ are R-anti-parallel, then
    $(\ora{b},\ora{a})$ are R-anti-parallel, $(\ora{A},\ora{B})$ are
    L-anti-parallel,
    and $(\ora{a},\ora{B})$
    are R-parallel.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Immediate from the definitions (and this lemma can be taken to be a
  definition of the ``L'' variants).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
  Let $a,x \in \Isom^+(\bbH^2)$ be hyperbolic elements that do not share any endpoints.
  Then $\ora{a}$ and~$\ora{x}$ R-cross (resp.\ L-cross) iff
  $(\ora{x},a \cdot \ora{x})$ are R-parallel (resp.\ L-parallel).
 \label{lem:rhs}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The statement that $(\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{x})$ are R-parallel is (by
  definition) equivalent to $a\cdot(x^-,x^+) \subsetneq (x^-,x^+)$,
  which in turn is equivalent to $a^+ \in (x^-,x^+)$ and
  $a^- \notin (x^-,x^+)$, which is turn is equivalent to
  $(\ora{a},\ora{x})$ R-crossing.
\end{proof}

We also have the
following. 

\begin{lemma}
  Let $a,b,x \in \pi_1(S)$ be group elements so that $(\ora{a},\ora{x})$ and $(\ora{b},\ora{x})$ R-cross. Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item All four geodesics $\ora{xa}$, $\ora{Xa}$, $\ora{ax}$, and
    $\ora{aX}$ R-cross $\ora{x}$.
  \item $(\ora{ab},\ora{x})$ R-cross.
  \item More generally, for any $n,k \in \bbZ$, $(\ora{a x^n},\ora{x}$
    and $(\ora{ax^nbx^k},\ora{x})$ R-cross.
  \end{enumerate}
    \label{lem:rhs_prod}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  For the first claim, consider the action of the respective group
  element on the interval $(x^-,x^+)$: we have
  $x^{\pm1} \cdot (x^-,x^+) = (x^-,x^+)$ and
  $a \cdot (x^-,x^+) \subsetneq (x^-,x^+)$, so four of $ax^{\pm1}$ and
  $x^{\pm1}a$ map this interval strictly inside itself. It follows
  that all these elements are hyperbolic and R-cross $\ora{x}$.

  For the second claim, we similarly have that both $a$ and $b$ map
  $(x^-,x^+)$ strictly inside itself, so their product does as well.

  The last claim is an immediate consequence of the first two parts.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation between connected/disconnected smoothing and crossing/parallel conditions} \DMGtodo[inline]{I moved the statement to the introduction. The proof is still here, but should we moved elsewhere.}

We are now ready to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}]
Extend $f$ linearly to $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$-formal linear combinations of $\Gamma$, i.e., to weighted multi-curves.
\[
f(s \cdot a + t \cdot b) = s\cdot f(a) + t \cdot f(b)
\]
for every $s,t \geq 0$, and $a,b \in \Gamma$.

The smoothing condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect_primeprime} is a priori stronger than the hypotheses of this theorem.
It remains to show the hypotheses of this theorem imply smoothing.
Suppose $a,b$ have an essential crossing. 
Then, either $(a,b)$ are $R$-crossing, or $(a,b)$ are $L$-crossing. If $(a,b)$ is $R$-crossing, we have
\[
f(a)+f(b) = f(a + b) \geq f(ab).
\]
Also, $(B,a)$ is $R$-crossing, and thus
\[
f(a) + f(b) = f(B)+f(a) \geq f(Ba) = f(aB)
\]
On the other hand, if $(a,b)$ are $L$-crossing, then $(b,a)$ is $R$-crossing, hence
\[
f(b)+f(a) \geq f(ba) = f(ab).
\]
Also, if  $(a,b)$ are $L$-crossing, thus $(a,B)$ is $R$-crossing, hence
\[
f(a)+f(b) = f(b)+f(B) \geq f(aB).
\]
All in all, if $a,b$ cross essentially, $f$ satisfies $f(a) + f(b) \geq \max \{ f(ab) , f(aB) \}$, so $f$ satisfies disconnected smoothing.

For $ab$ with a self-intersection, this is equivalent to $(a,b)$ being $R$-parallel or $(a,b)$ being $L$-parallel.
For any pair of $R$-parallel elements $(a,b)$, 
\[
f(ab)\geq \max \{ f(a) + f(b), f(aB)\}
\]
If $(a,b)$ are $L$-parallel, then $(b,a)$ are $R$-parallel, thus
\[
f(ab)=f(ba)\geq \max \{ f(b) + f(a), f(bA)\} = \max \{ f(a) + f(b), f(aB)\} 
\]
Hence, connected smoothing is satisfied.
\end{proof}

We will make heavy use of the \emph{band model} centered on
$\ora{x}$, meaning the conformal map from $\bbH^2$ to a horizontal
strip $(-\infty,\infty) \times [-v,v]$ mapping $x^-$ to infinity along
the negative $x$-axis and $x^+$ to infinity on the positive $x$-axis.
We choose the \emph{normalized} band model, picking $v$ so that the
action of $x$ is horizontal translation by $z \mapsto z+1$.

For any geodesic $\ora{y}$ that is parallel to
$\ora{x}$, there is a \emph{window} $w(\ora{y})$ cut out by the
endpoints on the band model, the interval between $y^\pm$ that
misses~$x^\pm$. Observe that $w(x^k\cdot\ora{y})$ is the horizontal
translate by $k$ of $w(\ora{y})$. See Figure~\ref{fig:abcrossings} for
examples.

\begin{lemma}
  Given $a,b, x \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ so that $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ cross $\ora{x}$ to
  the right and $g_1 = x^k ax^\ell$ with $k,\ell \in \bbZ$, we have
  $g_1^+ \in w(x^k a\cdot\ora{x})$ and
  $g_1^- \in w(x^{-\ell}A\cdot\ora{x})$. Similarly for
  $g_2 = x^k a x^\ell b x^n$ with $k,\ell,n\in\bbZ$, we have
  $g_2^+ \in w(x^ka \cdot \ora{x})$ and $g_2^- \in w(x^{-n} B
  \cdot\ora{x})$.
\label{lem:windows}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For the first part, it suffices to show that $\ora{g_1}$ crosses
  $x^ka \cdot \ora{x}$ and $x^{-\ell A} \cdot \ora{x}$ to the right,
  or (by translation) that $\ora{x^{\ell+k}a}$ and $\ora{ax^{k+\ell}}$
  cross $\ora{x}$ to the right. This follows from
  Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod}.

  For the second part, we similarly use Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod} to
  see that $\ora{g_2}$ crosses $x^ka \cdot \ora{x}$ and $x^{-n}B \cdot
  \ora{x}$ to the right.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:join-split-geom}]
  In each case, we will show that we can choose lifts of the curves to
  the normalized band model for which a
  relevant crossing is essential. We start with Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-join}.
  Since each window is a
  finite size, there is a finite range $[s_1,s_2]$ so that $w(x^rb\cdot\ora{x})$
  is to the left of (resp.\ to the right of) $w(a\cdot\ora{x})$ if $r < s_1$
  (resp.\ $r > s_2$). Similarly there
  is a range $[s_1',s_2']$ so that $w(A\cdot\ora{x})$ is disjoint from
  $w(x^rB\cdot\ora{x})$ if $r \notin [s_1',s_2']$, and determining the
  ordering of the windows. Set
  $s \coloneqq \max\{s_2,s_2',-s_1,-s_1'\}$.

  Now consider
  $g_1 = ax^n$ and $g_2 = x^rbx^{m-r}$, with $s < r < m-n-s$. We have

    r-m < -n-s \le -n+s_1',
  \]
  so $g_1^- \in w(x^{r-m}B\cdot\ora{x})$ is to the left of $g_2^- \in
  w(x^{-n}A\cdot\ora{x})$, and
  \[
    s_2 \le s < r,
  \]
  so $g_1^+ \in w(x^rb\cdot\ora{x})$ is to the right of $g_2^+ \in
  w(a\cdot\ora{x})$ (using Lemma~\ref{lem:windows}). Thus
  $\ora{x^rbx^{m-r}}$ crosses $\ora{ax^n}$ to the left. Similarly if
  $m-n+s < r < -s$, then $\ora{x^rbx^{m-r}}$ crosses $\ora{ax^n}$ to the
  right. In either case,
  \[
    [ax^n][bx^m] = [ax^n][x^r bx^{m-r}] \reducesto [ax^{n+r}bx^{m-r}].
  \]

  $g=x^r ax^n$ and $h=bx^{m-r}$,

  of $w(x^{-n}A\cdot\ora{x})$, and hence $h$ and $g$ are $R$-parallel, giving Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-split}.

We also need some lemmas about smoothings where we have high powers of
two different elements. We work with three elements $a,x,y\in\pi_1(S)$
so that $(\ora{y},\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{y})$ are R-parallel; this
implies, for instance, that $(a\cdot\ora{y},a\cdot\ora{x})$ are also
R-parallel.
We again work in the normalized band model
centered on~$\ora{x}$, starting with another lemma about endpoints.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ayx-window}
  For any triple $a,x,y\in\pi_1(S)$ so that
  $(\ora{y},\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{y})$ are R-parallel, and for
  $\ora{g}=\ora{x^k ay^n x^\ell}$ with $k,\ell,n\in\bbZ$, we have
  $g^+ \in w(x^k a \cdot\ora{y})$ and
  $g^- \in w(x^{-\ell}\cdot\ora{y})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Consider $g$ acting on $x^{-\ell} \cdot (y^-,y^+)$, the \emph{complement}
  of $w(x^{-\ell} \cdot \ora{y})$. We have
  $x^k ay^n \cdot(y^-,y^+) = x^k a\cdot (y^-,y^+) = w(x^k a \cdot
  \ora{y}) \subset (x^-,x^+) \subset x^{-\ell} \cdot (y^-,y^+)$,
  showing the claims about~$g^\pm$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fundamental-ayx}
  Let $a,x,y\in\pi_1(S)$ be chosen so that
  $(\ora{y},\ora{x},a\cdot\ora{y})$ are R-parallel. Then there exists
  $s \ge 0$ so that for all $r > s$ and $m,n \in \bbZ$,
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:crossuniform_n}
       [a y^m x^{n+r}][a y^m x^{n-r}] &\reducesto [(a y^m x^n)^2]\\
    \label{eq:crossuniform_m}
       [a y^{m+r} x^n][a y^{m-r} x^n] &\reducesto [(a y^m x^n)^2].
  \end{align}
  Furthermore, there is $s' > 0$ so that, for $n,m \ge s'$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:cancel-ayx}
    [a y^m x^n][a] \reducesto [y^m][x^n].
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We first prove Eq.~(\ref{eq:crossuniform_n}). We will have a number
  of windows $w(x^m \cdot \ora{y})$ on the top, all of equal width
  in the normalized band model, and $w(x^m a \cdot \ora{y})$ on the
  bottom, also of equal widths (but not necessarily equal to widths on
  the top). Take
  \[
    s_0 \coloneqq \lceil\max(\wid(w(\ora{y})),
    \wid(w(a\cdot\ora{y})))\rceil.
  \]
  For $r > s_0$, consider the elements $g_1 = x^n ay^m x^r$ and $g_2 =
  x^{n-r} a y^m$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ayx-window}, we have
  \begin{align*}
    g_1^+ &\in w(x^n a\cdot\ora{y}) & g_1^- &\in w(x^{-r}\cdot\ora{y})\\
    g_2^+ &\in w(x^{n-r} a \cdot\ora{y}) & g_2^- &\in w(\ora{y}).
  \end{align*}
  Since $r>s_0$, the endpoints appear in the cyclic order
  $(x^-,g_2^+,g_1^+,x^+,g_2^-,g_1^-)$ on $\bdy^\infty S$. In
  particular $[g_1][g_2] \reducesto [g_1 g_2]$ as desired.
  
  To see Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossuniform_n}, apply
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossuniform_m} with the change of variables
  $a' = a$, $y' = x$, and $x' = ayA$.
  This gives the desired result for some $s_1 \ge 0$. For the full
  statement, take $s \coloneqq\max(s_0,s_1)$.

  For Eq.~\eqref{eq:cancel-ayx}, we consider $g = a y ^m x^n$ and
  compare its endpoints to those of $a$. By
  Lemma~\ref{lem:ayx-window}, we have
  $g^- \in w(x^{-n} \cdot \ora{y})$, which for large enough $n$ is to
  the left of $a^- \in w(\ora{y})$. \DPTtodo{Consider adding this
    ``deeper look'' stuff into the general window lemmas} For $g^+$,
  we again have to look deeper at the endpoints: since
  $g \cdot (x^+,x^-) = a y^m \dot (x^+, x^=) = w(a y^m \cdot
  \ora{x})$, we have
  $g^+ \in w(a y^m \cdot \ora{x}) \subset w(a \cdot \ora{y})$.
  Similarly $a^+ \in w(a \cdot \ora{x}) \subset w(a \cdot \ora{y})$.
  Consider the bands model centered on $a \cdot \ora{y}$ shows that,
  for $m$ large enough, $g^+$ is to the right of $a^+$, and thus
  $\ora{g}$ crosses $\ora{a}$ to the right. We therefore have
  \[
    [a x^n y^m][a] = [g][a] \reducesto [x^n y^m].
  \]
  Finally, by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}, since $\ora{x^n}$
  and $\ora{y^m}$ are R-parallel, the geodesics $\ora{y^m x^n}$ and
  $\ora{x^n y^m}$ cross so $[x^n y^m] \reducesto [x^n][y^m]$.

\end{proof}
