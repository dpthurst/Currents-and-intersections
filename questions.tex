\subsection{Further questions}
\label{sec:questions}

\subsubsection{Open surfaces}

A natural question is if our results can be extended to the setting of open surfaces (i.e., compact surfaces with boundary, or finite type surfaces with punctures).
  Recall that geodesic currents can also be defined more generally for finite type hyperbolic surfaces. Depending on if we consider ends as cusps or funnels, we get two different spaces, that we will call $\Curr_{\mathrm{cusp}}(S)$ and $\Curr_{\mathrm{open}}(S)$, respectively.
In the first case, we define the space of geodesic currents analogously to Definition~\ref{def:currents} for closed surfaces, i.e., as invariant measures supported on the space of geodesics of the universal cover, noting that now the space of geodesics contains arcs going from cusp to cusp. 
In the second case, we consider geodesic currents supported on geodesics projecting to the convex core of the surface.

In the case of $\Curr_{\mathrm{cusp}}(S)$, 
we gave an example in~\cite[Remark~2.26]{MGT21:Smoothings} showing that intersection number is not continuous on currents. See also the recent works by Sasaki~\cite{Sas22:Currents} and Trin~\cite{T22:ThurstonCompactification}.

Recall that $\AConvex(S)$ denotes the space of curve functionals satisfying symmetry, additivity, smoothing and stability.

We think there should be an analog of our result for $\Curr_{\mathrm{cusp}}(S)$,
but presumably one would have to add conditions with regards to smoothings between arcs and arcs, and arcs and curves.
More importantly, most of our arguments rely on the fact that there is a natural cyclic order at the boundary at infinity $\partial_{\infty}\Sigma$, which we lose when $\Sigma$ has punctures, since the fundamental group will be free. Moreover, in the cusped case, one could have geodesic currents supported on arcs going from cusp to cusp, and more than one such arc can end at the same puncture. This breaks some of the arguments about lack of atoms in the boundary used throughout the paper (see Remark~\ref{rmk:no_atom_boundary_box} or proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:char_liouville}).

The case of $\Curr_{\mathrm{open}}(S)$ is different. Even though intersection number is continuous in this case, it is not true that our defining properties of $\AConvex(S)$ guarantee a dual geodesic current.
The key issue is that the intersection number is degenerate in this case: any geodesic current $\mu$ supported on boundary curves will have zero intersection number with all geodesic currents $\nu \in \Curr(S)$.
We can give a concrete example on how our theorem fails in this setting, similar to the second example in~\cite[Remark~2.26]{MGT21:Smoothings}.

Let $\bar{\Sigma}$ denote the (compact) convex core of $\Sigma$.
Let $\ell$ be the restriction to $\Sigma$ of the hyperbolic
length on the double of $\bar{\Sigma}$.
We note that $\ell$ satisfies additivity, stability and homogeneity
properties. Also, it satisfies smoothing: for non-boundary-parallel curves, smoothing is satisfied by the same argument as the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:domination}. Since boundary parallel curves do not intersect each other or other non-boundary parallel curves, smoothing is vacuously satisfied.

However, $\ell(C) \neq 0$ for $C$ a boundary parallel curve, whereas for any geodesic current $\mu$, we have $i(\mu,C)=0$, so $\ell$ cannot be realized as intersection number with a geodesic current. For more details, see~\cite[Proposition~2.2]{T22:ThurstonCompactification}.

\subsubsection{Functionals valued on ordered abelian groups} \DMGtodo{added this question}

We expect most of the results in this paper to extend, with only minor modifications, to the setting of $\Lambda$-valued functionals and $\Lambda$-valued geodesic currents, where $(\Lambda, <)$ denotes an ordered abelian group—that is, an abelian group equipped with a total order satisfying $a < b$ and $c \le d$ imply $a + c < b + d$.
This generalization is natural in the context of the theory of $\Lambda$-trees~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction} and has already proved fruitful in the study of compactifications of character varieties of surface-group representations into higher-rank Lie groups~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}.
We plan to explore these extensions and their applications in future work.

\subsubsection{Relation to quasi-morphisms} 
\label{sub:quasimorphism} \DMGtodo{added this question (might remove it)}

The hypotheses of \emph{oriented quasi-smoothing} are reminiscent of, but weaker than, the conditions for a map 
$f \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ to be a \emph{quasi-morphism}, that is, there exists $D > 0$ such that 
\[
|f(ab) - f(a) - f(b)| < D \quad \text{for all } a,b \in \pi_1(S).
\]
A quasi-morphism is called \emph{homogeneous} if $f(a^n) = n f(a)$ for every $a \in \pi_1(S)$ and $n \ge 1$.

Quasi-morphisms have played a prominent role in the study of stable commutator length and bounded cohomology of groups~\cite{Cal09:scl,Pic97:BoundedCohomology_currents}.

It would be interesting to understand whether one can obtain homogeneous quasi-morphisms from curve functionals satisfying oriented quasi-smoothing and stability.

\begin{question}
Let $f \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfy oriented quasi-smoothing and stability.  
Are there additional conditions on $f$ ensuring that the function
\[
g(a) \coloneqq f(a) - f(a^{-1})
\]
is a homogeneous quasi-morphism?

\end{question} 

\subsubsection{Smoothing vs multi-smoothing}

The hypotheses of smoothing in Equation~\ref{eq:smoothing} of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} apply to one essential crossing at a time. There is a stronger condition than smoothing, where multiple smoothings at essential crossings are performed at once, and the function is required to be non-increasing under such operation.
We will refer to this condition as \emph{multi-smoothing}.
It is clear multi-smoothing implies smoothing.
It is easily checked that intersection numbers satisfy the multi-smoothing property (see, for example, \cite{AH21:EffectiveCounting3}).
What is not so clear is that functionals of $\AConvex(S)$ (that is, satisfying stability, homogeneity, smoothing and additivity) must satisfy multi-smoothing. This is, of course, a consequence Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.
Other curve functionals that are not in $\AConvex(S)$ also satisfy multi-smoothing. For example, the same proof as  \cite{AH21:EffectiveCounting3}) shows that extremal length satisfies multi-smoothing.
This prompts the following question.

\begin{question}
If a curve functional satisfies homogeneity, stability, convex union and smoothing, does it satisfy multi-smoothing?
\end{question}

In this context, in recent work, Arenas--Neumann-Coto \cite{ANC24:TautSmoothings} consider smoothing for closed multi-curves (as well as arcs), and prove, under certain hypotheses for multi-curves, that given a closed multi-curve in minimal position there is always an essential crossing such that once smoothed decreases intersection number by at most 1. They call such a smoothing a \emph{taut smoothing}.
Can this be used to give a more efficient criterion for checking the smoothing property of a curve functional? That is,

\begin{question} \DMGtodo{does this question make sense? I put it here to justify citing Arenas--Neumann-Coto, but in retrospect it feels a bit forced, and I'm not so sure this is an interesting question}
Let $f$ be a curve functional. For every multi-curve $C$, let $C'_{\mbox{taut}}$ be a multi-curve obtained by a taut smoothing of $C$.
If $f(C) \geq f(C'_{\mbox{taut}})$ for all multi-curves $C$, does it follow that $f$ satisfies smoothing?
\end{question}


\subsubsection{Convexity on train-track charts and local compactness}
\label{subsubsec:convexity_charts}

One of the reasons multi-smoothing is a useful property is that, coupled with convex union, as well as homogeneity and stability, it yields convex functions when restricted to train-track coordinates. Precisely, let $f$ be a curve functional satisfying the aforementioned properties, and let $\tau$ be a maximal train-track (i.e., a train-track all of whose complementary components are trigons), let $V(\tau)$ the set of admissible weights, $\ML(\tau)$ the subspace of measured laminations carried by $\tau$, and $\phi_{\tau} \colon V(\tau)  \to \ML(\tau)$ the induced homeomorphism chart on measured laminations.
Since $f$ extends continuously to geodesic currents by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}, we can consider the composition $f_{\tau} \coloneqq f \circ \phi_{\tau} \colon V(\tau) \to \mathbb{R}$. Under these assumptions, the same proof as in~~\cite[Appendix~A]{Mir04:SimpleGeodesics},\cite[Theorem~2]{Thu16:Research},~\cite[Theorem~4.7]{AH21:EffectiveCounting3}, shows $f_{\tau}$ is convex and homogeneous. 

These curve functionals form a subset of the functionals that extend continuously to currents, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Moreover, the bijection is a homeomorphism onto its image when the space of curve functionals is equipped with the subspace topology coming from the product topology on $\mathbb{R}^{\Curves(S)}$ (by~\cite[Theorem~11]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}).

Thus, a restatement of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} is as follows.

\begin{theorem}
   Let $\Psi \colon \Curr(S) \to \mathbb{R}^{\Curves(S)}$ be the homeomorphism onto its image $\mu \mapsto i(\mu, \cdot)$.
   The image is equal to the subset of additive convex functionals $\AConvex(S)$.
\end{theorem}

It is a consequence of this and the local compactness of the space of geodesic currents (see Proposition~\ref{prop:local_compactness} below), that the space $\AConvex(S)$ with the product topology is locally compact, which is not obvious a priori by looking directly at the functionals. Connecting with the discussion in the above paragraph, a shadow of this fact can be seen as follows.

The sublevel set $\Lambda_{f_{\tau}} \coloneqq \{ \vec{x} \in V(\tau) : f_{\tau}(\vec{x}) \leq 1 \}$ is a compact convex subset of $\mathbb{R}^N$ (for some $N>0$ depending on the combinatorics of $\tau$).
Let $f_i(\cdot) = i(\mu_i, \cdot)$ for some sequence of geodesic currents $\mu_i \to \mu$, and $f(\cdot) = i(\mu, \cdot)$.
Then $\Lambda_{(f_i)_{\tau}} \to \Lambda_{f_{\tau}}$ in the topology induced by the Hausdorff metric. With respect to this topology, the subset of compact convex subsets of $\mathbb{R}^N$ is locally compact.
The same convexity argument would work if we considered $\Convex(S)$ as opposed to $\AConvex(S)$ (that is, we only required convex union).
Hence, we conjecture the following.

\begin{conjecture}
The set $\Convex(S)$ with the product topology is locally compact.
\end{conjecture}

The main examples of interest for us in $\Convex(S)$ would be extremal lengths associated to conformal structures on $S$ or to elastic graphs (see~\cite[Examples]{MGT21:Smoothings}).

\subsubsection{Domination}
\label{subsec:domination}
Another property that intersection numbers satisfy is domination. 
We say that a curve $C$ \emph{dominates} another curve $C'$ if $i(C,D) \geq i(C',D)$ for every curve $D$. We say $f$ satisfies \emph{domination} if for every pair of curves $(C,C')$ with $C$ dominating $C'$, we have $f(C) \geq f(C')$.
It is easy to see that if $C'$ is obtained from $C$ by multi-smoothing, then $C$ dominates $C'$ - there are however other pairs $(C,C')$ with $C$ dominating $C'$ but $C'$ not obtainable from $C$ via multi-smoothing (see~\cite[Example]{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}).
It is clear, by definition, that intersection numbers satisfy domination.

All in all, $f$ satisfying domination implies $f$ satisfying multi-smoothing which in turn implies $f$ satisfying smoothing.

From Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} we obtain the following weaker theorem (which, nevertheless, seems interesting from a conceptual viewpoint).

\begin{theorem}
A curve functional satisfies domination and additivity if and only if it is dual to a geodesic current.
\end{theorem}

This can be interpreted as saying that linear maps that preserve the poset structure of the space of curves correspond to intersection numbers.

For similar reasons as for the smoothing (or multi-smoothing) condition, the additivity hypothesis is necessary, since extremal length can be easily seen to satisfy domination, homogeneity and stability, but not additivity, and it is not dual to an intersection number.

The above prompts the following question.

\begin{question}
Suppose a curve functional $f$ satisfies homogeneity, stability and convex union. Does it follow $f$ satisfies domination if and only if it satisfies smoothing?
\end{question}


\subsubsection{Green metrics}
\label{subsec:green}
Let $\Gamma=\pi_1(\Sigma) \subset \Isom^+(\mathbb{H})=\PSL_2(\mathbb{R})$ be a countable, discrete cocompact subgroup, for some hyperbolic metric $\Sigma$ on $S$.
Let $\mu$ be a finitely supported probability measure on $\Gamma$ whose support contains a symmetric generating set of $\Gamma$.
Let $w_n \coloneqq g_1 \cdots g_n$ be the random walk with respect to $\mu$, where each $(g_i)$ is identically and independently distributed with distribution $\mu$. Let $(\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}, \mu^{\mathbb{N}})$ be the step space, and the map $\pi \colon \Gamma^{\mathbb{N}} \to \Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}$ as $\pi((g_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}) \coloneqq (w_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$. The image of $\pi$ is equipped with the measure $(\mathbb{P}_{\mu})_*(\mu^{\mathbb{N}})$.
Define the metric \[ d_{\mu}(x,y) \coloneqq - \log \mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\exists n : w_n x =y).\]
In words, we are considering the probability that a random walk departing from $x$ will reach $y$ after some number of steps.
This metric has an associated stable length function $\ell_{\mu}(g) \coloneqq \lim_n d_{\mu}(x, g^n x)/n$, for any hyperbolic element $g \in \Gamma$, which can then be thought as a curve functional $\ell_{\mu} \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$. The following question emerged from conversations of the first author with Eduardo Reyes.

\begin{question}
Does $\ell_{\mu}$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} for some admissible probability measure $\mu$ (and thus come from a geodesic current)?
\label{q:green-smoothing}
\end{question}

\begin{question}
Does $\ell_\mu$ further satisfy the
hypotheses in Theorem~\ref{thm:char_liouville} for some admissible probability measure $\mu$ (and thus come
from a hyperbolic metric)?
\label{q:Green}
\end{question}

If answers to either of these questions is negative for every $\mu$,
they would imply a negative answer to the following well-known question.

\begin{question}
Given $\Sigma$, is there a finitely-supported probability measure
$\mu$ on $\Gamma=\pi_1(\Sigma)$ so that the induced translation length
$\ell_{\mu}$ is dual to the Liouville current $\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$?
\label{q:singularity}
\end{question}

By work of Cantrell-Tanaka~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{CT25} and \cite[Theorem~1.2]{GMM18:Entropy}, a negative answer to this question would settle the so-called \emph{singularity conjecture} for $\SL_2(\mathbb{R})$ (see~\cite[Conjecture, page~~259]{KLP11:Singularity}). In short, by these results Question~\ref{q:singularity} is equivalent to asking if there is a probability measure $\mu$ on $\Gamma$ so that the induced `hitting measure' $\nu_{\mu}$ on $\partial \Gamma$ is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure (coming from the hyperbolic metric $\Sigma$) on $\partial \Gamma$.
Kosenko-Tiozzo~\cite{KT22:Inequality} have studied the metric $d_{\mu}$ in some cases of Fuchsian groups $\Gamma$ with centrally symmetric fundamental domains.
Even a positive answer to Question~\ref{q:Green} would be interesting, since then
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} would give a dual geodesic current for the
Green metric, and then one could study what types of geodesic currents
can arise. Note that, for punctured surfaces, some admissible metrics
coming from random walks can be realized as geodesic currents. For
example, consider a simple generating set $F$ (in the sense of
Erlandsson~\cite{Erlandsson:WordLength}, see also
Section~\ref{subsec:embedded_graphs}) on a punctured surface $S'$.
By Erlandsson's result, the word-length with respect to $F$ is dual to a geodesic
current $m_F$ which is a multi-curve plus a finite collection of weighted arcs going from cusp to cusp. If we take the uniform probability
measure on $F$, the stable length of the resulting Green metric is a multiple of the standard word-metric on $F \cong \pi_1(S')$ (by~\cite{GMM18:Entropy}), and hence by~\cite{Erlandsson:WordLength}, is a multiple of the intersection number with $m_F$, and consequently, a geodesic current. The situation
is different if $S$ is closed (the setting on which our Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} applies): again, by the results in~\cite{GMM18:Entropy}, the stable length of the Green metric induced by the uniform probability measure on the standard generating set cannot be a multiple of the word-metric in the case of a
closed surface.

\subsubsection{Questions about generalizations to hyperbolic groups}
In recent work of Cantrell and Reyes and, independently, Kapovich and the first author, the following property has played a key role in extending stable lengths coming from group actions of hyperbolic groups $\Gamma$ acting on uniformly quasi-geodesic hyperbolic spaces $X$~\cite{KMG:BBP,CR25:Manhattan}.
Roughly, a Gromov hyperbolic space is a metric space whose triangles are thin. A group $\Gamma$ is said to be Gromov hyperbolic if its Cayley graph is.
A metric space $X$ is uniformy quasi-geodesic if there exists constants $\lambda_1,\lambda_2 >0$ and $\epsilon \geq 0$, so that every two points $x, y \in X$ are connected by a $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\epsilon)$-quasi-geodesic.
We say that $\Gamma \acts X$ of a non-elementary hyperbolic group on a uniformly quasi-geodesic hyperbolic metric space $X$ has the \emph{bounded backtracking property} if given an orbit map $f \colon \Cay(\Gamma) \to X$, there exist a constant $C>0$ so that for every two points $x,y \in \Cay(\Gamma)$, and geodesic $[x,y]$, there exists some quasi-geodesic $[f(x),f(y)]$ in $X$ so that $f([x,y]) \subset N_C([f(x),f(y)])$, where $N_C([f(x),f(y)])$ denotes the $C$-neighborhood of $[f(x),f(y)])$ with respect to the Hausdorff metric.

We know that actions of $\pi_1(S)$ with bounded backtracking on hyperbolic spaces induce stable lengths satisfying quasi-smoothing (see Section~\ref{subsec:examples_and_non}). One can ask if all such actions arise in this way.

\begin{question}
Let $\Gamma$ be a surface group, and let $f \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{R}$ be a symmetric and stable functional satisfying quasi-smoothing.
Does there exists a uniformly quasi-geodesic Gromov hyperbolic space $X$ on which $\Gamma$ acts with the bounded backtracking property and so that $\ell_X = f$, where $\ell_X$ denotes the stable length of the action $\Gamma \acts X$?
\end{question}

Motivated by this, it would be interesting to generalize the quasi-smoothing and smoothing condition to the general setting of hyperbolic groups.

\begin{question}
What is a generalization of the properties of smoothing and quasi-smoothing for functionals on non-elementary hyperbolic groups that makes the answer to the above two questions work in that setting?
\end{question}

\subsection{Analogy to Poincar\'e duality}
\label{subsec:poincare_duality}
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} gives a new definition of geodesic currents in terms of functionals on curves, by giving a bijection between geodesic currents and a subset of the space of curve functionals.
As mentioned in the introduction, one can view this theorem in analogy to Poincaré duality for closed surfaces.  Given a 1-cohomology class $\alpha^*$ of an $n$-dimensional manifold (represented by homomorphisms from $\pi_1(S)$ to $\mathbb{R}$), Poincaré duality associates an $(n-1)$-dimensional homology class $\alpha$ (represented by real linear combinations of $(n-1)$-cycles). Since $n=2$, these cycles can be expressed as linear combinations of simple closed curves.  

In our setting, the role of a 1-cohomology class is played by a curve
functional that satisfies smoothing, additivity, and
stability. Although it is not a homomorphism from
$\pi_1(S)$ to anything, it preserves a certain combinatorial structure
(see Section~\ref{subsec:domination}). Likewise, the role of the homology
class is taken by a geodesic current. This perspective, viewing
geodesic currents as homology classes, aligns with the approach in
geometric measure theory, where differential currents—seen as
functionals on $k$-forms—naturally define
homology~\cite[Chapter~10]{CC00:Foliations}. Geodesic currents are
examples of such differential forms~\cite[1.8.7]{MG20:Thesis}.

Our theorem can be thought of in analogy to $\alpha \cap \beta =
  \alpha^*(\beta)$, where $\cap$ denotes the cap product in homology, and thus relating cap product to the homology-cohomology pairing.

  This aligns with our theorem, which establishes a duality via the intersection number $i(\mu_f, C) = f(C)$, suggesting that the geometric intersection number plays the role of the cap product. 

  
In Poincaré duality, one also has the standard relation $\alpha^* \cup \beta^* = \alpha \cap \beta$, where $\cup$ denotes the cup product in cohomology.  
A natural question that arises then is the following.

\begin{question} What is the appropriate analog of the cup product? In other words, how can one define the intersection of two geodesic currents via their dual curve functionals?
\end{question}
