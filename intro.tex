\section{Introduction}

Geodesic currents on a hyperbolic surface are measures realizing a suitable closure of the space of weighted closed geodesics. Many metric
structures embed in the space of currents, such as
hyperbolic metrics \cite[Theorem~12]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent},
 negatively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative} or, more generally, non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem}.



Geometric intersection number of multi-curves extends continuously to a bilinear function of currents by a result of Bonahon~\cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}. Many length of curves or \emph{curve functionals} $f \colon\Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ are intersection numbers with a dual geodesic current $\mu_{f}$, this is,
\[
  i(\mu_{f},C)=f(C)
\]
for any $C$ in the space of multi-curves $\mathcal{C}(S)$ (i.e. finite unions of non-negatively non-necessarily-simple closed curves on $S$). In this case, we say that the current $\mu_{f}$ is \emph{dual} to the curve functional $f$, or that $f$ \emph{has a dual current} $\mu_{f}$. 
For example, the hyperbolic length of curves $\ell_{\Sigma}$ is dual to
a distinguished geodesic current~\cite[Theorem~10]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}), the \emph{Liouville current} $\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$; similarly for the negatively curved and non-positively curved Riemannian metrics mentioned above. Other examples do not come from metrics on the surface. For example, word-length with respect to simple generating sets have a dual current \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}; or length functions arising naturally from certain representations of surface groups into higher rank Lie groups have corresponding dual currents (see~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} and, more recently~\cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}).

In this paper we give an axiomatic characterization of curve functionals that are dual to geodesic currents. 

\begin{mainthm}
    Suppose that a curve functional $f \colon\mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies the \emph{smoothing property}
     \[
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) \ge \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right), f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)\right\},
   \]
   for every essential crossing of a multi-curve on the surface and, moreover, $f$ is \emph{additive} on connected components, \emph{stable} and \emph{homogeneous} (see~\ref{subsec:mainhypotheses} for definitions).
Then, $f$ has a (unique) dual geodesic current $\mu_f$, i.e., for every curve $C$, we have
\[
i(\mu_f,C)=f(C).
\]
\label{thm:intersect}
\end{mainthm}
See~Definition~\ref{def:essential-crossing} for essential crossing, which can be roughly defined as a crossing that cannot be removed by homotopy.
Even though the result is stated for unoriented curves, we will be working in the more general setting oriented curve functionals (see Appendix~\ref{subsec:examples_and_non} for many natural examples and non-examples).

One can view this theorem in analogy to Poincaré duality for closed surfaces, where certain curve functionals satisfying smoothing correspond to cohomology classes and geodesic currents to homology classes. This duality is mediated via the geometric intersection number, suggesting it plays the role of the cap product in this analogy. See Section~\ref{subsec:newdef}.



As a consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} we obtain a 
many new dual geodesic currents, generalizing many previous constructions for Riemannian metrics of negative curvature on $S$, and certain locally $\CAT(-1)$ and $\CAT(0)$ metrics on $S$ (see among others~\cite{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative, CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem, DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics, HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone, Con18:MarkedNonpos}). In Appendix~\ref{subsec:examples_and_non} we discuss these examples and many more in detail.
In fact, we obtain dual geodesic currents for any general Riemannian metric on $S$ or, even more generally, any length w.r.t any length metric structure on $S$ (in particular, for any Finsler metric).
    Our result also obtains dual geodesic currents for stable lengths associated to embedded graphs and generalizes work of Erlandsson~\cite{Erlandsson:WordLength} (see Corollary~\ref{cor:generalization_wordlength}).

All the examples above extend continuously to geodesic currents, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings} or simply  Bonahon's theorem of continuous bilinear extension of geometric intersection number. 
This strengthens a previous result in~\cite[Theorem~1.5]{EPS:CountingCurves} given for arbitrary Riemannian lengths as well as for a much more general class of metrics: by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} we obtain a continuous extension \emph{as an intersection number}, 

Our result also obtains dual geodesic currents for stable lengths associated to embedded graphs and generalizes work of Erlandsson~\cite{Erlandsson:WordLength} (see Corollary~\ref{cor:generalization_wordlength}).
    
    As a further application of this fact, we show in Section~\ref{subsec:ineqintersection} that domination of intersection numbers with all closed curves implies domination of length spectra for any metric length on a closed surface. This, in particular, answers a question of Neumann-Coto~\cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.

    The smoothing condition as stated is easier to check for curve functionals arising from length or geodesic structures on the surface.
    For curve functionals arising from algebraic settings, such as the stable length of group actions on real trees (Section~\ref{sec:skora}), or cross-ratios (Section~\ref{sec:crossratios}), it is more convenient to decompose the smoothing condition into cases.

  For $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$,
  $\ora{a}, \ora{b}$ be their corresponding hyperbolic axes (for some hyperbolic structure fixed once and for all), and let $a^-, a^+$ and $b^-,b^+$ their respective repelling and attractive fixed points. We say the pair $(a,b)$  \emph{R-cross} if $a^-, b^-, a^+,b^+$ appear in this
      ccw order on the boundary at infinity. See
      Figure~\ref{fig:cross}: we say the pair $(a,b)$ is \emph{R-parallel} if $b^-,b^+, a^+, a^-$ appear in this ccw order on the boundary at infinity. See Figure~\ref{fig:parallel}. See Definition~\ref{def:righthandedcrossing} for more details.
      From this viewpoint it is more natural to consider curve functionals on oriented curves. This is a more general approach that we shall take in the rest of the paper.
      Using this we can state an equivalent version of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} can be phrased as follows.

      
\begin{mainthmprime}
Let $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$.
Let $f \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item class invariant $f(a)=f(b)$ whenever $a$ is conjugate to $b$.
\item stable: $f(a^n) = nf(a)$.
\item oriented smoothing:
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:conn_oriented} f(ab) &\geq  f(a) + f(b) & \mbox{ if } (a,b)\mbox{ are } \mbox{R-parallel} \\
    \label{eq:disconn_oriented} f(a) + f(b) &\geq f(ab)  & \mbox{ if }  (a,b)\mbox{ are } \mbox{R-crossing}.
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
Let $\overline{f} \coloneqq \frac{1}{2} \left(f(a) + f(A) \right)$.
Then, there exists a (unique) geodesic current $\mu_{\overline{f}}$ on $S$ so that
\[
i(\mu_{\overline{f}}, [g]) = \overline{f}(g)
\]
for every $g \in \Gamma$.
Moreover, any function of the type $i(\mu, \cdot)$, for a geodesic current $\mu$, satisfies the above assumptions.
\label{thm:intersections_group}
\end{mainthmprime}

See subsection~\ref{subsec:algebraic_vs_top} for a proof of the equivalence of the smoothing condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} to equations~\ref{eq:conn_oriented} \emph{and} \ref{eq:disconn_oriented} in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}.
These hypotheses might seem a priori weaker than smoothing. However, with the rest of the assumptions, oriented smoothing of $f$ implies smoothing of $\overline{f}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:oriented_smoothing_unoriented}).

Furthermore, neither one of conditions~\ref{eq:conn_oriented} and ~\ref{eq:disconn_oriented} imply each other: see Subsection~\ref{subsec:mainhypotheses}, Remark~\ref{rmk:reducible_action} or Example~\ref{ex:linEL}.

Our result can also be seen as a generalization of Feng
Luo's criterion~\cite[Theorem~1.1,Theorem~1.2]{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers}, where he characterizes curve
functionals on simple closed curves which can be realized as
intersection numbers with measured laminations, i.e., geodesic currents $\alpha$ for which $i(\alpha,\alpha)=0$ (see \cite[Proposition~17]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}). 
 
 In a previous paper, \cite[Proposition 4.11]{MGT21:Smoothings}, we have proven that the stable length associated to a quasi-fuchsian representation satisfies \emph{quasi-smoothing}, i.e., smoothing up to uniform additive error (see Definition \ref{def:properties}). Recently, Brody-Reyes~\cite[Proposition~1.8]{BR24:Cubulations}, relying essentially on work of Fricker-Furman \cite[Theorem A]{FF21:QF}, show that this additive error in the quasi-smoothing cannot be removed unless the representation is fuchsian. 
In the case the representation is fuchsian, by \cite[Proposition 14]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}, it is dual to a geodesic current, and hence smoothing is satisfied.
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} generalizes all of these results by showing that the smoothing property (plus additivity, stability/homogeneity) is \emph{sufficient and necessary} condition for \emph{any} curve functional to be dual to a geodesic current. 

\subsection{Characterize measured laminations and hyperbolic metrics}

As a consequence of the Theorem, we can further characterize which curve functionals correspond to measured laminations or to hyperbolic metrics (see Theorems~\ref{thm:char_laminations} and~\ref{prop:char_liouville}).

\begin{mainthm}
Let $f$ be a curve functional satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}. Then,
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mu \in \Curr(X)$ is a measured lamination if and only if $f$ satisfies the so-called \emph{disconnected max-smoothing property}:
\begin{equation*}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-10}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-11}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-12}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation*}
    \item $\mu \in \Curr(X)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ satisfies the following condition:\[
        \lambda \left(\mfig{curves-10}\right) = \lambda \left(\mfig{curves-11}\right) + \lambda \left(\mfig{curves-12}\right),
  \]
  where $\lambda( \cdot ) \coloneqq 2\cosh(f(\cdot)/2)$.
\end{itemize}
    The left-hand-side of both equations depicts an essential crossing between any pair of curves $C_1$ and $C_2$, and the right-hand-side shows the two possible resolutions.
    \label{mainthm:apps}
\end{mainthm} 

The result on hyperbolic lengths can be strengthened (see Theorem~\ref{thm:char_liouville_strong}) by replacing the equalities in the hypotheses by certain coarser inequalities.

We emphasize that this result characterizes hyperbolic metrics among all curve functionals. In particular, it can be applied to stable lengths induced from Green metrics arising from admissible random walks on $\pi_1(X)$, yielding potential applications to investigate the singularity of hitting boundary measures (see Subsection~\ref{subsec:green}).

These results generalize both of Luo's results on characterizing which functions on simple curves dual to measured laminations~\cite[Theorem~1.1,Theorem~1.2]{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers} (alluded above) and which to hyperbolic length functions~\cite{Luo98:GeodesicLength}.  We outline the relation between our conditions and Feng Luo's conditions in the Appendix, Proposition~\ref{prop:feng}.

 Our methods also recover the max-smoothing criterion in previous work of the second author~\cite{Thu00:GeometricIntersection}.

\subsection{Characterizing small actions on real trees}

As an additional application, we reprove a result of Skora, giving a characterization of actions of surface groups on $\mathbb{R}$-trees that are dual to measured laminations.
 If $\Gamma$ is a finitely generated group acting on $T$ by isometries, an isometry $g$ of $T$ either has no fixed points and an invariant axis in $T_g \subset T$ (\emph{hyperbolic}), or it has fixed points (\emph{elliptic})--see,~\cite[1.3]{CM87:GroupActions}. If $\Gamma=\pi_1(\Sigma)$ for a closed orientable hyperbolic surface $\Sigma$, we say the action \emph{preserves axes intersection} if every pair of isometries of the surface $g, h \in \Gamma$ with transversely intersecting axes in $\wt{\Sigma}$, and acting hyperbolically on $T$, have intersecting axes in the tree.
An example of such an action is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T_{\lambda}$ \emph{dual to a measured lamination} $\lambda$, which, roughly speaking, is obtained by taking the Hausdorff quotient of $\wt{\Sigma}$ with the pseudo-metric $d_{\lambda}(x,y)$ obtained by considering the measure of all geodesic transverse to the hyperbolic segment between the points $x, y \in \wt{\Sigma}$.
We give a new proof of the following result, that was originally proven by Skora in~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}.

\begin{mainthm}
A minimal action of $\pi_1(S)$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axes intersection.
\label{mainthm:skora}
\end{mainthm}

Culler-Morgan~\cite{CM87:GroupActions} give axioms for translation lengths $\Gamma$-acted $\mathbb{R}$-trees which, by a result of Walter Parry~\cite{Par91:Translation}, characterize conjugacy class invariant functions $f \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{R}$ (where $\Gamma$ is a finitely generated group) arising as translation lengths of $\Gamma$-invariant $\mathbb{R}$-trees.

From this perspective, Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} pushes further Parry's result when $\Gamma=\pi_1(S)$ by giving a set of axioms fully characterizing which  of those translation lengths correspond to \emph{small} $\Gamma$-invariant $\mathbb{R}$-trees (i.e. actions with cyclic edge stabilizers), or, equivalently, are dual to a measured lamination (i.e., a geodesic current satifying $i(\alpha, \alpha)=0$).

Precisely, we prove the following.

\begin{mainthm}
Let $\pi_1(S)$ act on a real tree $T$ minimally, and let $\ell \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ be its associated translation length on the tree.
The action $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ is irreducible and satisfies axis intersection if and only if $\ell$ satisfies the following conditions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For any pair of $R$-crossing elements $(a,b)$, 
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:small_disconn} \ell(a) + \ell(b) & = \max \{ \ell(ab), \ell(aB) \}.
  \end{align}
\item For any pair of $R$-parallel elements $(a,b)$, 
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:small_conn}  \quad \ell(ab) &= \max \{ \ell(a) + \ell(b), \ell(aB)\}.
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\label{thm:skora_maxsmoothing}
\end{mainthm}

It would be interesting to give similar conditions for 
small actions of a non-elementary hyperbolic group $\Gamma$. 

\subsection{Generalized cross-ratios}

Finally, we subsume previous work of Martone-Zhang and Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios} on associating a geodesic current to every generalized cross-ratio. Every generalized cross-ratio induces a curve functional, its \emph{period}, giving a function
\[
\pi \colon \operatorname{CR}(S) \to \CurveFunct(S).
\]
On the other hand, \cite{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios} associate, to every cross-ratio, a geodesic current,
\[
c \colon \operatorname{CR}(S) \to \GC(S).
\]
We show that period curve functionals (i.e., the image of the map $\pi$) satisfy the conditions of our main Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and thus induce geodesic currents.

\begin{mainthm}
There is a one-to-one correspondence between periods of generalized cross-ratios and geodesic currents.
\end{mainthm}
