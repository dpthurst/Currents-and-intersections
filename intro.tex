Geodesic currents on a hyperbolic surface are measures realizing a suitable closure of the space of weighted closed geodesics on the surface.
 Perhaps the most interesting trait of geodesic currents is that many metric
structures embed in the space of currents, such as
hyperbolic metrics \cite[Theorem~12]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent} or even
 negatively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}. 
The injectivity of the map from the space of metrics to the space of geodesic currents is known as the marked length rigidity property.
Other examples of geometric structures embedding inside the space of geodesic currents are given by
Croke-Fathi-Feldman for non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem},
Hersonsky-Paulin \cite[Theorem~A]{HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone} for
negatively curved metrics with conical singularities,
Duchin-Leininger-Rafi more explictly, as a subcase of the last one, give an embedding for singular Euclidean structures
associated to quadratic differentials/half-translation structures
\cite[Theorem~4]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics} or Bankovic-Leininger non-positively curved Euclidean cone metrics \cite[Main Theorem]{BL17:RigidityFlat}. The latest work in this direction is \cite{ELS21:Hyperbolic}.

The way the embedding works in each of the above cases is associating
a canonical geodesic current $\mathcal{L}_{\rho}$ to a given metric
structure $\rho$. This metric structure is usually defined up to a notion of homotopy
equivalence---as happens, for example, for Teichm\"uller space (see
Theorem~\cite[Theorem~10]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}).  Then one shows
that this assignment is in fact an injection. This canonical geodesic
current $\mathcal{L}_{\rho}$ was coined \emph{Liouville current} by
Bonahon \cite[Section~2]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent} for the case of
hyperbolic structures, but the term has also been used more generally
for the geodesic currents associated to other metric structures (see,
for instance, \cite[Section~3.1]{EU18:LengthCurrents}).
These Liouville currents are tied to the geometric structure as follows.
By Theorem~\cite[Proposition~4.4]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}, weighted multi-curves on a surface embed in the space of geodesic currents.
Also, in the same paper Bonahon
\cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds} that extends
the notion of geometric intersection
number as a continuous bilinear function the space of pairs of geodesic currents.
 This allowed him to extend hyperbolic length to geodesic currents by
following the principle of realizing it as an intersection number with
a distinguished geodesic current.
Similarly, other notions of length of curves $\ell_{\rho}$ associated to the above mentioned metric structures $\rho$ can be realized as intersection numbers with the corresponding Liouville current $\mathcal{L}_{\rho}$, this is
\[
  i(\mathcal{L}_{\rho},C)=\ell_{\rho}(C)
\]
for any closed multi-curve $C$ in $S$. We will say that the current $\mu_{\rho}$ is \emph{dual} to $\ell_{\rho}$.

There are many other lengths with an associated dual geodesic current.
For example, Erlandsson shows that word-length with respect to simple generating sets of the fundamental group can also be realized as an intersection number with such a Liouville current \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}.

In fact, the usual first step in the solution to a marked length rigidity problems for a metric structure $\rho$ is to find a geodesic current $\mu_{\rho}$ satisfying the above intersection  property
In this paper, we give an answer to the following question.

\begin{question}\label{q:intersection}
Let $\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S)$ denote the space of weighted multi-curves on $S$.
What are sufficient and necessary conditions for $f \colon\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ to have a dual geodesic current?
\label{que:mainquestion}
\end{question}

\subsection{Main results}

The main result of this paper can be succintly phrased as follows.

\begin{mainthm}
    Suppose that $f \colon\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies the \emph{smoothing property}
     \[
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) \ge \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right), f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)\right\},
   \]
   for every essential crossing of a multi-curve on the surface and, moreover, $f$ is \emph{additive} on connected components, \emph{stable} and \emph{homogeneous} (see~\ref{subsec:mainresult} for definitions).
Then, $f$ has a dual geodesic current, i.e., there exists $\mu_f$ so that
\[
i(\mu_f,C)=f(C)
\]
for every multi-curve $C$.
\label{mainthm:intersections}
\end{mainthm}

Feng Luo considered the version of this question for measured laminations, which can be seen as geodesic currents $\alpha$ for which $i(\alpha,\alpha)=0$ (see \cite[Proposition~17]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}). Luo studies functions defined on simple closed curves \cite[Theorem~1.1,Theorem~1.2]{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers}. We outline the relation between our conditions and Feng Luo's conditions in Proposition~\ref{prop:feng}.
See also \cite[Appendix~A.1]{Frazier12:SpectralRigidity}, for an observation on how Question~\ref{que:mainquestion} ties to rigidity problems.

As a corollary, we can further characterize which curve functionals correspond to measured laminations or to hyperbolic Liouville currents.

\begin{mainthm}
Let $f$ be a curve functional satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{mainthm:intersections}. Then,
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mu \in \Curr(X)$ is a measured lamination if and only if $f$ satisfies the so-called \emph{disconnected max-smoothing property}:
\begin{equation*}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation*}
    \item $\mu \in \Curr(X)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ satisfies the so-called \emph{disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing property}:
    \begin{equation}
      \lambda\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \lambda\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)+\lambda\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right).
    \end{equation}
    where $\lambda(C) \coloneqq 2\cosh\left(\frac{f(C)}{2}\right)$.
\end{itemize}
    The left-hand-side of both equations depicts an essential crossing between any pair of multi-curves $C_1$ and $C_2$, and the right-hand-side shows the two possible resolutions.
    \label{mainthm:apps}
\end{mainthm}

As an additional application, we reprove a result of Skora, giving a characterization of actions of surface groups on $\mathbb{R}$-trees that are dual to measured laminations.
 Let $G$ be a surface group, i.e. $G=\pi_1(X)$ for some closed hyperbolic surface $X$, and $T$ an $\mathbb{R}$-tree. Suppose $G$ acts on $T$ by isometries. It turns out that an isometry $g$ of $T$ either has no fixed points and an invariant axis in $T_g \subset T$ (hyperbolic), or it has fixed points (elliptic).
The action \emph{preserves axes intersection} if every pair of isometries of the surface $g, h \in G$ with transversely intersecting axes in $\wt{X}$, and acting hyperbolically on $T$, have intersecting axes in the tree.
An example of such an action is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T_{\lambda}$ \emph{dual to a measured lamination} $\lambda$, which, roughly speaking, is obtained by taking the Hausdorff quotient of $\wt{X}$ with the pseudo-metric $d_{\lambda}(x,y)$ obtained by considering the measure of all geodesic transverse to the hyperbolic segment between the points $x, y \in \wt{X}$.
We give a new proof of the following result, that was originally proven by Skora in~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}.

\begin{mainthm}
A minimal action of $\pi_1(S)$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axes intersection.
\label{mainthm:skora}
\end{mainthm}

\subsection{General context}
\label{subsec:mainresult}

In this section we discuss how our result fits into our previous work in~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings}.

In this paper, the geometric intersection number between curves will play a prominent role. In general, when one considers geometric intersection number $i(C, D)$ between two curves $C$ and $D$, it is assumed that $C,D$ are unoriented, or else assigns the same value to $i(C,D)$ and $i(C,D^{-1})$. 
Working with this intersection number is crucial for our results, although we will assume our curves to be oriented. In the sequel, whenever we say curves, we mean oriented curves, unless stated explicitly otherwise.
In terms of geodesic currents, this means we will work with oriented geodesic currents.
As we shall see in Section~\ref{sec:asymmetric} there are versions of
intersection numbers which are asymmetric (i.e. they distinguish the
orientations of the intersections). For those, the analog of the main duality
result, Theorem~\ref{mainthm:intersections}, will not hold.
Thus, even though our curve functionals will be defined on oriented curves, to state our results it will be necessary to assume our curve functionals are \emph{symmetric}, i.e., $f(C)=f(C^{-1})$.


\begin{definition}\label{def:properties}
  For $S$ a compact topological surface without boundary,
  let
  $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \bbR$ be a
  function defined on the space of oriented multi-curves,
  not-necessarily-simple oriented curves.
  We will also refer to $f$ as a \emph{curve functional} for short.
  (\emph{Functional} means that it takes values in scalars; it is not
  assumed to be linear.)
  We will also refer to \emph{unoriented} or \emph{weighted} curve
  functionals for real-valued functions defined on the appropriate
  type of multi-curves.
  We define several
  properties that $f$ might satisfy.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quasi-smoothing:} There is a constant $R\ge 0$ with the
      following property. Let $C$~be an oriented curve on~$S$, and
    let $x$~be an essential crossing of~$C$. Let $C'$ be the oriented
    smoothing of~$C$ at~$x$. Then $f(C) \ge f(C')-R$. Schematically, we
    have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:quasismoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right) \ge f\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right) -R
    \end{equation}
    See Subsection~\ref{subsec:essential} for ``essential
    crossing''. Loosely, it is a crossing that cannot be removed by homotopy. 
  \item \textbf{Smoothing:} We take $R=0$ in the above definition of quasi-smoothing:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right) \ge f\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right)
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Convex union:} Let $C_1$ and~$C_2$ be two oriented curves
    on~$S$. Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:union-convex}
      f(C_1 \cup C_2) \le f(C_1) + f(C_2).
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Additive union:} The inequality in convex union
    becomes an equality:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:union-add}
      f(C_1 \cup C_2) = f(C_1) + f(C_2).
    \end{equation}
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Natural examples curve lengths coming from length metric structures on the surface, word-lengths, stable lengths or extremal lengths.
See~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings} for more details and examples.

\begin{definition}
  For $C$ an oriented multi-curve, $nC$ is the oriented multi-curve
  that consists of $n$~parallel
  copies of~$C$ (so with $n$ times as many components or,
  in the context of weighted oriented multi-curves,
  with weights multiplied by~$n$), and
  $C^n$ is the oriented multi-curve with as many components as~$C$, in which each
  component of~$C$ is covered by an $n$-fold cover. That is, if
  $g \in \pi_1(S,x)$ represents $C$, $g^n$ represents~$C^n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:homogeneous-stable}
     Let $f$ be a curve
     functional and let $n>0$ be an
     integer. We define some
     properties $f$ might satisfy:
     \begin{itemize}
     \item \textbf{Homogeneity:} For an arbitrary oriented multi-curve~$C$,
       \begin{equation}
         \label{eq:homogeneous}
         f(nC) = nf(C).
       \end{equation}
     \item \textbf{Stability:} For an arbitrary oriented multi-curve~$C$,
       \begin{equation}
         \label{eq:stable}
         f(C^n) = f(nC)
       \end{equation}
     \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quasi-convex, convex, and additive-convex curve functionals]
Let $\CurveFunct(S)$ denote the set of all symmetric curve functionals
on $S$ satisfying convex union, stability, and homogeneity.
In decreasing order of generality, we consider the following subsets
of $\CurveFunct(S)$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Given $R>0$, let $\QConvex_R(S)$ be the set of symmetric
      curve functionals satisfying $R$-quasi-smoothing, convex union,
      stability and homogeneity. We call this the space of
      \emph{$R$-quasi-convex functionals}. Define $\QConvex(S)
      \coloneqq \cup_{R \in \mathbb{R}_+} \QConvex_R(S)$, the space of
      \emph{quasiconvex curve functionals}.
    \item Let $\Convex(S) \coloneqq \QConvex_{0}(S)$ be the space of \emph{convex curve functionals}.
    \item Let $\AConvex(S)$ be the space of \emph{additive convex curve functionals}, i.e., the subset of $\Convex(S)$ of curve functionals that moreover satisfy the additive property.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
Examples below show we have strict inclusions
\[
\AConvex(S) \subsetneq \Convex(S) \subsetneq  \QConvex_R(S) \subsetneq \QConvex(S) \subsetneq \CurveFunct(S).
\]


In previous work, we proved the following result.

\begin{theorem}{\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}}\label{thm:convex}
   Let $f \in \QConvex(S)$. Then there is a unique continuous homogeneous function
  $\bar{f} \colon \GC^+(S) \to \bbR_{\ge 0}$ that extends~$f$.
\end{theorem}

We note that the four conditions of smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity, defining the space $\AConvex(S)$ are obviously satisfied for any functional $i(\mu,\cdot) \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$, for $\mu$ a geodesic current.
Many natural notions of length do satisfy all these four properties and they have been shown to extend continuously to geodesic currents as intersection numbers. We list them here, along with the appropriate references.

\begin{enumerate}
   \item Intersection number with a fixed geodesic current. See \cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}.%\DMGtodo{In this paragraph would be better to cite the exact results where they show property of Liouville current, instead of injectivity of assignment.}
    \item Hyperbolic lengths  \cite[Theorem~12]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}.
    \item More generally, length w.r.t. negatively curved Riemannian metrics \cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative} 
    \item Even more generally, non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem}
\item Lengths with respect to singular Euclidean metrics coming from quadratic differentials \cite[Lemma~9]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}
\item More generally, lengths with respect to negatively curved  metrics with conical singularities \cite[Theorem~A]{HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone}
\item Non-positively curved Euclidean cone metrics \cite[Proposition~3.3]{BL17:RigidityFlat}.
    \item Word length with respect to simple generating sets \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}.
    \item Certain lengths associated to Anosov representations \cite[Proposition~2.24]{MZ18:PositiveReps}
\end{enumerate}

On the other hand, not all functions on curves satisfy the $\AConvex$ properties.  

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Square root of extremal length, although it satisfies smoothing, stability and homogeneity, does not satisfy additive union (see \cite[Subsection~4.8]{MGT21:Smoothings}).
    It's an example of curve functional illustrating the proper containtment \[\AConvex(S) \subsetneq \Convex(S)\]
    \item Although word length w.r.t. an arbitrary generating set of $\pi_1$ satisfies additivity (just by linear extension), it does not always satisfy smoothing nor stability (see~\cite[Example~4.10]{MGT21:Smoothings}). The stabilized version does not always satisfy smoothing, either (see the same example and also \cite[Theorem~B]{MGT21:Smoothings}). This illustrates the proper inclusion $\Convex(S) \subsetneq  \QConvex_R(S)$ for $R>0$.
        \item  Translation lengths associated to biautomatic structures. Roughly speaking, an automatic structure is a combinatorial structure associated to a group that furnishes it with typical features enjoyed by hyperbolic groups, such as solvable conjugacy or word problems. A biautomatic structure is an automatic structure plus further astringent conditions that describe how the automatic structure interacts with the geometry of its Cayley graph. We commend the reader to~\cite[Section~2.2]{HV22:Biautomaticity} for details.
    Given a biautomatic structure $\mathcal{L}$ on a surface group $\pi_1(\Sigma)$, there is a notion of translation length $\| \cdot \|_{\mathcal{L}}$ coming from its defining regular language. Hughes-Valiunas~\cite[Proposition~4.2]{HV22:Biautomaticity} have shown these translation lengths satisfy quasi-smoothing and used this to construct a new example of hierarchically hyperbolic group that is not biautomatic. These structures encompass examples of word length in the previous item, so they do not satisfy smoothing in general.
    \item Square root of self-intersection number satisfies stability,
      homogeneity and smoothing, but does not satisfy additivity or
      even convex union (see~\cite[Example~1.12]{MGT21:Smoothings}).
      This is an example of a curve functional which is not in
      $\QConvex(S)$, even though it does extend to geodesic currents
      by work of Bonahon~\cite{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}. 
\item Lorentzian length of an affine action of a Fuchsiaon group~\cite{GM00:FlatLorentzian} doesn't satisfy quasi-smoothing, since it satifies homogeneity, stability and additivity~\cite[p. 1053]{GML09:ProperAffine} but it takes both positive and negative values. Nevertheless, it extends continuously to geodesic currents, as shown in~\cite[Theorem]{GML09:ProperAffine}.
\end{enumerate}

In view of all this, it is natural to ask which additional conditions one needs to impose on a function $f$ in $\AConvex(S)$ in order to satisfy the property in Question~\ref{q:intersection}.

It turns out that one doesn't need more conditions. Theorem~\ref{mainthm:intersections} can thus be reestated, in the previous terminology, as follows.

\begin{mainthmprime}\label{thm:intersect}
 For every $f \in \AConvex(S)$, i.e., satisfying smoothing, additive union, homogeneity and stabilty properties, there exists a unique geodesic current $\mu_f$ such $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for all closed curve $C$.
\end{mainthmprime}

\begin{remark}
Observe that we do not require in the hypotheses that the curve functional $f \in \AConvex(S)$ is non-negative: in fact it follows from the other conditions (see Corollary~\ref{cor:positivity}).
In general, curve functionals need not be positive. For example, the signed
  Lorentzian length or Margulis invariant~\cite{GM00:FlatLorentzian})
  can take either positive or negative values. However, more
  generally, any curve functional
  in $\QConvex(S)$ is positive (see Corollary~\ref{cor:positivity2}). 
\end{remark}
This can be seen as a duality result giving a one-to-one correspondence between geodesic currents  $\GC(S)$ on surfaces $f \colon \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying extra conditions. See Section~\ref{subsec:newdef}.

One should compare this result to previous work of Feng
Luo~\cite{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers}, where he characterizes curve
functionals on simple closed curves which can be realized as
intersection numbers with measured laminations, obtaining thus a
characterization of measured laminations in terms of simple curve
functionals. In Section XXX, we use Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and an
argument about fundamental domains of intersecting curves (for the
$\pi_1(\Sigma)$-action on $G(\Sigma)$) to give a new characterization
of measured laminations in terms of curve functionals. Feng Luo also
characterized which functions on simple closed curves are dual to
hyperbolic lengths~\cite{Luo98:GeodesicLength}. Our
Theorem~\ref{prop:char_liouville} can be seen as an extension of that
theorem for closed curves.

Culler-Morgan~\cite{CM87:GroupActions} give axioms for translation lengths on $\mathbb{R}$-trees which relate to (but are weaker than) our smoothing condition. In fact, in Proposition~\ref{prop:small} we give an alternate characterization of measured laminations in terms of actions whose translation lengths satisfy some additional axioms obtained by rephrasing the smoothing condition in the context of translation length of $\mathbb{R}$-trees.
Walter Parry~\cite{Par91:Translation} characterized functions on
conjugacy classes in $G$ that are translation lengths of $G$-trees in
terms of Culler-Morgan axioms. One can see our result in terms of the
smoothing condition as further characterizing which functions on
conjugacy classes in $G$ come from small $G$-tree actions, when
$G=\pi_1(S)$. It would be interesting to see what can be said for
small actions of a general hyperbolic group $G$. Misha Kapovich and
the first author show \cite{KMG:BBP} that for one ended hyperbolic
groups acting via small action on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree, the stable
length extends continuously to geodesic currents.
See also Section~\ref{sec:questions}.

By \cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings} we obtain a continuous extension of arbitrary Riemannian length as an intersection number with a current. This also follows from the fact that $i(\mu_f, \cdot)$ extends continuously to geodesic currents by Bonahon's theorem of continuous bilinear extension of geometric intersection number (see \cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}).
    Note that a continuous extension of an arbitrary Riemannian length was previously given by \cite[Theorem~1.5]{EPS:CountingCurves}. However, as a consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} we obtain a stronger result: a continuous extension as an intersection number. In fact, this also works more generally for length w.r.t any length metric structure on $S$. As a further application of this fact, we show in Section~\ref{subsec:ineqintersection} that domination of intersection numbers with all closed curves implies domination of length spectra for any metric length on a closed surface.

\subsection{Open surfaces}

A natural question is if our results can be extended to the setting of open surfaces (i.e., compact surfaces with boundary, or finite type surfaces with punctures).
  Recall that geodesic currents can also be defined more generally for finite type hyperbolic surfaces. Depending on if we consider ends as cusps or funnels, we get two different spaces, that we will call $\GC_{\mathrm{cusp}}(S)$ and $\GC_{\mathrm{open}}(S)$, respectively.
In the first case, we define the space of geodesic currents analogously to Definition~\ref{def:currents-1} for closed surfaces, i.e., as invariant measures supported on the space of geodesics of the universal cover, noting that now the space of geodesics contains arcs going from cusp to cusp. 
In the second case, we consider geodesic currents supported on geodesics projecting to the convex core of the surface.

In the case of $\GC_{\mathrm{cusp}}(S)$, 
we gave an example in~\cite[Remark~2.26]{MGT21:Smoothings} showing that intersection number is not continuous on currents. See also the recent works by Sasaki~\cite{S20:CuspedCurrents} and Trin~\cite{T22:ThurstonCompactification}.

We think there should be an analog of our result for $\GC_{\mathrm{cusp}}(S)$,
but it's not clear that the conditions defining $\AConvex(S)$ would be enough or if one should also add conditions with regards to smoothings between arcs and arcs, and arcs and curves.
More importantly, most of our arguments rely on the fact that the is a natural cyclic order at the boundary at infinity $\partial_{\infty}\wt{\Sigma}$, which we lose when $\Sigma$ has punctures, since the fundamental group will be free.

The case of $\GC_{\mathrm{open}}(S)$ is different. Even though intersection number is continuous in this case, it is not true that our defining properties of $\AConvex(S)$ guarantee a dual geodesic current.
The key issue is that the intersection number is degenerate in this case: any geodesic current $\mu$ supported on boundary curves will have zero intersection number with all geodesic currents $\nu \in \GC(S)$.
We can give a concrete example on how our theorem fails in this setting, similar to the second example in~\cite[Remark~2.26]{MGT21:Smoothings}.

Let $\bar{\Sigma}$ denote the convex core of $\Sigma$.
Let $\ell$ be the restriction to $\Sigma$ of the hyperbolic
length on the double of $\bar{\Sigma}$.
We note that $\ell$ satisfies additivity, stability and homogeneity
properties. Also, it satisfies smoothing: for non-boundary-parallel curves, smoothing is satisfied by the same argument as the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:domination}. Since boundary parallel curves do not intersect each other or other non-boundary parallel curves, smoothing is vacuously satisfied.

However, $\ell(C) \neq 0$ for $C$ a boundary parallel curve, whereas for any geodesic current $\mu$, we have $i(\mu,C)=0$, so $\ell$ cannot be realized as intersection number with a geodesic current. For a related example, see~\cite[Proposition~2.2]{T22:ThurstonCompactification}.
