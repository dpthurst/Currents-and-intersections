\section{Introduction}

Geodesic currents on a hyperbolic surface are measures on the space of unoriented geodesics that complete the set of weighted closed geodesics in the weak$^*$-topology. From a dynamical viewpoint, they are orientation and geodesic-flow invariant measures on the unit tangent bundle~\cite{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}.
 Many metric structures embed in the space of currents---e.g.
hyperbolic metrics \cite[Theorem~12]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent},
 negatively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative} or, more generally, non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem}.



Geometric intersection number of multi-curves extends continuously to a bilinear function of currents by a result of Bonahon~\cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}. Many length functions on curves or \emph{curve functionals} $f \colon\Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ are intersection numbers with a \emph{dual} geodesic current $\mu_{f}$, this is,
\[
  i(\mu_{f},C)=f(C)
\]
for any $C$ in the space of multi-curves $\mathcal{C}(S)$ (i.e. finite unions of non-negatively non-necessarily-simple closed curves on $S$).  
For example, the hyperbolic length of curves $\ell_{\Sigma}$ has a dual geodesic current~\cite[Theorem~10]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}), the \emph{Liouville current} $\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$; similarly for the negatively curved and non-positively curved Riemannian metrics mentioned above. Other examples do not come from metrics on the surface. For example, word-length with respect to simple generating sets have a dual current \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}; or length functions arising from certain representations of surface groups into higher rank Lie groups have corresponding dual currents (see~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} and, more recently~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}).

We give an axiomatic characterization of curve functionals dual to geodesic currents. 

\begin{mainthm}
A curve functional $f \colon \Curves(S)\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfies the
\emph{smoothing property}
\[
f\!\left(\mfig{curves-10}\right)
\ge
\max\!\left\{
f\!\left(\mfig{curves-11}\right),
f\!\left(\mfig{curves-12}\right)
\right\},
\]
at every essential crossing, and is \emph{additive} on connected components
(see~\ref{subsec:mainhypotheses}),
if and only if there exists a unique dual geodesic current $\mu_f$ such that
\[
i(\mu_f,C)=f(C)
\quad\text{for all curves } C.
\]
\label{thm:intersect}
\end{mainthm}

See~Definition~\ref{def:essential-crossing} for essential crossing, which can be roughly defined as a crossing that cannot be removed by homotopy.
The hypotheses of the theorem are sharp: they are both independent and necessary (see Section~\ref{subsec:examples_and_non} for many natural examples and
non-examples).  See Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} below for an algebraic version of the result dealing with oriented curves.

One can view this theorem in analogy to Poincaré duality (see Section~\ref{subsec:poincare_duality}).

As a consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} we obtain 
many new dual geodesic currents, extending several previous constructions for Riemannian metrics of negative curvature on $S$, as well as certain locally $\CAT(-1)$ and $\CAT(0)$ metrics on $S$ (see among others~\cite{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative, CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem, DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics, HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone,BL17:RigidityFlat,Con18:MarkedNonpos}). In Section~\ref{subsec:examples_and_non} we discuss these examples and many more in detail.
In fact, we obtain dual geodesic currents for any general Riemannian metric on $S$ or, even more generally, any length w.r.t any length metric structure on $S$ (in particular, for any Finsler metric).
    Our result also obtains dual geodesic currents (in fact, dual multi-curves) for stable lengths associated to embedded graphs, thus generalizing the work of Erlandsson~\cite{Erlandsson:WordLength} (see Corollary~\ref{cor:generalization_wordlength}).

All the examples above extend continuously to geodesic currents, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings} or simply by Bonahon's theorem of continuous bilinear extension of geometric intersection number. 
A previous result in~\cite[Theorem~1.5]{EPS20:CountingCurves} provides continuous extensions for arbitrary Riemannian lengths on surfaces as well as for a much more general class of lengths on surfaces (and, more generally, on Gromov hyperbolic spaces). Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} strengthens this result in the surface setting: we obtain a continuous extension for many of those metrics \emph{as an intersection number}.
    
    As a further application of this fact, we show in Section~\ref{subsec:ineqintersection} that domination of intersection numbers with all closed curves implies domination of length spectra for any metric length on a closed surface. This, in particular, answers a question of Neumann-Coto~\cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.

    For curve functionals arising from algebraic settings, such as cross-ratios (Section~\ref{sec:crossratios}), or translation lengths of group actions on real trees 
    (Section~\ref{sec:skora}), it is more convenient to decompose the smoothing condition into cases, as follows.

  For $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$ non-trivial elements that are not common powers, let
  $\ora{a}, \ora{b}$ be their corresponding hyperbolic axes (for some/any hyperbolic structure), and let $a^-, a^+$ and $b^-,b^+$ their respective repelling and attractive fixed points. We say the pair $(a,b)$  \emph{R-cross} if $a^-, b^-, a^+,b^+$ appear in this
      ccw order on the boundary at infinity. See
      Figure~\ref{fig:cross}: we say the pair $(a,b)$ is \emph{R-parallel} if $b^-,b^+, a^+, a^-$ appear in this ccw order on the boundary at infinity. See Figure~\ref{fig:parallel}. See Definition~\ref{def:crossing} for more details.
      From this viewpoint it is more natural to consider curve functionals on oriented curves. This is a more general approach that we shall take in the rest of the paper.
      Using this we can state a version of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} with weaker hypotheses. 

\begin{mainthmprime}
Let $f \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function and $\overline{f}(g) \coloneqq \frac{1}{2} \left(f(g) + f(G) \right)$ for every $g \in \pi_1(S)$, with $G$ denoting the inverse of $g$.
There exists a (unique) geodesic current $\mu_{\overline{f}}$ on $S$ so that
\[
i(\mu_{\overline{f}}, [g]) = \overline{f}(g)
\]
for every $g \in \pi_1(S)$ if and only if $f$ satisfies the following conditions (for $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item class invariant: $f(a)=f(b)$ whenever $a$ is conjugate to $b$.
\item stability: 
$f(a^n)=nf(a) \mbox{ for all } n \geq 1$.
\item R-oriented smoothing:
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:conn_oriented} f(ab) &\geq  f(a) + f(b) && \mbox{ if } (a,b)\mbox{ are } \mbox{R-parallel;} \\
    \label{eq:disconn_oriented} f(a) + f(b) &\geq f(ab)  && \mbox{ if }  (a,b)\mbox{ are } \mbox{R-crossing}.
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\label{thm:intersections_group}
\end{mainthmprime}

See Section~\ref{subsec:algebraic_vs_top} for a proof of the equivalence of the smoothing condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} to equations~\ref{eq:conn_oriented} \emph{and} \ref{eq:disconn_oriented} in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}.
Since the hypotheses only consider oriented smoothing, they are a priori weaker than smoothing. However, with the rest of the assumptions, oriented smoothing of $f$ implies smoothing of the symmetrized functional $\overline{f}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:oriented_smoothing_unoriented}).

Furthermore, neither one of conditions~\ref{eq:conn_oriented} and ~\ref{eq:disconn_oriented} imply each other: see Section~\ref{subsec:mainhypotheses}, Lemma~\ref{lem:red_not_smoothing} or Example~\ref{ex:linEL}.
The averaging of $f(a)$ and $f(A)$ is also necessary; see
Appendix~\ref{sec:asymmetric}.

Our result can also be seen as a generalization of Feng
Luo's criterion~\cite[Theorem~1.1, Theorem~1.2]{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers}, where he characterizes curve
functionals on simple closed curves which can be realized as
intersection numbers with measured laminations, i.e., geodesic currents $\alpha$ for which $i(\alpha,\alpha)=0$ (see \cite[Proposition~17]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}). 
 
 In a previous paper, \cite[Proposition 4.11]{MGT21:Smoothings}, we have proven that the stable length associated to a quasi-fuchsian representation satisfies \emph{quasi-smoothing}, i.e., smoothing up to uniform additive error (see Definition \ref{def:properties}). Recent work of Fricker-Furman \cite[Theorem A]{FF22:QF} (see also Brody-Reyes~\cite[Proposition~1.8]{BR24:Cubulations}), shows that this additive error in the quasi-smoothing cannot be removed unless the representation is fuchsian. 
In the case the representation is fuchsian, by \cite[Proposition 14]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}, it is dual to a geodesic current, and hence smoothing is satisfied.
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} generalizes all of these results by showing that the smoothing property and additivity are \emph{sufficient and necessary} conditions for a curve functional to be dual to a geodesic current. 

\subsection{Characterize measured laminations and hyperbolic metrics}

As a consequence of the Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, we can further characterize which
curve functionals correspond to \emph{measured laminations} (i.e., geodesic currents satisfying $i(\alpha,\alpha)=0)$ by~\cite[Proposition~17]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}), or to hyperbolic
metrics.

\begin{mainthm}
Let $f$ be a curve functional satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, with dual current $\mu_f$. Then,
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mu_f \in \Curr(S)$ is a measured lamination if and only if $f$ satisfies the so-called \emph{disconnected max-smoothing property}: for every \emph{crossing} pair $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ 
\begin{equation*}
      f\left(a\right) +  f\left(b\right)  = \max \left\{ f\left(ab\right),f\left(ab^{-1}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation*}
    \label{item:lam-char}
    \item $\mu_f \in \Curr(S)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ satisfies the following condition: for every \emph{crossing} pair $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ \[
        \lambda \left(a\right) \lambda \left(b\right) = \lambda \left(ab\right) + \lambda \left(ab^{-1}\right),
  \]
  where $\lambda( \cdot ) \coloneqq 2\cosh(f(\cdot)/2)$.
  \label{item:hyp-char}
\end{enumerate}
    \label{mainthm:apps}
\end{mainthm} 

This is proved as Theorems~\ref{thm:char_laminations}
and~\ref{thm:char_liouville}.
(The $\lambda$-length functional appears naturally in the study of the
skein algebra of a surface; see, for example, \cite[Sec.\ 1, 2]{Pen12:Decorated}, \cite[Sec.\
3]{Thurston14:PBI}.)

We emphasize that this result characterizes hyperbolic metrics among all curve functionals. In particular, it can be applied to stable lengths induced from Green metrics arising from admissible random walks on $\pi_1(S)$, yielding potential applications to investigate the singularity of their hitting boundary measures (see Section~\ref{subsec:green}).

These results generalize both of Luo's results on characterizing which functions on simple curves dual to measured laminations~\cite[Theorem~1.1,Theorem~1.2]{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers} (alluded above) and to hyperbolic length functions~\cite{Luo98:GeodesicLength}.  We outline the relation between our conditions and Feng Luo's conditions in the Appendix, Proposition~\ref{prop:feng}.

 Our methods also recover the  result of the second author~\cite{Thu00:GeometricIntersection} that measured laminations satisfy the condition~ \ref{item:lam-char} in Theorem~\ref{mainthm:apps}.


\subsection{Characterizing small actions on real trees} 

We give a new characterization of actions of surface groups on $\mathbb{R}$-trees dual to measured laminations. In turn, we give a new and concise proof of a classical result by Skora~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}.

A \emph{$\Gamma$-tree} denotes an isometric action of a finitely-generated group $\Gamma$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $(T,d)$ by isometries. Let  $\|g\|\coloneqq \inf_{x \in T} d(x,gx)$ denote the \emph{translation length} of $g$.
The \emph{characterstic set} $T_g$ of $g \in \Gamma$ is the set of
points that are translated the least. Concretely,
an isometry $g$ of $T$ either has a non-empty set $T_g$ of fixed points (\emph{elliptic}) or
has no fixed points and translates points in an invariant
axis $T_g$ by a definite distance of $\|g\|$
(\emph{hyperbolic})---see,~\cite[1.3]{CM87:GroupActions}.
In either case, $T_g$ is the \emph{characterstic set} of $g \in \Gamma$.
An action
is \emph{irreducible} if it does not have a global fixed point
($\norm{g}$ does not identically vanish), $T$ has no ends fixed by all of
$\Gamma$, and there are no $\Gamma$-invariant lines (see Section~\ref{sec:skora}).
We say that the action is \emph{minimal} if the only invariant
non-empty subtree is $T$ itself.

 We let an \emph{$S$-tree} $(S,T)$ denote a $\pi_1(S)$-tree $T$, where $S$ is a closed orientable hyperbolic surface. We say the action \emph{preserves axis intersection} if, for
every $a, b \in \Gamma$ that act hyperbolically on $T$ and have
hyperbolic axes $\ora{a},\ora{b}$ crossing in $\wt{\Sigma}$, then their axes
$T_a,T_b$ in $T$ also intersect.
Note that this is weaker than presuming intersection of the characteristic sets $T_a,T_b$ if either $a$ or $b$ were elliptic in $T$. If the action satisfies this, we say it \emph{preserves characteristic intersection}.
An example of an action preserving axis intersection is the $S$-tree
$T_{\lambda}$ \emph{dual to a measured lamination} $\lambda$, which,
roughly speaking, is obtained by taking the Hausdorff quotient of
$\wt{\Sigma}$ with the pseudo-metric $d_{\lambda}(x,y)$ obtained by
considering the $\lambda$-measure of the geodesics transverse to the hyperbolic
segment between $x, y \in \wt{\Sigma}$.

We prove the following equivalences.
Here, if two hyperbolic isometries of $T$ have axes intersecting in a
non-degenerate arc (i.e., a non-singleton arc), we say they \emph{overlap incoherently} if the
isometries translate in opposite directions on the overlap.

\begin{mainthm}
Let $(S,T)$ denote an $S$-tree.
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[(A)]
\item There exist a measured lamination $\lambda$ on $S$ so that $T$ is $\pi_1(S)$-equivariantly isometric to $T_{\lambda}$;
\item The $S$-tree is irreducible and preserves axis intersection;
\item The $S$-tree is irreducible and preserves characteristic intersection;
\item The translation length $\| \cdot \| \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For any pair of $R$-crossing elements $(a,b)$, 
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:small_disconn} \|a\| + \|b\| & = \max \{ \|ab\|, \|aB\| \}.
  \end{align}
\item For any pair of $R$-parallel elements $(a,b)$, 
 \begin{align}
    \label{eq:small_conn}  \quad \|ab\| &= \max \{ \|a\| + \|b\|, \|aB\|\}.
  \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\item The $S$-tree $(S,T)$ does not have any parallel pair $(a,b)$ of hyperbolic isometries in the disk that overlap incoherently in $T$.
\end{enumerate}
\label{mainthm:skora}
\end{mainthm}

The equivalence between (A) and (D) follows by the first part of
Theorem~\ref{mainthm:apps}. In Theorem~\ref{thm:small}, we prove that
(B) implies (D). The fact that (A) implies
(B) follows from previous work of
Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths}. Hence, in particular, our
arguments give a new proof of the equivalence between (A) and (B),
which is a classical Theorem of Skora~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}. We
show that (B) is equivalent to (D) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:axis_incoherent_characterization}. Theorem~\ref{thm:small} also shows (B) implies (C), and (C) clearly implies (B).

Skora later showed~\cite{Sk96:Splittings} that there is another condition equivalent to an $S$-tree being dual to a measured lamination.

In the more general case of finitely-generated groups,
Culler-Morgan~\cite{CM87:GroupActions} stated a list of necessary axioms satisfied by the translation
lengths of $\Gamma$-actions on $\mathbb{R}$-trees, and asked if these axioms are also sufficient to characterize those translation lengths among conjugacy class invariant
functions $f \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{R}$. Parry~\cite{Par91:Translation} answered the question in the affirmative. 

These axioms can be recast (see Proposition~\ref{prop:max_twice}) by saying that a conjugacy class invariant function $\| \cdot \| \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{R}$ is the translation length of a $\Gamma$-tree if and only if for any pair $g,h \in \Gamma$, the maximum among the following three quantities appears (at least) twice:
\begin{equation}
\|g\|+\|h\|, \quad \|gh\|, \quad \|gH\|.
\end{equation}


From this perspective, Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} pushes further
Parry's characterization when $\Gamma=\pi_1(S)$ by giving a set of
axioms characterizing which of those translation lengths
correspond to \emph{small} $\Gamma$-actions on $\mathbb{R}$-trees or,
equivalently, are dual to a measured lamination.

\subsection{Generalized cross-ratios}

Finally, we subsume previous work of Martone-Zhang~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} and Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti~\cite{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios} on associating a geodesic current to every \emph{generalized cross-ratio} $[\cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot] \colon \partial_{\infty}{S} \to \mathbb{R}$ (see~Definition~\ref{def:general_crossratio}). A generalized cross-ratio is a function on oriented 4-tuples of the boundary at infinity satisfying certain invariance, symmetry, finite additivity and non-negativity properties. For example, if $\Sigma$ is a hyperbolic metric on $S$, the hyperbolic cross-ratio $[\cdot]_\Sigma$ coming from realizing the universal cover of $\Sigma$ as an isometric copy of the upper half-plane model is an example. Every generalized cross-ratio $[\cdot ]$ induces an additive curve functional called the \emph{period} $\ell_{[ \cdot ]}$. For the previous example of the hyperbolic cross-ratio, $\ell_{[\cdot]_\Sigma}$ is the hyperbolic length.
We show that periods satisfy the conditions of our main Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and thus induce a geodesic current.

\begin{mainthm}
Let $[\cdot, \cdot, \cdot, \cdot]$ be a generalized cross-ratio of period $\ell$. Then $\ell$ satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, and hence induces a geodesic current $\mu_{\ell}$.
\label{mainthm:crossratio}
\end{mainthm}

In Section~\ref{sec:functionals}, we describe the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, and place them in the broader setting of curve functionals. We give examples that satisfy these hypotheses, as well as natural ones that do not.

In Section~\ref{sec:backround}, we introduce the basic background. In particular, we recall the measure-theoretic background and the existence of limits of certain sequences used in the construction of the measure in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}. 

In Section~\ref{sec:smoothing}, we establish basic lemmas on essential crossings and smoothings. We review left and right actions of $\pi_1(S)$ on $\wt{S}$. We relate the topological, algebraic, and geometric notions of smoothing. We prove further results on crossings and parallelism used throughout the paper. We then derive more technical lemmas involving triples of elements of $\pi_1(S)$, which form a key step in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.

In Section~\ref{sec:smoothing-types}, we analyze the different notions of smoothing (oriented/unoriented, connected/disconnected). The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} does not rely on this section. We use these results to deduce Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} from Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.

In Section~\ref{sec:define_measure}, we define the dual measure associated to a curve functional by specifying its values on a dense family of right-handed boxes, which we show form a semi-ring. The measure is $\pi_1(S)$-invariant on this family. We prove that it is non-negative and finitely additive on the semi-ring.

In Section~\ref{sec:construct}, we finish the construction of the dual geodesic current. In particular, we show that the dual measure is countably additive and defines an oriented geodesic current. By averaging, we obtain a geodesic current. Finally, we show that this current is dual to the original curve functional. This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}. We then prove Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} and several variants as corollaries.

In Section~\ref{sec:special-classes}, we discuss subclasses of geodesic currents, including filling currents, multi-curves, measured laminations, and hyperbolic lengths/Liouville currents. We prove Theorem~\ref{mainthm:apps}, which characterizes measured laminations and Liouville currents by their curve functionals. We construct currents dual to embedded graphs. We explain how to obtain dual currents for arbitrary length metrics.

In Section~\ref{sec:crossratios}, we show that period functionals arising from generalized cross-ratios satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}. They therefore induce geodesic currents. This proves Theorem~\ref{mainthm:crossratio}.

In Section~\ref{sec:skora}, we prove Theorem~\ref{mainthm:skora}. We recall the necessary background on real trees and group actions.

The Appendix addresses topics not central to the main proofs. Appendix~\ref{sec:luo} relates Theorem~\ref{mainthm:apps} to work of Feng Luo. Appendix~\ref{sec:hyp-par} presents a hyperbolic parallelogram estimate used to characterize hyperbolic lengths. Appendix~\ref{sec:asymmetric} explains why asymmetric word metrics are not dual to right-handed intersection numbers, highlighting the role of symmetry in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}. Appendix~\ref{sec:necessary} gives an alternative proof of the necessity of the hypotheses in Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, using hyperbolic geometry and analysis on $\wt{S}$.
