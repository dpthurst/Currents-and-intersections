\section{Introduction}

Geodesic currents are positive measures on the space of geodesics of a surface. They were first introduced by Bonahon in
his seminal paper \cite{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}. Perhaps their most interesting trait is that many metric
structures embed in the space of currents, such as
hyperbolic metrics \cite[Theorem~12]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent},
 negatively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative} (which in fact do so densely, according to \cite[Lemma~2.8]{Ham99:Cocycles}),
Croke-Fathi-Feldman for non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem},
Hersonsky-Paulin \cite[Theorem~A]{HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone} for
negatively curved metrics with conical singularities,
Duchin-Leininger-Rafi more explictly for singular Euclidean structures
associated to quadratic differentials
or half-translation structures
\cite[Theorem~4]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics} and non-positively curved Euclidean cone metrics \cite[Main Theorem]{BL17:RigidityFlat}. \DMGtodo{Read the definitions of each metric, I think DLR10 is subsumed in BL17 and/or HP97}.
The way the embedding works is associating a canonical geodesic current $\mathcal{L}_{\rho}$ to a given metric structure $\rho$. Then one shows that this assignment is in fact an injection. This canonical geodesic current $\mathcal{L}_{\rho}$ was coined \emph{Liouville current} by Bonahon \cite[Section~2]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent} for the case of hyperbolic structures, but it has also been used more generally for canonical geodesic currents associated to other metric structures (see, for instance, \cite[Section~3.1]{EU18:LengthCurrents}).
The fundamental property that ties this Liouville currents to the geometric structure is the following.
It turns out that weighted multi-curves on a surface embed in the space of geodesic currents \footnote{In fact, they embed densely and so one can see the space of geodesic currents as the closure of the space of weighted curves on a surface. Previous work of Bonahon extended the notion of geometric intersection
number as a continuous bilinear function of two geodesic currents
\cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}. This
allowed him to extend hyperbolic length to geodesic currents by
following the principle of realizing it as an intersection number with
a distinguished geodesic current
\cite[Proposition~14]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}.
Similarly, other notions of length of curves $\ell_{\rho}$ associated to the above mentioned metric structures $\rho$ can be realized as intersection numbers with the corresponding Liouville current $\mathcal{L}_{\rho}$, this is

\[
  i(\mathcal{L}_{\rho},C)=\ell_{\rho}(C)
\]

for any closed multi-curve $C$ in $S$. 
Erlandsson shows that word-length with respect to simple generating sets of
the fundamental group can also be realized as an intersection number with a Liouville current \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}.
Let $\mathcal{C}(S)$ denote the closed curves on $S$. For a metric structure $\rho$, we define $\Lambda_{\rho}\coloneqq \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}(S)}$ the \emph{marked length spectrum of} $\rho$.
On the other hand, by Otal \cite[Th\'eor\`eme~2]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative} a geodesic current is uniquely determined by its intersection numbers with closed curves.
Thus, the assignment of a Liouville current to a metric structure provides with the first step towards attempting the \emph{rigidity problem}: is a family of metric structures determined by its marked length spectrum? \\
By the discussion above, this is equivalent to the problem: is the assignment $\rho \mapsto \mathcal{L}_{\rho}$, from the space of metric structures to the space of currents, an embedding?

As we have pointed out at the beginning, this problem has been solved for several metric structures.

We leave the following (to our knowledge) open question:

\begin{question}
Is Erlandsson's assignment of a Liouville current to word-length an embedding?
\end{question}

Let $\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S)$ denote the space of weighted multi-curves on $S$.
In this paper we consider the following question.

\begin{question}\label{q:intersection}
Under which conditions can a function $f :\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ be realized as an intersection number with a fixed geodesic current?
\end{question}


Feng Luo considered the version of this question for measured laminations, which can be seen as geodesic currents $\alpha$ for which $i(\alpha,\alpha)=0$ (see \cite[Proposition~17]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}). In this case, the functions are defined on simple closed curves \cite[Theorem~1.1,Theorem~1.2]{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers}.
Jeffrey Frazier asks Question \ref{q:intersection} and remarks how relevant it is for the study of spectral rigidity problems \cite[Appendix~A.1]{Frazier12:SpectralRigidity}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:properties}
  Let $f \colon \mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be a functional defined on weighted multi-curves. In the following, we define several properties that $f$ might satisfy.

  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(Smoothing)}  Let $C$~be an oriented curve on~$S$, and
    let $x$~be an essential crossing of~$C$. Let $C'$ be the oriented
    smoothing of~$C$ at~$x$. Then $f(C) \ge f(C')$. Schematically, we
    have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right) \ge f\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right)
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{(additive union)}  Let $C_1$ and~$C_2$ be two curves
    on~$S$. Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:union-add}
      f(C_1 \cup C_2) = f(C_1) + f(C_2).
    \end{equation}
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
  For $C$ a curve, $nC$ is the curve that consists of n parallel
  copies of $C$ (so with $n$ times as many components), and
  $C^n$ is the curve with as many components as~$C$, in which each
  component of~$C$ is covered by an $n$-fold cover, i.e. if $g \in \pi_1(S,x)$ represents $C$, $g^n$ represents $C^n$.
\end{definition}



\begin{definition}\label{def:homogeneous}
     Let $f \colon \mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be a functional defined on weighted multi-curves . We say
  that $f$ is \emph{homogeneous} if it satisfies the following
  scaling property for an arbitrary curve~$C$:
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:homogeneous}
    f(nC) = nf(C).
  \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:stable}
     Let $f \colon \mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be a functional defined on weighted multi-curves. We say
  that $f$ is \emph{stable} if it satisfies the following
   property for an arbitrary curve~$C$:
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:stable}
    f(C^n) = f(nC)
  \end{equation}
\end{definition}
Note that, if $f$ satisfies smoothing, then $f(nC) \le f(C^n)$.
The stability and homogeneity conditions mean that these two inequalities are
equalities, respectively.

The four conditions of smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity are obviously necessary conditions for a function satisfying the property of Question~\ref{q:intersection}.
Indeed, stability is needed for any such extension to be well-defined, since by definition of geodesic current, $C^n$ and $nC$ are represented by the same current.
On the other hand, homogeneity, additivity and smoothing are obviously satisfied by intersection number w.r.t a given current.

A functional satisfying additivity/convexity and smoothing can be upgraded to a functional that moreover satisfies stability and homogeneity (see \cite[Theorem~B]{MGT19:CurvesToCurrents}).

Many natural notions of length do satisfy all these four properties, and they have been shown to extend continuously to geodesic currents as intersection numbers.

\begin{enumerate}
   \item Intersection number with a fixed geodesic current. See \cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}.\DMGtodo{In this paragraph would be better to cite the exact results where they show property of Liouville current, instead of injectivity of assignment.}
    \item Hyperbolic lengths  \cite[Theorem~12]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}.
    \item More generally, length w.r.t. negatively curved Riemannian metrics \cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative} 
    \item Even more generally, non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem}
\item Lengths w.r.t singular Euclidean metrics coming from quadratic differentials \cite[Lemma~9]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}
\item More generally, lengths w.r.t. negatively curved  metrics with conical singularities \cite[Theorem~A]{HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone}
\item Even more generally, non-positively curved Euclidean cone metrics \cite[Main Theorem]{BL17:RigidityFlat}.
    \item word length w.r.t. simple generating sets \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}.
    \item Translation lengths associated to Anosov representations \cite[Proposition~2.24]{MZ18:PositiveReps}
\end{enumerate}



Not all functions on curves satisfy these conditions. 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Square root of extremal length, although it satisfies smoothing, stability and homogeneity, it does not satisfy additive union (see \cite[Section~3.8]{MGT19:CurvesToCurrents}). 
    \item Although word length w.r.t. an arbitrary generating set of $\pi_1$ satisfies additivity, it does not always satisfy smoothing (see \cite[Example~3.7]{MGT19:CurvesToCurrents}). 
    \item Square root of self-intersection number satisfies stability, homogeneity and smoothing, but does not satisfy additivity (see \cite[Example~1.5]{MGT19:CurvesToCurrents}).
\end{enumerate}

It is natural to ask which additional conditions one needs to impose to a function $f$ in order to satisfy the property in Question~\ref{q:intersection}.

It turns out that one doesn't need more conditions.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intersect}
 Let $f \colon \mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be a functional defined on weighted multi-curves satisfying smoothing, additive union, homogeneity and stabilty properties. Then there exists a unique geodesic current $\mu_f$ such that the extension $\wt{f}\colon \mathcal{GC}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies $\wt{f}(\alpha)=i(\alpha,\mu_f)$.
\end{theorem}
