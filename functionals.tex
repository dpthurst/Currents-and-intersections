\section{Curve functionals: properties, general context and examples}
\label{sec:functionals}




In this section we state the main technical hypotheses that we require of our curve functionals.

Then, we discuss how our result fits into our previous work
in~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings}. In particular we give examples of curve
functionals and discuss whether they satisfy the hypotheses of our results.
We then turn to further questions motivated by our results.

\subsection{Curve functionals and their properties}
\label{subsec:mainhypotheses}

We now describe in detail the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.

In this paper, the geometric intersection number between curves will play a prominent role. In general, when one considers geometric intersection number $i(C, D)$ between two curves $C$ and $D$, it is assumed that $C,D$ are unoriented, or else assigns the same value to $i(C,D)$ and $i(C,D^{-1})$. 
Let $\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}(S) \subset \{ (\gamma_1,\gamma_2) | \gamma_1 \cap \gamma_2 = \{ \mbox{pt} \} \}$ be the set of pairs of oriented curves intersecting transversely.
The map induces a continuous equivariant map $(\gamma_1,\gamma_2) \mapsto  \gamma_1 \cap \gamma_2$ from $\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}(S)$ to $\wt{S}$, hence the action of $\pi_1(S)$ is properly discontinuous and free. Let $\mathcal{F}$ denote a fundamental domain of such action. Following Bonahon~\cite{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}, for any two oriented currents $\mu, \nu$ we define
\begin{equation}
i(\mu, \nu) \coloneqq \int_{\mathcal{F}} \mu \times \nu.
\label{eq:intersection}
\end{equation}
Bonahon defined the same intersection number in the context of unoriented geodesic currents, but the definition is the same for oriented ones. Moreover, this intersection number remains unchanged upon flipping the orientation of any of its factors.
It enjoys all the other properties of Bonahon's intersection: it is continuous, extends the geometric intersection of closed curves.
Note that, however, two oriented currents are not uniquely determined by their intersections with all oriented curves, since for example $\sigma(\mu)$ and $\mu$ have the same intersection numbers. The result does hold for unoriented geodesic currents~\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~2]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}.

 In the sequel, whenever we say curves, we mean oriented curves, unless stated explicitly otherwise. In terms of geodesic currents, this means we will work with oriented geodesic currents.
As we shall see in the Appendix~\ref{sec:asymmetric}, there are versions of
intersection numbers for oriented curves which are asymmetric (i.e. they distinguish the
orientations of the intersections). For those, the analog of the main duality
result, Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, will not hold. This will also be reflected in the proof of the main theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.
However, in a lot of our examples the curve functionals will be assumed \emph{symmetric}, i.e., satisfying $f(C)=f(C^{-1})$.
Equivalently, they can be seen as curve functionals on unoriented curves.

\begin{definition}\label{def:properties}
  For $S$ a compact topological surface without boundary,
  let
  $f \colon \Curves(S) \to \bbR$ be a
  function defined on the space of oriented multi-curves,
  not-necessarily-simple oriented curves.
  We will also refer to $f$ as a \emph{curve functional} for short.
  (\emph{Functional} means that it takes values in scalars; it is not
  assumed to be linear, although the relevant examples to which our Theorem will apply will be additive).
  We define several
  properties that $f$ might satisfy.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Symmetry:} Let $C$ be an oriented multi-curve, and $C^{-1}$ the multi-curve with the opposite orientation. We have $f(C)=f(C^{-1})$.
        \item \textbf{Orientation invariance:} Let $C$ be an oriented closed multi-curve, and $C'$ the same unoriented multi-curve with a different choice of orientation. We have $f(C)=f(C')$. This condition is stronger than symmetry, but if $f$ is additive, they are clearly equivalent.
    \item \textbf{Oriented Smoothing:} Let $C$~be an oriented curve on~$S$, and
    let $x$~be an essential crossing of~$C$. Let $C'$ be the oriented
    smoothing of~$C$ at~$x$. Then $f(C) \ge f(C')$. Schematically, we
    have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right) \ge  f\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right),
    \end{equation}
    See Definition~\ref{def:essential-crossing} for ``essential
    crossing''. Loosely, it is a crossing that cannot be removed by homotopy. 
  \item \textbf{Additivity: } Let $C_1$ and~$C_2$ be two oriented curves
    on~$S$. Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:union-additive}
      f(C_1 \cup C_2) = f(C_1) + f(C_2).
    \end{equation}
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The oriented smoothing condition can be further divided into two types, following the convention in Definition~\ref{def:smoothing_types}, depending on if the essential crossing is between two connected components of the multi-curve, or if it is a sef-crossing of the one connected component. Accordingly, we will say $f$ satisfies a particular type of smoothing property if $f$ is non-increasing under such type.
We emphasize that oriented connected smoothing does not imply the oriented disconnected smoothing, or viceversa.
For example, the curve functional $f$ assigning to a multi-curve its number of connected components is a natural function satisfying oriented disconnected smoothing but not oriented connected smoothing. Also, the curve functional $g$ assigning to a multi-curve the maximal hyperbolic length among its connected components satisfies connected smoothing but not disconnected smoothing (by a computation using Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp_parallelogram}). For more examples see Remark~\ref{rmk:reducible_action} or Example~\ref{ex:linEL}.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}
  For $C$ an oriented multi-curve, $nC$ is the oriented multi-curve
  that consists of $n$~parallel
  copies of~$C$ (so with $n$ times as many components or,
  in the context of weighted oriented multi-curves,
  with weights multiplied by~$n$), and
  $C^n$ is the oriented multi-curve with as many components as~$C$, in which each
  component of~$C$ is covered by an $n$-fold cover. That is, if
  $g \in \pi_1(S,x)$ represents $C$, $g^n$ represents~$C^n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:stable}
     Let $f$ be a curve
     functional and let $n>0$ be an
     integer. We say $f$ satisfies \emph{stability} if, for an arbitrary oriented multi-curve~$C$,
       \begin{equation}
         \label{eq:stable}
         f(C^n) = f(nC).
       \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:homogeneous} For an arbitrary oriented multi-curve~$C$,
       \begin{equation}
         \label{eq:homogeneous}
         f(nC) = nf(C).
       \end{equation}
       Note that functionals satisfying additivity (Equation~\ref{eq:union-additive}) automatically satisfy homogeneity.
\end{definition}

There exist slight generalizations of both smothing as well as additivity that have been used in our previous work~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings}.

\begin{definition}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Oriented Quasi-smoothing:} There is a constant $R\ge 0$ with the
      following property. Let $C$~be an oriented curve on~$S$, and
    let $x$~be an essential crossing of~$C$. Let $C'$ be the oriented
    smoothing of~$C$ at~$x$. Then $f(C) \ge f(C')-R$. Schematically, we
    have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:quasismoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right) \ge f\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right) -R
    \end{equation}
    See Definition~\ref{def:essential-crossing} for ``essential
    crossing''. If $R=0$, we recover the oriented smoothing property.
  \item \textbf{Convex union:} Let $C_1$ and~$C_2$ be two oriented curves
    on~$S$. Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:union-convex}
      f(C_1 \cup C_2) \le f(C_1) + f(C_2).
    \end{equation}
    If the above inequalities are strengthened to equalities, we recover the additivity property (Equation~\ref{eq:union-additive}).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}




Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} considered curve functions on unoriented curves.

\begin{definition}
Let $C$ be an unoriented closed curve, and $C'$ any of the two smoothings, oriented or unoriented. We say $f \colon \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies \emph{smoothing} if $f(C) \geq f(C')$, Schematically, we have
     \[
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) \ge \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right), f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)\right\},
   \]
\end{definition}

We can extend this definition for orientation invariant curve functionals on oriented multi-curves.
If $f \colon \mathcal{C}^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ is orientation invariant, it induces a curve functional on unoriented multi-curves $\overline{f} \colon \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$.

\begin{definition}
We say $f$ satisfies \emph{smoothing} if its associated curve functional on unoriented curves $\bar{f}$ satisfies smoothing.
\end{definition}

For an orientation invariant curve functional $f$, we can similarly say $f$ satisfies \emph{quasi-smoothing} if the induced function on unoriented curves satisfies quasi-smoothing with respect to both smoothings (both oriented and unoriented).

\subsection{General context and examples}

Here we discuss how the results in this paper fit into a broader context initiated in~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings}, discuss how it relates to other recent results in the literature, and give exmaples of curve functionals that satisfy and do not satisfy the hypotheses of the main theorem.
 This part is independent from the rest of the paper, and is not
used in the proof in the main results.


\begin{definition}[Quasi-convex, convex, and additive-convex curve functionals]
Let $\CurveFunct(S)$ denote the set of all symmetric curve functionals
on $S$ satisfying convex union, stability, and homogeneity.
In decreasing order of generality, we consider the following subsets
of $\CurveFunct(S)$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Given $R>0$, let $\QConvex_R(S)$ be the set of symmetric
      curve functionals satisfying $R$-quasi-smoothing, convex union,
      stability and homogeneity. We call this the space of
      \emph{$R$-quasi-convex functionals}. Define $\QConvex(S)
      \coloneqq \cup_{R \in \mathbb{R}_+} \QConvex_R(S)$, the space of
      \emph{quasiconvex curve functionals}.
    \item Let $\Convex(S) \coloneqq \QConvex_{0}(S)$ be the space of \emph{convex curve functionals}.
    \item Let $\AConvex(S)$ be the space of \emph{additive convex curve functionals}, i.e., the subset of $\Convex(S)$ of curve functionals that moreover satisfy the additive property.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For a motivation for the use of the adjective ``convex'' in this setting, see Subsection~\ref{subsubsec:convexity_charts}.

Examples below show we have strict inclusions
\[
\AConvex(S) \subsetneq \Convex(S) \subsetneq  \QConvex_R(S) \subsetneq \QConvex(S) \subsetneq \CurveFunct(S).
\]

\subsection{Examples and non-examples}
\label{subsec:examples_and_non}
In previous work, we proved the following result.

\begin{theorem}{\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}}\label{thm:convex}
   Let $f \in \QConvex(S)$. Then there is a unique continuous homogeneous function
  $\bar{f} \colon \Curr^+(S) \to \bbR_{\ge 0}$ that extends~$f$.
\end{theorem}

It is easy to see that the conditions of smoothing, additivity,
stability and homogeneity, defining the space $\AConvex(S)$ are
obviously satisfied for any functional $i(\mu,\cdot) \colon \Curves(S)
\to \mathbb{R}$, for $\mu$ a geodesic current. This was proven
in \cite[Section~4]{MGT21:Smoothings}, and we give a different proof more in line of the types of argument in this paper in Section~\ref{sec:necessary}.
Many natural notions of length do satisfy all these four properties and they have been shown to extend continuously to geodesic currents as intersection numbers. We list them here, along with the appropriate references.

\begin{enumerate}
   \item Intersection number with a fixed geodesic current. See \cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}.
    \item Hyperbolic lengths  \cite[Proposition~14]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}.
    \item More generally, length w.r.t.\ negatively curved Riemannian metrics \cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}.
    \item Even more generally, non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem}.
\item Lengths with respect to singular Euclidean metrics coming from quadratic differentials \cite[Lemma~9]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}.
\item More generally, lengths with respect to negatively curved Riemannian  metrics with conical singularities of angle $\geq 2\pi$ \cite[Theorem~A]{HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone}.
\item Non-positively curved Euclidean cone metrics of angle $\geq 2\pi$ \cite[Proposition~3.3]{BL17:RigidityFlat}, \cite{Con18:MarkedNonpos}.
    \item Word length with respect to simple generating sets \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}.
    \item Certain lengths associated to Anosov representations induced by positive generalized cross-ratios \cite[Proposition~2.24]{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed}.
    \item Stable lengths associated to embedded graphs, as we showed in~\ref{thm:dual_embeddedgraph}. See also Theorem~\ref{cor:generalization_wordlength}.
\end{enumerate}

On the other hand, not all functions on curves satisfy the $\AConvex$ properties, not even some that extend continuously to geodesic currents.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Asymmetric curve functionals, such as the examples discussed in~\cite[Section~4.6]{MGT21:Smoothings}, stable lengths of Green metrics coming from asymmetric from generating sets (see Section~\ref{subsec:green}), or natural oriented length functions coming from Anosov representations~\cite{BridgemanCanaryLabourieSambarino18:SimpleRoots}.
    \item Square root of extremal length, although it satisfies smoothing, stability and homogeneity, does not satisfy additive union (see \cite[Section~4.8]{MGT21:Smoothings}). All of these examples extend to geodesic currents by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}.
    It's an example of curve functional illustrating the proper containment \[\AConvex(S) \subsetneq \Convex(S).\]
    \item Although word length w.r.t.\ an arbitrary generating set of $\pi_1$ satisfies additivity (just by linear extension), it does not always satisfy smoothing nor stability (see~\cite[Example~4.10]{MGT21:Smoothings}). The stabilized version does not always satisfy smoothing, either (see the same example and also \cite[Theorem~B]{MGT21:Smoothings}). This illustrates the proper inclusion $\Convex(S) \subsetneq  \QConvex_R(S)$ for $R>0$.
    \item  In future work the first author shows that if $\pi_1(S) \acts X$ an action on a hyperbolic quasi-geodesic metric space $X$ with bounded backtracking property, then $\ell_X$, the stable length of the action, satisfies $\ell_X \in \QConvex(S)$. In general, these actions do not satisfy smoothing. We give two examples in the next two items.
    \item A concrete example of the above are stable lengths associated to quasi-fuchsian representations. They satisfy quasi-smoothing by \cite[Lemma~4.3]{BridgemanTaylor08:ExtensionWP} or \cite[Proposition 4.11]{MGT21:Smoothings}, and they satisfy strict smoothing if only if they are fuchsian \cite[Theorem A]{FF21:QF}, illustrating another example of the  proper inclusion $\Convex(S) \subsetneq  \QConvex_R(S)$ for $R>0$.  
    \begin{question}
    If $K$ is the quasi-fuchsian constant of the limit set of a quasi-fuchsian representation $\rho$, and $R>0$ is the minimal constant so that $\ell_{\rho} \in \QConvex_R(S)$, do we have that $R \to \infty$ as $K \to \infty$? 
    \end{question} 
\item Another example are length functions coming from higher-rank representations $\rho \colon \pi_1(S) \to \PSL(n,\mathbb{R})$.
Martone-Zhang~\cite{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} proved that a certain class of such representations, called \emph{positively ratioed}, induce curve functionals dual to geodesic currents, and furthermore showed they satisfy smoothing. A wider class of \emph{$[P_1]$-Anosov} representations~\cite[Corollary~4.6]{KP22:eigenvalue}, encompassing those which are positively ratioed, induce curve length functionals arising from actions of $\pi_1(S)$ on a Gromov hyperbolic space with bounded backtracking~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{CT24:Invariant}. Hence, by the above forthcoming work, they also satisfy quasi-smoothing. In fact, they satisfy smoothing if and only if they are positively ratioed. 
        \item  Translation lengths associated to biautomatic structures. Roughly speaking, an automatic structure is a combinatorial structure associated to a group that furnishes it with typical features enjoyed by hyperbolic groups, such as solvable conjugacy or word problems. A biautomatic structure is an automatic structure plus further astringent conditions that describe how the automatic structure interacts with the geometry of its Cayley graph. We commend the reader to~\cite[Section~2.2]{HV22:Biautomaticity} for details.
    Given a biautomatic structure $\mathcal{L}$ on a surface group $\pi_1(\Sigma)$, there is a notion of translation length $\| \cdot \|_{\mathcal{L}}$ coming from its defining regular language. Hughes-Valiunas~\cite[Proposition~4.2]{HV22:Biautomaticity} have shown these translation lengths satisfy quasi-smoothing and used this to construct a new example of hierarchically hyperbolic group that is not biautomatic. These structures encompass examples of word length in the previous item, so they do not satisfy smoothing in general.
    \item Square root of self-intersection number satisfies stability,
      homogeneity and smoothing, but does not satisfy additivity or
      even convex union (see~\cite[Example~1.12]{MGT21:Smoothings}).
      This is an example of a curve functional which is not in
      $\QConvex(S)$, even though it does extend to geodesic currents
      by work of Bonahon~\cite{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}.
\item Translation lengths associated to embedded graphs on a surface whose actions are not proper \cite[Proposition 11]{Bonahon91:CurrentsGroups} do not satisfy quasi-smoothing in general and, moreover, do not extend continuously to geodesic currents. 
Suppose $G$ is acting on a geodesic space $X$ by isometries, and let $\ell$ be the stable length of the action. Suppose $\ell$ is non-trivial. Let $N$ be an infinite normal subgroup of $G=\pi_1(S)$ so that $\ell(b)=0$ for all $b \in N$. Let $a \in G$, and fix $b\in N$. Then $\frac{1}{2n} a^n b a^{-n} b$ converges to $a$ in the weak$^*$ topology, but since $a^n b a^{-n} b\in N$, we have $\ell(a^n b a^{-n} b)=0$.
Hence, $\ell$ cannot extend continuously. In fact, $\ell$ does not satisfy smoothing. By the above argument,  the endpoints of elements axes of elements of $N$ are dense in the space of geodesics of $G$. Hence, for a given $a \in G$ with $\ell(a)>0$, we can find $b \in N$ so that $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ cross.

If smoothing was satisfied, we would have, by Proposition~\ref{prop:join-split-geom}, Equation~\ref{eq:cross-cancel},
\[
\ell(ba^n ba^{-n}) \geq \ell(a^{2n})
\]
which is violated, since the left hand side is $0$.
On the other hand, the right hand side is not zero.
\item Lorentzian length of an affine action of a Fuchsian
  group~\cite{GM00:FlatLorentzian} doesn't satisfy quasi-smoothing,
  since it satisfies homogeneity, stability, and additivity~\cite[p.
  1053]{GML09:ProperAffine} but it takes both positive and negative
  values. Nevertheless, it extends continuously to geodesic currents,
  as shown in~\cite[6.1]{GML09:ProperAffine}. Strictly speaking, the
  paper deals with the case of surfaces with boundary, but the same
  arguments go through for closed surfaces, by~\cite{Lab01:Diffusion}.
\end{enumerate}

For an example of a curve functional that does not extend continuously to geodesic currents (and thus does not satisfy smoothing, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}), we have the following.
\begin{example}\label{ex:linEL}
Let $\sqrt{EL}$ denote the (square root of) extremal length.
In \cite[Examples]{MGT21:Smoothings} the authors proved that $\sqrt{EL}$ satisfies stability, homogeneity, smoothing, and convex union. By  \cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}, $\sqrt{EL}$ extends continuously to a continuous on geodesic currents.
$\sqrt{EL}$ does not satisfy additivity, so it is not dual to a geodesic current. However, $\sqrt{EL}$, as a function on connected curves can be linearly extended to a function $G$ on (weighted) multi-curves, 
(which is thus not equal to $\sqrt{EL}$ on multi-curves). $G$ cannot extend continuously to currents, since we know $G=\sqrt{EL}$ on (weighted) connected curves, which are dense in the space of geodesic currents.
Thus, the purported continuous extension $\overline{G}$ would have to satisfy $\overline{G}=\sqrt{EL}$ on all geodesic currents, hence on all multi-curves, a contradiction.
It follows from this that $G$ cannot satisfy smoothing, since it trivially satisfies convex union (in fact, additivity),
homogeneity and stability.  One can also see that, specifically, it does not satisfy connected smoothing.
Indeed, let $F=\sqrt{EL}$. For a crossing pair $(a,b)$, we have
\[
F(a + b) \geq F(ab).
\] 
Then, by disconnected smoothing of $F$, we have
\[
G(a+b)=G(a) + G(b)=F(a) + F(b) \geq F(a+b) \geq F(ab)=G(ab)
\]
where we used the fact that $F$ and $G$ take the same values on closed curves; hence,
$G$ satisfies disconnected smoothing.
This leads to the conclusion that $G$ cannot satisfy connected smoothing, i.e. there must be some pair of $(a,b)$ parallel so that
$G(ab) < G(a) + G(b)$. This is indeed the case. For an example, consider the extremal length of a figure-eight curve in a genus two surface $S$. Consider the pair of pants $P$ bounding $ab$, and let us think of it as obtained by gluing adjacent sides in a Swiss cross. The extremal metric has been computed numerically in~\cite[Page~35]{HZ20:SwissCross} as a function of the width $w$ of the Swiss cross. If the width is chosen to be a fourth of the total size of the Swiss cross, and the normalization $w=1$ is chosen, the extremal length of the figure-eight can be computed to be 2.4251
 (see Table in \cite[Page~36]{HZ20:SwissCross}, row $t=4$), hence its square root is $G_P(ab) \simeq 1.557273$. On the other hand, the length of each one of the resulting smoothings is $3$, while the area of the surface with the extremal metric associated to $ab$ is computed as $A=6.5977$. 
 
 Hence, $G_{S}(a) + G_S(b) =2G_S(a)=\sqrt{2}G_P(a)$ is lower bounded by $\simeq 1.6517335$, showing the inequality
 \[
 G_S(ab) < G_P(ab) < G_S(a) + G_S(b) = 2G_S(a)=\sqrt{2} G_P(a).
 \]
\end{example}



\begin{remark}
Observe that we do not require in the hypotheses that the curve
functional $f \in \AConvex(S)$ is non-negative: in fact it follows
from the other conditions (see Proposition~\ref{prop:curve-positive}).
In general, curve functionals need not be positive. For example, the signed
  Lorentzian length or Margulis invariant~\cite{GM00:FlatLorentzian})
  can take either positive or negative values. However, more
  generally, any curve functional
  in $\QConvex(S)$ is positive (see Proposition~\ref{prop:curve-positive2}). 
\end{remark}
