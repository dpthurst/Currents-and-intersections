\section{Curve functionals: properties, general context and examples}
\label{sec:functionals}

In this section we state the main technical hypotheses that we require
of our curve functionals, including examples and non-examples for the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.
In particular, we relate the results of this paper to our previous
work~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings}.
Throughout, $S$ will be a closed, orientable surface of genus at least~$2$.

\subsection{Curve functionals and their properties}
\label{subsec:mainhypotheses}

In our constructions, it will be crucial to work in the setting of curves.

\begin{definition}[multi-curve]\label{def:multi-curve}  
A \emph{concrete multi-curve} $\gamma$ on a surface $S$ is a smooth
oriented 1-manifold without boundary $X(\gamma)$ together with a continuous map
(also called $\gamma$) from $X(\gamma)$
into~$S$. $X(\gamma)$ is not necessarily connected.
We say that $\gamma$ is \emph{trivial} if it is
homotopic to a constant map.
Two concrete multi-curves $\gamma$ and~$\gamma'$ are \emph{equivalent} if
they are related by a sequence of the following moves:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{homotopy} within the space of all maps from $X(\gamma)$ to~$S$;
    \item \emph{orientation-preserving reparametrization} of $X(\gamma)$;
      and
    \item \emph{dropping} trivial components.
\end{itemize}
The equivalence class of $\gamma$ is denoted by~$[\gamma]$, and we will
call it a \emph{multi-curve}.
If $X(\gamma)$ is connected, we will call $\gamma$ a \emph{concrete curve}.
A concrete multi-curve~$\gamma$ is \emph{simple} if $\gamma$ is injective, and a multi-curve is simple if it has a concrete representative
that is simple.
We write $\Curves^+(S)$ for the space of all multi-curves.
Most of the multi-curves we consider will be oriented, as above. There
is a corresponding unoriented notion, which we call an
\emph{unoriented multi-curve}. This is defined as above, except that
the 1-manifold $X(\gamma)$ is taken without orientation, and the
equivalence relation is generated by reparameterizations that are not
required to preserve orientation.
\end{definition}


  \begin{notation*}
    From now on, for $a, b\in \pi_1(S)$, we write $[a]$ for the
    conjugacy class of~$a$, or, equivalently, the free homotopy class
    of the curve represented by $a$, and
    write $[a][b]$ to denote the multi-curve $[a] \cup [b]$.
  \end{notation*} 

At times we will allow multi-curves to have weights.

\begin{definition}[weighted multi-curve]\label{def:weighted-multi-curve}
  A \emph{weighted multi-curve} $C=\bigcup_i a_i C_i$ is a multi-curve in
  which each connected component is given a non-negative real
  coefficient~$a_i$. If coefficients are not specified, they are~$1$.
  We add further moves to the equivalence
  relation:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{merging} two parallel components and
    adding their weights; and
  \item \emph{nullifying}, deleting a
    component with weight~$0$.
  \end{itemize}
  For
  instance, $C \cup C$ is equivalent to $2C$.
\end{definition}

Oriented multi-curves are more natural from a group-theoretic
perspective, appearing in
the study of group actions on real trees
(Section~\ref{sec:skora}) and
of cross-ratios
(Section~\ref{sec:crossratios}).
Moreover, to verify smoothing for curve functionals that do not arise from natural length metrics on the surface, it is convenient to work in terms of the relative position of pairs of hyperbolic elements in $\pi_1(S)$, where orientation is essential (see Section~\ref{subsec:triality} and compare Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} with Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}).

The geometric intersection number will play a central role throughout.
The intersection number $i(C,D)$ of two unoriented multi-curves is
defined to be the minimal number of intersections among all
representatives. If $C$ and $D$ are oriented,
the
intersection number is defined by forgetting orientations. See also
Definition~\ref{def:intersection_currents}.

This gives an important example of curve functionals, $i(\mu, \cdot) \colon
\Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$, for a given oriented multi-curve or,
more generally, oriented geodesic current $\mu$.

As explained in Appendix~\ref{sec:asymmetric}, there is an asymmetric
variant of intersection number for curves which only record
crossings of a given orientation. For such notions, the analogue of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} fails,
and on the other hand curves are not determined by their asymmetric
intersection number functionals,
so we will not use it in the bulk
of this paper.

A curve functional that does not depend
on orientation may equivalently be viewed as a functional on
unoriented multi-curves. Most of our examples will be of this form.


\begin{definition}\label{def:properties}
Let $S$ be a \emph{closed} surface, i.e., an orientable compact topological surface without boundary, and let
$f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \bbR$ be a function on the space of oriented multi-curves.
We will refer to such an $f$ as a \emph{curve functional}.
(The term ``functional'' indicates that $f$ takes scalar values; it is not assumed to be linear, although in the applications of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} it will be additive.)
We introduce several properties that $f$ may satisfy.

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Symmetry:}
If $C$ and $C'$ are oriented multi-curves with the same underlying unoriented multi-curve, then
\begin{equation}
f(C)=f(C').
\label{eq:symmetry}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Oriented smoothing:}
Let $C$ be an curve on $S$, and let $x$ be an essential crossing of $C$.
Let $C'$ denote the oriented smoothing of $C$ at $x$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:or-smoothing}
f\!\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right)
\;\ge\;
f\!\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right).
\end{equation}
(An essential crossing is one that cannot be removed by
homotopy; see Section~\ref{subsec:crossings}.)

\item \textbf{Additivity:}
For curves $C_1$ and $C_2$ on $S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:union-additive}
f(C_1 \cup C_2) = f(C_1) + f(C_2).
\end{equation}

\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The oriented smoothing paper was called just ``smoothing'' in our prior work
\cite[Eq.~(1.3)]{MGT21:Smoothings}. In the present paper,
we distinguish this notions from its unoriented counterpart, and
similarly for quasi-smoothing (see below).
Oriented smoothing condition can be further divided into three
types; see
Definition~\ref{def:smoothing_types}.  Accordingly, we will
say $f$ satisfies a particular smoothing property if $f$ is
non-increasing under such a type of smoothing.

\begin{lemma}
  A curve functional $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies
  oriented smoothing iff the following three families of
  inequalities  are satisfied.
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Oriented disconnected smoothing:}
For any pair of elements $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$ whose hyperbolic axes intersect,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:or-disconn-smoothing}
f([a][b]) \ge f([ab]).
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Oriented connected smoothing:}
For any pair of elements $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$ whose hyperbolic axes are parallel
(i.e., disjoint and coherently oriented),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:or-conn-smoothing}
f([ab]) \ge f([a][b]).
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Power smoothing:}
For any element $a \in \pi_1(S)$ and integers $n,m \ge 1$,
\begin{equation}
f([a^{n+m}]) \ge f([a^m][a^n]).
\label{eq:power_smoothing}
\end{equation}

\end{itemize}
\label{lem:oriented_smoothing_lemma_top_alg}
\end{lemma}




Lemma~\ref{lem:oriented_smoothing_lemma_top_alg}
is proved in
Section~\ref{subsec:triality}.

\begin{definition}
  For $C$ an multi-curve,  $C^n$ is the multi-curve with as many components as~$C$, in which each
  component of~$C$ is covered by an $n$-fold cover. That is, if $C =
  [g]$ for
  $g \in \pi_1(S,x)$, then $C^n = [g^n]$.
  On the other hand, $nC$ is the multi-curve
  that consists of $n$~parallel
  copies of~$C$ (so with $n$ times as many components) or,
  in the context of weighted multi-curves,
  with weights multiplied by~$n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:stable}
  Let $f$ be a curve
  functional. We define some properties related to powers.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Stability:} For all $n \ge 0$ and all multi-curves~$C$,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:stable}
      f(C^n) = f(nC).
    \end{equation}%
  \item \textbf{Homogeneity:} for all $n \ge 0$ and all multi-curves~$C$,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:homogeneous}
      f(nC) = nf(C).
    \end{equation}
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

It is immediate that if $f$ is additive, then it is homogeneous and
stability implies power smoothing

We emphasize that oriented connected smoothing does not imply oriented
disconnected smoothing, nor vice versa.
For instance, the curve functional $f$ assigning to a curve its number
of connected components satisfies oriented disconnected smoothing but
not oriented connected smoothing. Its negative satisfies oriented
connected smoothing but not oriented disconnected smoothing.
We can also construct additive and
stable functionals exhibiting only one of these two smoothing
properties;
see Example~\ref{ex:linEL}.
There also exist functionals that satisfy stability and both varieties
of smoothing but
fail additivity; see
Example~\ref{examp:el}.

On the other hand, Proposition~\ref{prop:power_disconnected_imply_stable} shows that
smoothing implies stability. 

Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} considers curve functions on the space of unoriented multi-curves $\Curves(S)$.
When performing a smoothing for an oriented multi-curve, one could
also choose to reglue in the incoherent orientation. In this case,
there two possible (incoherent) orientations to equip the resulting
curve with. See also Section~\ref{subsec:crossings}.

\begin{definition}
  Let $f$ be an unoriented curve functional, or equivalently a curve
  functional invariant under symmetry. We define the most important
  property for this paper.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Smoothing:} If $C$ is an unoriented closed curve, and
    $C'$ either of the two smoothings, then $f(C) \geq f(C')$,
    Schematically, we have
     \[
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) \ge \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right), f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)\right\}.
    \]
  \item \textbf{Max-smoothing:} As above, but the inequality is
    strengthened to an equality.
  \end{itemize}
\label{def:smoothing_and_max}
\end{definition}

Like oriented smoothing, general smoothing and max-smoothing admit
algebraic reformulations.
For this it is convenient to work with oriented curves.
Given a curve functional on unoriented multi-curves
$f \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$, there is a canonical extension to
oriented multi-curves defined by forgetting orientation:
\[
f^+(C) \coloneqq f(\overline{C}),
\]
where $\overline{C}$ denotes the underlying unoriented multi-curve.
Conversely, if $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ is symmetric, then it induces a functional on unoriented multi-curves
\[
\overline{f} \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}, 
\qquad
\overline{f}(\overline{C}) \coloneqq f(C),
\]
where $C$ is any oriented representative of the unoriented
multi-curve~$\overline{C}$.


\begin{definition}
\label{def:smoothing_for_oriented}
  We define properties for an \emph{oriented} curve functional
  $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Disconnected smoothing:} For any pair of
  elements $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$ whose hyperbolic axes intersect, we have 
  \[ f([a] [b]) \geq \max\,\{\,f([ab]), f([aB])\,\}. \]
\item \textbf{Connected smoothing:} For any pair of elements $a,b \in
  \pi_1(S)$ whose hyperbolic axes are parallel, we have
  \[ f([ab]) \geq \max\,\{\,f([aB]), f([a] [b])\,\}. \]
 \item \textbf{Power smoothing:} For any element $a \in \pi_1(S)$, and any
  integers $n,m\geq 1$, we have
\begin{equation} f([a^{n+m}]) \geq f([a^{ m}][a^{ n}]).\label{eq:power_smoothing_unoriented}\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
All of the above have corresponding max-smoothing
properties, strengthening the inequality to an equality.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:smoothing-equiv}
  Let $f \colon \Curves^+(S)\to \mathbb{R}$ be a symmetric oriented curve
  functional. Then $f$ satisfies smoothing iff if it satisfies
  disconnected smoothing, connected smoothing, and power smoothing.
  Likewise, $f$ satisfies max-smoothing iff it satisfies disconnected
  max-smoothing, connected max-smoothing, and stability.
\label{lem:smoothing_lemma_top_alg}
\end{lemma}

We refer the reader to Sections~\ref{subsec:crossings}
and~\ref{subsec:triality} for precise
definitions and proofs of
Lemma \ref{lem:smoothing_lemma_top_alg}
and~\ref{lem:oriented_smoothing_lemma_top_alg}. See in particular
Proposition~\ref{prop:crossings-triality}.

\subsection{General context and examples}

To put the present work in the broader context of our previous
work~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings}, we recall some weakenings of
the properties above.
\begin{definition} Let $f$ be a curve functional. We define the
  following properties.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Oriented quasi-smoothing:} There is a constant $K\ge 0$ with the
      following property. Let $C$~be an curve on~$S$, and
    let $x$~be an essential crossing of~$C$. Let $C'$ be the oriented
    smoothing of~$C$ at~$x$. Then $f(C) \ge f(C')-K$. Schematically, we
    have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:or-quasismoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right) \ge f\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right) -K.
    \end{equation}
    If $K=0$, we recover the oriented smoothing property,
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:or-smoothing}.
\item \textbf{Quasi-smoothing:} There is a constant $K \ge 0$ so that,
  if $C$ is an unoriented closed curve and $C'$ is either of the two
  crossings, we have $f(C) \ge f(C') - K$. Schematically,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:quasismoothing}
    f\!\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)
    \ge
    \max\!\left\{
      f\!\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),
      f\!\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)
    \right\} - K
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Convex union:} Let $C_1$ and~$C_2$ be two curves
    on~$S$. Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:union-convex}
      f(C_1 \cup C_2) \le f(C_1) + f(C_2).
    \end{equation}
    If Eq.~\eqref{eq:union-convex} is strengthened to an equality, we recover additivity (Eq.~\eqref{eq:union-additive}).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Oriented and unoriented quasi-smoothing also have algebraic
formulations parallel to Lemmas \ref{lem:smoothing-equiv}
and~\ref{lem:smoothing_lemma_top_alg}.

\begin{definition}[Variations on convex curve functionals]
  \label{def:functional-variations}
  The space of \emph{convex curve functionals}, denoted $\Convex(S)$,
  is the set of functionals on unoriented multi-curves satisfying smoothing,
  convex union, stability, and homogeneity. We may equivalently think
  of these as functionals on multi-curves that satisfy the symmetry
  property.
  \DMGtodo{added this}
  We use the qualifier \emph{convex} because these functions extend continuously to the space of geodesic currents and, under a slight strengthening of the smoothing hypothesis, their restrictions to train-track charts are convex in the corresponding train-track coordinates (see Section~\ref{sec:convexity_charts}).

  For the qualifier $A$, the \emph{additive} functionals, replace the
  condition of convex union~\eqref{eq:union-convex} with
  additivity~\eqref{eq:union-additive}.

  For the qualifier $Q$, the \emph{quasi-convex} functionals, replace
  the condition of smoothing~\eqref{eq:smoothing} with
  quasi-smoothing~\eqref{eq:quasismoothing}.
  We can add a subscript, like $\QConvex_K(S)$, to
  indicate a fixed value of~$K$.

  For the qualifier ${}^+$, the \emph{oriented} functionals, we
  consider instead functions on oriented multi-curves. The smoothing
  property~\eqref{eq:smoothing} is replaced by the weaker oriented
  smoothing property~\eqref{eq:or-smoothing}, and likewise, if relevant,
  quasi-smoothing~\eqref{eq:quasismoothing} is replaced by oriented
  quasi-smoothing~\eqref{eq:or-quasismoothing}.

  Finally, the space $\Cont(S)$ is the much larger space of functions on the
  space of unoriented currents $\Curr(S)$ that are \emph{continuous}
  and \emph{homogeneous}: for $a \ge 0$, we have
  $f(a\mu) = a f(\mu)$.
  Here, the qualifiers $A$ and ${}^+$ make sense:
  $\Cont^+(S)$ is the larger space of homogeneous continuous functions on oriented
  geodesic currents, and $\ACont(S)$ is the space of functions that
  are linear on segments contained in the convex set $\Curr(S)$.
\end{definition}

All the inclusions in Figure~\ref{fig:curve-functionals} are immediate
from the definitions, with the
exception of the inclusions into $\Cont(S)$ and $\Cont^+(S)$, which
was the main result of our previous work.

\begin{theorem}{\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}}\label{thm:convex}
   Let $f \in \QConvex^+(S)$. Then there is a unique continuous homogeneous function
  $\bar{f} \colon \Curr^+(S) \to \bbR$ that extends~$f$.
\end{theorem}

For some motivation for the terminology, if we strengthen the
smoothing assumption for a
functional in $\Convex(S)$ to multi-smoothing, then it induces
a convex function in every train-track coordinate chart in the space
of measured laminations. See Sections~\ref{subsec:multismoothing}
and~\ref{sec:convexity_charts}.

\subsection{Examples}
\label{subsec:examples}
The properties of symmetry,
smoothing, additivity, and stability---defining the space
$\AConvex(S)$---are satisfied by every intersection functional
\[
i(\mu,\cdot) \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R},
\]
where $\mu$ is a geodesic current. This was established
in~\cite[Section~4]{MGT21:Smoothings} by first verifying the
properties for multi-curves and then extending to arbitrary currents
using the density of weighted curves together with the continuity and
bilinearity of the intersection pairing. (Smoothing is the only
non-obvious property.)
For completeness, we provide an alternative proof in
Appendix~\ref{app:necessary}, more closely aligned with the arguments
in this paper
involving lifts to the universal cover.

Many natural notions of ``length'' satisfy the conditions of
$\AConvex(S)$ and were previously known
to extend continuously to geodesic currents, and furthermore have dual
geodesic currents. We list several such examples below, along with the
appropriate references. These are all subsumed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}; we give pointers to the particular
section discussing the examples in more detail.

\begin{enumerate}
   \item Intersection number with a fixed geodesic current
     \cite[Proposition~4.5]{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}
     (Appendix~\ref{app:necessary}).
   \item \label{item:metric-hyperbolic}\label{item:metric-first}
     Length with respect to a fixed hyperbolic metric
      \cite[Proposition~14]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}
      (Section~\ref{subsec:liouville}).
    \item More generally, length w.r.t.\ negatively curved Riemannian
      metrics \cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}
      (Section~\ref{subsec:length-metrics} for this and the next
      several examples).
    \item More generally, length w.r.t.\ non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
\cite[Theorem~A]{CFF92:RigidityNonPosCurvedRiem}.
\item Length w.r.t.\ to a negatively curved Riemannian metric with
  conical singularities of angle $\geq 2\pi$
  \cite[Theorem~A]{HP97:RigidityNegCurvedCone}.
\item Length w.r.t.\ a singular Euclidean metric coming from quadratic
  differentials \cite[Lemma~9]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}.
\item \label{item:metric-last}
  More generally, length w.r.t.\ a non-positively curved Euclidean
  cone metric of angle $\geq 2\pi$
  \cite[Proposition~3.3]{BL17:RigidityFlat},
  \cite{Con18:MarkedNonpos}. As an important case, see the recent construction of dual currents for $\frac{1}{n}$-translation structures~\cite[Definition~2.2]{PS26:1ntranslation}.
\item \label{item:simple-gen}
  Word length w.r.t.\ simple generating sets for $\pi_1(S)$
  \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Erlandsson:WordLength}
  (Section~\ref{subsec:embedded_graphs}).
\item \label{item:positively-ratioed}
  Lengths associated to Anosov representations induced by positive
  generalized cross-ratios. Specifically,
  Martone-Zhang \cite[Proposition~2.24]{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} showed that a family of representations equipped with positive H\"older generalized
cross-ratios and termed \emph{positively ratioed} induce curve
functionals, via their \emph{periods}, dual to geodesic currents. They further established that
these functionals satisfy connected smoothing~\cite[Proposition~4.5]{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed}.
Burger-Iozzi-Parreau-Pozzetti later extended their construction to
generalized cross-ratios that need not be continuous
\cite[Theorem~1.6]{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}
(Section~\ref{sec:crossratios}).
\end{enumerate}

We have generalizations of many of these. In
Section~\ref{subsec:length-metrics}, we further generalize items
\ref{item:metric-first}--~\ref{item:metric-last} to show than any curve length arising from a length
(pseudo)metric structure on~$S$ induces a dual geodesic current. In
Section~\ref{subsec:embedded_graphs} we show that
item~\ref{item:simple-gen} admits a further generalization to stable lengths associated
to embedded graphs on the surface.
Theorem~\ref{mainthm:crossratio},
proved in Section~\ref{sec:questions}, gives an alternate proof
of item~\ref{item:positively-ratioed} using only the periods and not
the cross-ratio values on general boxes.

\subsection{Non-examples}
\label{subsec:non-examples}

In order to illustrate the complexity of the problem,
we now show examples of many natural functions on curves that do
\emph{not} satisfy the $\AConvex$ properties, even though many of them
still extend continuously to geodesic currents. Unless stated
otherwise, we give
the largest class the example is contained in.

\begin{example}[Directed graphs]
  \label{examp:embedded-dir-graph}
  Generalizing item~\ref{item:simple-gen} in
  Section~\ref{subsec:examples}, we can consider length with
  respect to embedded \emph{directed} graphs, giving examples in
  $\bm{\AConvex^+(S)}$. See Appendix~\ref{sec:asymmetric}, expanding on
  \cite[Section~4.6]{MGT21:Smoothings}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Non-embedded graphs]
  \label{examp:non-emb-graph}
  Continuing from the previous example, we can consider length of
  curves with respect to a graph that need not be embedded.
  An important special case is when the graph has a single vertex, in
  which case we are looking at word length with respect to an
  arbitrary (not simple) generating set of $\pi_1(S)$. Such word need
  not satisfy smoothing or stability
  (see~\cite[Example~4.10]{MGT21:Smoothings},
  \cite[Theorem~1.5]{EPS20:CountingCurves},
  \cite{Erlandsson:WordLength}). The associated stable length
  satisfies quasi-smoothing bot
  not necessarily smoothing
  (see~\cite[Example~4.10]{MGT21:Smoothings}
  and~\cite[Theorem~B]{MGT21:Smoothings}), thus giving examples in
  $\bm{\AQConvex(S)}$ or $\bm{\AQConvex^+(S)}$ depending on
  whether the graph is
  undirected or directed, or correspondingly whether a generating
  set for $\pi_1(S)$ is symmetric
  under inverse or not.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Extremal length]
  \label{examp:el}
  The square root of extremal length w.r.t.\ a fixed conformal
  structure on~$S$ satisfies symmetry, smoothing, stability, convex
  union, homogeneity, but not additivity
  \cite[Section~4.8]{MGT21:Smoothings} and therefore provides an example in
  $\bm{\Convex(S)}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Extremal length for graphs]
  \label{examp:el-graph}
  Continuing from the previous examples, there is a version of
  extremal length with respect to a graph~$\cG$ generating $\pi_1(S)$;
  see \cite[Section~4.9]{MGT21:Smoothings}, following
  \cite{Thurston19:Elastic}. As explained there, the
  resulting functionals $\sqrt{\EL}(C; \cG)$ give examples in
  $\bm{\Convex(S)}$ or
  $\bm{\QConvex(S)}$
  depending on whether $\cG$ is embedded or not \cite[Propositions 4.22
  and~4.23]{MGT21:Smoothings}. (For the case of non-embedded graphs we
  need to stabilize, as in
  Example~\ref{examp:non-emb-graph}.) We can also allow $\cG$ to be a
  directed graph generating $\pi_1(S)$; the same proofs show
  the stabilization of $\sqrt{\EL}$ is in $\bm{\Convex^+(S)}$ or
  $\bm{\QConvex^+(S)}$.

  This example can be further generalized to energies $E_p(C; \cG)$,
  depending on a parameter $1 \le p \le \infty$,
  with the same properties for $p < \infty$
  \cite[Proposition~4.26]{MGT21:Smoothings}.
\end{example}

Examples~\ref{examp:embedded-dir-graph}, \ref{examp:non-emb-graph},
and~\ref{examp:el-graph} give a variety of examples of different types
of functionals:
\[
  \left\{ \begin{matrix} \mathrm{length} \\ \sqrt{\EL} \end{matrix} \right\}
  \text{ with respect to a(n) }
  \left\{ \begin{matrix} \mathrm{embedded} \\ \mathrm{general} \end{matrix} \right\}
  \left\{ \begin{matrix} \mathrm{undirected} \\ \mathrm{directed} \end{matrix} \right\}
  \text{ graph on $S$ generating $\pi_1(S)$}
\]
gives a curve functional in
\[
  \left\{ \begin{matrix} \mathrm{A} \\ \emptyset \end{matrix} \right\}
  \left\{ \begin{matrix} \emptyset \\ \mathrm{Q} \end{matrix} \right\}
  \Convex
  \left\{ \begin{matrix} \emptyset \\ {}^+ \end{matrix} \right\}
  (S).
\]
In particular this shows that all the inclusions in
Figure~\ref{fig:curve-functionals} are proper inclusions, except for
those involving the space $\Cont(S)$ or a variant thereof.
It is easy to see those inclusions are also proper, and we give some
examples below.

\begin{example}[Quasi-Fuchsian representations]
  \label{examp:qf}
A quasi-Fuchsian representation $\rho \colon \pi_1(S) \to
\PSL(2,\mathbb{C})$ is a
conjugate of Fuchsian group by a (properly normalized) quasi-conformal
self-homeomorphism of the 2-sphere $f_{\rho}$
\cite[Definition~6.12.2]{Hub06:Vol1}.
Stable lengths associated to
quasi-Fuchsian representations into $\PSL(2,\bbC)$ satisfy quasi-smoothing
\cite[Proposition~4.11]{MGT21:Smoothings}, and satisfy strict
smoothing if and only if the representation is Fuchsian
(\cite[Theorem~A]{FF22:QF}, \cite[Proposition~1.8]{BR24:Cubulations}).
These examples are in $\bm{\AQConvex_K(S)}$ for some $K > 0$.
\end{example}

Recall that the
\emph{quasi-Fuchsian} constant of $\rho$ is the smallest quasi-conformal
constant for~$f_{\rho}$.
\begin{question}\label{quest:qf-const}
  Let $\rho$ be a
quasi-Fuchsian representation, and let $K>0$ be the \emph{quasi-smoothing
constant}, i.e., the minimal $K>0$ so that $\ell_{\rho} \in \AQConvex_K(S)$. 
Does $K \to \infty$ as the quasi-Fuchsian constant of~$\rho$ goes to~$\infty$?
\end{question} 

\begin{example}[Fibered 3-manifolds]
  \label{examp:fibered}
  Continuing from Example~\ref{examp:qf},
  lengths associated to more general discrete faithful
  representations into $\PSL(2,\bbC)$ need \emph{not} extend continuously to
  currents, as we can see explicitly for mapping tori. Let $\phi$ be a
  pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism of a closed surface~$S$, and let
  $M_\phi$ denote the associated hyperbolic mapping torus. Let
  $\ell_{M_\phi}(\gamma)$ denote the translation length of
  $\gamma\in\pi_1(S)$. Since $M_\phi$ is closed
  hyperbolic, there exists $\epsilon>0$ such that
  $\ell_{M_\phi}(\gamma)\geq \epsilon$ for every non-trivial~$\gamma$.
  
  But $\ell_{M_\phi}(\phi^n\gamma)=\ell_{M_\phi}(\gamma)$ for all
  $n\in\mathbb Z$; if $\ell_{M_\phi}$ extended continuously to
  currents, we would get a contradiction to
  Proposition~\ref{prop:pos-systole} below.
  Thus this example is \textbf{not in $\bm{\Cont(S)}$}.
  In particular,
  by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}, $\ell_{M_\phi}(\gamma)$
  cannot satisfy $K$-quasi-smoothing for any $K>0$.

  This $\PSL(2,\bbC)$
  representation corresponding to $M_\phi$ is a limit of degenerating
  quasi-Fuchsian
  representations. Even though the limit is not convex cocompact, it is
  still discrete
  and faithful.
\end{example}


Here we used the following standard fact, useful in curve
counting applications (see \cite[Corollary~3.8]{ES22:GeodesicCount}).
We include it for completeness.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:pos-systole}
  If $f \in \Cont^+(S)$ has positive systole, then for each~$K$, there are
  only finitely many oriented multi-curves $C$ so that $f(C) \le K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a point $X \in \Teich(S)$.
Consider the function $h \colon \mathbb{P}\Curr(X) \to \mathbb{R}$
given by $[\mu] \mapsto \frac{f(\mu)}{\ell_X(\mu)},$ which is
well-defined, continuous and strictly positive by the assumptions.
Since $\mathbb{P}\Curr(S)$ is compact
(see Proposition~\ref{prop:local_compactness}), there exist positive
constants $m,M>0$ depending on $X,f$ so that
\[
m \leq \frac{f(\mu)}{\ell_X(\mu)} \leq M
\]
for every $\mu \in \Curr(S)$.
Since given $K>0$, there are finitely many multi-curves of $X$-hyperbolic length at most $Km$, it follows there are finitely many multi-curves $C$ with $f(C) \leq K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Higher rank length functions]
  \label{examp:higher-rank}
Example~\ref{examp:qf} fits into the broader framework of length
functions arising from higher-rank representations of
$\pi_1(S)$ into a connected real semisimple algebraic group~ $\mathbb{G}$ of
non-compact type, such as $\SL(n,\mathbb{R})$.
Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a Cartan subspace with an associated set of simple restricted
roots $\Pi$, and let $\mathfrak{a}^+ \subset \mathfrak{a}$ be the Weyl chamber cone. Given $\Theta\subset\Pi$, let
$\mathfrak{a}_\Theta \coloneqq \bigcap_{\alpha \in \Pi-\Theta} \ker
\alpha \subset\mathfrak{a}$, let $p_\Theta \colon \mathfrak{a} \to
\mathfrak{a}_\Theta$ be the natural projection, and let
$P_\Theta<\mathbb{G}$ be the
parabolic subgroup. Let $\lambda \colon \mathbb{G} \to \mathfrak{a}^+$ denote the vector of generalized eigenvalues, and $\lambda_\Theta \coloneqq p_\Theta \circ \lambda$.
If
$\rho\colon\pi_1(S)\to G$ is $P_\Theta$-Anosov, we define the
\emph{Benoist limit cone} $\mathcal{L}_\Theta^\rho$ as the smallest closed cone containing all the elements $\lambda_\Theta(\rho(\gamma))$ for $\gamma \in \pi_1(S)$.
For every 
linear functional
$\varphi\in\mathfrak{a}^*$ that is positive on $\mathcal{L}_\Theta^\rho$, the function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:higher-rank-funct}
\varphi\circ\lambda_\Theta\circ\rho\colon\pi_1(S)\to\mathbb{R}
\end{equation}
extends continuously to the space of oriented geodesic currents
by \cite[Appendix~A]{JMG26:Horoboundary}
and \cite[Section~7]{CMGR26:GreenMetrics}, giving natural examples of
curve functionals in $\bm{\ACont^+(S)}$ (and maybe in a subspace).
% (For Eq.~\eqref{eq:higher-rank-funct} to
% extend continuously, it
% suffices that $\varphi$ is positive on the Benoist limit
% cone~$\mathcal{L}_\Theta^\rho \subset \mathfrak{a}_\Theta^+$.)

If $\Theta$ is symmetric with respect to the natural Cartan involution, Martone-Zhang
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed} show
that Eq.~\eqref{eq:higher-rank-funct}
satisfies full smoothing for $\varphi=\omega_\alpha$, $\alpha \in
\Theta$
(where $\omega_\alpha$ denotes the fundamental weight of $\alpha$) if
and only
$\rho$ is $\Theta$-positively ratioed
(see
item~\ref{item:positively-ratioed} in Section~\ref{subsec:examples}).
Hence, for non-positively-ratioed representations we get examples that
are in $\ACont(S)$ but not in
$\AConvex(S)$.

These non-positively-ratioed examples generalize
Example~\ref{examp:qf} on
quasi-Fuchsian representations, though in the broader context it is
not clear whether or not these functionals are in the
intermediate space
$\AQConvex(S)$.

When $\mathbb{G}$ is the non-semisimple group $\SO(n+1,n) \ltimes \bbR^{2n+1}$, the signed
Lorentzian length associated to a \emph{Margulis space time} (i.e.,
representations $\rho$ where $\rho(\Gamma)$ acts properly on
$\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}$) \cite{GM00:FlatLorentzian} is a natural example
of asymmetric curve functional satisfying stability, additivity
and extending continuously to geodesic
currents \cite[Section~6.1]{GML09:ProperAffine}\cite{Lab01:Diffusion}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Biautomatic structures]
\label{examp:biautomatic}
Automatic structures provide regular-language normal forms for group
elements and yield efficient solutions to the word problem. Many
geometrically defined groups, including hyperbolic groups, admit such
structures. A biautomatic structure satisfies additional
fellow-traveling properties that ensure compatibility with both left
and right multiplication and imply, in particular, solvability of the
conjugacy problem (see~\cite[Section~2.2]{HV24:Biautomaticity}).

Given a biautomatic structure $\mathcal{L}$ on a surface group
$\pi_1(S)$, one obtains an associated translation length
$\norm{\cdot}_{\mathcal{L}}$ defined via its regular language.
Hughes-Valiunas~\cite[Proposition~4.2]{HV24:Biautomaticity} showed that
these translation lengths satisfy oriented quasi-smoothing, and used
this property to construct a hierarchically hyperbolic group that is
not biautomatic. 

Since this class includes ordinary word length (cf.\
Example~\ref{examp:non-emb-graph}), such translation lengths do not
satisfy smoothing in general and lie in $\bm{\AQConvex(S)}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Self intersection]
  \label{examp:self-intersect}
The square root of the self-intersection number $\mu \mapsto
\sqrt{i(\mu,\mu)}$ satisfies stability,
homogeneity, and smoothing, but fails to satisfy additivity and even
convex union (see~\cite[Example~1.12]{MGT21:Smoothings}). 
Thus it provides an example of a curve functional lying outside
$\QConvex(S)$, despite extending to geodesic currents by
Bonahon’s work~\cite{Bonahon86:EndsHyperbolicManifolds}, so it lies in
$\bm{\ACont(S)}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Exotic boundary metrics]\label{examp:exotic-lengths}
  Another example of functionals that are only continuous are exotic
  length functions on surface groups
  which vanish precisely on simple closed
  geodesics~\cite[Section~9]{CMGR26:GreenMetrics}: these extend to
  geodesic currents, since they satisfy the bounded-backtracking
  property (see~\cite[Section~7]{CMGR26:GreenMetrics}). However, they cannot
  satisfy $K$-quasi-smoothing for any $K>0$: indeed, we can take
  sequences of simple elements $a_n,b_n \in \pi_1(S)$, with $\ora{a_n}, \ora{b_n}$
  crossing, so that $[a_nb_n] \reducesto [a_n][b_n]$ and $\lim_{n\to
    \infty}\ell(a_n b_n) = \infty$. This shows they violate disconnected
  $K$-quasi-smoothing for every~$K$, giving examples in $\bm{\ACont(S)}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Lengths from quotient graphs]
  \label{examp:quotient-graph}
  Consider a $\pi_1$-surjective map $\phi$ from $S$ to to a
  graph~$\cG$. For instance, take a pair-of-pants decomposition of~$S$
  and let $\cG$ be the corresponding trivalent graph. The map $\phi^*
  \colon \pi_1(S) \to \pi_1(\cG)$ necessarily has a large kernel. Now
  consider the length
  function $\ell_{\cG}(C) = \ell(\phi(C))$, assigning to a curve the
  length of its image, up to homotopy in~$\cG$. In particular
  $\ell_\cG$ vanishes on $\ker \phi^*$. Note that $\ell_\cG$ can be
  equivalently defined as as an infimum over the translation distances
  in the universal cover~\cite[Exercise~8.3.13]{BBI01:MetricGeometry},
  and, since the universal cover of $\cG$ is a tree, it follows that
  $\ell_\cG$ is stable
  \cite[Section~2.6]{Gromov1981HyperbolicManifolds}. Bonahon
  used the large kernel to show
  $\ell_\cG$ is \textbf{not in $\bm{\Cont(S)}$}
  \cite[Proposition~11]{Bonahon91:CurrentsGroups}. In
  particular, $\ell_\cG$ does not satisfy quasi-smoothing. We now give a direct proof that quasi-smoothing is violated.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}
Suppose $\Gamma=\pi_1(S)$ acts on a geodesic space $X$ by isometries, and
let $\ell$ be the stable length of the action. Suppose $\ell$ is
non-trivial and there is an infinite normal subgroup~$N < \Gamma$ so
that $\ell(b)=0$ for all $b \in N$. Then $\ell$ does not satisfy
quasi-smoothing.
\label{prop:graph_collapse}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  We first recall Bonahon's proof that $\ell$ does not extend
  continuously to currents.
  Let $a \in \Gamma$, with $\ell(a)>0$, and fix $b\in N$.
  Then $\frac{1}{2n} b a^n b a^{-n}$ converges to $a$ in the weak$^*$
  topology on the space of unoriented currents, but since
  $a^n b a^{-n} b\in N$, we have $\ell(a^n b a^{-n} b)=0$.
  Hence, $\ell$ cannot extend continuously.

  To see explicitly that
$\ell$ does not satisfy quasi-smoothing, find crossing
$\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ as above.
Then Proposition~\ref{prop:join-split-geom},
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cross-split} and~\eqref{eq:cross-cancel} says that, for
some fixed $s > 0$ and all sufficiently large $n$,
\[
  [b a^{-n} b a^n] \reducesto [b a^{-n+s}][b a^{n-s}] \reducesto
     [a^{2n - s}].
\]
Thus if $\ell$ satisfied $K$-quasi-smoothing, we would have
\[
0 = \ell(ba^n ba^{-n}) \geq \ell(a^{2n-s})-2K = (2n-s)\ell(a)-2K,
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Additive version of $\sqrt{\EL}$]
  \label{ex:linEL}
Let $\sqrt{EL}$ denote the (square root of) extremal length.
In \cite[Section 4.8]{MGT21:Smoothings} the authors proved that $\sqrt{EL}$ satisfies stability, homogeneity, smoothing, and convex union. By  \cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}, $\sqrt{EL}$ extends continuously to a continuous on geodesic currents.
$\sqrt{EL}$ does not satisfy additivity, so it is not dual to a
geodesic current. However, we can consider $\sqrt{EL}$ as a function
on \emph{connected} curves and extended it additively to a function
$G$ on (weighted)
multi-curves,
which is thus not equal to $\sqrt{EL}$ on multi-curves. $G$ is
\textbf{not in $\bm{\ACont(S)}$}, since we know $G=\sqrt{EL}$ on
(weighted) connected curves, which are dense in the space of geodesic
currents.
Thus, the purported continuous extension $\overline{G}$ would have to satisfy $\overline{G}=\sqrt{EL}$ on all geodesic currents, hence on all multi-curves, a contradiction.

Since $G$ is additive and stable, it follows from
\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings} that $G$ cannot satisfy oriented
smoothing or quasi-smoothing. We go further, and show that $G$
satisfies disconnected smoothing
but not connected smoothing.
Indeed, suppose $(\ora{a}, \ora{b})$ are a crossing pair. Then
\begin{align*}
  G([a][b]) &= G([a]) + G([b]) &&\text{additivity of $G$}\\
   &= \sqrt{\EL}([a]) + \sqrt{EL}([b]) &&\text{$G=\sqrt{\EL}$ on connected curves}\\
   &\ge \sqrt{\EL}([a][b]) &&\text{$\sqrt{\EL}$ satisfies convex union}\\
   &\ge \sqrt{\EL}([ab]) &&\text{$\sqrt{\EL}$ satisfies smoothing}\\
   &= G([ab]) &&\text{$G=\sqrt{\EL}$ on connected curves}.
\end{align*}
Since $G$ satisfies disconnected smoothing, it cannot satisfy
connected quasi-smoothing; i.e., for any $K$ there is some parallel
pair $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ so that $G([ab]) < G([a]) + G([b]) - K$.
\end{example}

In Appendix~\ref{sec:asymmetric} we define a continuous asymmetric intersection
number $\vec\imath(\alpha,\beta)$ for $\alpha,\beta \in \Curr^+(S)$.
The function
\[
\mu \mapsto \sqrt{\vec\imath(\mu,\mu)}
\]
is in $\bm{\Cont^+(S)}$ but not in $\QConvex^+(S)$, by the same reasoning as
in Example~\ref{examp:self-intersect}.
\end{example}

\subsection{Non-negativity}
\label{subsec:nonnegative}

Observe that we do not assume in the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} that the curve
functional is non-negative: in fact it follows
from the other conditions. Here we prove, more generally, that any
functional $f \in \QConvex(S)$ is non-negative, in two slightly
different forms. (We only use the first version in this paper.)

\begin{proposition}
  Let $f$ be a curve functional satisfying convex union and smoothing. Then $f([a]) \geq 0$ for any $a \in \pi_1(S)$.
  \label{prop:curve-positive}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $a \in \pi_1(S)$ and let $x \in \pi_1(S)$ be so that $\ora{a}$
crosses $\ora{x}$ so (by oriented smoothing)
$[a][x] \reducesto [ax]$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod} below,
$(\ora{ax},\ora{a})$ cross, so
(by unoriented smoothing) $[ax][a]
\reducesto [x]$. We thus have
\begin{align*}
    f([a]) + f([x]) &\geq f([a] [x]) \geq f([ax])\\
     f([ax]) + f([a]) &\geq f([ax] [a]) \geq f([x]).
\end{align*}
Adding the equations, we get $f([a]) \geq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
  Let $f$ be a curve functional satisfying convex union, quasi-smoothing,
  stability, and homogeneity. Then $f([a]) \geq 0$ for any $a \in
  \pi_1(S)$.
  \label{prop:curve-positive2}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $x \in \pi_1(S)$ be so that $\ora{a}$ crosses $\ora{x}$. As in
Proposition~\ref{prop:curve-positive}, we have
\begin{align*}
[a^n][x] &\reducesto [a^nx]\\
[a^nx][a^n]&\reducesto [x].
\end{align*}
Since $f$ satisfies convex union and $K$-quasi-smoothing (oriented and
unoriented) for
some $K \ge 0$, we get
\begin{align*}
    f([a^n]) + f([x]) &\geq f([a^n] [x]) \geq f([a^nx]) - K\\
     f([a^nx]) + f([a^n]) &\geq f([ax] [a^n]) \geq f([x]) - K
\end{align*}
for some $K>0$.
Adding the equations, we get $f([a^n]) \geq -2K$. By stability and
homogeneity, this yields $n f([a]) \geq -2K$, and taking $n\to\infty$
we see that $f([a]) \geq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The use of both the oriented and the unoriented (quasi-)smoothing hypothesis
is essential in both of the above results. In fact, there exist
natural examples of functionals in $\AConvex^+(S)$ (satisfying
oriented but not unoriented smoothing, as well as the other
hypotheses above) that take negative values. For instance, curve
functionals from an element of $H^1(S; \bbZ)$ have this property. More
generally, the much larger space of
homogeneous quasi-morphisms is in $\AQConvex^+(S)$. All of these take negative
values; see
Section~\ref{sub:quasimorphism}.
\end{remark}
