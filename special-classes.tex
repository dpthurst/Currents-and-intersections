\section{Special classes of geodesic currents}
\label{sec:special-classes}

In this section we characterize special classes of geodesic currents
as curve functionals, such as filling currents, hyperbolic metrics,
measured laminations, integral multi-curves, and give a criterion for
currents dual to embedded graphs.

\subsection{Filling currents}
\label{subsec:filling}
Using a result of Burger, Iozzi, Parreau, and Pozzetti, we can give a
characterization of filling geodesic currents.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:systole}
  Let $S$ a closed surface,
  $\mu$ a geodesic current and $f$ the functional on curves associated
  to $\mu$, i.e., so that $\mu_f=\mu$. The following subsets are in
  bijection.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The set of filling currents (Definition~\ref{def:filling_current}).
    \item The set of functionals $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to
      \mathbb{R}$ which satisfy symmetry, oriented smoothing,
      additivity, and stability, and so that their continuous extension to geodesic currents is positive.
    \item The set of curve functionals $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to
      \mathbb{R}$ satisfying symmetry, oriented smoothing, additivity,
      and stability for which their systole
      \[\sys(f)\coloneqq \inf \{ f(\vec C) \mid \vec C \in \Curves^+(S),\ \vec C \mbox{ non-trivial} \}\]
      is
      positive.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
These equivalences follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group} and \cite[Theorem~1.3]{BIPP21:Systoles}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Multi-curves}

We say that a weighted multi-curve $M$ is \emph{integral}
if its a weighted multi-curve whose weights are integers. We say that
$M$ is a \emph{half-integral} multi-curve if its weights are in
$\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}$. Note that, by definition, all integral
multi-curves are also half-integral.

Also define $\AConvex_{\bbZ}(S)$ to be the functionals in
$f \in \AConvex(S)$ so that $f(C) \in \bbZ$ for all closed curves~$C$.

For $M$ a multicurve and $\vec M$ any orientation, let $h(\vec M) \in
H_1(S; \bbZ)$ and
$h_2(M) \in H_1(S;\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$
denote the respective homology classes.
\begin{corollary}
A curve functional $f$ is in $\AConvex_{\bbZ}(S)$ iff it is dual to a
half-integral multi-curve $\mu=\frac{1}{2}M$ with $h_2(M) = 0$.
\label{cor:int_multicurve}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Suppose $f$ as in the statement is $\bbZ$-valued.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} there exists a geodesic current $\mu$ so that
\[
i(\mu, C) = f(C)
\]
for every closed curve $C$.
We must show $\mu$ is a half-integral multi-curve. Fix a crossing pair $a,b\in\pi_1(S)$, and let $R_n^\pm$ and $B_{a,b}^\pm$
be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:measures_parallelogram}. By parts (1) and (3) of
that lemma, together with Lemma~\ref{lem:onepoint},
\[
2\,\mu(B_{a,b}^+)
= \lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\mu(R_n^+)+\mu(R_n^-)\bigr)
= \lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(f(a^n)+f(b^n)-f(a^nb^{-n})\bigr).
\]
Since $f$ is $\mathbb{Z}$-valued and stable, every term on the right is
an integer, and a convergent sequence in the discrete set $\mathbb{Z}$ is
eventually constant; hence $\mu(B_{a,b}^+)$ is one-half of an integer. As
$(a,b)$ ranges over crossing pairs the quadruples $(a^+,b^-,a^-,b^+)$
range over all quadruples in cyclic order, so these boxes are dense;
choosing the quadruples nested and using continuity from below and above,
$\mu(B)$ is one-half of an integer for every box $B$. (For the
discreteness argument itself, see
\cite[Appendix~A]{JMG26:CoarseNegativeCurvature}.)
By \cite[Proposition~4.12]{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}, it follows
that $2\mu$ must be an integral multi-curve~$M$, and hence
$\mu=\frac{1}{2} M$ is half-integral.

We furthermore claim that $h_2(M)=0$.
Indeed, if $h_2(M) \ne 0$, there exists a simple closed curve $C$ so
that
\[
  1 = \langle h_2(M), h_2(C) \rangle_{\bbZ/2\bbZ}
  \equiv \langle h(\vec M), h(\vec C)\rangle_{\bbZ}
  \equiv i(M, C) \pmod 2,
\]
where $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ denotes the algebraic
intersection number (with $\bbZ/2\bbZ$ or $\bbZ$ coefficients, as
indicated),

$\vec M$ and $\vec C$ are arbitrary orientations of the respective multicurves,
and equivalences are modulo~2. It follows that $i(\mu,C)
\notin \bbZ$, a contradiction.

For the other direction, if $h_2(M) = 0$ then
for every $C \in \Curves(S)$, we have
$i(M,C) \equiv \langle
h(\vec M), h(\vec C)\rangle_{\bbZ} \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ so 
$i(\frac12 M, C) \in \mathbb{Z}$.
\end{proof}
It is easy to see that if $M = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i C_i$, where $\lambda_i \geq 0$ are non-negative real numbers, then for every closed curve $C$, there exist integer numbers $n_i$ so that
\begin{equation}
i(M, C) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i n_i.
\label{eq:weighted_multicurve}
\end{equation}
i.e., $\{i(\mu,C)\}_{\Curves(S)}$ is contained in a finite rank subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$. In this case, we say $\mu$ has \emph{finite rank spectrum}.
This property does not characterize weighted multi-curves, since certain negatively curved metrics that satisfy this condition can be constructed (see~\cite{Agol25:Spectrum}).

Hence, it is natural to ask the following two questions, from
conversations of the first author with, respectively, Meenakshy
Jyothis and Viveka Erlandsson.

\begin{question}
    What is the subspace of geodesic currents with finite rank spectrum?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
    What conditions on the (marked) length spectrum characterize weighted multi-curves?
\end{question}

\subsection{Embedded graphs}
\label{subsec:embedded_graphs}

Related to multi-curves, we also obtain geodesic currents dual to stable lengths associated to embedded graphs in surfaces.

 Let $\iota \colon \mathcal{G} \hookrightarrow S$ be an \emph{embedded
   graph}, i.e., an embedding of a finite graph in $S$ that is
 filling, in the sense that the complementary regions are disks, or
 equivalently $i_*$ is surjective on~$\pi_1$.
 Endow $\mathcal{G}$ with a length metric $g$. Then any closed multi-curve $C$ on $S$ can be homotoped so that it factors through $\mathcal{G}$, in many different ways. Let $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}(C)$ be the length of the smallest
 multi-curve $D$ on $\mathcal{G} $ so that $\iota(D)$ is homotopic to $C$. It is easy to see that this length is realized and is positive. 

\begin{theorem}
Let $\iota \colon \mathcal{G} \to S$ be a filling embedded graph. Then $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}$ is dual to a geodesic current $\mu_{\mathcal{G} }$.
\label{thm:dual_embeddedgraph}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In \cite[Sec.\ 4.4]{MGT21:Smoothings} we show that such lengths are in $\AConvex(S)$, hence by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} they have a dual geodesic current. 
\end{proof}

If furthermore $\mathcal{G}$ is an embedded graph with the edge metric
on $\pi_1(S)$ (i.e., each edge has length~$1$), then as stated in the
Introduction, $\mu_{\mathcal{G}}$ must
be $1/2$ of an integral multi-curve. This generalizes the main result
in~\cite{Erlandsson:WordLength} in the case of closed surfaces, where
Erlandsson finds that
word metric arising from \emph{simple generating set} are dual to 1/2
of an integral multi-curve. Such generating sets correspond to
choosing $\mathcal{G}$ to be a rose graph with only one vertex.

\begin{taggedcor}{\ref{maincor:embedded}}
Let $\mathcal{G} \hookrightarrow S$ be a filling embedded graph
endowed with the edge metric. Then
the functional $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}$ is dual to a
geodesic current $\mu_{\mathcal{G}} = \frac12 M$ where $M$ is a
multi-curve so that $h(M)=0$ in
$H_1(S; \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$.
\end{taggedcor}
\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal{G}$ is equipped with the edge-metric, $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}(C) \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Hence, Corollary~\ref{cor:int_multicurve} implies the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Embedded graph but not simple generating set]
As an example of an embedded graph which does not arise as a simple generating set, consider a square punctured torus $S'$ with an embedded two-vertex graph with four edges, $r, g, c, b$ (for red, green, cyan, blue), see Figure~\ref{fig:embedded-graph}. Since it has more than one vertex, it does not arise from a simple generating set in the sense of Erlandsson. 
Indeed, we have
\[
[x]=[rB]=[gC] , \qquad [y]=[rG]=[bC]
\]
and thus
\[
\ell([x])=2, \qquad 
\ell([y])=2.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\ell([xy])=\ell([(rB)(bC)])=\ell([rC])=2,
\qquad
\ell([xY])=\ell([(gC)(cB)])=\ell([gB])=2.
\]

This phenomenon does not occur for any of the simple generating sets of $\pi_1(S') \simeq F_2$, namely
\[
\{ x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1} \}, \quad
\{ x^{\pm1},(xy)^{\pm1} \}, \quad
\{ x^{\pm1},(xY)^{\pm1} \}, \quad
\{ y^{\pm1},(xY)^{\pm1} \}, \quad
\{ y^{\pm1},(xY)^{\pm1} \},
\]
nor for the analogous generating sets obtained by acting diagonally by the mapping class group. 
Indeed, in none of these cases are $x$, $y$, $xy$, $xY$ the shortest
elements in $\pi_1$ with equal length.

Even though this example corresponds to a punctured surface and hence does not fall in the scope of our theorem, one can produce similar examples in genus 2 from this one.
\end{example}

We note that our result says nothing concrete about the dual multi-curve, whereas Erlandsson's result explicitly constructs it in the specific case of simple generating sets. It would be interesting to describe these multi-curves in general.

\begin{question}
  Is the converse of Corollary~\ref{maincor:embedded} true? That is,
let $C=\frac{1}{2}M$, where $M$ is an integral
multi-curve with $h(M) = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-homology.
Does there exist a filling embedded graph $\mathcal{G} \hookrightarrow S$ endowed with the edge metric so that
\[
\ell_{\mathcal{G}}(D)=i(C,D)
\]
for every closed curve $D \in \Curves(S)$?
\end{question}

A positive answer would give an equivalence between
\begin{itemize}
\item $\AConvex_{\mathbb{Z}}(S)$;
\item $1/2$-integral multi-curves $M$ which are zero in
  $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-homology; and
\item filling embedded graphs in~$S$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Length metrics}
\label{subsec:length-metrics}
We now prove Corollary~\ref{maincor:length_dual}: any length induced
by a metric (in fact, by a pseudometric) on $S$ has a dual geodesic
current. We will then use that result to derive some corollaries about
domination.

We first recall the definition of a length induced by a pseudometric on $S$. See~\cite[Chapter~I.1]{BH11:NonPosCurvature} for a definition in the context of metrics. A \emph{pseudometric space} is a pair $(X,d)$, where $X$ is a set and $d \colon X \times X \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is a function satisfying, for all $x,y,z \in X$,
\[
d(x,x)=0,\quad d(x,y)=d(y,x),\quad d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z).
\]
 The \emph{$d$-length} $\ell(c)$ of a continuous path $c \colon [a,b] \to X$
is defined as
\begin{equation}
\ell(c) \coloneqq \sup_{a=t_0 \leq t_1 \leq \cdots \leq t_n=b} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} d(c(t_i),c(t_{i+1})),
\label{eq:length_of_path}
\end{equation}
where the supremum is taken over all possible partitions of $[a,b]$ (no bound on $n$), with
$a=t_0 \leq t_1 \leq \cdots t_n = b$.
The length of a path $c$ is either a non-negative number or it is infinite.



We say
a concrete curve $c$ is \emph{$d$-rectifiable} if its $d$-length is finite.
Consider now a pseudometric $(X,d)$ on $S$.
For a curve $C$ on $S$, we define the \emph{$d$-length} of $C$ as
\[
\ell_d(C) \coloneqq \inf_{\gamma \in C} \ell_d(\gamma)
\]
where $\gamma$ runs over all the concrete curves in the equivalence class of $C$.
We extend this definition of multi-curves linearly.
We call this the \emph{length induced by $d$}.
If there exists at least one $d$-rectifiable concrete curve
representative for each curve $C$, then $\ell_d$ induces a curve
functional. This is the case, for example, when  $(X,d)$ is a
\emph{length pseudometric} on $S$, i.e.,
$d$ is given by the infimum of lengths of paths~\cite[Chapter~I.2, Definition~3.1]{BH11:NonPosCurvature}. Precisely, this means
\begin{equation}\label{eq:length-metric}
d(x,y) = \inf \{ \ell_d(\gamma) \mid \gamma\colon [0,1] \to X, \gamma(0)=x, \gamma(1)=y\}.
\end{equation}
We now prove the corollary from the introduction.
\begin{taggedcor}{\ref{maincor:length_dual}}
  An additive curve functional satisfies the smoothing
  property~\eqref{eq:smoothing}, and is thus dual to a geodesic
  current, if and only if it is the induced length on curves from a
  length pseudo-metric on~$S$.
\end{taggedcor}
\begin{proof}
Additivity is satisfied by definition of $\ell_d$ on multi-curves. It is clear that $\ell_d$ is a symmetric curve functional, since $d$ is symmetric.
The key point is to check that $\ell_d$ satisfies smoothing. 
Let $C$ be a multi-curve with an essential crossing $x$, and let $C'$ be obtained by smoothing $C$ at $x$.

Fix $\epsilon>0$. Since $\ell_d$ is a curve functional, $\ell_d(C)<\infty$ for every multi-curve $C$. By definition of $\ell_d(C)$, there exists a concrete representative $\gamma$ of $C$ with an essential crossing $x'$ of the same homotopy type as $x$ such that
\[
\ell_d(\gamma) \le \ell_d(C) + \epsilon.
\]
In particular, $\gamma$ is $d$-rectifiable.
Let $\gamma' \in C'$ be the concrete multi-curve obtained by smoothing $\gamma$ at $x'$.

By abuse of notation, let $\gamma \colon I \to X$ be a continuous $d$-rectifiable concrete curve parameterizing the concrete multi-curve $\gamma$ where $I$ is a finite union of intervals. Let $t' \neq t''$ be points in (possibly different components of) $I$ such that $\gamma(t') = \gamma(t'') = x'$ correspond to the essential crossing. Let $\gamma' \colon I' \to X$ be a continuous parametrization of the $d$-rectifiable concrete curve $\gamma'$ such that
\[
\gamma(I) = \gamma'(I')
\]
and $I'$ is another finite union of intervals.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:essential-cross}, the chosen representative $\gamma$ has a crossing
$x'$ corresponding to $x$, whose prescribed smoothing represents
$C'$. Cut the parameter domain of $\gamma$ at the two preimages of
$x'$. The smoothed curve $\gamma'$ is obtained by concatenating
exactly the same finitely many restricted paths, only with different
pairings at their common endpoints. Since length in a pseudometric
space is additive under concatenation of rectifiable paths,
\[
\ell_d(\gamma')=\ell_d(\gamma).
\]

By definition of $\ell_d(C')$ as an infimum over representatives of $C'$, we have
\[
\ell_d(C') \le \ell_d(\gamma') = \ell_d(\gamma) \le \ell_d(C) + \epsilon.
\]
Since $\epsilon>0$ was arbitrary, it follows that
\[
\ell_d(C') \le \ell_d(C),
\]
so $\ell_d$ satisfies smoothing.
Thus, by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_d$ dual to $\ell_d$. 


Finally, we prove the other implication. Given a geodesic current $\mu$ and a choice of hyperbolic metric $\Sigma$ on $S$, there is an induced invariant pseudometric on $\wt{S}$ defined via

\begin{equation}\label{eq:d_mu}
\wt{d^\Sigma_\mu}(x,y) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\mu(G[x,y)) + \mu(G(x,y])\right),
\end{equation}
inducing a metric $d^\Sigma_\mu$ on~$S$
so that 
\[
\ell_{d^\Sigma_\mu}(C)=i(\mu, C).
\]
See~\cite[Section~4]{BIPP21:Systoles} and~\cite{dRMG22:Duals}.
\end{proof}

One corollary is the following folklore result.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:length-stable}
  Any length pseudometric $(S,d)$ on a surface~$S$ is stable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  This follows from Corollary~\ref{maincor:length_dual}. More
  precisely, this is a consequence of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:power_disconnected_imply_stable}.
\end{proof}

We continue with some remarks.

\begin{remark}\label{rmk:pseudo_metrics}
 For any geodesic current~$\mu$, the pseudometric $\wt{d_\mu}$ on $\wt{X}$ is
 \emph{straight} (\cite[Proposition~4.1]{BIPP21:Systoles}) in the
 sense that if $x,y,z \in l$ for any $l \in G(\Sigma)$, and $x<y<z$ in
 the natural order, then
\[
\wt{d_\mu^\Sigma}(x,z)=\wt{d_\mu^\Sigma}(x,y)+\wt{d_\mu^\Sigma}(y,z).
\]
(The $\wt{d_\mu^\Sigma}$ pseudometric need not be continuous with respect to the topology of $\wt{\Sigma}$.)
If $d$ is a length pseudometric on $S$ and
$\widetilde d$ is its lifted length pseudometric on $\widetilde S$,
then Corollary~\ref{maincor:length_dual} produces a geodesic current $\mu$ such that the
associated straight invariant pseudometric $d^\Sigma_\mu$ has the
same marked length spectrum:
\[
\ell_{d^\Sigma_\mu}(C)=\ell_d(C)
\]
for every closed curve $C$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Suppose $(S,d)$ is a length pseudometric structure on $S$. Let
$\wt{d}$ be the lift of the metric $d$ to $\wt{S}$, where $\pi_1(X)$
acts by isometries. Then $\ell_d$ is the same
as translation length on $\wt S$
\cite[8.3.13]{BBI01:MetricGeometry}:
\[\ell_d([g])=\inf_{x \in \wt{X}} \wt{d}(x, g x) \eqqcolon \tau_{\wt{d}}(g),\]
This translation length is stable by Corollary~\ref{cor:length-stable}.
More broadly,
Corollary~\ref{cor:length-stable} holds for \emph{convex} Gromov
hyperbolic spaces~\cite[Section~2.6]{Gromov1981HyperbolicManifolds}
but not for more general spaces like geodesic Gromov hyperbolic
spaces \cite[Proposition~6.4]{CDP90:Lectures}; see also Example~\ref{examp:qf}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  The pseudometric $d_\mu$ constructed from a geodesic current~$\mu$
  by Eq.~\eqref{eq:d_mu} is generally not continuous or positive. In
  fact, $d_\mu$ is continuous iff $\mu$ has no atoms
  \cite[Propositions~4.7, 4.12]{dRMG22:Duals}.
\end{remark}

\begin{question}\label{quest:pseudometric-continuous}
  Can the length pseudometrics in Corollary~\ref{maincor:length_dual}
  be improved so that $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ is continuous on $S \times S$?
  If furthermore the systole of the curve functional is positive, can
  $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ be made into an ordinary length metric? (See
  Corollary~\ref{cor:systole}.)
\end{question}

As motivation for Question~\ref{quest:pseudometric-continuous},
consider the easiest case where continuity and positivity both fail
for the construction above,
when $\mu = \delta_C$ is supported on a multi-curve~$C$. In this case
we can make the metric continuous, as we now sketch. Pick a minimal position
for~$C$, with only transverse self-intersections and no triple
intersections, and pick a small tubular thickening $N(C)$. Define the
length pseudometric on small scales as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[start=0]
\item Within a single connected component of the complement of
  $N(C)$, distances are identically zero.
\item Within a thickening of a single strand of~$C$, measure distances
  by the transverse measure of~$C$.
\item Within a thickening of a crossing, topologically a square, use
  the Manhattan metric.
\end{enumerate}
These pieces are then assembled into a global length pseudometric as
usual using Eq.~\eqref{eq:length-metric}.
Several variations are possible. In fact, if $C$ is filling, we can
make the pseudometric into an honest metric as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[start=0]
\item The complementary regions of $N(C)$ are all disks, by definition
  of ``filling''. Collapse those disks to points; this does not change
  the topology of~$S$.
\item Inside thickenings of a single strand, add a longitudinal
  component to make a Manhattan metric. This does not change lengths
  of curves: shortest paths will cut straight across these regions,
  and the longitudinal component will not matter.
\item No change inside thickenings of crossings.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Domination}\label{sec:domination}
We take a brief detour from the special classes of currents to
consider questions related to domination.
Let $\mu_1,\mu_2 \in \Curr(S)$. Say $\mu_1 \leq \mu_2$, or $\mu_2$ \emph{dominates} $\mu_1$, if $i(\mu_1,C) \leq i(\mu_2,C)$ for all $C \in \Curves^+(S)$.
As an application of Corollary~\ref{maincor:length_dual}, we answer positively the following question at the end of \cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.

\begin{question}[Neumann-Coto, 2001]
Let $C_1,C_2$ be two multi-curves. If $C_2$ dominates $C_1$, does it follow that $\ell_g(C_1) \leq \ell_g(C_2)$ for all Riemannian metrics $g$ on $S$?
\end{question}

In fact, we give a stronger result.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:domination}
Let $\mu_1, \mu_2$ be two geodesic currents. If $\mu_2$ dominates $\mu_1$, then
    $\ell_d(\mu_1) \leq \ell_d(\mu_2)$ for all length pseudometrics $d$ on $S$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{maincor:length_dual}, for every length pseudometric $d$ on $S$, there exist a dual geodesic current $\mu_d$ for the induced curve functional $\ell_d$.
Approximating the current $\mu_d$ by weighted multi-curves and using continuity of intersection number on currents the result follows.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Measured laminations}
\label{subsec:laminations}

We will now characterize measured laminations among curve functionals.

We will need a relation about the measure of sets intersecting pairs of geodesic segments.

We introduce the following notation. Given two half-closed half-open
geodesics segments $[x,w)$ and $[y,z)$, let $\mu(G([x,w), [y,z)))$ be the set of bi-infinite geodesics in $G(S)$ intersecting $[x,w)$ and $[y,z)$ transversely. We will refer to sets of this type as \emph{double transversals}. Given a geodesic segment $[x,y)$, a geodesic $\gamma$ intersects $x$ transversely if $x\in\gamma$ and $[x,y) \nsubseteq \gamma$.

Let $x,y,z,w \in \wt{\Sigma}$ appear counter-clockwise as vertices of an
embedded convex geodesic quadrilateral on $\wt{\Sigma}$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:otalclaim}
Given the setup described above, we have 
\begin{multline*}
\mu(G([x,w), [y,z))) + \mu(G((x,w], (y,z])) = \mu(G[x,z)) + \mu(G[w,y))- \mu(G([w,z)) - \mu(G([x,y)).
\end{multline*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is~\cite[Lemma~6.2]{dRMG22:Duals}.
\end{proof}
We also need the following hyperbolic geometry lemma, about \emph{hyperbolic parallelograms} (Appendix~\ref{sec:hyp-par}). It can be seen as a refinement of~\cite[Lemma~5.6]{NCS23:Property}.

Consider Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}. 


\begin{proposition}
Consider two elements $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ with hyperbolic axes $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ that intersect at a point $O$, and translation lengths $\ell(a),\ell(b)$.
We define the following four points:

\begin{itemize} \item $P$ is the point on $\ora{a}$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(a)/2$;
\item $Q$ is the point on $\ora{b}$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $\ell(b)/2$;
\item $Q'$ is the point on $\ora{b}$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(b)/2$;
\item $P'$ is the point on $\ora{a}$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $\ell(a)/2$.
\end{itemize}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The segment $(Q,P]$ is on the line $\ora{ab}$ and has length $\ell(ba)/2$.
\item The segment $(P',Q']$ is on the line $\ora{ba}$ and has length $\ell(ba)/2$.
\item The segment $(Q',P]$ is on the line $\ora{bA}$ and has length $\ell(bA)/2$.
\item The segment $(P',Q]$ is on the line $\ora{Ab}$ and has length $\ell(Ab)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, $G((Q,P])\cup a\cdot
G((P',Q'])$ is a Borel fundamental domain for the
action of $\langle ab \rangle$ on $G(\ora{ab})$.
Similarly, $G((Q',P]) \cup b \cdot G((P',Q])$ is a Borel fundamental domain for
 the action of $\langle bA\rangle$ on $G(\ora{bA})$.
\label{prop:keygeometry}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We prove the first case.
  For $z$ any of the five points in the statement, let $r_z$ be
  the hyperbolic rotation by angle $\pi$ fixing~$z$. Then $a$, as
  acting on the hyperbolic plane, is $r_P r_O$, while $b = r_O r_Q$.
  (Recall that a composition $r_zr_w$ of two rotations by $\pi$ is a
  translation on the line $\overleftrightarrow{zw}$ by twice the
  distance between $z$ and~$w$.)
  It follows that $ab = r_P (r_O)^2 r_Q =
  r_Pr_Q$, from which the statement about $(Q,P]$
  follows. The other cases are similar.

For the assertion on fundamental domains, we note that the segment
$(P',Q']$ on the axis of $\ora{ba}$, is sent by $a$ to a segment
$(P,R]$, where $R=a(Q')$ appears forward of $P$ in according to the
orientation of $\ora{ab}$, and the segment $(Q,R]$ is a fundamental
domain for the action of $ab$ on its axis $\ora{ab}$. Thus $\{
\ora{ab} \} \times G((Q,R])= \{ \ora{ab} \} \times  (G((Q,P]) \cup a
\cdot G((P',Q']))$ is a fundamental domain for the action of $ab$ on
$\DG(\Sigma)$, as desired.
The other claim is similar.
\end{proof}

Using it, we characterize measured laminations among curve
functionals.
We start with a  lemma, that first requires defining some sets of interest.

Given elements $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ which R-cross,
consider the hyperbolic parallelogram~$H$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:keygeometry}, bounded by the vertices $Q,P,P',Q'$.
Consider the sets
\begin{itemize} \item $R_{a,b}^+ \coloneqq G([Q,P), [P', Q'))$, and $(R_{a,b}^{\perp})^+ \coloneqq G([P',Q) \times [Q', P))$, 
\item $R_{a,b}^- \coloneqq G((Q,P], (P', Q'])$, and $(R_{a,b}^{\perp})^- \coloneqq G((P',Q] \times (Q', P])$
\end{itemize}
consisting, respectively, of geodesics crossing from the segment of $H$ contained in $\ora{ab}$ to the open segment of $H$ contained in $\ora{ba}$, and from the segment of $H$ contained in $\ora{a^{-1}b}$ to the one in $\ora{ba^{-1}}$.
Consider also the boxes of geodesics:
\begin{itemize} \item $B_{a,b}^+ = (a^+, b^-] \times [a^-, b^+)$ and  $(B_{a,b}^{\perp})^+ = (b^-, a^-] \times [b^+, a^+)$, 
\item $B_{a,b}^- = [a^+, b^-) \times (a^-, b^+]$ and  $(B_{g,h}^{\perp})^- = [b^-, a^-) \times (b^+, a^+]$.
\end{itemize}
We have $R_{a,b}^{\pm} \subset B_{a,b}^{\pm}$ and $(R_{a,b}^{\perp})^{\pm}  \subset (B_{a,b}^{\perp})^{\pm} $.

We will resort to this setup several times in the proofs below.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:onepoint}
Let $\mu$ be any geodesic current and let $a,b\in\pi_1(S)$ cross. Then
\[
\mu\bigl((B_{a,b}^+\cup B_{a,b}^-)\setminus B_{a,b}\bigr)=0,
\]
and likewise with $B_{a,b}^\perp$ in place of $B_{a,b}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each piece of the difference is of the form $\{x\}\times J$, where $x$ is
one of $a^\pm,b^\pm$ and $J$ is a half-open interval in
$\partial_\infty S\setminus\{x\}$ whose other endpoint is the other fixed
point of the element $g\in\{a,b\}$ fixing $x$. In the coordinate on the
pencil $P(x)$ in which $g$ acts by translation, $J$ is a half-line, hence
a disjoint union $\bigsqcup_{k\ge0} g^{-k}J_0$ of bounded pieces. Since
$g$ fixes $x$, the sets $\{x\}\times g^{-k}J_0$ are translates of one
another under $g$, so by $\pi_1(S)$-invariance they all carry the same
$\mu$-mass $m$. Then $\mu(\{x\}\times J)$ is $0$ if $m=0$ and $+\infty$
otherwise; but it is bounded above by
$\mu(B_{a,b}^+)+\mu(B_{a,b}^-)$, which is finite. Hence $m=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma} 
Given elements $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ which R-cross,
let $B_{a,b}^{\perp},(B_{a,b}^{\pm})^{\perp}, R_{a,b}^{\pm}, (R_{a,b}^{\pm})^\perp$ as above. Suppose $\mu$ is an arbitrary geodesic current.

For every $n > 0$, let $R_n^{\pm}  \coloneqq (R_{a^n,b^n})^{\pm} $ and $(R_n^{\perp})^{\pm}  \coloneqq (R_{a^n,b^n}^{\perp})^{\pm}$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate} 
\item $R_n^{\pm}  \subset R_{n+1}^{\pm}$, and $\bigcup_{n \geq 1} R_n^{\pm}  = B_{a,b}^{\pm}$ and $(R_n^{\perp})^{\pm}  \subset (R_{n+1}^{\perp})^{\pm}$ and $\bigcup_{n \geq 1} (R_n^{\perp})^{\pm} = (B_{a,b}^{\perp})^{\pm}$.
\item We have $\lim_{n} \mu(R_n^{\pm}) \to \mu(B_{a,b}^{\pm})$ and $\lim_n \mu((R_n^{\perp})^{\pm})=\mu((B_{g,h}^{\perp})^{\pm})$.
\item  If we let $c_n=a^n b^{-n}$ and $d_n=a^n b^n$, we have 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rn-meas}
\mu(R_n^+)+\mu(R_n^-)=f(a^n) + f(b^n)-f(c_n),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rn-perp-meas}
\mu((R_n^\perp)^{+}) + \mu((R_n^\perp)^{-})=f(a^n) + f(b^n) -f(d_n).
\end{equation}
\item We have $\mu(\partial B_{a,b}^{\pm})=0$ and
$\mu(\partial (B_{a,b}^{\perp})^{\pm})=0$, provided $\mu$ has no atom at a
lift of $[a]$ or of $[b]$. In general the difference
$(B_{a,b}^+\cup B_{a,b}^-)\setminus B_{a,b}$, and likewise for
$B_{a,b}^{\perp}$, is $\mu$-null (Lemma~\ref{lem:onepoint}).
\end{enumerate}
\label{lem:measures_parallelogram}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Item (1) is by construction of $R_n^{\pm}$.
Item (2) follows by Item (1) and lower semi-continuity of measures.
Item (3) follows by Lemma~\ref{lem:otalclaim} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:keygeometry}. Finally, Item (4): by
Lemma~\ref{prop:spikes} the only possible atom in the pencil at
$a^\pm$ is $\ora{a}$ (and at $b^\pm$, $\ora{b}$), and these are
excluded from the half-open boxes; the general bound on the boundary
is Lemma~\ref{lem:onepoint}.
\end{proof}

Recall that a geodesic current is a measured lamination if and only if for every open box of geodesics $B$, we have $\min \{\mu(B),\mu(B^{\perp})\}=0$ (see, e.g.,~\cite[Proposition~2.1]{BIPP24:PositiveCrossratios}).
We now prove  Theorem~\ref{thm:lam-char}, whose statement we recall.

\begin{taggedthm}{\ref{thm:lam-char}}
Let $f \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a functional on~$\pi_1(S)$.
Then there exists a measured lamination~$\mu_{f}$ on $S$ such that
\[
\text{for every } g\in \pi_1(S),\quad
i(\mu_{f},[g])=f(g)
\]
if and only if $f$ satisfies the conditions of symmetry, class invariance,
stability, oriented smoothing,
and, when $(\ora{g}, \ora{h})$ are crossing,
\[
f(g)+f(h)=\max\{f(gh),f(gH)\}.
\]
\end{taggedthm}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first $f$ satisfies the conditions in the hypotheses including
disconnected max-smoothing.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_f$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for all closed curves $C$.
It remains to show that $\mu_f$ is a measured lamination.
Given elements $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ which R-cross,
consider the hyperbolic parallelogram~$H$ at the center of
Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}, and the notation and setup of Lemma~\ref{lem:measures_parallelogram}.
By the hypothesis of max-smoothing, for every $n$, we have $\mu((R_n^\perp)^+)=\mu((R_n^\perp)^-)=0$ or $\mu(R_n^{+})=\mu(R_n^{-})=0$, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rn-meas} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rn-perp-meas}.
Since each sequence of sets $(R_n^{\pm})$ and $(R_n^{\perp})^{\pm}$ is nested, if $\mu(R_1^{\pm}) \neq 0$, then $\mu(R_n^{\pm}) \neq 0$ for all $n \geq 1$ and thus either $\mu(R_n^{\pm}) = 0$ for every $n$, in which case Lemma~\ref{lem:measures_parallelogram}(1) gives $\mu(B_{a,b}^{\pm})=0$; or there is some $N$ with $\mu(R_N^{\pm}) \neq 0$, and then $\mu((R_n^{\perp})^{\pm})=0$ for all $n \geq N$, whence $\mu((B_{a,b}^{\perp})^{\pm})=0$.
By density of endpoints of closed geodesics, these boxes have vertices on a dense set of points of $\partial_{\infty} S$.
The pairs of the diagonal endpoints of the family of boxes $B_{a,b}^{\pm}$ (as $a,b$ runs over crossing pairs in $\pi_1(S)$) are dense in the space of geodesics. Hence, we can find sequences of crossing pairs $a_n, b_n \in \pi_1(S)$ so that if $B_n^{\pm} \coloneqq B_{a_n, b_n}^{\pm}$, we have $B_n^{\pm} \subset B_{n+1}^{\pm}$, $(B_{n+1}^{\perp})^{\pm} \subset (B_{n}^{\perp})^{\pm}$, $B=\bigcup_n B_n^{\pm}$, and $B^\perp \subseteq \bigcap_n (B_n^{\perp})^{\pm}$.
Choosing the periodic endpoints successively from the appropriate
sides, we may arrange that
\[
B_n^\pm\subset B_{n+1}^\pm,\qquad
(B_{n+1}^\perp)^\pm\subset(B_n^\perp)^\pm,
\]
with
\[
\bigcup_n B_n^\pm=B,\qquad
\bigcap_n(B_n^\perp)^\pm=B^\perp.
\] For every $n$,
\[
\min\{\mu(B_n),\mu(B_n^\perp)\}=0.
\]
If $\mu(B_n)=0$ for every $n$, continuity from below gives
$\mu(B)=0$. Otherwise, choose $N$ with $\mu(B_N)>0$. Then
$\mu(B_n^\perp)=0$ for every $n\ge N$, and continuity from above
gives $\mu(B^\perp)=0$, and thus $\mu$ is a measured lamination.

Now we prove the other implication, by proving the contra-positive.
If disconnected max-smoothing is \emph{not} satisfied for the elements
$a,b$, then by Equations~\eqref{eq:Rn-meas}
and~\eqref{eq:Rn-perp-meas}, both:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mu(R_{a,b}^{+})>0$ or $\mu(R_{a,b}^{-})>0$;
    \item and
$\mu((R_{a,b}^{\perp})^{+})>0$ or $\mu((R_{a,b}^{\perp})^{-})>0$
\end{itemize} 
For every $\gamma \in R_{a,b}^+ \cup R_{a,b}^-$ and every $\gamma' \in (R_{a,b}^\perp)^+\cup (R_{a,b}^\perp)^-$, $\gamma$ and $\gamma'$ cross. Consequently, we have $i(\mu, \mu) >0$,
and hence $\mu$ is not a measured lamination.
\end{proof}

The above result shows that if one knows $f$ is dual to a geodesic
current, then it is only necessary to check disconnected max-smoothing
to conclude $f$ is a measured lamination, i.e., one does not have to
check essential \emph{self}-crossings of connected components of multi-curves. It is however true that intersection
number with a measured lamination also satisfies connected
max-smoothing.
We state this result along the lines of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.

\begin{theorem}
A curve functional $f \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it satisfies additivity and the \emph{max-smoothing property}:
    For any essential crossing of a multi-curve $C$, we have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:MLsmoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    \label{thm:char_laminations_plus}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Suppose $f$ is a symmetric curve functional satisfying max-smoothing and
  additivity. Max-smoothing clearly implies smoothing, as well as
  disconnected max-smoothing. Thus, by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:lam-char}, $f$ is dual to a measured
  lamination. On the other hand, if $f$ is dual to a measured
  lamination, Theorem~\ref{thm:lam-char} implies disconnected
  max-smoothing. Connected max-smoothing follows from tracing through
  the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cont_conn_disc}, using the fact
  that $i(\mu, \cdot)$ is continuous, symmetric, additive and stable,
  and noting that if $f$ satisfies disconnected max-smoothing, all the
  inequalities become equalities in the proof of disconnected
  smoothing implies connected smoothing.
\end{proof}

The fact that measured laminations satisfy max-smoothing was proven by
the second author in~\cite{Thu00:GeometricIntersection} using
different techniques. 

\subsection{Liouville currents}
\label{subsec:liouville}
Bonahon gives an algebraic characterization for a geodesic current $\mu$ to be a Liouville current $\mathcal{L}_\Sigma$ for a marked hyperbolic structure $\Sigma \in \Teich(S)$, the \emph{Teichm\"uller space} of $S$.

\begin{theorem}[{\cite[Theorem~13]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}}]   \label{thm:bonahonliouville}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \Curr(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, for any four points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} S$, we have
   \[
      e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} + e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])}=1.
   \]
\end{theorem}
Whether the endpoints of the intervals are included or excluded in the
above statement is immaterial, since it follows from the hypothesis that the current is non-atomic. 

We use this to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:hyp-char}. Again we recall the statement.

\begin{taggedthm}{\ref{thm:hyp-char}}
Let $f \colon \pi_1(S) \to \mathbb{R}$.
Then there exists a  hyperbolic metric~$d$
on $S$, with associated Liouville current $\mu_d$, such that
\[
\text{for every } g\in \pi_1(S),\quad
f(g) = \ell_d([g]) = i(\mu_d,[g])
\]
if and only if $f$ satisfies the conditions of symmetry, class invariance,
stability, oriented smoothing,
positivity ($f(g) > 0$ for every non-identity element~$g$),
and, when $(\ora{g},\ora{h})$ are crossing,
\[
\lambda(g)\lambda(h)
  = \lambda(gh)+\lambda(gH)
\]
where $\lambda(g)\coloneqq 2\cosh\!\bigl(f(g)/2\bigr)$.
\end{taggedthm}

\begin{proof}
If $\mu$ is hyperbolic Liouville current then smoothing, additivity, and
stability follow from the fact these are necessary conditions for a
geodesic current, as shown in Appendix~\ref{prop:necessary}. The
$\lambda$-smoothing relation~\eqref{eq:lambda-smoothing} follows from
the relation between the hyperbolic lengths of the sides of a
hyperbolic parallelogram (see Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp_parallelogram})
applied to the hyperbolic parallelogram~$H$ at the center of
Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}, and the fact that the hyperbolic Liouville current is the dual current for the hyperbolic length, by~\cite[Proposition~14]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}.

The $\lambda$-length functional arises naturally in the study of the
skein algebra of a surface; see, for example,
\cite[Secs.~1--2]{Pen12:Decorated} and
\cite[Sec.~3]{Thurston14:PBI}.
For the other direction, by the assumptions on~$f$ and
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}, there exists a
geodesic current $\mu_f$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for every closed
curve $C$.
It remains to show that $\mu_f$ is a Liouville current, which is where Equation~\eqref{eq:lambda-smoothing} will play a role.
Given elements $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ which R-cross, consider the hyperbolic parallelogram~$H$ at the center of
Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}, and the notation and setup of Lemma~\ref{lem:measures_parallelogram}.

Let $A_n \coloneqq f(a^n)=nf(a)$, $B_n \coloneqq f(b^n)=nf(b)$, $C_n \coloneqq f(a^nb^n)$ and $D_n \coloneqq f(a^n b^{-n})$.
Now, we compute
\[
e^{(-\mu(R_n^+)-\mu(R_n^-))/2} + e^{(-\mu((R_n^{\perp})^+)-\mu((R_n^{\perp})^-))/2} = e^{-(A_n+B_n-C_n)/2} + e^{-(A_n+B_n-D_n/2}.
\]
The identity
\[
\lambda(a^n)\lambda(b^n)
=\lambda(a^n b^{-n})+\lambda(a^n b^n)
\]
gives, after expansion and multiplication by $e^{-(A_n+B_n)/2}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e^{-(A_n+B_n-C_n)/2}+e^{-(A_n+B_n-D_n)/2}
={}&1+e^{-A_n}+e^{-B_n}+e^{-(A_n+B_n)}\\
&-e^{-(A_n+B_n+C_n)/2}
-e^{-(A_n+B_n+D_n)/2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:comp}
\end{equation}
Because $f(a)>0$ and $f(b)>0$ (since $f(C)>0$ for every curve $C$), it follows that $A_n \to \infty$ and $B_n \to \infty$. 
Thus, since $C_n, D_n \geq 0$, every non-constant term on the
right-hand side of Equation~\ref{eq:comp} tends to zero. Hence
\[
1 = \lim_{n\to\infty}
\left(e^{-(A_n+B_n-C_n)/2}+e^{-(A_n+B_n-D_n)/2}\right)
= \lim_{n\to\infty} e^{\frac{-\mu(R_n^+)-\mu(R_n^-)}{2}} + e^{\frac{-\mu((R_n^{\perp})^+)-\mu((R_n^{\perp})^-)}{2}}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:measures_parallelogram}(2) we also get $\lim_{n^+} \mu(R_n^{\pm})=\mu(B_{a,b}^{\pm})$ and $\lim_{n^-} \mu((R_n^{\perp})^{\pm})=\mu((B_{a,b}^{\perp})^{\pm})$.
 Letting $B_{a,b}$ and $B_{a,b}^\perp$ denote the corresponding open versions of these boxes, we obtain the relation
\[
e^{-\mu(B_{a,b})} + e^{-\mu(B_{a,b}^{\perp})} =1
\]
for any intersecting pair $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$.

We next show that $\mu$ is non-atomic. Suppose, for
contradiction, that an oriented geodesic $\ell=(\xi,\eta)$ is an atom
of mass $w\coloneqq\mu(\{\ell\})>0$.

By Proposition~3.19, $\ell$ is a lift of a closed geodesic, and it is
the unique possible atom in the pencil $P(\xi)$.

Choose a box $B$ such that $\ell$ lies on exactly one boundary side
of $B$ and is disjoint from $\overline{B^\perp}$. Choose the other
three vertices outside the countable set of endpoints of lifts of
closed geodesics. Thus the only possible boundary atom of either box
is $\ell$.

We approximate the same
cyclic quadruple by periodic-endpoint boxes in two ways. In the first
approximation, the limiting first box contains $\ell$; in the second,
it does not. The limiting transverse box is the same in both
approximations and does not contain $\ell$. Writing $u$ and $v$ for
the common masses away from $\ell$, continuity from below and above
gives respectively $e^{-(u+w)}+e^{-v}=1$
and $e^{-u}+e^{-v}=1$. Subtracting these equalities gives $e^{-u}(e^{-w}-1)=0$,
and therefore $w=0$, a contradiction. Hence $\mu$ is non-atomic. Now we use the
density of $B_{a,b}$ in the space of boxes of geodesics as $a,b$ run
over intersecting pairs in $\pi_1(S)$. Hence, given any open box $B$,
we can find a nested sequence. Specifically, set $B_n \coloneqq
B_{a_n,b_n}$. Then $B_n\subset B_{n+1}$ and $\bigcup_n B_n=B$;
continuity from below gives $\lim_n\mu(B_n)=\mu(B)$. Therefore
\[
e^{-\mu(B)} + e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}=1.
\]
Therefore, Theorem~\ref{thm:bonahonliouville} shows $\mu$ is a Liouville current.
\end{proof}
