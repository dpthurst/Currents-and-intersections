\section{Boxes and their measures}
\subsection{Right-handed boxes}
\label{sec:boxes}

We now turn to the outline of the construction and its principal
ingredients.
Let $f\colon \mathbb{R}\Curves^+(\Sigma) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a
functional satisfying symmetry, additive union, smoothing, stability
and homogeneity.
Recall that since $f$ satisfies the symmetry condition,
$f([a])=f([a^{-1}])$ for any $a \in \pi_1(S,\ast)$.
Recall, also, that the fact that $f$ satisfies the smoothing condition
for unoriented curves means $f$ is non-increasing under smoothing
essential crossings for either of the two possible unoriented resolutions
(the one coherent with the chosen orientation, and the one which
isn't).

We wish to construct a current $\mu_f$ and show that
$f(C) = i(C, \mu_f)$. To construct~$\mu_f$, as mentioned above, we fix
(arbitrarily) a simple closed oriented curve~$[x]$ passing through the
basepoint. The endpoints of the lifts of $[x]$ are dense in
$\partial_{\infty} \Sigma$, by minimality of the action of $\pi_1(S)$.\DMGtodo[inline]{Add reference to this in actions section.}
% \DMGtodo[inline]{(Punctures) For surface with punctures, it is density in the limit set, not the whole circle.}

The main objects in our proof are certain boxes in the
space of geodesics to which we will assign measures. The prototype for
the kind of boxes we consider is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:newmodel3}: we have four lines, all lifts of~$x$, with
two lifts $\ell_1,\ell_2$ on the left and two $r_1,r_2$ on the right,
and consider the window
$[\ell_2^-,\ell_1^-) \times [r_2^+,r_1^+) \subset
\partial_\infty \wt S \times \partial_\infty \wt S$ as a basic domain
for defining the measure. The lifts are defined by a 4-tuple of
elements $[p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2]$ of $\pi_1(S)$ by
$\ell_i = (p_i)^{-1} \cdot \wt x$ and $r_i = q_i \cdot \wt x$, with
conditions explained below.

The measure that we ultimately assign to the associated window should
be invariant under
\begin{itemize}
\item changing a $p_i$ preserving $\ell_i$, by $p_i \mapsto xp_i$;
\item changing a $q_i$ preserving $r_i$, by $q_i \mapsto q_ix$; and
\item translating all the lifts by $g \in \pi_1(S)$:
  \[
    [p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2] \mapsto g \cdot [p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2] \coloneqq
      [p_1 g^{-1}, gq_1, p_2 g^{-1},  gq_2].
  \]
\end{itemize}
In fact, ultimately we will take a limit of applying the first
transformations: we look at the limit as $n\to \infty$ of $p_i \mapsto x^np_i$
and $q_i \mapsto q_i x^n$.

  If we are only interested in the relative position of these lines
  (for instance to construct $\pi_1(S)$-invariant functionals), we
  can parameterize them instead by group elements $(a,b,c,d)$ related
  to the $p_i$ and $q_i$ by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  a &= p_1 q_1 &\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad b &= p_1 q_2\\[2pt]
  c &= p_2 q_1 & d &= p_2 q_2.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:relationdatum}
\end{equation}
Note that, by Equation~\eqref{eq:double_coset}, the relative position
of (say) $\ell_1$ and $r_1$ is given by the double coset
$\langle x \rangle a \langle x \rangle$.

The action of translating all lifts by $\pi_1(S)$ has been baked in to
this alternate parametrization,
but the measure corresponding to the associated window should be
invariant under the following four transformations:
\begin{itemize}
\item translating on $\ell_1$: $a \mapsto xa$ and $b \mapsto xb$;
\item translating on $\ell_2$: $c \mapsto xc$ and $d \mapsto xd$;
\item translating on $r_1$: $a \mapsto ax$ and $c \mapsto cx$; and
\item translating on $r_2$: $b \mapsto bx$ and $d \mapsto dx$.
\end{itemize}

Conversely, given elements $a,b,c,d \in \pi_1(S)$ with
$ac^{-1}db^{-1} = 1$, we can
construct four lifts of $x$ with the above relations, for instance
setting $p_1 = 1$, $q_1 = a$, $q_2 = b$, and $p_2 = ca^{-1}$.

As mentioned above, we need some conditions on these lifts; for
instance, we need the window
$[\ell_2^-,\ell_1^-) \times [r_2^+,r_1^+)$ to avoid the
diagonal. For the proofs to go through easily, we will impose more
conditions on the configurations of these lifts.

\begin{definition}[RHS lift system]
\label{def:RH-lift-sys}
Let $a,b,c,d \in \pi_1(S,\ast)$ so that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:box-close} $a c^{-1} d b^{-1} = 1$;
\item\label{item:box-edges-cross} $a,b,c,d$ cross $x$ to the right; and
\item\label{item:box-diags-cross} for sufficiently large~$n$,
  $dx^na c^{-1}
  = cAbx^na c^{-1}$ crosses
  $cx^n$ to the right.
\end{enumerate}
Then we call the datum $(a,b,c,d)$ a \emph{right-handed lift system}.
As explained above, this determines a $\pi_1(S)$-orbit of windows. In
terms of the alternative data $[p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2]$ for a window (rather
than for an orbit), condition~(1) is automatic and the others
become
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*$^\prime$),start=2]
\item for $i,j \in \{1,2\}$, $p_iq_j$ crosses $x$ to the right; and
\item for sufficiently large~$n$, $q_2x^np_1$ crosses $q_1x^np_2$ to
  the right.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
See Figure~\ref{fig:box4} for examples of
RH lift systems, and Figure~\ref{fig:box4} for non-examples.
 We
distinguish three types of RH lift systems: type 1 has
no nested lifts; type 2 has one parallel pair of nested lifts; and
type 3 has two parallel pairs of nested lifts of $x$.
Note that since $x$ is simple, lifts
cannot intersect each other, and nested lifts cannot be anti-parallel
by the right-handed condition.

\begin{definition}\label{def:RH-box}
Fix once and for all a lift $\tilde{x}$ of $[x]$. Every right handed lift system given by the datum $[p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2]$  determines uniquely a \emph{right-handed box}, a half-closed half-open box in $\partial_{\infty} \Sigma \times \partial_{\infty} \Sigma - \Delta$ given by, following the notation above,
\[
B_{p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2} \coloneqq [\ell_2^-,\ell_1^-) \times [r_2^+,r_1^+)
\]

By construction, a different choice of lift $\tilde{x}$ determines a different box $B_{p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2}'$, whose endpoints differ from the endpoints of $B_{p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2}$ by translation by some element in $\pi_1(S)$.
On the other hand, the RH lift system $(a,b,c,d)$
determines a $\pi_1(S)$-orbit of boxes that we will denote
$[B_{p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2}]$ or $[B]_{a,b,c,d}$ depending on if we want to
draw attention to a concrete representative of the orbit or to the
orbit itself. 

We will denote by $\mathcal{B}$ the set of right-handed boxes.
\end{definition}


Given a right-handed lift system $(a,b,c,d)$ and a choice of lift $\tilde{x}$,  this yields configurations of lifts of Type 1, Type 2 and Type 3, where the lifts are placed as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rhbox}. respectively.

\begin{lemma}
Let $x \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$.
Given any $z \in \partial \Sigma$,

and any $\epsilon>0$, there exists a lift $g \cdot \wt{x}$ whose endpoints $g\wt{x}_+$ and $g\wt{x}_-$ are in $(z-\epsilon,z+\epsilon)$, and they are ordered counter-clockwise (or clockwise)

\label{lem:diagonaldense}

\begin{proof}

Then, for large enough $n$, by north-south dynamics of $g$, we have that $(g^n x g^{-n})^{\pm} \in (z-\epsilon,z+\epsilon)$ in clockwise (resp. counter-clockwise) order.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
The family of right handed boxes $\mathcal{B}$ is dense in the space
of rectangles in $G^+(\Sigma)$, in the following sense.
For any rectangle $R = [p,q) \times [r,s) \subset G^+(\Sigma)$,
and any $\epsilon>0$, there exist $a,b,c,d \in \pi_1(S)$ forming a
right-handed box so that for some $B \in [B]_{a,b,c,d}$, with
$B=[p',q') \times [r',s')$, we have $p' \in B_{\epsilon}(p)$, $q' \in
B_{\epsilon}(q)$, $r' \in B_{\epsilon}(r)$ and $s' \in
B_{\epsilon}(s)$.
\label{prop:density}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
   Consider a lift $\tilde{x}$ of $[x]$ with startpoint $x^-$ and endpoint $x^+$.
   Given a points $p,q,r,s \in \partial_{\infty} \tilde{\Sigma}$ in this clockwise order, and any $\epsilon>0$, we will produce lifts $\tilde{x}_p,\tilde{x}_q,\tilde{x}_r,\tilde{x}_s$ of $[x]$ with endpoints within $\epsilon$ of $p,q,r,s$, respectively, and with the right orientations.
   For $r$ (resp. $s$), by Lemma~\ref{lem:diagonaldense}, we find a translate $g_r$ of $\wt{x}$ with endpoints in $B_{\epsilon}(r)$ (resp. a translate $g_s$ with endpoints in $B_{\epsilon}(r)$), with clockwise orientations.

\subsection{Measures of boxes}

Fix $x$ and a lift $\tilde{x}$ once and for all.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:limitexists}
Let $B_{p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2}$ be a right-handed box so that $[B_{p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2}]=[B]_{a,b,c,d}$.
The following limit exists:
\[
\mu_{f}(B_{p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\lim_{n \to \infty}f([bx^n]) + f([cx^n])-f([ax^n]) - f([dx^n]).
\]
\end{proposition}
Note this function on boxes (which will eventually become a pre-measure and
then a measure) is automatically $\pi_1(S)$-invariant.

We prove Proposition~\ref{prop:limitexists} by breaking it into several lemmas.

\begin{lemma}
Given $x, y \in \pi_1(S)$, there exist $a,b \in \pi_1(S)$ so that $b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x}$, $\wt{y}$, $a \cdot \wt{x}$ and $ab \cdot \wt{y}$ are parallel, in this order, as in Figure~\ref{fig:initial}
\label{lem:initial}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given $y$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:diagonaldense}, we can find a translate of $\wt{x}$, $b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x}$, so that $b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x}$ and $\wt{y}$ and  are parallel. Therefore, $\wt{x}$ and $b \cdot \wt{y}$ are parallel, for any $b$, $a \wt{x}$ and $ab \cdot \wt{y}$ are parallel.
Find $g \in \pi_1(X)$ so that $g^{+}$ is in the interval $(y_-, y_+)$ and $g^-$ is in the interval $((b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x})^+, (b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x})^-)$.
If $n$ is large enough, letting $a=g^n$, by the North-South dynamics of $g$ we can ensure that $a \cdot \wt{x}$ is parallel to $\wt{y}$, and the configuration in Figure~\ref{fig:initial} follows.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fundamental-aybx}
Let $a,b,x,y \in \pi_1(S)$ be arbitrary curves, ordered as in Figure~\ref{fig:initial}.
There exist $r_0,s_0$ so that for all $r > r_0$ and $s>s_0$, we have

  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:crossuniform_n} \mbox{For all } m \in \mathbb{Z}, \mbox{ } [b y ^m a x^{n+r}] [b y^m a x^{n-r}] &\reducesto [(b y^m a x^{n})^2]\\
    \label{eq:crossuniform_m} \mbox{For all } n \in \mathbb{Z},\mbox{ } [b y^{m+s} a x^{n} ] [b y^{m-s} a x^{n}] &\reducesto [(b y^m a x^{n})^2]
  \end{align}
\end{proposition}

The following lemma follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs}.

\begin{lemma}
Given $a,b, x \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$, so that $b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x}$ and $a \cdot \wt{x}$ are parallel, for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, $(ax^n b)^+ \in w(a)$ and $(ax^n b)^- \in w(b^{-1})$.
\label{lem:cornering}
\end{lemma}




\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:fundamental-aybx}]
  We prove Equation~\ref{eq:crossuniform_m} by showing  choose lifts of conjugates of the curves in the left hand-side of the smoothing for which the crossing is  essential. Use the band model, centered on~$\wt{y}$, and normalized so that the translation length of $y$ along $\wt{y}$ is equal to 1. This is what we will mean by \emph{normalized bands model}.  \DMGtodo[inline]{We use this several times, we should extract a definition} We will
  consider the windows on $\bdy^\infty\wt \Sigma$ cut out by certain other
  lifts of~$x$. Specifically, let $w(y^m a)$ be the interval on the
  boundary at infinity cut out by the endpoints of $y^m a \cdot \wt x$
  that does not contain the endpoints $y^\pm$ of $\wt y$. Similarly for
  $w(y^{m-s} a)$, $w(y^{-s} b^{-1})$, and $w(b^{-1})$. All of these are intervals
  on the upper or lower boundary in the bands model, by the initial configuration assumption of Lemma~\ref{lem:initial}.
  See Figure~\ref{fig:aybx}; ``left'' and ``right'' will refer
  to the order on the boundary (minus $x^\pm$) of that figure.

  Observe that (for instance) $w(y^{m} a)$ is a translate of $w(a)$.
  Furthermore, each window is a
  finite size, so there is a number $s>0$ so that $w(y^{m-s}a)$
  is to the left of $w(y^m a)$, and similarly,
   $w(y^{-s}b^{-1})$ is to the left of
  $w(b^{-1})$.
  In fact, since the translation length of $y$ is 1, it suffices to take
  \begin{equation*}
  s \geq s_0 = \lfloor \max \{ \wid{y}(w(a^{-1})),\wid{y}(w(b)) \} \rfloor
  \end{equation*}
  where $\wid{y}(w(b))$ denotes the length of the interval defining the window $w(a)$ at the boundary at infinity of the normalized bands model, where distances in the boundary are measured with the Euclidean distance.
  Consider now the curves
  $y^{m-s}ax^nb$ and $y^m ax^n by^s \in [y^{m+s}ax^nb]$.
  We note that, by the north-south dynamics of $y$, $w(y^{m-s}a)$ is to the left of $w(y^m a)$, and
  $w(y^{-s} b^{-1})$ to the left of $w(b^{-1})$.
  \begin{claim}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $(y^{m-s} a x^n b)^+ \in w(y^{m-s} a)$
  \item $(y^{m-s} a x^n b)^- \in w(b^{-1})$
  \end{itemize}
  \end{claim}
  \begin{proof}
  It follows by Lemma~\ref{lem:cornering}, taking as the elements $a,b$ in that Lemma, the elements $y^{m-s} a$ and $b$. Note that the assumptions of the Lemma are satisfied: indeed, $b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x}$ and $a \cdot \wt{x}$ are parallel, by the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:initial} and north-south dynamics of $y$ guarantees then that $b^{-1} \cdot \wt{x}$ and $y^{m-s} a \cdot \wt{x}$ are parallel.
  \end{proof}
  We also need the following claim, which follows similarly by Lemma~\ref{lem:cornering}.
  \begin{claim}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $(y^{m} a x^n b y^{s})^+ \in w(y^{m} a)$
  \item $(y^{m} a x^n b y^{s})^- \in w(y^{-s} b^{-1})$
  \end{itemize}
  \end{claim}
  
Combined, these mean that
  $y^m a x^{n} b y^s$ crosses $y^{m-s}a x^nb$ to the right.  We have, thus,
  \[
    [a x^{n} b y^{m+s}][y^{m-s}ax^nby^{m-s}]=[y^m ax^{n}b y^s][y^{m-s}ax^nb] \reducesto [(y^max^nb)^2]= [(ax^nby^m)^2]
  \]
  proving Equation~\eqref{eq:crossuniform_n}.
  Equation~\eqref{eq:crossuniform_n} follows similarly: indeed, $\wt{y}$ being parallel to $a \cdot \wt{x}$ and $a \cdot \wt{x}$ being parallel to $ab \cdot \wt{y}$ is equivalent, by acting by $a^{-1}$, to $a^{-1} \cdot \wt{y}$ being parallel to $\wt{x}$ and $\wt{x}$ being parallel to $b \cdot \wt{y}$.
  Thus, we can consider a normalized bands model centered on $\wt{x}$ this time to prove Equation~\eqref{eq:crossuniform_n}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:join-split-geom}
Let $a,b,x \in \pi_1(S)$ be arbitrary curves, with $a$ and $b$
crossing $x$ to the right. Then there is a number $s \ge 0$
  so that, for all
 $n, m, r \in \mathbb{Z}$ with either $s < r < m-n-s$ or $m-n+s < r < -s$, we have
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:cross_a} [a x^n] [b x^m] &\reducesto [ax^{n+r}bx^{m-r}]\\
    \label{eq:cross_b} [a x^nb x^m] &\reducesto [ax^{n+r}][bx^{m-r}].
  \end{align}
  If $x$ is simple and $a=b$, we may take $s=0$ and we have, for all $n$,
  \begin{align}
    [a x^{n+1}] [a x^{n-1}] &\reducesto 2[ax^{n}].\label{eq:cross-red2} \\
    \intertext{If $n \ne m$, there exists $s=s(a) \geq 0$ (with $s=0$ if $x$ is simple), and for all $n,m$ so that for $|n-m| \geq s_0$,}
    [ax^n][ax^m] &\reducesto [x^{\abs{n-m}}].    \label{eq:cross-red3}
  \end{align}
\end{proposition}
We will also use this lemma in Section~\ref{sec:recovery} to show
$\mu_f$ recovers the value of $f$ on curves, so we consider cases when
$x$ is not simple.


\begin{proof}
  We first show that we can choose lifts for which the crossing is
  essential. Use the band model, centered on~$\wt{x}$. We will
  consider the windows on $\bdy^\infty\wt S$ cut out by certain other
  lifts of~$x$. Specifically, let $w(x^na)$ be the interval on the
  boundary at infinity cut out by the endpoints of $x^n a \cdot \wt x$
  that does not contain the endpoints $x^\pm$ of $\wt x$. Similarly for
  $w(x^n a^{-1})$, $w(x^n b)$, and $w(x^n b^{-1})$. All of these are intervals
  on the upper or lower boundary in the bands model, by the assumption
  that $a$ and~$b$ cross $x$ to the right, and the geodesic lift of
  $x^kax^\ell$ has endpoints $(x^kax^\ell)^- \in w(x^{-\ell}a^{-1})$
  and $(x^kax^\ell)^+ \in w(x^ka)$.
  See Figure~\ref{fig:abcrossings}; ``left'' and ``right'' will refer
  to the order on the boundary (minus $x^\pm$) of that figure.

  Observe that (for instance) $w(x^n a)$ is a translate of $w(a)$.
  Furthermore, each window is a
  finite size, so there is a finite range $[s_1,s_2]$ so that $w(a)$
  is to the left of to the right of $w(x^rb)$ if $r \notin [s_1,s_2]$. Similarly there
  is a range $[s_1',s_2']$ so that $w(a^{-1})$ is disjoint from
  $w(x^rb^{-1})$ if $r \notin [s_1',s_2']$. Set
  $s \coloneqq \max\{s_2,s_2',-s_1,-s_1'\}$. Now consider the curves 
  $ax^n$ and $x^rbx^{m-r}$, with $s < r < m-n-s$. We have
  \[
    r-m < -n-s \le -n+s_1',
  \]
  so $w(x^{r-m}b^{-1})$ is to the left of $w(x^{-n}a^{-1})$, and
  \[
    s_2 \le s < r,
  \]
  so $w(x^rb)$ is to the right of $w(a)$. Combined, these mean that
  $x^rbx^{m-r}$ crosses $ax^n$ to the left. Similarly if
  $m-n+s < r < -s$, the curve $x^rbx^{m-r}$ crosses $ax^n$ to the
  right. In either case, we have
  \[
    [ax^n][bx^m] = [ax^n][x^r bx^{m-r}] \reducesto [ax^{n+r}bx^{m-r}]
  \]
  proving Equation~\eqref{eq:cross_a}.

  For Equation~\eqref{eq:cross_b}, write $ax^nbx^m$ as $gh$ with
  $g=ax^{n+r}$ and $h=x^{-r}bx^m$. Note the endpoints of $gh$
  are in $w(x^{-m}b^{-1})$ and $w(a)$, while the endpoints of its
  conjugate $hg =x^{-r}bx^max^{n+r}$ are in $w(x^{-n-r}a^{-1})$ and
  $w(x^{-r}b)$. Then if $s < r < m-n-s$, we have
  \[
    -r < -s \le -s_1,
  \]
  so $w(x^{-r}b)$ is to the left of $w(a)$, while
  \[
    -m \le -m+s+s_1' < -n - r + s_1',
  \]
  so $w(x^{-n-r}a^{-1})$ is to the right of  $w(x^{-m}b^{-1})$, and
  $gh$ has an essential self-crossing giving Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross_b}.
  We similarly get an essential self-crossing if $m-n+s < r < -s$.

  In the cases when $x$ is simple, two windows are disjoint, nested,
  or identical; this implies that $s_1 = s_2$ and $s_1' = s_2'$. If in
  addition $a=b$, we also see that all the $s_i$ are equal to~$0$.
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-red2} follows immediately.

  To see Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-red3}, suppose without loss of generality
  that $n < m$. Let $s_0(a) \coloneqq \lfloor \wid{x}(w(a)) \rfloor$. Observe that, since the bands model is normalized so that the translation length of $x$ is 1, if $x$ is simple, necessarily $\wid{x}(w(a))<1$ (for otherwise two lifts of $x$ would intersect), and thus in that case $s_0=0$. Assume $n-m > s_0$.  Then by the
  analysis above, $(ax^n)^-$ is in $w(x^{-n}a^{-1})$, and thus to the
  right of $(ax^m)^-$, which is in $w(x^{-m}a^{-1})$. For the forward
  endpoints, the above simple analysis just says that both endpoints
  lie in $w(a)$. We can do better by looking deeper in the words:
  $(ax^n)^+$ lies in $w(ax^na)$, meaning the interval between the
  endpoints of $ax^na\cdot\wt x$ that is disjoint from $x^\pm$.
  Similarly $(ax^m)^+$ lies in $w(ax^ma)$, which is to the right of
  $w(ax^na)$. It follows that $ax^n$ crosses $ax^m$ to the right, and
  so
  \[
    [ax^n][ax^m] \reducesto [(ax^n)^{-1}ax^m] = [x^{m-n}]
  \]
  as desired.
\end{proof}
