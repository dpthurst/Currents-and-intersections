\section{Boxes and their measures}

Our goal is to define a measure on the space of geodesics---in fact, a geodesic current---associated to a curve functional~$f$. 
Roughly speaking, our strategy is to associate a positive number, in a
countably additive way, to a collection of boxes of geodesics
sufficiently rich to generate the $\sigma$-algebra of open sets of
$G(\Sigma)$.

To define the measure of our elementary boxes, we look at the
functional~$f$ applied to longer and longer curves~$C_i$. We look at
two different types of asymptotics of $f(C_i)$. First we look at the
normalized growth rate and use this to relate different properties of
curve functionals (Section~\ref{subsec:smoothing_consequences}), in
particular showing that how oriented smoothing and smoothing related, and that connected and disconnected smoothing
imply each other under the assumption of
continuity~(Proposition~\ref{prop:cont_conn_disc}). 

We then turn to the family of boxes of geodesics central to our proof
(Section~\ref{sec:boxes}). Here we look deeper in the asymptotics: the
measure of a box is a linear combination of $f(C_i)$ chosen so that
the highest growth term vanishes, and the limit of interest picks out
the next asymptotics. This is similar to the construction of Otal
\cite{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}.

\label{subsec:smoothing_consequences}

Now we turn to relations between different types of smoothing.
A priori the hypothesis of oriented smoothing on $f$ is weaker than
smoothing, since it does not seem to include unoriented smoothing.
However, it follows from the other hypotheses; the key ingredient is
our previous work in~\cite{MGT21:Smoothings}. We prove the following,
which in particular will allow the simpler algebraic statement in
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections_group}; it is not necessary for the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.

\begin{proposition}
If $f$ is orientation invariant and satisfies oriented smoothing
(connected and disconnected), convex union,
stability, and homogeneity, then $f$ satisfies smoothing.
\label{prop:oriented_smoothing_unoriented}
\end{proposition}

The proof uses the following result, which can be seen as a weak
converse of the main result in our previous paper.
\begin{proposition}
Suppose $f$ is an orientation invariant curve functional satisfying convex union that extends continuously to geodesic currents and is homogeneous.
If $f$ satisfies disconnected smoothing (resp.\ connected smoothing) then $f$ satisfies connected smoothing (resp.\ disconnected smoothing).
\label{prop:cont_conn_disc}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:oriented_smoothing_unoriented}]
  Since $f$ disconnected oriented smoothing and is orientation invariant, it also
  satisfies disconnected unoriented smoothing: indeed, let
  $(\ora{a},\ora{b})$ cross. Then $(\ora{B},\ora{a})$ cross, so by
  disconnected oriented smoothing
\[
f([B][a]) \geq f([Ba]).
\]
Since $f$ is orientation invariant, we have $f([b][a])=f([B][a]) \geq
f([Ba])$, as desired.

Since $f$ satisfies oriented smoothing, convex union, stability and
homogeneity, it extends continuously and homogeneously
to oriented geodesic currents, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Hence by
Proposition~\ref{prop:cont_conn_disc}, it satisfies connected
smoothing, and thus all cases of smoothing.
\end{proof}

As an immediate corollary, given that a symmetric and additive
functional is orientation invariant, we have the following.

\begin{corollary}
If $f$ is symmetric and satisfies oriented smoothing, additivity and
stability, then $f$ satisfies smoothing.
\label{cor:oriented_smoothing_unoriented}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cont_conn_disc}]
Suppose first $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies convex union, disconnected smoothing, homogeneity and is continuous.
Given a pair $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ of parallel elements (so
$[ab] \reducesto [a][b]$ and $[ab]\reducesto[aB]$), we show first $f$
satisfies oriented connected
smoothing, i.e., $f([ab]) \geq f([a][b])$.
We will start by showing, by induction on $n$, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power_ineq}
2nf([ab]) \geq f([a^{2n}b^{2n}]).
\end{equation}
The base case $n=1$ follows from the fact that if $\ora{a},\ora{b}$
are parallel, $\ora{ab}$ and $\ora{ba}$ cross, and hence
\[
2[ab]=[ab] + [ba] \reducesto [ab^2a]=[a^2b^2].
\]
Hence, by homogeneity and disconnected smoothing, the base case follows.
Now, we prove the induction step.
\begin{align*}
2nf([ab]) = 2(n-1)f([ab]) + 2f([ab])
&\geq f([a^{2(n-1)}b^{2(n-1)}]) + f([b^2a^2])  \\
&\geq f([a^{2(n-1)}b^{2(n-1)}][b^2a^2]) \\
&\geq f([a^{2(n-1)}b^{2(n-1)} b^2a^2]) = f([a^{2n}b^{2n}]).
\end{align*}
In the first inequality we used induction step, in the second convex union, then Lemma~\ref{lem:anbn_vs_bkak} and the assumption that $f$ satisfies disconnected smoothing.
Divide Eq.~\eqref{eq:power_ineq} by $2n$, let $n$ go to infinity, and
use Lemma~\ref{lem:gnhn_to_gsumh} to obtain
\[
f([ab]) \geq f([a] [b])
\]
which proves oriented disconnected smoothing.

Second, we prove unoriented connected smoothing: with $a,b$ as above,
$f([ab]) \ge f([aB])$.

We split into two cases, depending on the relative position of $\ora{aB}$
and $\ora{Ba}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:ab_parallel_aB}, $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$ are either anti-parallel
or cross.

We first consider the case when $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$ are anti-parallel. We will prove by induction that
\begin{equation}
2nf([ab]) \geq f([(aB)^n (Ab)^n]).
\label{eq:antiparallel_induction}
\end{equation}
The base case, $n=1$, follows since $\ora{ab}$ and $\ora{ba}$ cross, hence
\[
[ba]+[ab] \reducesto [ba(ab)^{-1}] = [(aB)(Ab)].
\]
By homogeneity and disconnected smoothing, we get the base case.
Now, we prove the induction step:
\begin{align*}
2nf([ab]) = 2(n-1)f([ab]) + 2f([ab]) &\geq f([(aB)^{n-1} (Ab)^{n-1}]) + f([(Ab)(aB)]) \\
  &\geq f([(aB)^{n-1} (Ab)^{n-1} (Ab)(aB)]) = f([(aB)^n (Ab)^n]).
\end{align*}
In the first inequality we used the induction step. The second inequality
uses convex union and disconnected smoothing applied to
$[(aB)^{n-1}(Ab)^{n-1}][(Ab)(aB)] \reducesto [(aB)^{n-1} (Ab)^{n-1}
(Ab)(aB)]$. For this reduction, note that $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$ are
anti-parallel, so $\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ab}$ are parallel. Hence, we
can apply Lemma~\ref{lem:anbn_vs_bkak} to conclude that
$\ora{(aB)^{n-1} (Ab)^{n-1}}$ and $\ora{(Ab)(aB)}$ cross.
Divide~\ref{eq:antiparallel_induction} by $2n$, let $n$ go to
infinity, and use Lemma~\ref{lem:gnhn_to_gsumh} to get
\[
f([ab]) \geq \frac{f([aB] [Ab])}{2} = f([aB])
\]
where we used orientation invariance and homogeneity in the last equality.

Now, still in the unoriented connected smoothing case, suppose
$\ora{aB}$ and $\ora{Ba}$ cross.
If $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ are $R$-parallel it follows by Lemma~\ref{lem:a_parallel_Ba} that $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{bA}$ are R-parallel.
Hence, 
\[
f([ba]) = f([bA)(a^2)]) \geq f([bA]) + f(a^2) \geq f([bA])
\]
where in the first inequality we used oriented connected smoothing, and in the last inequality the fact that $f(a^2) \geq 0$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:curve-positive} below.
Similarly, if $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ are $L$-parallel it follows that $\ora{b}$ and $\ora{a}$ are $R$-parallel, and so by Lemma~\ref{lem:a_parallel_Ba}, $\ora{b}$ and $\ora{aB}$ are $R$-parallel.
Hence, 
\[
f([ab]) = f([aB)(b^2)]) \geq f([aB]) + f(b^2) \geq f([aB])
\]
as before.
This finishes the proof that disconnected smoothing implies
connected smoothing.

We now prove the second part, namely, if $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies orientation invariance, connected smoothing, homogeneity and continuity, then $f$ satisfies disconnected smoothing. The proof is very similar to the first part. Namely, given $a$ and $b$ crossing, we prove, by induction on $n$, the following inequality.
\[
f([a^{2n}b^{2n}]) \geq 2nf([ab]).
\]
The base case follows by noting that if $\ora{a}$, $\ora{b}$ R-cross, then $\ora{ab}$ and $\ora{ba}$ are R-parallel, and then by connected smoothing
\[
f([a^2b^2])=f([ab^2a]) \geq 2f([ab]).
\]
For the induction step, note that
\begin{align*}
f([a^{2n}b^{2n}]) = f([a^2(a^{2(n-1)}b^{2(n-1)})b^2]) = f([b^2a^2(a^{2(n-1)}b^{2(n-1)})]) \\
\geq f([b^2a^2]) + f([a^{2(n-1)}b^{2(n-1)})]) = 2f([ab]) + 2(n-1)f([ab]) = 2nf([ab]).
\end{align*}
To get the inequality we used that $b^2a^2$ and $a^{2(n-1)}b^{2(n-1)}$ are parallel, which follows from the fact that $a$ and $b$ are crossing, and Lemma~\ref{lem:anbn_vs_bkak_parallel}.
Finally, using Lemma~\ref{lem:gnhn_to_gsumh} as above, we get
\[
f([a] [b]) \geq f([ab])
\]
which proves oriented disconnected smoothing. The unoriented one is proved in the same way, replacing $b$ by $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The statements of
Propositions~\ref{prop:oriented_smoothing_unoriented}
and~\ref{prop:cont_conn_disc} can be strengthened by replacing each
type of smoothing by the same type of $C$-quasi-smoothing.
\end{remark}

As an immediate corollary, we get the following.

\begin{corollary}
Suppose $f$ is a symmetric, additive curve functional that extends continuously to geodesic currents.
If $f$ satisfies disconnected smoothing (resp.\ connected smoothing) then $f$ satisfies connected smoothing (resp.\ disconnected smoothing).
\label{cor:cont_conn_disc}
\end{corollary}

For example, curve lengths from length metric structures on the surface
or word-lengths with respect to some generating sets give natural such
functionals.
See Section~\ref{subsec:examples_and_non} for many examples and non-examples of curve functionals.

We can now reestate Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} from the perspective of curve functionals on oriented curves.

\begin{mainthmprimeprime}
    Suppose that a curve functional $f \colon \mathcal{C}^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies the oriented smoothing property
     \[
      f\left(\mfig{curves-3}\right) \ge f\left(\mfig{curves-4}\right)
   \]
   for every essential crossing of a multi-curve on the surface and, moreover, $f$ additive, symmetric and stable.
Then $f$ has a dual geodesic current, i.e., there exists $\mu_f$ so that
\[
i(\mu_f,C)=f(C)
\]
for every curve $C$.
\label{thm:intersect_oriented}
\end{mainthmprimeprime}





\subsection{Right-handed boxes}
\label{sec:boxes}

We now turn to the outline of the construction of the ``sufficiently rich family of boxes'' on which our measure will be defined.

Let $f\colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ be a
functional satisfying symmetry, additive union, smoothing and stability.

We wish to construct a current $\mu_f$ and show that
$f(C) = i(C, \mu_f)$. To construct~$\mu_f$, as mentioned above, we fix
(arbitrarily) a simple closed oriented curve~$[x]$ passing through the
basepoint. The endpoints of the lifts of $[x]$ are dense in
$\partial_\infty S$, by minimality of the action of $\pi_1(S)$
(reviewed in Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal}).

The main objects in our proof are certain boxes in the
space of geodesics to which we will assign measures. The prototype for
the kind of boxes we consider is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:newmodel3}: we have four geodesics, all lifts of~$[x]$, with
two lifts $\ora{\ell_1},\ora{\ell_2}$ on the left and two $\ora{r_1},\ora{r_2}$ on the right,

The measure that we ultimately assign to the associated windows will
be invariant under
\begin{itemize}
\item changing $p_i$ preserving $\ell_i$, by $p_i \mapsto xp_i$ and
\item changing $q_i$ preserving $r_i$, by $q_i \mapsto q_ix$.; and
\end{itemize}
These two conditions are clearly necessary since the endpoints of $\ell_i$ and $r_i$ are invariant under such transformations.
In fact, ultimately we will take a limit of these
transformations: we look at the limit as $n\to \infty$ of $p_i \mapsto x^np_i$
and $q_i \mapsto q_i x^n$.

The measure $\mu$ will also be $\pi_1(S)$ invariant, which translates to
invariance under
\begin{itemize}
\item translating all the lifts by $g \in \pi_1(S)$:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:box-translate}
    (p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2) \mapsto g \cdot (p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2) \coloneqq
      (p_1 G, gq_1, p_2 G,  gq_2).
  \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
We will put off imposing this last invariance for the moment.

We now turn to the conditions on the lifts; for
instance, the window
$(\ell_1^-,\ell_2^-] \times [r_2^+,r_1^+)$ must avoid the
diagonal.

\begin{definition}
  A \emph{right-handed lift system} is a collection of elements
  $(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2)$ in $\pi_1(S)$ so that
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item\label{item:box-nontriv-lift} $p_1P_2$ and $Q_1q_2$ are not powers of~$x$;
  \item\label{item:box-edges-cross-lift} for $i,j \in \{1,2\}$,
    $\ora{p_iq_j}$ crosses $\ora{x}$ to the right; and
  \item\label{item:box-diags-cross-lift} for sufficiently large~$n$,
    $\ora{q_2x^np_1}$ crosses $\ora{q_1x^np_2}$ to the right.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

See Figure~\ref{fig:rhbox} for examples of
RH systems, and Figure~\ref{fig:box4} for a non-example.
 We
distinguish three types of RH systems: type 1 has
no nested lifts of $[x]$; type 2 has one parallel pair of nested lifts; and
type 3 has two parallel pairs of nested lifts.
Note that since $[x]$ is simple, lifts
cannot intersect each other, and nested lifts cannot be anti-parallel
by condition \ref{item:box-edges-cross}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:RH-box}
  Every right handed lift system
  $(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2)$ determines a \emph{right-handed box}, a
  half-closed half-open box in
  $\partial_{\infty} \Sigma \times \partial_{\infty} \Sigma - \Delta$
  given by
\[
B(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2) \coloneqq (\ell_1^-,\ell_2^-] \times [r_2^+,r_1^+)
\]
where $\ora{\ell_i} = P_i \cdot \ora{x}$ and $\ora{r_i} = q_i \cdot \ora{x}$.

Modifying the right-handed lift system by changing $p_i$ preserving
$\ora{\ell_i}$, or changing $q_i$ preserving $\ora{r_i}$, determines
the same right-handed box.
The set of right-handed boxes is denoted $\mathcal{B}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rh-boxes}
  Let $(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2)$ be a RH lift system determining a RH box with $\ora{\ell_i} =
  P_i \cdot \ora{x}$ and $\ora{r_i} = q_j \cdot \ora{x}$. Then the
  endpoints $\ell_1^-, \ell_2^-, r_2^+, r_1^+$ appear in that
  counter-clockwise order. In particular the boxes in $\cB$ avoid the
  diagonal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We first note that condition \ref{item:box-edges-cross-lift} implies
  that for any $i,j \in \{1,2\}$, $\ora{x}$ and $p_iq_j\cdot \ora{x}$
  are R-parallel (Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs}) and thus (by translation by
  $P_i$) that $(\ora{\ell_i}, \ora{r_j})$ are R-parallel.

  To exploit condition~\ref{item:box-diags-cross-lift}, we prove a lemma.

  \begin{lemma}\label{lem:box-endpoints}
    In the setting above, fix $n \in \bbZ$ and $i,j \in \{1,2\}$, and
    set $g = q_j x^n p_i$. Then $g$ is hyperbolic, with
    \begin{align*}
      g^- &\in G P_i \cdot (x^+,x^-) \subset (\ell_i^+,\ell_i^-)\\
      g^+ &\in g q_j \cdot (x^-,x^+) \subset (r_j^+,r_j^-).
%      g^- &\in P_i x^{-n}Q_j P_i \cdot (x^+,x^-) \subset (\ell_i^+,\ell_i^-)\\
%      g^+ &\in q_j x^n p_i q_j \cdot (x^-,x^+) \subset (r_j^+,r_j^-).
    \end{align*}
  \end{lemma}

  \begin{proof}
    First note that $g \cdot (\ell_i^-,\ell_i^+) = q_j x^n p_i P_i
    \cdot (x^-,x^+) = (r_j^-,r_j^+) \subset (\ell_i^-,\ell_i^+)$, so
    $g^-,g^+$ are in the regions cut out by $\ora{\ell_i}, \ora{r_j}$,
    respectively. 

    For convenience, set
    $\ora{\ell_{i,j,n}} = P_i x^{-n}Q_j \cdot \ora{\ell_i}$ and
    $\ora{r_{j,i,n}} = q_j x^n p_i \cdot \ora{r_j}$. Now consider
    translating $(\ora{\ell_i}, \ora{r_j})$ by $g^\pm$. We find
    \begin{align*}
      g \cdot (\ora{\ell_i}, \ora{r_j}) &= (\ora{r_j}, \ora{r_{j,i,n}})\\
      G \cdot (\ora{\ell_i}, \ora{r_j}) &= (\ora{\ell_{i,j,n}}, \ora{\ell_i}).
    \end{align*}
    The result follows.
  \end{proof}

  Returning to the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rh-boxes}, note that
  $r_j \cdot \ora{r_{j,i,n}} = \ora{r_{j,i,n+1}}$ (setting
  $r_j = q_jxQ_j$ to match the convention on geodesics). Thus, by NS
  dynamics of $r_j$, we have
  $\lim_{n\to\infty} r_{j,i,n}^\pm = r_j^+$. Similarly
  $\lim_{n\to\infty} \ell_{i,j,n}^\pm = \ell_i^-$. If we set
  $g_{i,j,n} = q_j x^n p_i$, it follows that
  $\lim_{n\to\infty} g_{i,j,n}^+ = r_j^+$ and
  $\lim_{n\to\infty} g_{i,j,n}^- = \ell_i^-$.

  By condition~\ref{item:box-diags-cross-lift}, for sufficiently large $n$
  the endpoints $g_{1,2,n}^-, g_{2,1,n}^-, g_{1,2,n}^+, g_{2,1,n}^+$
  appear in that ccw order, which by the limiting behavior implies
  that $\ell_1^-,\ell_2^-,r_2^+,r_1^+$ appear in that order, as desired. (At
  the last step we need condition~\ref{item:box-nontriv-lift} to
  guarantee that $\ell_1 \ne \ell_2$ and $r_1 \ne r_2$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[RH orbit]
\label{def:RH-lift-sys}
Let $a,b,c,d \in \pi_1(S,\ast)$ be such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*${}'$),start=0]
\item\label{item:box-close} $a C d B = 1$;
\item\label{item:box-nontriv} $aC(=bD)$ and $Ab(=Cd)$ are not powers of~$x$;
\item\label{item:box-edges-cross} $\ora{a},\ora{b},\ora{c},\ora{d}$ cross $\ora{x}$ to the right; and
\item\label{item:box-diags-cross} for sufficiently large~$n$,
  $\ora{dx^na C}
  (= \ora{cAbx^na C})$ crosses
  $\ora{cx^n}$ to the right.
\end{enumerate}

Then we call the datum $[a,b,c,d]$ a \emph{right-handed lift orbit} (or \emph{RH orbit}, for short).
\end{definition}

As explained above, this determines a $\pi_1(S)$-orbit of windows.
The one non-obvious point to check is that
condition~\ref{item:box-diags-cross} corresponds
to condition~\ref{item:box-diags-cross-lift}:
\begin{align*}
  cx^n &= p_2 q_1 x^n& dx^naC &= p_2 q_2 x^n p_1 P_2\\
  \intertext{which after conjugating by $P_2$ yields}
  q_1 x^n p_2 &= g_{2,1,n} & q_2 x^n p_1 &= g_{1,2,n}.
\end{align*}
Thus the two crossing conditions are equivalent.

  $[B]_{a,b,c,d}$ depending on whether we want to
  draw attention to a concrete representative of the orbit or to the
  orbit itself.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Measures of boxes}

We will now start setting up the definition of the measure of an orbit
of right-handed box $[B]_{a,b,c,d}$. In particular, if $\mathcal{B}$
denotes the family of right-handed boxes, we will define a function
$\mu_f \colon \mathcal{B} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ in terms of a limit
involving the $f$-value of a certain combination of words involving
$a,b,c,d,x$. We will then prove the limit exists using the properties
of~$f$ introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:mainhypotheses} and the
results about how certain words behave under smoothings proven in
Section~\ref{subsec:crossings}. Finally, we will show $\mathcal{B}$ is
a semi-ring (in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:semiring}) and
$\mu_f$ is finitely additive.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:limitexists}
Let $B(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2)$ be a right-handed box so that $[B(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2)]=[B]_{a,b,c,d}$.
Then following limit exists:
\[
\mu_{f}(B(p_1,q_1,p_2,q_2))\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\lim_{n \to \infty}f([bx^n]) + f([cx^n])-f([ax^n]) - f([dx^n]).
\]
\end{proposition}
Note this function on boxes (which will eventually become a pre-measure and
then a measure) is automatically $\pi_1(S)$-invariant.

To prove Proposition~\ref{prop:limitexists}, we
first we look at a key ingredient limit.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:additivity-limit}
Let $f$ be a curve functional satisfying additivity, smoothing, and
stability.
Let $a,x \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$, with $\ora{a}$ crossing $\ora{x}$ to the right. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:additivity-limit}
    \lim_{n \to \infty} f([a x^n]) - f([x^n])
  \end{equation}
  exists.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  By Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-join}, with $b=a$, there exists $r>0$ so
  that, for all $n$,
  \[
    f([ax^{n-r}]) + f([ax^{n+r}]) \geq 2 f([ax^n]).
  \]
  (If $x$ is simple, we can take $r=1$.) Here we used smoothing and
  convex union. (Quasi-smoothing does not suffice; see
  Remark~\ref{rmk:nonconvex}.)
  Hence, $n \mapsto f([a x^n])$ is $r$-convex, and
  thus so is $g(n) \coloneqq f([a x^n]) - f([x^n]) = f([a x^n]) -
  nf([x])$, using stability of~$f$.
 
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod} and additivity and (oriented)
 smoothing of~$f$, we get
 \[
 f([a]) + f([x^{n}]) \geq f([ax^{n}]).
 \]
 By Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-cancel} with $m=0$, and by
additivity and (unoriented)
smoothing of $f$, there is $N>0$ (depending on $a,x$) so that, for $n > N$,
  \[
    f([a x^n]) \ge f([a]) + f([x^n]).
  \]
  This shows that $g$ for $n > N$ is an $r$-convex and
  bounded function, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:limexistrmidconvex}, the
  limit \eqref{eq:additivity-limit} exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:limitexists}]
This follows immediately from Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity-limit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The limit in Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity-limit} need not exist if one
only assumes $f$ satisfies quasi-smoothing.
We give an example on the once-holed torus.
Let $S$ be the once-punctured torus with generating set $\{a,b,b^2\}$,
where $a$ and $b$ are the standard generators of $\pi_1(S)$, chosen so
$\ora{a}$ crosses $\ora{b}$ to the right.
Let $f$ be the stable word-length with respect to this generating set. Note that $f$ satisfies quasi-smoothing instead of smoothing (see \cite[Example~4.10]{MGT21:Smoothings}) for details).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rhs_prod}, $\ora{ab}$ also crosses $\ora{b}$ to the
right.
The expression in the limit in \eqref{eq:additivity-limit} for the
pair $ab$ and $b$ is
\begin{equation*}
  f([ab^{n+1}])-f([b^n])=\begin{cases} 1+ \frac{n+2}{2} - \frac{n}{2}=2& \mbox{ if } n \mbox{ is even } \\
    1+ \frac{n+1}{2} - \frac{n+1}{2}=1 & \mbox{ if } n \mbox{ is odd }
  \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
which does not approach a limit.
This example can be promoted to one on the genus-two surface.
\label{rmk:nonconvex}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Convexity of $f([ax^n])$ as a function of $n$ plays a key role in the work of Feng Luo of characterization of intersection numbers for simple closed curves \cite[Corollary~4.4]{Luo98:IntersectionNumbers}.
Luo's work characterizes functions $f \colon \mathcal{S}(S) \to
\mathbb{R}$ on simple closed curves that come from intersection
numbers in terms of an infinite family of polynomial equations
involving the $f$-value of triples of simple curves in the $(\max,+)$
semi-ring structure on the real numbers.
One of the tools in the proof of this characterization is the fact
that, for $a,b$ simple curves, the function $h(n) \coloneqq f([a^n
b])$ is convex.
In the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity-limit}, we generalize this to the case where $a,b$ might be non-simple, obtaining  $r$-convexity of~$g$.
See Proposition~\ref{prop:feng} for how our conditions on a curve function $f$ imply Luo's conditions for the restriction of $f$ to simple curves.
\end{remark}

We will also later need a two-variable version of
Lemma~\ref{lem:additivity-limit}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:additivity-limit-2}
  Let $a,b,x,y \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ be so that
  $(B \cdot \ora{x}, \ora{y}, a \cdot \ora{x})$ are R-parallel, and
  let $f \colon \Curves(S) \to \bbR$ satisfy additivity, smoothing,
  stability, and homogeneity. Then
  \begin{equation}
    \lim_{n,m \to \infty} f([ax^nby^m]) - nf([x]) - mf([y])
  \end{equation}
  exists.  
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Define $g(n,m) \coloneqq f([ax^nby^m]) - nf([x]) - mf([y])$. First,
  by Corollary~\ref{cor:fund-axby}, there is some $s$ so that $g(n,m)$
  is $(s,s)$-axis-convex: apply $f$ to the left hand side of
  Eqs.~\eqref{eq:axby-cross-n} and~\eqref{eq:axby-cross-m}, and use
  convex union, smoothing, stability, and homogeneity. (The extra
  linear terms in $g(n,m)$ are irrelevant for convexity.)

  Next we check that $g$ is bounded.
  On the one hand,
  \[
    [ab][x^n][y^m] \reducesto [ax^nb][y^m] \reducesto [ax^nby^m].
  \]
  For the first reduction, the hypotheses imply that
  $(\ora{x}, ba\cdot\ora{x})$ are R-parallel. For the second one, note
  that
  $ax^n b\cdot(B\cdot x^+, B\cdot x^-) = (a\cdot x^+, a\cdot x^-)$;
  then a look at the bands model centered on $\ora{y}$ shows that
  $\ora{a x^n b}$ crosses $\ora{y}$.   Applying $f$ and using additivity
  and smoothing shows that
  \[
    g(n,m) \le f([ab]).
  \]

  To get a lower bound, applying $f$ to both sides of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:cancel-axby} and using additivity and smoothing shows
  that, for $n, m$ sufficiently large,
  \[
    g(n,m) \ge -f([ab]).
  \]

   Thus by
   Lemma~\ref{lem:limexistrmidconvextwovar} the limit exists.
\end{proof}

Now we check the hypotheses to apply
 Theorem~\ref{thm:caratheodory} to construct a measure.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:join-split-alg}
Let $a,b,x$ be arbitrary elements of $\pi_1(S)$.
  If $a$ and $b$ cross $x$ to the right, then
  \[
    \lim_{n,m\to\infty} f([a x^n b x^m]) - f([ax^n]) - f([bx^m]) = 0.
  \]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  First, Lemmas~\ref{lem:additivity-limit}
  and~\ref{lem:additivity-limit-2} imply that the limit
  exists. We must now show that the limits as $n,m \to \infty$ of
  \begin{align*}
    g(n,m) &= f([ax^nbx^m]) - (n+m)f([x])\\
    \shortintertext{and}
    h(n,m) &= f([ax^n]) + f([bx^m]) - (n+m)f([x])
  \end{align*}
  are equal.
  Equation \eqref{eq:cross-join} says that there are arbitrarily large
  $n,m$ so that $g(n',m') \le h(n,m)$ with $n'+m' = n+m$, and $n',m'$
  closer to the diagonal. This immediately implies
  $\lim_{n,m \to\infty} g(n,m) \le \lim_{n,m\to\infty} h(n,m)$.
  (Take
  $n,m$ large enough so that both $h(n,m)$ and $g(n',m')$ are within
  $\epsilon$ of their limiting value.) Conversely,
  Equation~\eqref{eq:cross-split} implies the opposite inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:box-positive}
  In the terminology of Section~\ref{sec:boxes}, for a right-handed
  box $B\in \mathcal{B}$, we have $\mu_f(B) \geq 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Set $[B] = [B]_{a,b,c,d}$.
  By condition~\ref{item:box-diags-cross}, for sufficiently large~$n$,
  \[
    [bx^n][cx^n] = [dx^naC][cx^n] \reducesto [dx^nax^n].
  \]
  and thus
\[
f([bx^n]) + f([cx^n]) -f([dx^nax^n]) \geq 0.
\]
  Then by Proposition~\ref{prop:join-split-alg} and the
  limit as $n \to \infty$, we obtain
  \[
  \mu_f(B) \geq 0. \qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark} 
 The existence of the double limits in Lemma~\ref{prop:join-split-alg} (and the other limits in this section) is reminiscent of the existence of
 similar limits in the context of the eigenvalue projection function
 on reductive Lie groups (see~\cite[Lemma~7.10]{BQ16:Random}). See also Section~\ref{subsec:examples_and_non} for examples of length functions arising from generalized eigenvalues of higher rank representations.
 \end{remark}

  Suppose first that there is no (vertical or horizontal) ``long''
  whisker, passing through the interior of the upper or right edge
  of~$B_0$. (Such a whisker necessarily also passes through the bottom
  or left edge as well.) Then the partition is in fact trivial, since
  the only possible box with these whiskers as sides that contains a
  point near the upper-right corner of~$B_0$ is $B_0$ itself.

  Otherwise, there is at least one long whisker. Pick one such long
  whisker and use it to write $B_0 = B_1' \sqcup B_2'$. Subdivide any
  of the $B_i$ that intersect both $B_1'$ and $B_2'$, in a
  side-by-side way, getting sets of boxes $B_{i,1}$ and $B_{i,2}$,
  partitions of $B_1'$ and $B_2'$ respectively. These give
  subdivisions of $B_1'$ and $B_2'$ that involve fewer
  whiskers. Thus by induction,
  \[
    \mu_f(B_0) = \mu_f(B_1') + \mu_f(B_2') = \sum_j \mu_f(B_{j,1}) +
    \sum_k \mu_f(B_{k,2}) = \sum_i \mu_f(B_i). \qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}
