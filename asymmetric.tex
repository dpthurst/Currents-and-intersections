\section{Asymmetric intersection number and non-examples}
\label{sec:asymmetric}
One might wonder whether Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} applies in the more general setting of
not-necessarily-symmetric curve functionals~$f$. One could hope that
asymmetric intersections with $\mu_f$ (Definition~\ref{def:asymmetric}) recover $f$ in the more
general context. In this section, we dash this hope: there are
asymmetric curve functionals satisfying oriented smoothing and additivity that
are not realized as asymmetric intersection numbers with any oriented
geodesic currents.

  Recall that in~Definition~\ref{def:asymmetric} we introduced an asymmetric
intersection number $\vec i (\cdot, \cdot)$ between oriented geodesic currents and showed how to evaluate the intersection between a geodesic current and an oriented curve Lemma~\ref{lem:asymmetric_intersection_current}. In particular, $\vec i(\vec C, \vec D)$ recovers the right-handed intersections between oriented curves $\vec C$ and $\vec D$.
We will emphasize curves are oriented by writing
$\vec C$ or $\vec{[g]}$ instead of $C$ or $[g]$, and will write $\vec
C^{-1}$ or $\vec{[G]}$ for the oriented
curve with the opposite orientation.


\begin{definition}
Recall that $h(\vec C)\in H_1(\Sigma)$ denotes the homology class
  associated to~$\vec C$. Let
  $\langle h(\vec C), h(\vec D)\rangle$ or
  $\langle \vec C, \vec D\rangle$ be the algebraic intersection
  number, the number of right-handed crossings minus the number of
  left-handed crossings in any generic representatives of $\vec C$
  and~$\vec D$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:asym-intersect}
  The asymmetric intersection number on curves satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:asym-intersect}
    \vec{\imath}(\vec C, \vec D) = {\textstyle\frac12}\,i(C, D) +
    {\textstyle\frac12}\langle h(\vec C), h(\vec D)\rangle.
  \end{equation}
  It extends continuously to oriented currents.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The equality is 
  immediate from the definitions.

  The continuous extension to currents follows from the fact that the map
  $h \colon \Curves^+(S) \to H_1(S)$ sending an oriented curve to a
  homology class extends continuously to
  oriented currents.
  (The map~$h$ vanishes on oriented currents that are flip-invariant.) Indeed, given $\mu \in \Curr(S)$, we can define a linear map  $h_\mu \colon H^1(S,\mathbb{R})\to \mathbb{R}$ on de Rham cohomology
  \[
  [\omega] \mapsto \int_{T^1 S} \omega_x(v)d\hat{\mu}(x,v)
  \]
  where $\hat{\mu}$ is the geodesic flow invariant measure on
  $T^1 S$
  induced by $\mu$. The map $h$ is then defined by sending $\mu
  \mapsto h_\mu \in
  H_1(S;\mathbb{R})$; see
  \cite{Schwartzman:AsymptoticCycles,RuelleSullivan:CurrentsFlowsDiffeomorphisms}
  for further
  details.
  Using this fact, both terms on the RHS of \eqref{eq:asym-intersect}
  are continuous on currents.

  Alternately, the standard proof of continuity of $i(\cdot,\cdot)$
  reduces to carefully upper
bounding the measure of a current $\alpha \times \beta$ near the
diagonal of the locus of transversely intersecting geodesics
$\mathcal{D}(S)$ (see,
e.g.,~\cite[Theorem~8.2.21]{Martelli16:IntroGeoTop}).
Since by definition $\vec\imath(\alpha,\beta) \leq i(\alpha,\beta)$,
the same
proof gives that $\vec\imath(\cdot,\cdot) \colon \Curr^+(S) \times
\Curr^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous.
\end{proof}

As a result, we have strong constraints on which asymmetric curve
functionals can come from an asymmetric intersection number.

For any curve $\vec C$, let
$r(\vec C) = \OneHalf(\vec C - \vec C^{-1})$ be the anti-symmetrized
virtual copy of $\vec C$. Here a virtual curve is a weighted
curve where the coefficients are allowed to be negative. Similarly
for an oriented multi-curve $\vec C = \sum_i a_i \vec C_i$, define
$r(\vec C) = \sum_i a_i r(\vec C_i)$.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:asym-constraint}
  For an oriented multi-curve $\vec C$ so that $h(\vec C) = 0$ and for
  any curve~$\vec D$, we have
  \[
    \vec{\imath}(r(\vec{C}), \vec D) = 0.
  \]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Immediate from Lemma~\ref{lem:asym-intersect}: both terms vanish.
\end{proof}

This shows that an oriented geodesic current is \emph{not} determined
by its asymmetric intersection numbers with all curves, and
hence the space of oriented geodesic currents cannot be realized as
subspace of the space of (asymmetric) curve functionals. In contrast,
flip-invariant geodesic currents are determined by their geometric
intersection numbers with all closed
curves~\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~2]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}.

In the other direction, we can construct asymmetric functionals
satisfying smoothing, stability and additivity that cannot come
from asymmetric intersection numbers, from looking at directed graphs
on a surface; compare \cite[Section~4.6]{MGT21:Smoothings}

\begin{example}\label{examp:flat-torus}
  We start with an example on the unpunctured torus~$T$,  not quite in the
  framework of this paper.
  On~$T$, consider the oriented graph $\Delta$ that divides the torus into two
  triangles (with opposite orientation):
  \[
    \Delta = \mfig{hex-torus-0}
  \]
  Label the edges $\vec a, \vec b, \vec c$, as shown, and let
  $\ell_\Delta \colon \Curves^+(T) \to \bbZ$ be
  word-length with respect to this monoid generating set. (In terms of
  standard generators  $a,b$, we have $c = (ab)^{-1}$.) We have the
following behavior:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l_Delta}
  \ell_\Delta(a^k b^\ell) =
%  \mathcenter{\resizebox{50mm}{!}{\svgfig{length_asymmetric}}}.
  \mfig{hex-torus-10}.
\end{equation}
Here we have drawn the $(k,\ell)$ grid slanted, to reflect the triangular
symmetry of the generating set.
  This word length satisfies oriented smoothing: we are looking at
  length with respect to the embedded, oriented graph~$\Delta$.
As
such, given a concrete representative for an oriented multi-curve in
a neighborhood of~$\Delta$, we can smooth any essential
crossing and get a new oriented multi-curve still in the neighborhood
by the same smoothing argument as in Corollary~\ref{maincor:length_dual}.
Concretely, in this setting, smoothing
amounts to requiring that the function $f(a^k b^\ell)$ is a convex
function of $k,\ell$, which can readily be verified directly.

Now consider the multi-curve
$\vec C = \vec{[a]} + \vec{[b]} + \vec{[c]}$. We have $h(\vec C) = 0$,
so, by Proposition~\ref{prop:asym-constraint},
$\vec\imath(\vec C, \vec D)$ for any curve $\vec D$. But the
values of $\ell_\Delta$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:l_Delta} do not satisfy this
constraint: the alternating sum of the values around the central
hexagon is not zero.
Contrast this with
asymmetric intersection numbers: here is $\vec\imath(\vec{[\gamma]},
\cdot)$ where $[\gamma]$ ranges over the shortest curves on the
hexagonal torus,
up to symmetries:
\begin{align*}
    \vec\imath(\vec{[a]}, \cdot) &= \mfig{hex-torus-11} &
    \vec\imath(\ora{[ac^{-1}]}, \cdot) &= \mfig{hex-torus-12}\\
    \vec\imath(\ora{[a^2c^{-1}]}, \cdot) &= \mfig{hex-torus-13} &
    \vec\imath(\ora{[a^3c^{-1}]}, \cdot) &= \mfig{hex-torus-14}.
\end{align*}
These functions all satisfy
Proposition~\ref{prop:asym-constraint}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
  We can upgrade Example~\ref{examp:flat-torus} to one living on a
  genus~$2$ surface (so hyperbolic and in the context of this paper.)
  Divide the genus~$2$ surface into two once-punctured tori, consider
  monoid generators $a,b,c$ on the right-hand torus as above,
  considered as edges with very long length in an embedded directed
  graph, and supplement them with short monoid generators on the
  left-hand torus and the dividing meridian so lengths are very close
  to the above lengths in the unpunctured torus. Let $\Gamma$ be the
  resulting metric graph; arrange for $\Gamma$ to be embedded. Then
  the argument above shows $\ell_\Gamma$ satisfies oriented smoothing,
  while similar arguments show that the constraint of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:asym-constraint}
  is not satisfied so $\ell_\Gamma$ cannot be realized as an asymmetric
  intersection number.
\end{example}
