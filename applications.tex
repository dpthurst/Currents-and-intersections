\begin{proposition}
Given two elements $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ that are not conjugates of each other, with hyperbolic axes $A_g$ and $A_h$ that intersect at a point $O$, and translation lengths $\ell(g),\ell(h)$.
Let $P$ be the point on $A_g$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(g)/2$. Let $Q$ be the point on $A_h$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $-\ell(h)/2$.
Let $Q'$ be the point on $A_h$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(h)/2$. Let $P'$ be the point on $A_g$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $-\ell(g)/2$.
we have the following (see Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item The hyperbolic axis of $A_{gh}$ is determined by the geodesic line going from $Q$ to $P$, and the hyperbolic distance from $Q$ to $P$ is $\ell(gh)/2$.
\item The hyperbolic axis of $A_{hg}$ is determined by the geodesic line going from $P'$ to $Q'$, and the hyperbolic distance from $Q'$ to $P'$ is $\ell(hg)/2$.
\item The hyperbolic axis of $A_{gh^{-1}}$ is determined by the geodesic line going from $Q'$ to $P$, and the hyperbolic distance from $Q'$ to $P$ is $\ell(gh^{-1})/2$.
\item The hyperbolic axis of $A_{h^{-1}g}$ is determined by the geodesic line going from $P'$ to $Q$, and the hyperbolic distance from $P'$ to $Q$ is $\ell(h^{-1}g)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We prove the first case. Since $A_g$ and $A_h$ intersect at $O$, so do the curves $[g]$ and $[h]$ at some point $p \in \Sigma$.
Perform a smoothing opperation obtaining a new curve $\alpha$ of length $\ell(g) + \ell(h)$.
A lift $a$  of this curve in $\wt{X}$ going through $O$ is a piecewise geodesic consisting of a segment on $A_h$ of length $\ell(h)$, then a segment of $A_{h^{-1}gh}$ of length $\ell(g)$, etc.
Let $\alpha_*$ be the geodesic representative in the homotopy class of $\alpha$.
Now, connecting the midpoints of the geodesic segments of $a$ we obtain another piecewise geodesic $a'$ which is, in fact, a geodesic segment.
Indeed, we note that $a$ is invariant under angle $\pi$ rotation around any of the intersection points between $a$ and $a'$.
Since $a$ and $a'$ have the same stabilizer, they are lifts of the same geodesic $\alpha$.
Thus, the lift of $\alpha_*$ is $a'$, which is determined by two of the midpoints defining $a$, such as $P'$ and $Q'$.
As for the translation lengths, we note that $h=r_O r_{Q'}$ where $r_O$ denotes the reflection along the geodesic perpendicular to $A_h$ at $O$,
and $r_{Q'}$ is the corresponding reflection at $Q'$.
Similarly, we can write $g=r_{P'}r_O$, and thus $gh=(r_{P'}r_O)(r_O r_{Q'})=r_{P'}r_{Q'}$, which corresponds to a translation along $a'$ of translation length
twice the distance between $P'$ and $Q'$.
The other cases follow similarly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a measured lamination if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies the so-called \emph{max-smoothing property}:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:MLsmoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    \label{prop:char_laminations}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first $f$ satisfies max-smoothing.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_f$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for all closed curve $C$.
It remains to show that $\mu_f$ is a measured lamination.
Consider the hyperbolic parallelogram $H$ at the center of Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}.
The set of geodesics $R_1 \coloneqq R_{g,h}$ consisting of the geodesics intersecting the sides of $H$ contained in $A_{h^{-1}g}$ and $A_{gh^{-1}}$ is equal to the set of geodesics intersecting the diagonals and separating the sides on $A_{gh}$ and $A_{hg}$.
Let $B$ be a box of geodesics. By density of endpoints of closed geodesics, for every $\epsilon>0$, we can find geodesics $g$ and $h$ with endpoints very close to the diagonals of $B$.
Taking large enough powers $g^n$ and $h^n$, and obtaining a corresponding hyperbolic parallelogram $H_n$, we obtain a set of geodesics $R_n \colon R_{g^n,h^n}$, so that $|\mu(R_n)-\mu(B)|< \epsilon$.
If we let $a=g^n$ and $b=h^n$, we have
$\mu(R_n)=1/2(f(a) + f(b)-f(ab^{-1}))$.
Furthermore, if $R_n^T$ denotes the set of geodesics intersecting the sides of $H_n$ contained in $A_{gh}$ and $A_{hg}$, we also have
$|\mu(R_n^T)-\mu(B^T)|< \epsilon$.
Furthermore,
$\mu(R_n^T)=1/2(f(a) + f(b)-f(ab))$.
By taking a subsequence, and by max-smoothing, we have either $\mu(R_n^T)=0$ or $\mu(R_n)=0$ for all $n$.
Thus, either $\mu(B)<\epsilon$ or $\mu(B^T)<\epsilon$. Since $\epsilon$ is arbitrary, it follows that $\mu$ is a measured lamination.
Now we prove the other implication.
First, note that max-smoothing means that for every box $R_{g,h}$, either $\mu(R_{g,h})=0$ or $\mu(R_{g,h})=0$ for any pair of non-conjugate intersecting $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ (it means more, still need to figure out the self-intersecting case).
We proceed by proving the counter-positive.
Suppose max-smoothing is not satisfied, so that we have $g,h$ for which both $\mu(R_{g,h})>0$ and $\mu(R_{g,h}^T)>0$.
Thus, there are geodesics in the support of $\mu$ that cross the north-south segments of $R_{g,h}$ and geodesics in the support of $\mu$ that cross the east-west segments of $R_{g,h}$. These geodesics intersect, and thus $\mu$ cannot be a measured lamination.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
One can use Feng Luo's work alone to prove that if $f$ satisfies max-smoothing, then $f=i(\lambda,\cdot)$ must be a measured lamination.
Suppose now that Equation~\ref{eq:MLsmoothing} for a functional $f$ is satisfied, as well as additivity, homogeneity and stability. 

By Proposition~\ref{prop:feng}, the restriction of $f$ to simple multi-curves, $f|_{\mathcal{S}}$, satisfies Feng Luo's conditions.
Thus, there exists a measured lamination $\lambda$ so that
\[
f(s)=i(\lambda, s)
\]
for all simple closed curves $s$.
By max-smoothing, for every closed multi-curve $c$, there is a sequence of smoothings on $c$ that yields a simple multi-curve $s_c$ so that
\[
f(c)=f(s_c)
\]
Thus, for every closed curve $c$,
\[
f(c)=f(s_c)=i(\lambda,s_c).
\]
Note that $c$ dominates $s_c$, and thus,
$i(\lambda,c) \geq i(\lambda,s_c)$.
Hence, $i(\lambda,c) \geq f(c)$.
On the other hand, $i(\lambda,\cdot)$ also satisfies max-smoothing, thus for every closed multi-curve $c$, there is a multi-curve $s_c'$ so that
\[
i(\lambda,c)=i(\lambda,s_c').
\]
Thus,
\[
i(\lambda,s_c') \geq i(\lambda,s_c)
\]
But, at the same time, $c$ dominates $s_c'$, and hence
\[
f(c) \geq f(s_c')=i(\lambda,s_c').
\]
Thus,
\[
f(c) \geq f(s_c')=i(\lambda,s_c') \geq i(\lambda, s_c)=f(c),
\]
and thus it follows that $f(c)=i(\lambda,s_c')=i(\lambda,c)$
for all closed multi-curves $c$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Measured laminations and $\mathbb{R}$-trees}

As an application, we prove a conjecture of Morgan and Otal, giving another characterization of small actions of surface groups on $\mathbb{R}$-trees.
An \emph{$\mathbb{R}$-tree} is a metric space $T$ such that for
all $x,y \in T$, the intersection of all connected subsets containing both $x$ and $y$ isometric to the interval $[0, d(x, y)]$. This intersection is denoted
$[x, y]$, and called an \emph{arc}. If $G$ is a group, a $G$-tree is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree together with a group action $G \acts T$ by isometries.
There are two types of isometries: those with fixed points, called \emph{elliptic}, and those without fixed points, called \emph{hyperbolic}.
Given $g\in G$, $\| g\|=\inf_{x \in T} d(x,gx)$, and let $T_g \coloneqq \{ x\in T : d(x,gx)=\| g\| \}$.
If $g$ is elliptic, then $\| g \| =0$ (and the converse is also true).
If $g$ is hyperbolic, then $\| g | >0$, and $g$ translates along $T_g$ by distance $\| g\|$, thus leaving $T_g$ invariant. We call $T_g$ the \emph{axis} of $g$.
We say that $G \acts T$ is \emph{small} if the stabilizer of any (non-degenerate) arc is (virtually) cyclic.
Given two hyperbolic elements $g, h \in G$, their $\mathbb{R}$-tree axes $T_g,T_h$ can have the following relative positions, and translation length relations (by work of Culler-Morgan~\cite{CM87:GroupActions}).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $T_g \cap T_h =\emptyset$. $|gh|=|g|+|h|+2d(T_g,T_h)$.
    \item $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $T_g \cap T_h$ is a singleton. $|gh|=|gh^{-1}|=|g|+|h|$
        \item The overlap $T_g \cap T_h$ is a non-degenerate interval.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item The two axes overlap with the same orientation. $|gh|=|g|+|h|$, $|gh^{-1}|=|g|+|h|-2\ell(T_g \cap T_h)$
            \item The two axes overlap with opposite orientations. $|gh^{-1}|=|g|+|h|$, $|gh|=|g|+|h|-2\ell(T_g \cap T_h)$
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

Given $G=\pi_1(S)$, and $\Sigma$ a hyperbolic structure on $S$, let $A_g, A_h$ be the hyperbolic axes corresponding to these elements.
If two axes $A_g,A_h$ with respect to $\Sigma$ intersect in $\tilde{\Sigma}$, the axes with respect to any other hyperbolic structure also intersect. Thus, intersection of hyperbolic axes is a topological invariant of a pair $g,h$.
Given a measured lamination $\lambda$, one can produce an $\mathbb{R}$-tree by lifting the measured lamination to the universal cover, defining a pseudo-distance between $x,y \in \wt{\Sigma}$ given by the transverse measure of the segment $[x,y]$, and taking the quotient metric space. This $\mathbb{R}$-tree has a $\pi_1(S)$ action induced by the action on $\tilde{\Sigma}$. We call this graph the dual graph $T_{\lambda}$ of $\lambda$.
Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths} first showed that an action of a surface group is dual to a measured lamination if and only if 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item it is small,
    \item for any pair $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$ of hyperbolic elements, we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $A_g \cap A_h \neq \emptyset$.
\end{enumerate}
In~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}, Skora showed that $G \acts T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if (1). It follows that (1) implies (2).
Morgan-Otal asked if (2) implies (1). We answer this question in the affirmative.


\begin{proposition}
An action of $\pi_1(S)$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T$ is small if and only if condition (1) is satisfied.
\label{prop:small}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the above, it only remains to show that (2) implies (1).
On the other hand, assume that for any pair $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$, we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $A_g \cap A_h \neq \emptyset$.
We will verify that $| \cdot |$, the translation length on $T$, satisfies the max-smoothing condition, and thus it comes from a measured lamination.
First, suppose that $g,h$ represent distinct closed curves on $S$ that intersect essentially.
The two possible smoothings are then represented by $gh$ and $gh^{-1}$, so max-smoothing can be phrased as

\[
|g| + |h| = \max \{ |gh|,|gh^{-1}| \}.
\]

This relation is true by result~\cite[1.8]{CM87:GroupActions}, since $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$.
Now, suppose that we have a closed curve with an essential self-intersection, represented by $g h$, where the concatenation is at the basepoint.
Max-smoothing amounts to

\[
|gh| = \max \{ |g|+|h|,|gh^{-1}| \}.
\]

First, we note that the self-intersection corresponds to the intersection of the hyperbolic axes of the elements $gh$ and $hg$. Thus, we know that $T_{gh} \cap T_{hg} \neq \emptyset$.
There are three cases to consider.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset, \{ p \}$. Then $|gh|=|gh^{-1}| \geq |g|+|h|$ by the result~\cite[1.5]{CM87:GroupActions}, and the condition is satisfied.
    \item $T_g$ and $T_h$ intersect in a non-degenerate interval with coherent orientations
    Then, 
    $|gh|=|g|+|h|>|gh^{-1}|$, and the condition is satisfied by the result~\cite[1.8]{CM87:GroupActions}
    \item  $T_g$ and $T_h$ intersect in a non-degenerate interval with opposite orientations
    Then, 
    $|gh^{-1}|=|g|+|h|>|gh|$, so the max-smoothing condition would be violated. However, this case cannot arise, since that would mean that $T_{g^{-1}h}$ and $T_{hg^{-1}}$ intersect in a non-degenerate interval with coherent orientations, and analysis of the axes shows that, in fact, $T_{gh} \cap T_{hg} = \emptyset$, a contradiction.
\end{enumerate}

\end{proof}

We emphasize that condition (2) is not enough if instead of $\mathbb{R}$-trees one considers $\mathbb{Z}$-trees (see \cite[Section~4]{Sko90:GeometricAction}).

We finish this section by posing a question.
In \cite[Theorem~13]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}, Bonahon gives an algebraic characterization for a geodesic current to be a Liouville current for a hyperbolic structure $X$.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, for any four points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$, we have
   \[
      e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} + e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])}=1.
   \]
\end{theorem}


An improvement of this theorem is given by Hamenst\"adt in \cite[Corollary~2.12]{Ham99:Cocycles}.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, there exist a number $\rho>1$ so that for any four ordered points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$, we have
    \[
      \frac{1}{\rho} e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} \leq 1- e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])} \leq \rho e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} 
   \]
   \label{thm:ham}
\end{theorem}

The following question is interesting.

\begin{question}
What are necessary and sufficient conditions on a functional on curves $f \colon \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ in order to induce a Liouville current for some hyperbolic structure $\Sigma$? (in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}).
\label{question:liouville}
\end{question}

In \cite[2.4(3)]{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, it is shown that a geodesic current $\mu$ is a measured lamination if and only if $\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])=0$ for all 4-tuples of ordered points $x,y,z,w \in  \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$.
This condition is phrased in a similar way as Theorem~\ref{thm:ham}, this is, in terms of boxes of geodesics $[x,y,z,w]$ and their ``opposite'' box, $[y,z]\times [w,x]$.
This condition translates into our condition in terms of smoothings in Theorem~\ref{prop:char_laminations}.
A more concrete way to phrase Question~\ref{question:liouville}, is as follows:
is there a similar way to translate the condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:ham} for Liouville currents in terms of boxes and opposite boxes as a condition in terms of smoothing triples?

\subsection{Inequalities between intersection numbers}
\label{subsec:ineqintersection}

Finally, we give an application of our main result to domination of spectra of metric lengths on a surface. Let $\mu_1,\mu_2 \in \mathcal{G}\mathcal{C}(S)$. Say $\mu_1 \leq \mu_2$, or $\mu_2$ \emph{dominates} $\mu_1$ if $i(\mu_1,C) \leq i(\mu_2,C)$ for all $C \in \mathcal{C}(\Sigma)$.
We can use these extension results to study what intersection numbers dominating one curve over another imply about Liouville currents.

For example, we answer positively the following question at the end of \cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.
In fact, we have something much stronger

\begin{corollary}
If $C_2$ dominates $C_1$, then
    $\ell_g(C_1) \leq \ell_g(C_2)$ for all metric lengths $g$ satisfying conditions of theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} (not necessarily Riemannian).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First note that any length metric satisfies smoothing.
This argument is outlined essentially in \cite[Section~4.1]{MGT21:Smoothings}, but we reproduce it here.
 Take a
curve~$C$ with an
essential self-intersection~$x$, and a representative~$\gamma$. Then $\gamma$ has
a self-intersection point~$x'$ of the homotopy type of~$x$. If we
consider the
curve representative~$\gamma' \in C'$ obtained by smoothing at~$x'$, then,
since $\ell_g$ is an infimum, we have
\[
  \ell_g(C')\le \ell_g(C') = \ell_g(\gamma) = \ell_g(C),
\]
as desired. By Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_g$ so that $\ell_g(C)=i(\mu_g,C)$ for all closed curve $C$. Approximating the current $\mu_g$ by weighted multi-curves and using continuity of intersection number on currents the result follows.
\end{proof}
