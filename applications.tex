\subsection{New definition of geodesic currents, filling currents and measured laminations}
\label{subsec:newdef}
This result gives a new definition of geodesic currents in terms of functionals on curves, by giving a bijection between geodesic currents and a subset of the space of curve functionals. These curve functionals form a subset of the functionals that extend continuously to currents, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Moreover, the bijection is a homeomorphism onto its image when the space of curve functionals is equipped with the subspace topology coming from the product topology on $\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}(S)}$ (by~\cite[Theorem~11]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}).

Thus, a reestatement of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} is as follows.

\begin{theorem}
   Let $\Psi \colon \GC(S) \to \CurveFunct(S)$ be the homeomorphism onto its image $\mu \mapsto i(\mu, \cdot)$.
   The image is equal to the subset of additive convex functionals $\AConvex(S)$.
\end{theorem}

We will now characterize measured laminations as curve functionals in $\AConvex(S)$.

We will need the following observation about the measure of sets intersecting pairs of geodesic segments.
We introduce the following notation. Given two geodesics segments $k$ and $k'$, we denote with $G(k)$ the set of geodesics intersecting $k$ transversely and let $G(k , k')$ be the set of geodesics intersecting both $k$ and $k$ transversely.
Let $G(k | k)$ be the set of geodesics separating $k$ from $k'$, i.e., $\gamma \in G(k | k')$ if $k$ and $k'$ lie in distinct connected components of $\wt{X}-\gamma$.
\begin{lemma}
Let $\mu$ be a geodesic current.
Let $k=[x,y]$,$k'=[w,z]$ be two geodesic segments.
Let $p=[x,w]$, $p'=[y,z]$
Then, the following equation holds.
\begin{align*}
2\mu(G(p,p'))&=\mu(G(l))+\mu(G(l'))-\mu(G(k)) - \mu(G(k')).
\end{align*}
\label{lem:otalclaim}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Note that the set of geodesics intersecting both $p$ and $p'$ is the same as the set of geodesics separating $k$ and $k'$, i.e., $G(p,p')=G(k|k')$.
The claim then follows by an observation by Otal~\cite[Page~154]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}, which is proven in detail in \cite[Lemma~5.2]{dRMG22:Duals}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}

\begin{remark}
One can use Feng Luo's work alone to prove that if $f$ satisfies max-smoothing, then $f=i(\lambda,\cdot)$ must be a measured lamination.
Suppose now that Equation~\ref{eq:MLsmoothing} for a functional $f$ is satisfied, as well as additivity, homogeneity and stability. 

By Proposition~\ref{prop:feng}, the restriction of $f$ to simple multi-curves, $f|_{\mathcal{S}}$, satisfies Feng Luo's conditions.
Thus, there exists a measured lamination $\lambda$ so that
\[
f(s)=i(\lambda, s)
\]
for all simple closed curves $s$.
By max-smoothing, for every closed multi-curve $c$, there is a sequence of smoothings on $c$ that yields a simple multi-curve $s_c$ so that
\[
f(c)=f(s_c)
\]
Thus, for every closed curve $c$,
\[
f(c)=f(s_c)=i(\lambda,s_c).
\]
Note that $c$ dominates $s_c$, and thus,
$i(\lambda,c) \geq i(\lambda,s_c)$.
Hence, $i(\lambda,c) \geq f(c)$.
On the other hand, $i(\lambda,\cdot)$ also satisfies max-smoothing, thus for every closed multi-curve $c$, there is a multi-curve $s_c'$ so that
\[
i(\lambda,c)=i(\lambda,s_c').
\]
Thus,
\[
i(\lambda,s_c') \geq i(\lambda,s_c)
\]
But, at the same time, $c$ dominates $s_c'$, and hence
\[
f(c) \geq f(s_c')=i(\lambda,s_c').
\]
Thus,
\[
f(c) \geq f(s_c')=i(\lambda,s_c') \geq i(\lambda, s_c)=f(c),
\]
and thus it follows that $f(c)=i(\lambda,s_c')=i(\lambda,c)$
for all closed multi-curves $c$.
\end{remark}

As an application, we reprove a result of Skora, giving a characterization of actions of surface groups on $\mathbb{R}$-trees that are dual to measured laminations.
An \emph{$\mathbb{R}$-tree} is a metric space $T$ such that for
all $x,y \in T$, the intersection of all connected subsets containing both $x$ and $y$ isometric to the interval $[0, d(x, y)]$. This intersection is denoted
$[x, y]$, and called an \emph{arc}. If $G$ is a group, a $G$-tree is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree together with a group action $G \acts T$ by isometries.
There are two types of isometries: those with fixed points, called \emph{elliptic}, and those without fixed points, called \emph{hyperbolic}.
Given $g\in G$, $\| g\|=\inf_{x \in T} d(x,gx)$, and let $T_g \coloneqq \{ x\in T : d(x,gx)=\| g\| \}$.
If $g$ is elliptic, then $\| g \| =0$ (and the converse is also true).
If $g$ is hyperbolic, then $\| g | >0$, and $g$ translates along $T_g$ by distance $\| g\|$, thus leaving $T_g$ invariant. We call $T_g$ the \emph{axis} of $g$.
We say that $G \acts T$ is \emph{small} if the stabilizer of any (non-degenerate) arc is (virtually) cyclic.
Given two hyperbolic elements $g, h \in G$, their $\mathbb{R}$-tree axes $T_g,T_h$ can have the following relative positions, and translation length relations (by work of Culler-Morgan~\cite{CM87:GroupActions}).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $T_g \cap T_h =\emptyset$. $|gh|=|g|+|h|+2d(T_g,T_h)$.
    \item $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $T_g \cap T_h$ is a singleton. $|gh|=|gh^{-1}|=|g|+|h|$
        \item The overlap $T_g \cap T_h$ is a non-degenerate interval.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item The two axes overlap with the same orientation. $|gh|=|g|+|h|$, $|gh^{-1}|=|g|+|h|-2\ell(T_g \cap T_h)$
            \item The two axes overlap with opposite orientations. $|gh^{-1}|=|g|+|h|$, $|gh|=|g|+|h|-2\ell(T_g \cap T_h)$
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

Given $G=\pi_1(S)$, and $\Sigma$ a hyperbolic structure on $S$, let $A_g, A_h$ be the hyperbolic axes corresponding to these elements.
If two axes $A_g,A_h$ with respect to $\Sigma$ intersect in $\tilde{\Sigma}$, the axes with respect to any other hyperbolic structure also intersect. Thus, intersection of hyperbolic axes is a topological invariant of a pair $g,h$.
A measured lamination $\lambda$ defines a (dual) $\pi_1(\Sigma)$ minimal irreducible action on $\mathbb{R}$-tree as follows (following~\cite{BIPP21:Systoles}). Lift the lamination to the universal cover $\wt{\Sigma}$ and define the pseudo-distance 

\begin{equation}d_{\lambda}(x,y) \coloneqq 1/2(\lambda(G[x,y)) + \lambda(G(x,y])) 
\label{eq:pseudo}
\end{equation}
on $\tilde{\Sigma}$, where $[x,y),(x,y]$ denote the half-closed/open geodesic segments between $x$ and $y$ in $\wt{\Sigma}$, and $G[x,y)$ denotes the space of geodesics transverse to $[x,y)$. One can check that the natural quotient metric space is $0$-hyperbolic and thus can be canonically embedded in an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T(\lambda)$. An equivalent definition of $T(\lambda)$ is given in \cite[Chapter~11]{Kap09:HyperbolicManifold}.
It follows from the construction of $T(\lambda)$ is that the translation length on $T$ of $g \in \pi_1(S)$, $\ell_T(g)$, is equal to $i(\lambda,[g])$, where $[g]$ denotes the curve associated to $g$.
Given a minimal action of a surface group $G$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree, if there is a measured lamination $\lambda$ and $\pi_1(\Sigma)$-equivariant isometry $\phi \colon T(\lambda) \to T$, we say that $T$ is \emph{dual to the measured lamination} $\lambda$.

Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths} first showed that an action of a surface group is dual to a measured lamination if and only if 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item it is small,
    \item it \emph{preserves axis intersection}: for any pair $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$ of hyperbolic elements, we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $A_g \cap A_h \neq \emptyset$.
\end{enumerate}
An action of a group $G$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree with non-trivial translation length $\| \cdot \|$ is of three types~\cite[Page~583]{CM87:GroupActions}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fixed end: $\| \cdot \|$ trivial on $[G,G]$
\item Dihedral: $\| \cdot \|$ non-trivial on $[G,G]$, but $\| [g,h] \|=0$ for all $g,h$ hyperbolic in $G$.
\item Irreducible:  $\| [g,h] \| \neq 0$ for some $g,h$ hyperbolic in $G$.
\end{enumerate}
Morgan-Otal asked in~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths}: if an action preserves axis intersection, does it follow it is small?
Without further assumptions, the axis intersection being preserved alone does not imply in general the action is small, as the example of a dihedral action of a surface group acting on $\mathbb{R}$ shows (this was noted by Skora in~\cite[Section~4]{Sko90:GeometricAction}).
However, in~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}, Skora showed that a minimal action $G \acts T$ of a surface group $G$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axis intersection.
In~\cite{Sk96:Splittings}, under the further assumption that if $S$ has cusps then parabolic elements in $G$ must be elliptic in $T$, Skora showed that $G \acts T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if the action is small. It follows from this that the action being small implies axis intersection being preserved.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, for any four points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$, we have
   \[
      e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} + e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])}=1.
   \]
   \label{thm:bonahonliouville}
\end{theorem}


An improvement of this theorem is given by Hamenst\"adt in \cite[Corollary~2.12]{Ham99:Cocycles}.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, there exist a number $\rho>1$ so that for any four ordered points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$, we have
    \[
      \frac{1}{\rho} e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} \leq 1- e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])} \leq \rho e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} 
   \]
   \label{thm:ham}
\end{theorem}

We can prove the following characterization.

\begin{theorem}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies additivity, stability, homogeneity and the so-called \emph{disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing property}:
    For any pair of multi-curves $C_1$ and $C_2$ with an essential crossing between $C_1,C_2$, we have \begin{equation}\label{eq:MLsmoothing}
      \lambda\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) =  \lambda\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)+\lambda\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right).
    \end{equation}
    where $\lambda(C) \coloneqq 2\cosh\left(\frac{f(C)}{2}\right)$.
    \label{prop:char_liouville}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof is similar to that of Theorem~\ref{prop:char_laminations}.
Suppose the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing is satisfied. Then, by convexity of $\cosh$, it is easy to check that the smoothing property for $f$ follows.
Hence, $f$ is dual to a geodesic current $\mu$.
Given a box of geodesics $B$, and $\epsilon>0$, approximate a box of geodesics $B$ by a set $R=R_{g^n,h^n}$ of north-south geodesics (same as in the notation of that proof), for a pair of non-conjugate intersecting elements $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$.
Taking $n$ arbitrarily large, the hyperbolic length of $a \coloneqq g^n$ and $b \coloneqq h^n$ can be considered arbitrarily long.
On the other hand, if we let $R^{\perp}$ denote the set of east-west geodesics,
 $A,B$ (half) the measure of the set of geodesics intersecting each diagonal of $R$, and $C,D$ (half) the measure of the set of geodesics intersecting each of the vertical and horizontal sides of the parallelogram $H$ associated to $R$, we get
 \[
\mu(R)=A+B-C
 \]
 and
 \[
\mu(R)=A+B-D.
 \] 
Thus, we get
\[
e^{-\mu(R)} + e^{-\mu(R^{\perp})} = e^{-(A+B)+C} + e^{-(A+B)+D} = e^{-(A+B)} (e^{C} + e^{D}).
\]
On the other hand, if $a,b$ are long enough,
the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing relation yields\DMGtodo[inline]{This is rough, and in any case should show where it comes from. Also, what does $\sim$ mean.}
\[
e^{A}e^{B} \sim e^{C}+e^{D}
\]
and thus, Theorem~\ref{thm:bonahonliouville} shows $\mu$ is a Liouville current.
On the other hand, if Bonahon's relation for a geodesic current is satisfied then it is a hyperbolic Liouville current, and then the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing property follows from the trace relation.
\end{proof}


\begin{remark}
Following exactly the same resolutions as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:feng}, one can check that the trace conditions in Feng Luo's work that guarantee that a curve functional on simple curves $f$ is a hyperbolic length~\cite{Luo98:GeodesicLength} follow from the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing property.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Inequalities between intersection numbers}
\label{subsec:ineqintersection}

Finally, we give an application of our main result to domination of spectra of metric lengths on a surface. Let $\mu_1,\mu_2 \in \mathcal{G}\mathcal{C}(S)$. Say $\mu_1 \leq \mu_2$, or $\mu_2$ \emph{dominates} $\mu_1$ if $i(\mu_1,C) \leq i(\mu_2,C)$ for all $C \in \mathcal{C}(\Sigma)$.
We can use these extension results to study what intersection numbers dominating one curve over another imply about Liouville currents.

For example, we answer positively the following question at the end of \cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.
In fact, we have something much stronger

\begin{corollary}
If $C_2$ dominates $C_1$, then
    $\ell_g(C_1) \leq \ell_g(C_2)$ for all length metrics $g$ on $\Sigma$.
    \label{cor:domination}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First note that any length metric satisfies smoothing.
This argument is outlined essentially in \cite[Section~4.1]{MGT21:Smoothings}, but we reproduce it here.
 Take a
curve~$C$ with an
essential self-intersection~$x$, and a representative~$\gamma$. Then $\gamma$ has
a self-intersection point~$x'$ of the homotopy type of~$x$. If we
consider the
curve representative~$\gamma' \in C'$ obtained by smoothing at~$x'$, then,
since $\ell_g$ is an infimum, we have
\[
  \ell_g(C')\le \ell_g(C') = \ell_g(\gamma) = \ell_g(C),
\]
as desired. $\ell_g$ satisfies homogeneity and stability by \cite[Lemma~4.2]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Additivity is satisfied by definition of $\ell_g$ on weighted multi-curves by extending linearly.
It is clear that $\ell_g$ is a symmetric curve functional, since it is naturally defined on unoriented curves.
Thus, by theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_g$ so that $\ell_g(C)=i(\mu_g,C)$ for all closed curves $C$. Approximating the current $\mu_g$ by weighted multi-curves and using continuity of intersection number on currents the result follows.
\end{proof}
