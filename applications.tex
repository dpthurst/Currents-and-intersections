\section{Measured laminations and hyperbolic metrics as curve functionals}
\label{sec:appsintersection}

\subsection{New definition of geodesic currents, filling currents and measured laminations}
\label{subsec:newdef} 
Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} gives a new definition of geodesic currents in terms of functionals on curves, by giving a bijection between geodesic currents and a subset of the space of curve functionals.
As mentioned in the introduction, one can view this theorem in analogy to Poincaré duality for closed surfaces.  Given a 1-cohomology class $\alpha^*$ of an $n$-dimensional manifold (represented by homomorphisms from $\pi_1(S)$ to $\mathbb{R}$), Poincaré duality associates an $(n-1)$-dimensional homology class $\alpha$ (represented by real linear combinations of $(n-1)$-cycles). Since $n=2$, these cycles can be expressed as linear combinations of simple closed curves.  

In our setting, the role of a 1-cohomology class is played by a curve
functional that satisfies smoothing, additivity, and
stability/homogeneity. Although it is not a homomorphism of
$\pi_1(S)$, it preserves a certain combinatorial structure (see Section~\ref{sec:questions}). Likewise, the role of the homology class is taken by a geodesic current. This perspective, viewing geodesic currents as homology classes, aligns with the approach in geometric measure theory, where differential currents—seen as functionals on $k$-forms—naturally define homology~\cite[Chapter~10]{CC00:Foliations}. Geodesic currents are examples of such differential forms~\cite[1.8.7]{MG20:Thesis}.

In Poincaré duality, the standard relation $\alpha^* \cup \beta^* = \alpha \cap \beta$ holds, where $\cap$ denotes the cap product in homology and $\cup$ the cup product in cohomology. This aligns with our theorem, which establishes a duality via the intersection number $i(\mu_f, C) = f(C)$, suggesting that the geometric intersection number plays the role of the cap product.  

A natural question that arises is: what is the appropriate analog of the cup product? In other words, how can one define the intersection of two curve functionals?

These curve functionals form a subset of the functionals that extend continuously to currents, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Moreover, the bijection is a homeomorphism onto its image when the space of curve functionals is equipped with the subspace topology coming from the product topology on $\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}(S)}$ (by~\cite[Theorem~11]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}).

Thus, a restatement of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} is as follows.

\begin{theorem}
   Let $\Psi \colon \GC(S) \to \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}(S)}$ be the homeomorphism onto its image $\mu \mapsto i(\mu, \cdot)$.
   The image is equal to the subset of additive convex functionals $\AConvex(S)$.
\end{theorem}

We can also characterize filling geodesic currents.

\begin{corollary}
  Let $S$ a closed surface,
  $\mu$ a geodesic current and $f$ the functional on curves associated
  to $\mu$, i.e., so that $\mu_f=\mu$. The following subsets are in
  bijection.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The set of filling currents, i.e., $ \GC_{\mbox{fill}}(S)$
    \item The set of functionals $f \colon \Curves^+(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ which satisfy symmetry, oriented smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity properties, and so that their continuous extension to geodesic currents is positive.
    \item The set of curve functionals $f \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ for which their systole \[\sys(f)\coloneqq \inf \{ f(C) : C \in \Curves(S), C \mbox{ non-trivial} \}\] is positive and satisfy symmetry, oriented smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity properties.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
These equivalences follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and \cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIPP17:StructureThmCurrents}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Since the space of geodesic currents is locally compact (see Proposition~\ref{prop:local_compactness}), this shows $\AConvex$ is locally compact in the product topology, which is not obvious.
See also Section~\ref{subsubsec:convexity_charts} for a related discussion.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Multi-curves}

We have the following characterization of curves that are one-half of an integral multi-curve.

\begin{corollary}
Let $f \in \AConvex(S)$ such that $f(C) \in \mathbb{Z}$ for every $C \in \Curves(S)$. Then there exists an integral multi-curve $M$ so that
\[
i(1/2 M,\cdot)=f(\cdot).
\]
\label{cor:int_multicurve}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $f \in \AConvex(S)$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} there exists a geodesic current $\mu$ so that
\[
i(\mu, C) = f(C)
\]
for every closed curve $C$.
It suffices to show $\mu$ is an integral multi-curve. By the same argument in the proof of~\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{Otal90:SpectreMarqueNegative}, there exists a family of rectangles of geodesics $\mathcal{R}$ with dense endpoints in $\partial_{\infty} S$ whose measure can be approximated by a sequence of one-half of the difference of sums of intersection numbers. That is, for every $R \in \mathcal{R}$, there exist sequences $\alpha_n, \beta_n, \gamma_n, \eta_n \in \Curves(S)$ so that
\[
\mu(R) = \frac{1}{2}\lim_n i(\mu, \alpha_n) + i(\mu, \beta_n) - i(\mu, \gamma_n) - i(\mu, \eta_n).
\]
Since the expression in the limit is one-half of an integer number for every $n$, for $n$ large enough the sequence must be constant (by discreteness of $\mathbb{Z}$).
Hence, $\mu(R)$ is one-half of an integer for every $R \in \mathcal{R}$.
By~\cite[Proposition~4.12]{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, it follows that $2\mu$ must be an integral multi-curve.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The factor of $1/2$ is necessary, since there are examples of
  word-lengths (whose marked length spectrum is integral) that are
  dual to one-half of an integral
  multi-curve~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Erlandsson:WordLength}. For another
  concrete example, let $\mu$ be the geodesic current supported on a
  separating simple closed curve. Then $1/2 \mu$ has an integral
  length spectrum but it is not an integral multi-curve.
\end{remark}

It is easy to see that if $M = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i C_i$, where $\lambda_i \geq 0$ are non-negative real numbers, then for every closed curve $C$, there exist integer numbers $n_i$ so that
\begin{equation}
i(M, C) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i n_i.
\label{eq:weighted_multicurve}
\end{equation}

This property does not seem to characterize weighted multi-curves (see~\cite{Agol25:Spectrum}).

\subsection{Embedded graphs}
\label{subsec:embedded_graphs}
Related to multi-curves, we also obtain geodesic currents dual to stable lengths associated to embedded graphs in surfaces.

 Let $\iota \colon \mathcal{G} \to S$ be an \emph{embedded graph}, i.e., an embedding of a finite graph in $S$ that is filling, in the sense that the complementary regions are disks, or equivalently $i_*$ is surjective on $\pi_1$.
 Endow $\mathcal{G}$ with a length metric $g$. Then any closed multi-curve $C$ on $S$ can be homotoped so that it factors through $\mathcal{G}$, in many different ways. Let $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}(C)$ be the length of the smallest
 multi-curve $D$ on $\mathcal{G} $ so that $\iota(D)$ is homotopic to $C$. It is easy to see that this length is realized and is positive. 

\begin{theorem}
Let $\iota \colon \mathcal{G} \to S$ be an embedded graph. Then $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}$ is dual to a geodesic current $\mu_{\mathcal{G} }$.
\label{thm:dual_embeddedgraph}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In~\cite[4.4]{MGT21:Smoothings} we show that such lengths are in $\AConvex(S)$, hence by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} they have a dual geodesic current. 
\end{proof}

If furthermore $\mathcal{G}$ is an embedded graph with the edge-metric on $\pi_1(S)$ (i.e., each edge has weight 1), $\mu_{\mathcal{G}}$ must be $1/2$ of an integral multi-curve. This generalizes the main result in~\cite{Erlandsson:WordLength}, where Erlandsson finds that word-metric arising from \emph{simple generating set} are dual to 1/2 of an integral multi-curve dual. Such generating sets correspond to choosing $\mathcal{G}$ to be a rose graph with only one vertex and edges of length 1.

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathcal{G}$ be an embedded graph with the edge-metric on $\pi_1(S)$ (i.e., each edge has weight 1). Then $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}$ is dual to a geodesic current $\mu_{\mathcal{G}}$ which is $1/2$ of an integral multi-curve.
\label{cor:generalization_wordlength}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal{G}$ is equipped with the edge-metric, $\ell_{\mathcal{G}}(C) \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Hence, Corollary~\ref{cor:int_multicurve} implies the result.
\end{proof}

We note that our result says nothing explicit about the dual multi-curve, whereas Erlandsson's result explicitly constructs it in the specific case of simple generating sets. It would be interesting to describe these multi-curves in general.

\subsection{Measured laminations}

We will now characterize measured laminations as curve functionals in $\AConvex(S)$.

We will need the following observation about the measure of sets intersecting pairs of geodesic segments, proven in \cite[Lemma~6.2]{dRMG22:Duals}.

\begin{lemma}
For any points $x,y,z,w \in \wt{\Sigma}$, we have the following equalities:
\begin{align*}
\mu(G([{x},{w}), [{y},{z}))) +  &\mu(G(({x},{w}], ({y},{z}])) = \\
&\mu(G[{x},{z})) + \mu(G[{w},{y})) \\
&- \mu(G([{w},{z})) - \mu(G([{x},{y}))
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
\mu(G(({x},{w}], ({y},{z}])) +   &\mu(G([{x},{w}), [{y},{z})))= \\
&\mu(G({x},{z}]) + \mu(G({w},{y}]) \\
&- \mu(G(({w},{z}]) - \mu(G(({x},{y}])
\end{align*}
\label{lem:otalclaim}
\end{lemma}

We need first the following hyperbolic geometry lemma.

\begin{proposition}
Consider two elements $a, b \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ with hyperbolic axes $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ that intersect at a point $O$, and translation lengths $\ell(a),\ell(b)$.
We define the following four points:

\begin{itemize} \item $P$ is the point on $\ora{a}$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(a)/2$;
\item $Q$ is the point on $\ora{b}$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $\ell(b)/2$;
\item $Q'$ is the point on $\ora{b}$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(b)/2$;
\item $P'$ is the point on $\ora{a}$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $\ell(a)/2$.
\end{itemize}
We have the following (see Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\ora{ab}$ is the line containing the segment $(Q,P]$ of length $\ell(ba)/2$.
\item $\ora{ba}$ is the line containing the segment $(P',Q']$ of length $\ell(ba)/2$.
\item $\ora{ab^{-1}}$ is the line containing the segment $(Q',P]$ of length $\ell(ab^{-1})/2$.
\item $\ora{b^{-1}a}$ is the line containing the segment $(P',Q]$ of length $\ell(b^{-1}a)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, the union $G((Q,P])\cup G((P',Q'])$ (resp. $G((Q',P]) \cup G((P',Q])$) is a fundamental domain for the action of $ab$ on the set of geodesics $G(\Sigma)$, (resp. a fundamental domain for the action of $ab^{-1}$).
\label{prop:keygeometry}
\end{proposition}

  \begin{figure}
\centering{
\svgfignormal{smoothing_1}}
\caption{Hyperbolic geometry of the smoothing between two distinct curves.}
\label{fig:smoothing_1}
\end{figure}

\begin{proof}
We prove the first case. Since $\ora{a}$ and $\ora{b}$ intersect at $O$, so do the curves $[a]$ and $[b]$ at some point $o \in \Sigma$.
Perform a smoothing operation obtaining a new curve $\alpha$ of length $\ell(a) + \ell(b)$.
A lift $\alpha$  of this curve in $\wt{X}$ going through $O$ is a piecewise geodesic consisting of a segment on $\ora{b}$ of length $\ell(b)$, then a segment of $\ora{b^{-1}ab}$ of length $\ell(a)$, etc.
Let $\alpha_*$ be the geodesic representative in the homotopy class of $\alpha$.
Now, connecting the midpoints of the geodesic segments of $\alpha$ we obtain another piecewise geodesic $\alpha'$ which is, in fact, a geodesic segment.
Indeed, we note that $\alpha$ is invariant under angle $\pi$ rotation around any of the intersection points between $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$.
Since $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ have the same stabilizer, they are lifts of the same geodesic $\alpha$.
Thus, the lift of $\alpha_*$ is $\alpha'$, which is determined by two of the midpoints defining $\alpha$, such as $P'$ and $Q'$.
As for the translation lengths, we note that $b=r_O r_{Q'}$ where $r_O$ denotes the reflection along the geodesic perpendicular to $\ora{b}$ at $O$,
and $r_{Q'}$ is the corresponding reflection at $Q'$.
Similarly, we can write $a=r_{P'}r_O$, and thus $ab=(r_{P'}r_O)(r_O r_{Q'})=r_{P'}r_{Q'}$, which corresponds to a translation along $\alpha'$ of translation length
twice the distance between $P'$ and $Q'$.
The other cases follow similarly.
As for the assertion on fundamental domains, we note that the segment $(P',Q']$ on the axis of $\ora{ba}$, is sent by $a$ to a segment $(P,R]$, where $R=a(Q')$ appears forward of $P$ in according to the orientation of $\ora{ba}$, and the segment $(Q,R]$ is a fundamental domain for the action of $ab$ on its axis $\ora{ab}$, thus $G((Q,R])=G((Q,P]) \cup a G((P',Q'])$ is a fundamental domain for the action of $ba$ on $G(\Sigma)$, thus the claim follows.
The other claim is similar.
\end{proof}

The above lemma can be seen as a refinement of~\cite[Lemma~5.6]{NCS23:Property}.
Using this result, we can prove the following characterization of measured laminations among curve functionals.

\begin{theorem}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a measured lamination if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and the \emph{disconnected max-smoothing property}:
    For any pair crossing elements $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$, we have
    \begin{equation}
      f\left([g][h]\right) = \max \left\{ f\left([gh]\right),f\left([gh^{-1}]\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    \label{thm:char_laminations}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first $f$ satisfies the hypotheses of the statement.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_f$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for all closed curve $C$.
It remains to show that $\mu_f$ is a measured lamination, using the disconnected max-smoothing property.
Given elements $g, h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ with intersecting axes,
consider the hyperbolic parallelogram $H$ (see Appendix~\ref{sec:hyp-par} for the definition) at the center of Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}.
Consider the set $R_1 \coloneqq R_{g,h}$ consisting of the geodesics intersecting both sides of $H$ contained in $\ora{h^{-1}g}$ and $\ora{gh^{-1}}$. 

Let $R_1^{\perp}$ be the set of geodesics intersecting the sides of $H$ on the axes $\ora{gh}$ and $\ora{hg}$.

Given elements $g$ and $h$  with intersecting axes, let $B_{g,h}$ be the box with vertices the endpoints of $g$ and $h$.

Taking powers $g^n$ and $h^n$, and obtaining a corresponding hyperbolic parallelogram $H_n$ for $g^n,h^n$, we obtain the set $R_n \coloneqq R_{g^n,h^n}$ defined analogously.
Observe that $R_n \subset R_{n+1}$, and $\cup_{n \geq 1} R_n = B_{g,h}$.
Hence, by continuity of measures from below, we have
\[
\lim_n \mu(R_n) \to \mu(B_{g,h}).
\]
If we let $a=g^n$ and $b=h^n$, we have, by Lemma~\ref{lem:otalclaim} and Proposition~\ref{prop:keygeometry},
\[\mu(R_n)=1/2(f(a) + f(b)-f(ab^{-1})).\]

Similarly, let $R_n^{\perp}$ denote the set of geodesics intersecting the sides of $H_n$ contained in $\ora{ab}$ and $\ora{ba}$. Again, by Lemma~\ref{lem:otalclaim} and Proposition~\ref{prop:keygeometry}, we get
\[
\mu(R_n^{\perp})=1/2(f(a) + f(b)-f(ab)).
\]
We also have $R_n^{\perp} \subset R_{n+1}^{\perp}$ and $\cup_{n \geq 1} R_n^{\perp} = B_{g,h}^{\perp}$, where $B_{g,h}^{\perp}$ denotes the box complementary to $B_{g,h}$.
Thus, as above, we have $\lim_n \mu(R_n^{\perp})=\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})$.
By max-smoothing, for every $n$, we have$\mu(R_n^T)=0$ or $\mu(R_n)=0$.
Since the sets $R_n$ and $R_n^{\perp}$ are nested, if $\mu(R_1) \neq 0$, then $\mu(R_n) \neq 0$ for all $0$ and thus $\mu(R_n^{\perp})=0$ for all $n \geq 1$.
Hence, $\mu(B_{g,h})=0$ or $\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})=0$.
By density of endpoints of closed geodesics, these boxes have vertices on a dense set of points of $\partial_{\infty} S$.
Therefore, by~\cite[Proposition~2.1]{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, $\mu$ is a measured lamination.

Now we prove the other implication.
First, note that disconnected max-smoothing means that $\mu(R_{g,h})=0$ or $\mu(R_{g,h}^{\perp})=0$ for any pair of intersecting $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$.
We proceed by proving the counter-positive.
Suppose max-smoothing is not satisfied, so that we have $g,h$ for which both $\mu(R_{g,h})>0$ and $\mu(R_{g,h}^T)>0$.
This implies that $\mu(B_{g,h}) >0$ and $\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp}) >0$, so by~\cite[Proposition~2.1]{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, $\mu$ is not a measured lamination.
\end{proof}

The above result shows that if one knows $f$ is dual to a geodesic current, then it is only necessary to check disconnected max-smoothing to conclude $f$ is a measured lamination, i.e., one does not have to check essential self-crossings. It is however true that intersection number with a measured lamination also satisfies connected max-smoothing.

\begin{theorem}
A curve functional $f$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it satisfies additivity, homogeneity, stability and the \emph{max-smoothing property}:
    For any essential crossing of a multi-curve $C$, we have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:MLsmoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    \label{prop:char_laminations_plus}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $f$ is a curve functional satisfying max-smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity. Max-smoothing clearly implies smoothing, as well as disconnected max-smoothing. Thus, by Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations}, $f$ is dual to a measured lamination. On the other hand, $f$ is dual to a measured lamination,  Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations} implies disconnected max-smoothing. Connected max-smoothing follows from tracing through the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cont_conn_disc}, using the fact that $i(\mu, \cdot)$ is continuous and homogeneous, and noting that if $f$ satisfies disconnected smoothing, all the inequalities become equalities in the proof of disconnected smoothing implies connected smoothing.
\end{proof}

The fact that measured laminations satisfy max-smoothing was proven by
the second author in~\cite{Thu00:GeometricIntersection} using
different techniques. A related characterization of measured
laminations in terms of valuations in the character variety has been
proven recently in~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{MS21:Valuations}.

\subsection{Liouville currents}
\label{subsec:liouville}
In \cite[Theorem~13]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}, Bonahon gives an algebraic characterization for a geodesic current to be a Liouville current for a hyperbolic structure $X$.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, for any four points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} S$, we have
   \[
      e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} + e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])}=1.
   \]
   \label{thm:bonahonliouville}
\end{theorem}

We can prove the following characterization.

\begin{theorem}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies additivity, stability, homogeneity and,
    for any pair crossing elements $g, h \in \pi_1(S)$ we have the \emph{$\lambda$-smoothing relation}  \[
        \lambda \left(g\right)\lambda \left(h\right) = \lambda \left(gh\right) + \lambda \left(gh^{-1}\right),
  \]
  where $\lambda( \cdot ) \coloneqq 2\cosh(f(\cdot)/2)$.
    \label{prop:char_liouville}
  \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof is similar to that of Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations}.

By the assumptions on $f$, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_f$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for all closed curve $C$.
It remains to show that $\mu_f$ is a Liouville current.
Given elements $g, h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ with $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ crossing, we consider powers $g^n$ and $h^n$, and obtaining a corresponding hyperbolic parallelogram $H_n$ for $g^n,h^n$, we obtain the set $R_n \coloneqq R_{g^n,h^n}$ defined as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations}.
By the same considerations as in that proof, if we let $a_n=g^n$, $b_n=h^n$, $c_n=a_n b_n^{-1}$ and $d_n=a_n b_n$,
we get
\[\mu(R_n)=A_n + B_n-C_n\]
and
\[
\mu(R_n^T)=A_n + B_n -D_n,
\]
where $A_n=f(a_n)/2$, $B_n=f(b_n)/2$, $C_n=f(a_nb_n^{-1})/2$ and $D_n=f(a_nb_n)/2$.
Now, we compute
\[
e^{-\mu(R_n)} + e^{-\mu(R_n^{\perp})} = e^{-(A_n+B_n)+C_n} + e^{-(A_n+B_n)+D_n} = e^{-(A_n+B_n)} (e^{C_n} + e^{D_n}).
\]
On the other hand, when $n$ goes to infinity, we have $e^{A_n} \sim \lambda(a_n)$, $e^{B_n} \sim \lambda(b_n)$, $e^{C_n} \sim \lambda(c_n)$ and $e^{D_n} \sim \lambda(d_n)$ (where $\sim$ means that the ratio of the left hand side to the right hand side tends to 1 as $n$ tends to infinity). Since the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing relation for $a_n, b_n$ is given by
\[
\lambda(a_n)\lambda(b_n) = \lambda(c_n) + \lambda(d_n).
\]
A straightforward computation shows $\lambda(c_n) + \lambda(d_n) \sim e^{C_n}+e^{D_n}$, and hence, we get
\[
e^{A_n}e^{B_n} \sim e^{C_n}+e^{D_n}.
\]
Therefore, we have
\[
\lim_n e^{-\mu(R_n)} + e^{-\mu(R_n^{\perp})}=1.
\]
We also have $R_n^{\perp} \subset R_{n+1}^{\perp}$ and $\cup_{n \geq 1} R_n^{\perp} = B_{g,h}^{\perp}$, where $B_{g,h}^{\perp}$ denotes the box complementary to $B_{g,h}$, and $B_{g,h}$ is the open box determined by the endpoints of $g$ and $h$.
Thus, we have $\lim_n \mu(R_n)=\mu(B_{g,h})$ and $\lim_n \mu(R_n^{\perp})=\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})$.
Therefore, we have the relation
\[
e^{-\mu(B_{g,h})} + e^{-\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})} =1
\]
for any intersecting pair $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$.
By density of $B_{g,h}$ in the space of boxes of geodesics as $g,h$ runs over intersecting pairs in $\pi_1(\Sigma)$, given any box $B$, we can find a sequence of intersecting pairs $g_n, h_n \in \pi_1(S)$ so that if we let $B_n \coloneqq B_{g_n, h_n}$, we have $B_n \subset B_{n+1}$, $B_{n+1}^{\perp} \subset B_{n}^{\perp}$, $\cup_n B_n = B$, and $\cap_n B_n^{\perp} = B$.
Then, by lower and upper continuity of measures, respectively, we get $\lim_n \mu(B_n) =\mu(B)$ and $\lim_n \mu(B_n^{\perp}) = \mu(B)$, and hence
\[
e^{-\mu(B)} + e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}=1.
\]
Therefore, Theorem~\ref{thm:bonahonliouville} shows $\mu$ is a Liouville current.
On the other hand, if $\mu$ is hyperbolic Liouville current then the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing property follows from the relation between the hyperbolic lengths of the sides of a hyperbolic parallelogram (see Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp_parallelogram}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The $\lambda$-length functional appears naturally in the study of the skein algebra of a surface (see, for example, \cite[3. Skein algebra]{Thurston14:PBI}). 
\end{remark}

In fact, an improvement of Theorem~\ref{thm:bonahonliouville} was given by Hamenst\"adt in \cite[Corollary~2.12]{Ham99:Cocycles}.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, there exist a number $\rho>1$ so that for any four ordered points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} S$, we have
    \[
      \frac{1}{\rho} \cdot  e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} \leq 1- e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])} \leq \rho \cdot e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} 
   \]
   \label{thm:ham}
\end{theorem}

Using this, we can prove the following.

\begin{theorem}
A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies smoothing, additivity, stability, homogeneity and the following inequalities.
    There exists a constant $C \geq 1$ so that, for any pair of \emph{crossing} elements $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$, we have
    \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \exp \left[ f\left([g][h]\right)/2-f\left([gh]\right)/2 \right] - \exp \left[ f\left([gh^{-1}]\right)/2-f\left([gh]\right)/2 \right] \leq C
    \end{equation}
    and
    \begin{equation}
     1/C \leq  \exp \left[ f\left([g][h]\right)/2-f\left([gh^{-1}]\right)/2 \right] - \exp \left[ f\left([gh]\right)/2-f\left([gh^{-1}]\right)/2 \right] \leq C,
    \end{equation}
    where the crossing is assumed to be between two any pair of curves with an essential crossing.
    \label{thm:char_liouville_strong}
  \end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assumption can be written as 

 \begin{equation}
     1/C \leq  \frac{1- \exp \left[-f\left([g][h]\right)/2 + f\left([gh^{-1}]\right)/2 \right]}{\exp \left[ f\left([g][h]\right)/2 + f\left([gh]\right)/2 \right]} \leq C
    \end{equation}
    and
 \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \frac{1- \exp \left[-f\left([g][h]\right)/2 + f\left([gh]\right)/2\right]}{\exp \left[-f\left([g][h]\right)/2 + f\left([gh^{-1}]\right)/2\right]} \leq C.
    \end{equation}

    
    Now, as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:char_liouville}, this gives us

     \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \frac{1 - e^{-\mu(B)}}{e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}} \leq C
    \end{equation}
    and
    \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \frac{1 - e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}}{e^{-\mu(B)}} \leq C
    \end{equation}
which, by Theorem~\ref{thm:ham}, implies $\mu$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current.
On the other hand, if $\mu$ is a Liouville current, by Theorem~\ref{prop:char_liouville}, letting $x \coloneqq f\left([gh]\right)/2$, $y \coloneqq f\left([gh^{-1}]\right)/2$ and $z \coloneqq f\left([g][h]\right)/2$, we get
\[
e^{x} - e^{y}  = e^{-x} -e^{-y}  + e^{z} - e^{-z}.
\]
Thus, since $y \leq x$, we have $e^{-x} -e^{-y} >0$, and therefore
\[
e^{z} \leq e^{x} - e^{y} \leq 3 e^{z}
\]
which gives one pair of inequalities. The other follows by an
analogous computation.
\end{proof}

\section{Skora's theorem: \texorpdfstring{$\protect\mathbb{R}$-trees}{Rtrees} dual to measured laminations}
\label{sec:skora}
As an application, we reprove a result of Skora, giving a characterization of actions of surface groups on $\mathbb{R}$-trees that are dual to measured laminations.
An \emph{$\mathbb{R}$-tree} is a metric space $T$ such that for
all $x,y \in T$, the intersection of all connected subsets containing both $x$ and $y$ isometric to the interval $[0, d(x, y)]$. This intersection is denoted
$[x, y]$, and called an \emph{arc}. If $\Gamma$ is a finitely generated group, a $\Gamma$-tree is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree together with a group action $\Gamma \acts T$ by isometries.
There are two types of isometries: those with fixed points, called \emph{elliptic}, and those without fixed points, called \emph{hyperbolic}.
Given $g\in \Gamma$, let $\| g\|\coloneqq\inf_{x \in T} d(x,gx)$, and let $T_g \coloneqq \{ x\in T : d(x,gx)=\| g\| \}$.
If $g$ is elliptic, then $\| g \| =0$ (and the converse is also true, see~\cite[1.3]{CM87:GroupActions}).
If $g$ is hyperbolic, then $\| g | >0$, and $g$ translates along $T_g$ by distance $\| g\|$, thus leaving $T_g$ invariant. We call $T_g$ the \emph{axis} of $g$.
We say that $\Gamma \acts T$ is \emph{small} if the stabilizer of any (non-degenerate) arc is (virtually) cyclic.
We say that $\Gamma \acts T$ is \emph{minimal} if there is no proper non-trivial subtree of $T$ that is $\Gamma$-invariant.
Consider two hyperbolic elements $g, h \in \Gamma$ with axes in $\mathbb{R}$-tree given respectively by $T_g,T_h$.
If $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, we say $T_g$ and $T_h$ \emph{intersect}. Otherwise, we say that they do not intersect.
Let $\Delta \coloneqq T_g \cap T_h$. Suppose $\Delta$ is not a singleton, and $g$ and $h$ translate in the same direction along $\Delta$, we say that $g,h$ and \emph{overlap coherently}.
If $\Delta$ is not a singleton, and $g$ and $h$ translate in opposite directions along $\Delta$, we say that $g,h$ and \emph{overlap incoherently}.
Following~\cite[3.2]{CM87:GroupActions}, we say that $g,h$ form a \emph{good pair} if 
\begin{itemize} 
\item they are both hyperbolic, 
\item their axes in the tree intersect and their overlap is not a singleton,
\item they overlap coherently 
\item and $\Delta< \min \{ \| g \|, \| h \| \}$.
\end{itemize}


Given $\Gamma=\pi_1(S)$, and $\Sigma$ a hyperbolic structure on $S$, let $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ be the hyperbolic axes corresponding to these elements.
If two axes $\ora{g},\ora{h}$ with respect to $\Sigma$ intersect transversely in $\tilde{\Sigma}$, the axes with respect to any other hyperbolic structure also intersect. Thus, intersection of hyperbolic axes is a topological invariant of a pair $g,h$.
It is standard that a measured lamination $\lambda$ defines a (dual) minimal irreducible $\Gamma$-action on $\mathbb{R}$-tree (see, for example, \cite[Chapter~11]{Kap09:HyperbolicManifold}).
It follows from the construction of $T_\lambda$ that the translation length on $T$ of $g \in \pi_1(S)$, $\| g \|$, is equal to $i(\lambda,[g])$, where $[g]$ denotes the free homotopy class of a closed curve associated to $g$.
Given a minimal action of a surface group $\Gamma$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree, if there is a measured lamination $\lambda$ and $\Gamma$-equivariant isometry $\phi \colon T_\lambda \to T$, we say that $T$ is \emph{dual to the measured lamination} $\lambda$.

Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths} first showed that a minimal action of a surface group is dual to a measured lamination if and only if 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item it is small,
    \item it \emph{preserves axes intersection}: for any pair $g,h \in \Gamma$ acting as hyperbolic isometries on $T$, we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $\ora{g}, \ora{h}$ cross.
\end{enumerate}
An action of a finitely generated group $\Gamma$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree with non-trivial translation length $\| \cdot \|$ is of three types~\cite[Page~583]{CM87:GroupActions}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fixed end: $\| \cdot \|$ trivial on $[G,G]$
\item Dihedral: $\| \cdot \|$ non-trivial on $[G,G]$, but $\| [g,h] \|=0$ for all $g,h$ hyperbolic in $T$.
\item Irreducible:  $\| [g,h] \| \neq 0$ for some $g,h$ hyperbolic in $T$.
\end{enumerate}
Morgan-Otal asked in~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths}: if a minimal action preserves axes intersection, does it follow it is small?
Without further assumptions, the axes intersection being preserved alone does not imply in general the action is small, as the example of a dihedral action of a surface group acting on $\mathbb{R}$ shows (this was noted by Skora in~\cite[Section~4]{Sko90:GeometricAction}).
However, in~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}, Skora showed that a minimal action $\Gamma \acts T$ of a surface group $\Gamma$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axes intersection.
In~\cite{Sk96:Splittings}, under the further assumption that if $S$ has cusps then parabolic elements in $\Gamma$ must be elliptic in $T$, Skora showed that $\Gamma \acts T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if the action is small. It follows from this that the action being small implies axes intersection being preserved. 
We give a new proof of Skora's theorem, as a consequence of our main result~Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and Proposition~\ref{prop:char_laminations_plus}.
The main result of this section will be the following.

\begin{theorem}
Let $\pi_1(S) \acts T$ on a $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T$, irreducible and that preserves axes intersection. Then it follows that $\| \cdot \|$ satisfies max-smoothing.
\label{prop:small}
\end{theorem}

Before we embark on the proof of the Theorem, we start with some elementary lemmas about irreducibility.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely generated group. $\Gamma$ on $T$ is irreducible if and only if there exist at least one good pair.
\label{lem:char_irred_good}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By~\cite[Theorem~2.7]{CM87:GroupActions} $\Gamma$ on $T$ is irreducible if and only if there exist a pair of hyperbolic elements whose axes have bounded non-empty intersection. By~\cite[Theorem~3.3]{CM87:GroupActions} this implies the existence of a good pair. On the other hand, one of the requirements of a good pair is that both elements are hyperbolic and their axes have bounded overlap.
\end{proof}

Another way to characterize irreducibility is in terms of non-intersection of axes.
\begin{lemma}
A finitely generated group $\Gamma$  acting on a real tree $T$ is irreducible if and only if there exist hyperbolic elements $g,h$ in $T$ so that $T_g \cap T_h = \emptyset$.
\label{lem:char_irred_noninter}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Follows by combining Lemmas~\ref{lem:char_irred_good} and \ref{lem:oppgoodpair}.
\end{proof}

Even though we will not needed it, as a warm-up, it is worth noticing that a reducible action does not satisfy smoothing.

In what follows, when we say a pair $a, b \in \pi_1(S)$ \emph{overlaps coherently} (resp. \emph{incoherently}) we mean the axes $T_a$ and $T_b$ in the tree overlap coherently (resp. incoherently).

\begin{lemma}
Let $\Gamma=\pi_1(S)$ be a surface group acting on a real tree $T$. Let $\| \cdot \|$ denote the translation length of this action.
If $\| \cdot \| \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies smoothing, then $(\Gamma, T)$ is irreducible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We start with a reducible action and show it cannot satisfy smoothing.
If the action is reducible, every pair $(g,h)$ of elements acting hyperbolically on $T$ has axes overlapping in a ray. By~\cite[Proposition~1.6~(3)]{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}, either $g,h$ overlap in the same direction, in which case
\begin{equation}
\|gh \|=\| g \| + \| h \| \mbox{ and } \|gh^{-1} \| = |\|g \|- \| h \| |,
\label{eq:reducible1}
\end{equation}
or $g,h$ overlap in opposite directions in which case
\begin{equation}
\|gh^{-1} \| =\| g\| + \| h \| \mbox{ and } \| gh \| = | \| g \| - \| h \||.
\label{eq:reducible2}
\end{equation}

Take a pair $a,x \in \Gamma$, so that $(a,x)$ $R$-cross.
WLOG, assume $\| a \|<\| x \|$.

\begin{claim}
Without loss of generality, we can furthermore assume that $a$ and $x$ overlap coherently.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Claim]
If $a,x$ overlap incoherently, then note $(xa^{-1}, ax)$ also $R$-cross, by Lemma~\ref{lem:righthandedfoundation}. 
Then $\| ax \| =\| x\|- \|a \|$ and $\| xa^{-1} \| =\| a \| + \| x \|$. Since $\| (xa^{-1})(ax) \| =\| x^2 \|$ is equal to $\| ax \| + \| xa^{-1} \|= 2\| x \|$, $ax$ and $xa^{-1}$ overlap coherently, and they do it in a subray of the overlap between $a$ and $x$.
\end{proof}
If $(a,x)$ R-cross, we use Equation~\ref{eq:cross-split} in Proposition~\ref{prop:join-split-geom}, with $m=-n$, $a=b$, and $n$ large enough, and choosing some admissible $r$ for the inequalities there, by smoothing,
\begin{equation}
 \| ax^nax^{-n} \| \geq \| ax^{n+r} \| + \| ax^{-n-r} \|.
 \label{eq:unoriented1}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, by a similar argument as in the proof of the above Claim, $ax^n, ax^{-n}$ overlap incoherently, so, we have that the left hand side is
\begin{equation}
\|ax^nax^{-n} \| = \| ax^n \| - \|ax^{-n} \| = \| a \| + n\| x \| - (n\| x \|-\|a\|) = 2\|a\|
\label{eq:lhs1}
\end{equation}
where we used that Equation~\ref{eq:reducible2} applies to the pair ($ax^n,ax^{-n}$) overlapping incoherently in the first equality, and Equation~\ref{eq:reducible1} for the coherent pair $(a,x^n)$ and Equation~\ref{eq:reducible2} for the incoherent pair $(a,x^{-n})$ in the second equality.
A similar computation, yields the following result for the right hand side:
\begin{equation}
\|ax^{n+r}\|=\|a\|+(n+r)\|x\| + (n+r)\|x\|-\|a\| = 2(n+r)\|x\|.
\label{eq:rhs1}
\end{equation}
Combining Equation~\ref{eq:unoriented1} with Equations~\ref{eq:lhs1} and~\ref{eq:rhs1} we get $\|a \| \geq (n+r)\|x\|$, which contradicts our assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Note that by Equations~\ref{eq:reducible1}~and~\ref{eq:reducible2} a reducible action always satisfies disconnected smoothing, but it never satisfies connected smoothing. Since a reducible action cannot be dual to a measured lamination, this shows once again that one cannot strengthen Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} by assuming only disconnected smoothing. Another example in the setting of the linear extension of extremal length can be seen in Example~\ref{ex:linEL}.
\label{rmk:reducible_action}
\end{remark}

Let's finally address the minimality assumption.
The translation length does not determine the tree uniquely.
Given a curve functional satisfying max-smoothing, it satisfies in particular Parry's axioms~\cite{Par91:Translation}, and hence it is dual to an action of a real tree. Since the length is non-zero, and the action is assumed irreducible, by \cite[Proposition~3.1]{CM87:GroupActions}, there is a unique minimal invariant subtree (i.e., a subtree invariant under $\Gamma$ that does not contain any proper non-degenerate invariant subtree). The length function for the restriction to this minimal invariant subtree is given by the same curve functional. Hence, without loss of generality, one can always restrict to the action on the minimal invariant subtree without changing the length functional.

The following result will be useful in the proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:small}.

\begin{lemma}
  For any action of a finitely-generated group on an $\bbR$-tree, if $g,h$ form a good pair, then $gh^{-1}$ and $h^{-1}g$ are hyperbolic isometries of $T$ and $T_{gh^{-1}}, T_{h^{-1}g}$ do not intersect.
    \label{lem:oppgoodpair}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
   This is contained in the proof of~\cite[Lemma~3.4]{CM87:GroupActions}.
\end{proof}

Let us now address the proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:small}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:small}]
We assume $(\Gamma, T)$ is irreducible and preserves axes intersection, and will prove $\| \cdot \|$ satisfies max-smoothing. 
Assume that for any hyperbolic pair $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$ on $T$ we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $\ora{g}$ and $\ora{h}$ cross, where $T_g$ denotes the axis of $g$ in the tree.
We will verify that $\| \cdot \|$, the translation length on $T$, satisfies the max-smoothing condition, and thus it comes from a measured lamination.
There are several cases to consider, depending on the relative position of the axes of the elements in the tree. For each case, different inequalities between $\| g\| + \| h \|$, $\| gh \|$ and $\| gh^{-1} \|$ are satisfied, and were analyzed by Paulin~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}. The strategy of the proof will be to compare the intersection of axes in the surface versus those in the tree, for each one of those cases.
We will see that max-smoothing is not satisfied in all cases. In such cases, we will find, using Lemma~\ref{lem:oppgoodpair}, pairs of elements whose axes do not intersect in the tree, but they do in the surface, which will allow us to rule them out, as that would contradict the assumption of axes preservation.

In order to prove Theorem~\ref{prop:small}, we consider several cases.

\begin{enumerate}[wide, labelindent=0pt]
\item Case 1 (hyperbolic): we suppose that for a pair of elements $g,h \in \pi_1(X)$, all $g,h$ act hyperbolically on $T$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Case 1a. First, suppose that $\ora{g}$ and $\ora{h}$ cross.
The two possible smoothings are then represented by $gh$ and $gh^{-1}$, so max-smoothing can be phrased as

\[
\|g \| + \| h \| = \max \{ \| gh \|, \| gh^{-1} \| \}.
\]

This relation is true by result~\cite[1.8]{CM87:GroupActions}, since $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$.
\item Case 1b. Suppose that $\ora{g}$ and $\ora{h}$ are parallel.
Max-smoothing amounts to

\[
\| gh \| = \max \{ \| g \| +\| h \| , \| gh^{-1} \| \}.
\]

 We have that $\ora{gh}$ and $\ora{hg}$ cross. Thus, by the axes preservation hypothesis, we know that $T_{gh} \cap T_{hg} \neq \emptyset$.

\begin{enumerate}

    \item $T_g$ and $T_h$ intersect in a non-degenerate interval with coherent orientations.

    $\|gh\|=\|g\|+\|h\|>\|gh^{-1}\|$, and the condition is satisfied by the result~\cite[1.8]{CM87:GroupActions}
    \item  $T_g$ and $T_h$ intersect in a non-degenerate interval with opposite orientations. Then, we would have

\subsection{Inequalities between intersection numbers}
\label{subsec:ineqintersection}

Finally, we give an application of our main result to domination of spectra of metric lengths on a surface. Let $\mu_1,\mu_2 \in \Curr(S)$. Say $\mu_1 \leq \mu_2$, or $\mu_2$ \emph{dominates} $\mu_1$ if $i(\mu_1,C) \leq i(\mu_2,C)$ for all $C \in \mathcal{C}(\Sigma)$.
We can use these extension results to study what intersection numbers dominating one curve over another imply about Liouville currents.

For example, we answer positively the following question at the end of \cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.
In fact, we have something much stronger

\begin{corollary}
If $C_2$ dominates $C_1$, then
    $\ell_g(C_1) \leq \ell_g(C_2)$ for all length metrics $g$ on $S$.
    \label{cor:domination}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First note that any length metric satisfies smoothing.
This argument is outlined essentially in \cite[Section~4.1]{MGT21:Smoothings}, but we reproduce it here.
 Take a
curve~$C$ with an
essential self-intersection~$x$, and a representative~$\gamma$. Then $\gamma$ has
a self-intersection point~$x'$ of the homotopy type of~$x$. If we
consider the
curve representative~$\gamma' \in C'$ obtained by smoothing at~$x'$, then,
since $\ell_g$ is an infimum, we have
\[
  \ell_g(C')\le \ell_g(C') = \ell_g(\gamma) = \ell_g(C),
\]
as desired. $\ell_g$ satisfies homogeneity and stability by \cite[Lemma~4.2]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Additivity is satisfied by definition of $\ell_g$ on weighted multi-curves by extending linearly.
It is clear that $\ell_g$ is a symmetric curve functional, since it is naturally defined on unoriented curves.
Thus, by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_g$ so that $\ell_g(C)=i(\mu_g,C)$ for all closed curves $C$. Approximating the current $\mu_g$ by weighted multi-curves and using continuity of intersection number on currents the result follows.
\end{proof}
