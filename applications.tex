\section{Measured laminations and hyperbolic metrics as curve functionals}
\label{sec:appsintersection}

\subsection{New definition of geodesic currents, filling currents and measured laminations}
\label{subsec:newdef}
This result gives a new definition of geodesic currents in terms of functionals on curves, by giving a bijection between geodesic currents and a subset of the space of curve functionals. These curve functionals form a subset of the functionals that extend continuously to currents, by~\cite[Theorem~A]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Moreover, the bijection is a homeomorphism onto its image when the space of curve functionals is equipped with the subspace topology coming from the product topology on $\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}(S)}$ (by~\cite[Theorem~11]{DLR10:DegenerationFlatMetrics}).

Thus, a restatement of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} is as follows.

\begin{theorem}
   Let $\Psi \colon \GC(S) \to \CurveFunct(S)$ be the homeomorphism onto its image $\mu \mapsto i(\mu, \cdot)$.
   The image is equal to the subset of additive convex functionals $\AConvex(S)$.
\end{theorem}

We can also characterize filling geodesic currents.

\begin{corollary}
  Let $S$ a closed surface,
  % \DMGtodo[inline]{(Punctures) For non- closed
  %   surfaces, one has to be more careful, see BIPP17 theorem.}
  $\mu$ a geodesic current and $f$ the functional on curves associated
  to $\mu$, i.e., so that $\mu_f=\mu$. The following subsets in
  bijection.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The set of filling currents, i.e., $ \GC_{\mbox{fill}}(S)$
    \item The set of functionals $f \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ which satisfy smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity properties, and so that their continuous extension to geodesic currents is positive.
    \item The set of curve functionals $f \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ for which their systole \[\sys(f)\coloneqq \{ f(C) : C \in \Curves(S), C \mbox{ non-trivial} \}\] is positive and satisfy smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity properties.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
These equivalences follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and \cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIPP17:StructureThmCurrents}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Measured laminations}

We will now characterize measured laminations as curve functionals in $\AConvex(S)$.

We will need the following observation about the measure of sets intersecting pairs of geodesic segments, proven in \cite[Lemma~6.2]{dRMG22:Duals}.

\begin{lemma}
For any points $x,y,z,w \in \wt{\Sigma}$, we have the following equalities:
\begin{align*}
\mu(G([{x},{w}), [{y},{z}))) +  &\mu(G(({x},{w}], ({y},{z}])) = \\
&\mu(G[{x},{z})) + \mu(G[{w},{y})) \\
&- \mu(G([{w},{z})) - \mu(G([{x},{y}))
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
\mu(G(({x},{w}], ({y},{z}])) +   &\mu(G([{x},{w}), [{y},{z})))= \\
&\mu(G({x},{z}]) + \mu(G({w},{y}]) \\
&- \mu(G(({w},{z}]) - \mu(G(({x},{y}])
\end{align*}
\label{lem:otalclaim}
\end{lemma}

We need first the following hyperbolic geometry lemma.

\begin{proposition}
Consider two elements $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ with hyperbolic axes $A_g$ and $A_h$ that intersect at a point $O$, and translation lengths $\ell(g),\ell(h)$.
We define the following four points:

\begin{itemize} \item $P$ is the point on $A_g$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(g)/2$;
\item $Q$ is the point on $A_h$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $\ell(h)/2$;
\item $Q'$ is the point on $A_h$ obtained by moving forward from $O$ by distance $\ell(h)/2$;
\item $P'$ is the point on $A_g$ obtained by moving backward from $O$ by distance $\ell(g)/2$.
\end{itemize}
We have the following (see Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A_{gh}$ is the line containing the segment $(Q,P]$ of length $\ell(hg)/2$.
\item $A_{hg}$ is the line containing the segment $(P',Q']$ of length $\ell(hg)/2$.
\item $A_{gh^{-1}}$ is the line containing the segment $(Q',P]$ of length $\ell(gh^{-1})/2$.
\item $A_{h^{-1}g}$ is the line containing the segment $(P',Q]$ of length $\ell(h^{-1}g)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, the union $G((Q,P])\cup G((P',Q'])$ (resp. $G((Q',P]) \cup G((P',Q])$) is a fundamental domain for the action of $gh$ on the set of geodesics $G(\Sigma)$, (resp. a fundamental domain for the action of $gh^{-1}$).
\label{prop:keygeometry}
\end{proposition}

  \begin{figure}
\centering{
\resizebox{115mm}{!}{\svgfignormal{smoothing_1}}}
\caption{Hyperbolic geometry of the smoothing between two distinct curves.}
\label{fig:smoothing_1}
\end{figure}

\begin{proof}
We prove the first case. Since $A_g$ and $A_h$ intersect at $O$, so do the curves $[g]$ and $[h]$ at some point $o \in \Sigma$.
Perform a smoothing opperation obtaining a new curve $\alpha$ of length $\ell(g) + \ell(h)$.
A lift $a$  of this curve in $\wt{X}$ going through $O$ is a piecewise geodesic consisting of a segment on $A_h$ of length $\ell(h)$, then a segment of $A_{h^{-1}gh}$ of length $\ell(g)$, etc.
Let $\alpha_*$ be the geodesic representative in the homotopy class of $\alpha$.
Now, connecting the midpoints of the geodesic segments of $a$ we obtain another piecewise geodesic $a'$ which is, in fact, a geodesic segment.
Indeed, we note that $a$ is invariant under angle $\pi$ rotation around any of the intersection points between $a$ and $a'$.
Since $a$ and $a'$ have the same stabilizer, they are lifts of the same geodesic $\alpha$.
Thus, the lift of $\alpha_*$ is $a'$, which is determined by two of the midpoints defining $a$, such as $P'$ and $Q'$.
As for the translation lengths, we note that $h=r_O r_{Q'}$ where $r_O$ denotes the reflection along the geodesic perpendicular to $A_h$ at $O$,
and $r_{Q'}$ is the corresponding reflection at $Q'$.
Similarly, we can write $g=r_{P'}r_O$, and thus $gh=(r_{P'}r_O)(r_O r_{Q'})=r_{P'}r_{Q'}$, which corresponds to a translation along $a'$ of translation length
twice the distance between $P'$ and $Q'$.
The other cases follow similarly.
As for the assertion on fundamental domains, we note that the segment $(P',Q']$ on the axis of $A_{hg}$, is sent by $g$ to a segment $(P,R]$, where $R=g(Q')$ appears forward of $P$ in according to the orientation of $A_{hg}$, and the segment $(Q,R]$ is a fundamental domain for the action of $gh$ on its axis $A_{gh}$, thus $G((Q,R])=G((Q,P]) \cup g G((P',Q'])$ is a fundamental domain for the action of $hg$ on $G(\Sigma)$, thus the claim follows.
The other claim is similar.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a measured lamination if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies the so-called \emph{disconnected max-smoothing property}:
    For any pair of multi-curves $C_1$ and $C_2$ with an essential
    crossing between $C_1,C_2$, we have
    \begin{equation}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    \label{thm:char_laminations}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first $f$ satisfies the disconnected max-smoothing.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_f$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for all closed curve $C$.
It remains to show that $\mu_f$ is a measured lamination.
Given elements $g, h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ with intersecting axes,
consider the hyperbolic parallelogram $H$ at the center of Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing_1}.
Consider the set $R_1 \coloneqq R_{g,h}$ consisting of the geodesics intersecting both sides of $H$ contained in $A_{h^{-1}g}$ and $A_{gh^{-1}}$. 

Let $R_1^{\perp}$ be the set of geodesics intersecting the sides of $H$ on the axes $A_{gh}$ and $A_{hg}$.

Given elements $g$ and $h$  with intersecting axes, let $B_{g,h}$ be the box with vertices the endpoints of $g$ and $h$.

Taking powers $g^n$ and $h^n$, and obtaining a corresponding hyperbolic parallelogram $H_n$ for $g^n,h^n$, we obtain the set $R_n \coloneqq R_{g^n,h^n}$ defined as analogously.
Observe that $R_n \subset R_{n+1}$, and $\cup_{n \geq 1} R_n = B_{g,h}$.
Hence, by continuity of measures from below, we have
\[
\lim_n \mu(R_n) \to \mu(B_{g,h}).
\]
If we let $a=g^n$ and $b=h^n$, we have, by Lemma~\ref{lem:otalclaim} and Proposition~\ref{prop:keygeometry},
\[\mu(R_n)=1/2(f(a) + f(b)-f(ab^{-1})).\]

Similarly, let $R_n^T$ denote the set of geodesics intersecting the sides of $H_n$ contained in $A_{ab}$ and $A_{ba}$. Again, by Lemma~\ref{lem:otalclaim} and Proposition~\ref{prop:keygeometry}, we get
\[
\mu(R_n^T)=1/2(f(a) + f(b)-f(ab)).
\]
We also have $R_n^{\perp} \subset R_{n+1}^{\perp}$ and $\cup_{n \geq 1} R_n^{\perp} = B_{g,h}^{\perp}$, where $B_{g,h}^{\perp}$ denotes the box complementary to $B_{g,h}$.
Thus, as above, we have $\lim_n \mu(R_n^{\perp})=\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})$.
By max-smoothing, for every $n$, we have$\mu(R_n^T)=0$ or $\mu(R_n)=0$.
Since the sets $R_n$ and $R_n^{\perp}$ are nested, if $\mu(R_1) \neq 0$, then $\mu(R_n) \neq 0$ for all $0$ and thus $\mu(R_n^{\perp})=0$ for all $n \geq 1$.
Hence, $\mu(B_{g,h})=0$ or $\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})=0$.
By density of endpoints of closed geodesics, these boxes have vertices on a dense set of points of the boundary of $\partial \wt{\Sigma}$.
Therefore, by~\cite[Proposition~2.1]{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, $\mu$ is a measured lamination.

Now we prove the other implication.
First, note that disconnected max-smoothing means that $\mu(R_{g,h})=0$ or $\mu(R_{g,h}^{\perp})=0$ for any pair of intersecting $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$.
We proceed by proving the counter-positive.
Suppose max-smoothing is not satisfied, so that we have $g,h$ for which both $\mu(R_{g,h})>0$ and $\mu(R_{g,h}^T)>0$.
This implies that $\mu(B_{g,h}) >0$ and $\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp}) >0$, so by~\cite[Proposition~2.1]{BIPP21:PositiveCrossratios}, $\mu$ is not a measured lamination.
\end{proof}

Hence, it is only necessary to check max-smoothing for essential crossings between multi-curves (disconnected max-smoothing) to conclude $f$ is a measured lamination, i.e., one does not have to check essential self-intersections of a multi-curve with itself. It is however true that intersection number with a measured lamination satisfies max-smoothing condition in this bigger generality. Putting everything together, we get the following result.

\begin{theorem}
A curve functional $f$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it satisfies additivity, homogeneity, stability and the \emph{max-smoothing property}:
    For any essential crossing of a multi-curve $C$, we have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:MLsmoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right) \right\}.
    \end{equation}
    \label{prop:char_laminations_plus}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $f$ is a curve functional satisfying max-smoothing, additivity, stability and homogeneity. Max-smoothing clearly implies smoothing, as well as disconnected max-smoothing. Thus, by Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations}, $f$ is dual to a measured lamination. On the other hand, $f$ is dual to a measured lamination,  Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations} implies disconnected max-smoothing. Connected max-smoothing follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:smoothing_eq}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Liouville currents}
\label{subsec:liouville}
In \cite[Theorem~13]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent}, Bonahon gives an algebraic characterization for a geodesic current to be a Liouville current for a hyperbolic structure $X$.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, for any four points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$, we have
   \[
      e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} + e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])}=1.
   \]
   \label{thm:bonahonliouville}
\end{theorem}

We can prove the following characterization.

\begin{theorem}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies additivity, stability, homogeneity and,
    for any pair of multi-curves $C_1$ and $C_2$ with an essential crossing between $C_1,C_2$, we have \begin{equation}
     e^{f(C_1) + f(C_2)/2}= e^{f\left(\mfig{curves-10}\right)/2} =  e^{f\left(\mfig{curves-11}\right)/2}+e^{f\left(\mfig{curves-12}\right)/2}
    \end{equation}
    \label{prop:char_liouville}
  \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof is similar to that of Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations}.

By the assumptions on $f$, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_f$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu_f,C)$ for all closed curve $C$.
It remains to show that $\mu_f$ is a Liouville current.
Given elements $g, h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$ with intersecting axes, we consider powers $g^n$ and $h^n$, and obtaining a corresponding hyperbolic parallelogram $H_n$ for $g^n,h^n$, we obtain the set $R_n \coloneqq R_{g^n,h^n}$ defined as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:char_laminations}.
By the same considerations as in that proof, if we let $a=g^n$ and $b=h^n$, we get
\[\mu(R_n)=A_n + B_n-C_n\]
and
\[
\mu(R_n^T)=A_n + B_n -D_n,
\]
where $A_n=f(a)/2$, $B_n=f(b)/2$, $C_n=f(ab^{-1})/2$ and $D_n=f(ab)/2$.
Now, we compute
\[
e^{-\mu(R_n)} + e^{-\mu(R_n^{\perp})} = e^{-(A_n+B_n)+C_n} + e^{-(A_n+B_n)+D_n} = e^{-(A_n+B_n)} (e^{C_n} + e^{D_n}).
\]
On the other hand, when $n$ goes to infinity,
the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing relation yields
\[
e^{A_n}e^{B_n} = e^{C_n}+e^{D_n}
\]
Therefore, we have
\[
e^{-\mu(R_n)} + e^{-\mu(R_n^{\perp})} =1.
\]
We also have $R_n^{\perp} \subset R_{n+1}^{\perp}$ and $\cup_{n \geq 1} R_n^{\perp} = B_{g,h}^{\perp}$, where $B_{g,h}^{\perp}$ denotes the box complementary to $B_{g,h}$, determined by the endpoints of $g$ and $h$.
Thus, we have $\lim_n \mu(R_n)=\mu(B_{g,h})$ and $\lim_n \mu(R_n^{\perp})=\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})$.
Therefore, we have the following relation
\[
e^{-\mu(B_{g,h})} + e^{-\mu(B_{g,h}^{\perp})} =1
\]
for any intersecting pair $g,h \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$.
By density of $B_{g,h}$ in the space of boxes of geodesics as $g,h$ runs over intersecting pairs in $\pi_1(\Sigma)$, given any box $B$, we can find pairs $g',h'$ and $g'', h''$ so that $B_{g',h'} \subset B \subset B_{g'',h''}$ and $B_{g'',h''}^{\perp} \subset B^{\perp} \subset B_{g',h'}^{\perp}$.
Therefore, we get the inequalities
\[
e^{-\mu(B_{g'',h''})} \leq e^{-\mu(B)} \leq e^{-\mu(B_{g',h'})}
\]
and
\[
1-e^{-\mu(B_{g'',h''})} \leq 1- e^{-\mu(B)} \leq 1-e^{-\mu(B_{g',h'})}.
\]
Adding them up, we get
\[
e^{-\mu(B)} + e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}=1.
\]
Therefore, Theorem~\ref{thm:bonahonliouville} shows $\mu$ is a Liouville current.
The other implication follows from an elementary computation of hyperbolic cross-ratio.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Another characterizing condition that turns out to be equivalent to the hypothesis in     Theorem~\ref{prop:char_liouville}  is the \emph{disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing property}

\[
\lambda\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) =  \lambda\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)+\lambda\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)
\]
where $\lambda(C) \coloneqq 2\cosh(f(C)/2)$.
Indeed, from this relation, following the proof, one obtains
\[
e^{A_n}e^{B_n} \sim e^{C_n}+e^{D_n}
\]
where $\sim$ means the ratio of the left hand side to the right hand side tends to $1$ as $n$ goes to infinity (since $e^{A_n} \sim 2\cosh(f(A_n)/2)$). \DMGtodo[inline]{add here why this relation is interesting and reference to lambda length in positivity paper, maybe}
On the other hand, if $\mu$ is hyperbolic Liouville current then the disconnected $\lambda$-smoothing property follows from the trace relation in $\SL(2,\mathbb{R})$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  The countable set of equalities in Theorem~\ref{thm:bonahonliouville} can be seen as an extreme case of a more general set of inequalities satisfied by any so-called \emph{strongly hyperbolic} space (with parameter $\epsilon=1$), in the sense of~\cite{BS16:StrongHyp}. One can check these spaces satisfy the inequality
  \[
       e^{f(C_1) + f(C_2)}= e^{f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2} \leq e^{f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)/2}+e^{f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)/2}.
  \]
  It would be interesting to see if this condition characterizes currents whose dual pseudo-metric spaces are strongly hyperbolic. For more details see~\cite{JMG:StrongDuals}.
\end{remark}

In fact, an improvement of Theorem~\ref{thm:bonahonliouville} was given by Hamenst\"adt in \cite[Corollary~2.12]{Ham99:Cocycles}.

\begin{theorem}
   A geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(S)$ is $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$ for some $\Sigma$ if and only if, there exist a number $\rho>1$ so that for any four ordered points $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$, we have
    \[
      \frac{1}{\rho} \cdot  e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} \leq 1- e^{-\mu([y,z]\times [w,x])} \leq \rho \cdot e^{-\mu([x,y]\times[z,w])} 
   \]
   \label{thm:ham}
\end{theorem}

Using this, we can prove the following.

\begin{theorem}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies additivity, stability, homogeneity and the following inequalities.
    There exists a constant $C \geq 1$ so that, for any pair of multi-curves $C_1$ and $C_2$ with an essential crossing between $C_1,C_2$, we have \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \exp \left[ f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2-f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)/2 \right] - \exp \left[ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)/2-f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)/2 \right] \leq C
    \end{equation}
    and
    \begin{equation}
     1/C \leq  \exp \left[ f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2-f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)/2 \right] - \exp \left[ f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)/2-f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)/2 \right] \leq C
    \end{equation}
    \label{thm:char_liouville_strong}
  \end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assumption can be written as 

 \begin{equation}
     1/C \leq  \frac{1- e^{-f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2 + f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)/2}}{e^{- f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2 + f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)/2}} \leq C
    \end{equation}
    and
 \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \frac{1- e^{-f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2 + f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right)/2}}{e^{-f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2 + f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)/2}} \leq C.
    \end{equation}

    
    Now, as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:char_liouville}, this gives us

     \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \frac{1 - e^{-\mu(B)}}{e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}} \leq C
    \end{equation}
    and
    \begin{equation}
    1/C \leq \frac{1 - e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}}{e^{-\mu(B)}} \leq C
    \end{equation}
which, by Theorem~\ref{thm:ham}, implies $\mu$ is a hyperbolic Liouville current.
On the other hand, if $\mu$ is a Liouville current, by Theorem~\ref{prop:char_liouville}, letting $x \coloneqq f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2$, $y \coloneqq f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right)/2$ and $z \coloneqq f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right)/2$, we get
\[
e^{x} - e^{y}  = e^{-x} -e^{-y}  + e^{z} - e^{-z}.
\]
Thus, since $y \leq x$, we have $e^{-x} -e^{-y} >0$, and therefore
\[
e^{z} \leq e^{x} - e^{y} \leq 3 e^{z}
\]
which gives one pair of inequalities. The other follows by an
analogous computation.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Observe that when $C=1$, the hypothesis simplifies to that of Theorem~\ref{prop:char_liouville}. This condition is however, in principle, easier to check.
\end{remark}






\section{Skora's theorem: $\mathbb{R}$-trees dual to measured laminations}
\label{sec:skora}
As an application, we reprove a result of Skora, giving a characterization of actions of surface groups on $\mathbb{R}$-trees that are dual to measured laminations.
An \emph{$\mathbb{R}$-tree} is a metric space $T$ such that for
all $x,y \in T$, the intersection of all connected subsets containing both $x$ and $y$ isometric to the interval $[0, d(x, y)]$. This intersection is denoted
$[x, y]$, and called an \emph{arc}. If $G$ is a group, a $G$-tree is an $\mathbb{R}$-tree together with a group action $G \acts T$ by isometries.
There are two types of isometries: those with fixed points, called \emph{elliptic}, and those without fixed points, called \emph{hyperbolic}.
Given $g\in G$, $\| g\|=\inf_{x \in T} d(x,gx)$, and let $T_g \coloneqq \{ x\in T : d(x,gx)=\| g\| \}$.
If $g$ is elliptic, then $\| g \| =0$ (and the converse is also true).
If $g$ is hyperbolic, then $\| g | >0$, and $g$ translates along $T_g$ by distance $\| g\|$, thus leaving $T_g$ invariant. We call $T_g$ the \emph{axis} of $g$.
We say that $G \acts T$ is \emph{small} if the stabilizer of any (non-degenerate) arc is (virtually) cyclic.
We say that $G \acts T$ is \emph{minimal} if there is no proper subtree of $T$ that is $G$-invariant.
Consider two hyperbolic elements $g, h \in G$ with axes in $\mathbb{R}$-tree given respectively by $T_g,T_h$.
If $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$, we say $T_g$ and $T_h$ \emph{intersect}. Otherwise, we say that they do not intersect.
Let $\Delta \coloneqq T_g \cap T_h$. Suppose $\Delta$ is not a singleton, and $g$ and $h$ translate in the same direction along $\Delta$, we say that $g,h$ and \emph{overlap coherently}.
If $\Delta$ is not a singleton, and $g$ and $h$ translate in opposite directions along $\Delta$, we say that $g,h$ and \emph{overlap incoherently}.
Following~\cite[3.2]{CM87:GroupActions}, we say that $g,h$ form a \emph{good pair} if they are both hyperbolic, their overlap $\Delta$ is not a singleton, they overlap coherently and $\Delta< \min \{ \| g \|, \| h \| \}$.


Given $G=\pi_1(S)$, and $\Sigma$ a hyperbolic structure on $S$, let $A_g, A_h$ be the hyperbolic axes corresponding to these elements.
If two axes $A_g,A_h$ with respect to $\Sigma$ intersect transversely in $\tilde{\Sigma}$, the axes with respect to any other hyperbolic structure also intersect. Thus, intersection of hyperbolic axes is a topological invariant of a pair $g,h$.
It is standard that a measured lamination $\lambda$ defines a (dual) $\pi_1(\Sigma)$ minimal irreducible action on $\mathbb{R}$-tree (see, for example, \cite[Chapter~11]{Kap09:HyperbolicManifold}).
It follows from the construction of $T(\lambda)$ is that the translation length on $T$ of $g \in \pi_1(S)$, $\| g \|$, is equal to $i(\lambda,[g])$, where $[g]$ denotes the free homotopy class of a closed curve associated to $g$.
Given a minimal action of a surface group $G$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree, if there is a measured lamination $\lambda$ and $\pi_1(\Sigma)$-equivariant isometry $\phi \colon T(\lambda) \to T$, we say that $T$ is \emph{dual to the measured lamination} $\lambda$.

Morgan-Otal~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths} first showed that a minimal action of a surface group is dual to a measured lamination if and only if 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item it is small,
    \item it \emph{preserves axes intersection}: for any pair $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$ of hyperbolic elements, we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $A_g \cap A_h \neq \emptyset$.
\end{enumerate}
An action of a group $G$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree with non-trivial translation length $\| \cdot \|$ is of three types~\cite[Page~583]{CM87:GroupActions}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fixed end: $\| \cdot \|$ trivial on $[G,G]$
\item Dihedral: $\| \cdot \|$ non-trivial on $[G,G]$, but $\| [g,h] \|=0$ for all $g,h$ hyperbolic in $G$.
\item Irreducible:  $\| [g,h] \| \neq 0$ for some $g,h$ hyperbolic in $G$.
\end{enumerate}
Morgan-Otal asked in~\cite{MO93:RelativeGrowths}: if a minimal action preserves axes intersection, does it follow it is small?
Without further assumptions, the axes intersection being preserved alone does not imply in general the action is small, as the example of a dihedral action of a surface group acting on $\mathbb{R}$ shows (this was noted by Skora in~\cite[Section~4]{Sko90:GeometricAction}).
However, in~\cite{Sko90:GeometricAction}, Skora showed that a minimal action $G \acts T$ of a surface group $G$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axes intersection.
In~\cite{Sk96:Splittings}, under the further assumption that if $S$ has cusps then parabolic elements in $G$ must be elliptic in $T$, Skora showed that $G \acts T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if the action is small. It follows from this that the action being small implies axes intersection being preserved. 
We give a new proof of Skora's theorem, as a consequence of our main result~Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and Proposition~\ref{prop:char_laminations_plus}.

\begin{theorem}
A minimal action of $\pi_1(S)$ on an $\mathbb{R}$-tree $T$ is dual to a measured lamination if and only if it is irreducible and preserves axes intersection.
\label{prop:small}
\end{theorem}

Assume that for any hyperbolic pair $g,h \in \pi_1(S)$ on $T$ we have $T_g \cap T_h \neq \emptyset$ whenever $A_g \cap A_h \neq \emptyset$, where $T_g$ denotes the axis of $g$ in the tree, and $A_g$ the hyperbolic axis on $\Sigma$.

There are several cases to consider, depending on the relative position of the axes of the elements in the tree. For each case, different inequalities between $\| g\| + \| h \|$, $\| gh \|$ and $\| gh^{-1} \|$ are satisfied, and were analyzed by Paulin~\cite{Pa89:Paulin1989TheGT}. The strategy of the proof will be to compare the intersection of axes in the surface versus those in the tree, for each one of those cases.
In some of them, max-smoothing will be satisfied, but it will not in all of them. In the latter case, we will find a pair of elements whose axes do not intersect in the tree, but they do in the surface, which will allow us to rule that case out.

\end{lemma}

\subsection{Inequalities between intersection numbers}
\label{subsec:ineqintersection}

Finally, we give an application of our main result to domination of spectra of metric lengths on a surface. Let $\mu_1,\mu_2 \in \mathcal{G}\mathcal{C}(S)$. Say $\mu_1 \leq \mu_2$, or $\mu_2$ \emph{dominates} $\mu_1$ if $i(\mu_1,C) \leq i(\mu_2,C)$ for all $C \in \mathcal{C}(\Sigma)$.
We can use these extension results to study what intersection numbers dominating one curve over another imply about Liouville currents.

For example, we answer positively the following question at the end of \cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.
In fact, we have something much stronger

\begin{corollary}
If $C_2$ dominates $C_1$, then
    $\ell_g(C_1) \leq \ell_g(C_2)$ for all length metrics $g$ on $\Sigma$.
    \label{cor:domination}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First note that any length metric satisfies smoothing.
This argument is outlined essentially in \cite[Section~4.1]{MGT21:Smoothings}, but we reproduce it here.
 Take a
curve~$C$ with an
essential self-intersection~$x$, and a representative~$\gamma$. Then $\gamma$ has
a self-intersection point~$x'$ of the homotopy type of~$x$. If we
consider the
curve representative~$\gamma' \in C'$ obtained by smoothing at~$x'$, then,
since $\ell_g$ is an infimum, we have
\[
  \ell_g(C')\le \ell_g(C') = \ell_g(\gamma) = \ell_g(C),
\]
as desired. $\ell_g$ satisfies homogeneity and stability by \cite[Lemma~4.2]{MGT21:Smoothings}. Additivity is satisfied by definition of $\ell_g$ on weighted multi-curves by extending linearly.
It is clear that $\ell_g$ is a symmetric curve functional, since it is naturally defined on unoriented curves.
Thus, by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu_g$ so that $\ell_g(C)=i(\mu_g,C)$ for all closed curves $C$. Approximating the current $\mu_g$ by weighted multi-curves and using continuity of intersection number on currents the result follows.
\end{proof}
