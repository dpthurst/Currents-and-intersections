\subsection{Redefining geodesic currents}
This result gives a new definition of geodesic currents in terms of functionals on curves. These functionals form a subset of the functionals that extend continuously to currents, by Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}.

\begin{corollary}
Let $S$ a closed surface, $\mu$ a geodesic current and $f$ the functional on curves associated to $\mu$, i.e., so that $\mu_f=\mu$. The following are equivalent:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mu \in \GC_{\mbox{fill}}(S)$
    \item $f \colon \Curves(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ has a positive extension to geodesic currents.
    \item $\sys(f)>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
(1) is equivalent to (2) by our Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} and Proposition~\ref{prop:filling}.
(2) is equivalent to (3) by\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIPP17:StructureThmCurrents}, where the lower bound in (3) is given by the systole of $\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
$\mu \in \GC(S)$ is a measured lamination if and only if the functional $f$ corresponding to $\mu$ satisfies
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:MLsmoothing}
      f\left(\mfig{curves-0}\right) = \max \left\{ f\left(\mfig{curves-1}\right),f\left(\mfig{curves-2}\right) \right\}
    \end{equation}
    \label{prop:char_laminations}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let us call equation~\ref{eq:MLsmoothing} condition (1). Recall that Bonahon shows in \cite[Proposition~17]{Bonahon88:GeodesicCurrent} that $\mu$ is a measured lamination if and only if $i(\mu,\mu)=0$. Let us call this condition (2).
First we show (2) implies (1).
Suppose $\mu$ is a measured lamination, and let us assume for now $\mu$ is just a simple curve. Then Condition (1) is \cite[Lemma~1]{Thu00:GeometricIntersection}. If $\mu$ is any measured lamination, then by continuity of intersection number and density of weighted simple closed geodesics in the space of measured laminations, \cite[Lemma~1]{Thu00:GeometricIntersection} also holds, so the conclusion follows.
On the other hand, suppose that condition (1) for a functional $f$ is satisfied, as well as additivity, homogeneity and stability. Since condition (1) implies the (unoriented) smoothing condition, by Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, there exists a geodesic current $\mu$ so that $f(C)=i(\mu,C)$ for all curves $C$. We want to show $\mu$ is a measured lamination. 
First, $f|_{\mathcal{S}}(s)=i(\lambda,s)$ for any simple curve $s$, by work of Feng Luo. Indeed, let $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ be a triple of elements (triangle). The relations in \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Lu10} follows by the max smoothing relation. \DMGtodo[inline]{We did the computations, write them out.}
Thus,
\[
i(\mu, s)=i(\lambda, s)
\]
for any simple curve.
By max-smoothing, a sequence of smoothings on $\mu$ yields a simple multi-curve $\mu_0$ so that
\[
i(\mu,\mu)=i(\mu,\mu_0)
\]
If $i(\lambda, \mu_0)=0$, then $i(\mu,\mu_0)=i(\mu,\mu)=0$, so we are done.
If $i(\lambda, \mu_0) \neq 0$, then $i(\lambda, \mu) \neq 0$, since $\mu$ dominates $\mu_0$, but we know $i(\lambda, \mu)=i(\lambda,\lambda)= 0$, which is a contradiction. This finishes the proof.

\subsection{Topology on functionals}

Note that by Subsection~\ref{sec:intersectioncurrents}, for functionals coming from currents, $f_i \to f$ if and only if $f_i(c) \to f(c)$ for each $c \in \Curves(S)$, i.e., pointwise convergence.
This is therefore the natural topology in the space of functionals on curves.

\subsection{A new expression for the Thurston metric}


In \cite[Theorem~8.5]{Thu98:MinimalStretch} Bill Thurston defines the following (asymmetric) metric on $\Teich(S)$.

\[
d_{\mathit{Thu}}(\Sigma,\Sigma') \coloneqq \sup_{\lambda \in \ML}\log \frac{\ell_{\Sigma}(\lambda)}{\ell_{\Sigma'}(\lambda)}
\]

As an immediate application of Theorem \ref{thm:intersect} we give a new characterization of Thurston metric in terms of a supremum over functionals on curves.

\begin{corollary}
\[
d_{\mathit{Thu}}(\Sigma,\Sigma') \coloneqq \sup_{f} \log \frac{f(\Sigma)}{f(\Sigma')}
\]

where $f$ runs over all non-zero functionals $f \colon \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying smoothing, smoothing, stability and homogeneity properties.
\label{cor:Thurston}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\[
d_{\mathit{Thu}}(\Sigma,\Sigma') \coloneqq sup_{f} \log \frac{f(\Sigma)}{f(\Sigma')} = \sup_{\mu \in \mathcal{G}\mathcal{C}(S)} \log \frac{i(\mu,\Sigma)}{i(\mu,\Sigma')} = \sup_{\lambda \in \ML(S)} \log \frac{i(\lambda,\Sigma)}{i(\lambda,\Sigma')}
\]
The first equality follows by our Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}, and the second equality follows because, by \cite[Theorem~8.5]{Thu98:MinimalStretch}, the distance is attained by a minimal stretch map, which dilates the hyperbolic lengths by the same bounded amount.
\end{proof}

By considering different families of continuous functionals $\mathcal{F}$ on geodesic currents over which we take the supremum in Corollary~\ref{cor:Thurston}, we can obtain new distances.
A possible future question is the following.

\begin{question}

What is the distance given by
 \[
d_{\mathcal{F}}(\Sigma,\Sigma')=\sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \log \frac{f(\mathcal{L}_\Sigma)}{f(\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma'})}
\]
where $\mathcal{F}$ consists of positive functionals $f : \mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying convex union, quasi-smoothing (for a fixed uniform quasi-smoothing constant), stability and homogeneity properties.
 
\end{question}

By the formula above, each family of functionals $\mathcal{F}$ also gives a distance between currents.
There are other natural notions of distances between measures, coming from the theory of optimal transport (e.g. Wasserstein metric).
These can be adapted for geodesic currents. Are these two families of distances on geodesic currents related?

\begin{proposition}
 $B_{\rho}^{\alpha}$ is positive if and only if $l_{\rho}^{\alpha}$ satisfies smoothing condition.
\end{proposition}

\[ 
B_{\rho}^{\alpha}(a,b,c,d)\coloneqq \mu_{\rho}^{\alpha}([a,b)\times[c,d)).
\]

\subsection{Inequalities between intersection numbers}
\label{subsec:ineqintersection}

Let $\mu_1,\mu_2 \in \mathcal{G}\mathcal{C}(S)$. Say $\mu_1 \leq \mu_2$, or $\mu_2$ \emph{dominates} $\mu_1$ if $i(\mu_1,C) \leq i(\mu_2,C)$ for all $C \in \mathcal{C}(\Sigma)$.
We can use these extension results to study what intersection numbers dominating one curve over another imply about Liouville currents.

For example, we answer positively the following question at the end of \cite{NC01:ShortestGeodesics}.
In fact, we have something much stronger

\begin{corollary}
If $C_2$ dominates $C_1$, then
    $\ell_g(C_1) \leq \ell_g(C_2)$ for all metric lengths $g$ satisfying conditions of theorem \ref{thm:intersect} (not necessarily Riemannian).
\end{corollary}
