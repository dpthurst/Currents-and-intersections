\subsection{Lengths, stable lengths and cross-ratios associated to geodesic currents}

In this section we mention some constructions present in the literature that are related to Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect}.
First, given a geodesic current $\mu$ one can define a pseudo-distance on $\tilde{\Sigma}$ and use it to define a length of paths on $\Sigma$, which coincides with intersection number with $\mu$. 
Second, given a geodesic current one can further define a notion of stable length on an associated space (not on $\Sigma$), which coincides with intersection number when properly interpreted.
Third, given a generalized cross-ratio, one can construct a geodesic current. The first two constructions are just given to motivate the problem, and other than exposing them here, won't be pursued further. The third construction will play a role in Subsection~\ref{subsec:poscross}.

\subsection{Relation of intersection number to lengths of paths and stable lengths}

A \emph{pseudo-distance}  $d : \tilde{\Sigma} \times \tilde{\Sigma} \to [0,\infty]$ on $\tilde{\Sigma}$ is a map which is zero on the diagonal and satisfies the triangular inequality. For any two points $x,y \in \tilde{\Sigma}$, and a geodesic current $\mu \in \GC(\Sigma)$. In \cite[Proposition~4.1]{BIPP19:CurrentSystoles} a pseudo-distance is defined as follows. Let $G([x,y)) \subset G(\Sigma)$ denote the subset of geodesics intersecting the geodesic segment $[x,y)$ in $\tilde{\Sigma}$ transversely.
We define
\[
d_{\mu}(x,y) \coloneqq \frac{1}{2} (\mu(G([x,y)) + \mu(G((x,y])).
\]
A similar pseudo-distance had been previously defined by Glorieux in \cite[Definition~2.2]{Glo17:CriticalExponents}. The one we just defined has the added feature that is \emph{straight} on points lying on a $\Sigma$-geodesic, that is, $d_{\mu}(x,y)=d_{\mu}(x,z) + d_{\mu}(z,y)$.
We note that it is possible that $d_{\mu}(x,y)=0$ even though $x \neq y$, For instance, if $\mu=C$ for some closed curve, then any $x,y \in \tilde{\Sigma}$ for which the $\Sigma$-geodesic segment connecting $x$ and $y$ does not intersect a lift of $C$ will satisfy this.
If $\mu$ is filling, then $d_{\mu}$ is a proper distance (see arguments in \cite[Proposition~2.8]{Glo17:CriticalExponents}).

\subsection{Intersection and lengths of paths on $\tilde{\Sigma}$}

Associated to a pseudo-distance $d$, there is a length of paths on $\tilde{\Sigma}$ defined as follows,
\[
  L_d(c) \coloneqq \sup \left\{ \sum_{i=0}^n d(c(t_i),c(t_{i+1}) : n \geq 1, t_0=r \leq t_1 \leq \cdots t_n = s \right\}.
\]
It satisfies the following properties.
\cite[Lemma~4,3]{BIPP19:CurrentSystoles}.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $c : [r,s] \to \tilde{\Sigma}$, then $L(c) \geq d(c(r),c(s))$, and if $c$ parametrizes a geodesic segment then equality holds.
    \item If a path $c$ is a concatenation of two paths $c_1 \circ c_2$, then
    \[
      L(c) = L(c_1) + L(c_2).
    \]
\end{enumerate}


For the pseudo-distance $d_{\mu}$, we will denote the corresponding length by $L_{\mu}$.


$L_{\mu}$ induces a length function on the surface $\Sigma$ by defining, for a continuous path $c : [r,s] \to S$, the length $L_{\mu}(c)$ to be $L_{\mu}(\tilde{c})$ for any continuous lift $\tilde{c}$.

It follows \cite[Proposition~4.4]{BIPP19:CurrentSystoles} that if $c$ is a closed concrete curve, then $L_{\mu}(c) \geq L_{\mu}(\gamma)$ where $\gamma$ is the $\Sigma$-hyperbolic representative.

In fact, it follows \cite[Lemma~4.7]{BIPP19:CurrentSystoles} that

\begin{proposition}
For $C=[\gamma]$, where $\gamma$ is a closed $\Sigma$-geodesic,
\[
i(\mu,C) = L_{\mu}(\gamma).
\]
\end{proposition}

Observe that from $L_{\mu}$ we can define a function $\bar{L}_{\mu} : \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathbb{R}$ as follows. Fix some hyperbolic metric $\Sigma$. For $C=[\gamma]$ and $\gamma$ a $\Sigma$-geodesic representative, let
\[
\bar{L}_{\mu}(C) \coloneqq L_{\mu}(\gamma).
\]
Our Theorem~\ref{thm:intersect} provides the inverse method to the construction of $\bar{L}_{\mu}$.

\subsection{Intersection and stable lengths}

One can also consider the metric space $X_{\mu} \coloneqq \tilde{\Sigma} / \sim$, endowed with the induced distance $[d_{\mu}]$, where $\sim$ here means that two points $x,y \in \tilde{\Sigma}$ are identified  if and only if $d_{\mu}(x,y)=0$, and $[d_{\mu}]([x],[y])\coloneqq d_{\mu}(x,y)$, for $[x],[y] \in X_{\mu}$.
Note that $X_{\mu}$ might not be Hausdorff. For instance, if $\mu=\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}$, $X_{\mu} = \tilde{\Sigma}$ endowed with the hyperbolic distance. If $\mu$ is a measured lamination, then $X_{\mu}$ is 0-hyperbolic in the sense of Gromov (see \cite[Example~4.2]{BIPP19:CurrentSystoles}).
$\pi_1(\Sigma)$ acts on $X_{\mu}$ by isometries, since $\mu$ is $\pi_1(\Sigma)$-invariant, and also co-compactly.
If $\mu$ is filling, this action is also discrete. 
Given a closed curve $C$, let $\gamma \in \pi_1(S)$ be a representative.
One can define a stable length on $X_{\mu}$.

\[
   \|C\|_{X_{\mu}}=\lim_{n \to \infty} \inf_{x \in X_{\mu}} [d_{\mu}](x,\gamma \cdot x).
 \] 
 
Erlandsson-Uyanik show in \cite[Theorem~3.5]{EU18:LengthCurrents} that

\begin{theorem}
For a filling geodesic current $\mu$, and for any closed curve $C$,
\[
\|C\|_{X_{\mu}} = i(\mu,C).
\]
\end{theorem}

Note that this stable length is defined on $X_{\mu}/\pi_1(S)$, not on $S$.
In general, stable lengths on $S$ cannot be realized as intersection numbers, since they might not satisfy the smoothing condition (see Example~\ref{ex:puncturedtorus}), but they do satisfy quasi-smoothing (see Section~\ref{sec:length-emb-graph}).

\begin{notation}
To ease notation, we will sometimes write $[x][y]$ to denote $[x] \cup [y]$ as multicurves.
\end{notation}

\subsection{Cross-ratios}

In Section~\ref{subsec:Liouville} we have seen several equivalent ways to define a geodesic current associated to a hyperbolic metric $\Sigma$ on a closed surface $S$. One of them was in terms of the cross-ratio defined on $(\partial_{\infty} \Sigma)^4$.
It turns out that one can similarly construct geodesic currents from (generalized) cross-ratios. 

There are several definitions in the literature of what a cross-ratio should be.
Here we consider the one by Ledrappier~\cite[Definition~1.f]{L95:BordDesVar}, Hamenst\"adt~\cite[page~101]{Ham99:Cocycles} and Martone-Zhang~\cite[Definition~2.4]{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed}.

Consider the set 
\[
\partial_{\infty} \Sigma^{[4]} \coloneqq \{ (x,y,z,w) \in (\partial_{\infty} \Sigma)^4 : \{ x,y \} \cap \{z,w\} = \emptyset  \}
\]

\begin{definition}
A \emph{cross ratio} is a continuous function $B : \partial_{\infty} \Sigma^{[4]} \to \mathbb{R}$ that is invariant under the diagonal action of $\pi_1(\Sigma)$ and satisfies the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item (Symmetry) $B(x,y,z,w) = B(z,w,x,y)$
    \item (Additivity) $B(x,y,z,w) + B(x,y,w,u)=B(x,y,z,u)$
    for all $x,y,z,w,u \in \partial_{\infty} \Sigma$ so that $(x,y,z,w),(x,y,w,u),(x,y,z,u) \in \partial_{\infty}^{[4]} \Sigma.$
\end{enumerate}
\label{def:crossratio}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
For any $C=[\gamma]$, for $\gamma \in \pi_1(\Sigma)$, the \emph{$B$-period of $C$} is defined as
    \[
       \ell_B(C) \coloneqq B(\gamma^-,\gamma^+, \gamma \cdot x,x) 
    \]
    for some $x \in \partial_{\infty}S - \{ \gamma_-, \gamma_+ \}$.
\end{definition}

It is easily shown, by the properties of cross-ratio, that $\ell_B$ is well-defined.
Also, a cross-ratio is determined by its periods.

\begin{theorem}
Let $B_1,B_2$ be two cross-ratios. If $\ell_{B_1}(C)=\ell_{B_2}(C)$ for all connected closed curves $C \in \Curves(S)$, then $B_1=B_2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
See \cite[Theorem~2.2]{Otal92:Symplectique}.
\end{proof}

This Theorem is probably reminiscent of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersectioncurves}, that showed that geodesic currents are determined by their intersection numbers with connected closed curves.
Indeed, this is not a coincidence.

\begin{definition}
We say a cross-ratio $B$ is \emph{positive} if for all $x,y,z,w \in \partial_{\infty} S$ in this cyclic order, we have $B(x,y,z,w) \geq 0$.
\label{def:poscrossratio}
\end{definition}

The following is a Theorem by Hamenst\"adt \cite[Theorem~2.10]{Ham99:Cocycles}.

\begin{theorem}
A positive cross-ratio defines a geodesic current.
\label{thm:currentfromcrossratio}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Given a positive cross-ratio $B$, one can define a geodesic current by defining the measure of a box $[a,b) \times [c,d) \subset G(\Sigma)$ to be $\mu_B([a,b] \times [c,d]) \coloneqq B(a,b,c,d)$. The symmetry condition of $B$ shows $\mu_B$ is flip-invariant, it is $\pi_1(S)$-invariant by definition, and the additivity condition of $B$ shows it is a finitely additive pre-measure defined on the family of finite unions of products of half-open boxes in $\partial_{\infty} S$. By continuity of the cross-ratio, one can show that moreover $\mu_B$ is countably additive. By positivity of the cross-ratio, it follows it's a positive current. The details can be found in \cite[Proposition~A.3]{MZ19:PositivelyRatioed}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rmk:poscrossratio}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Not all geodesic currents come from positive cross-ratios. Indeed, the geodesic currents coming from positive cross-ratios have no atoms (by continuity in the definition of cross-ratio). If one doesn't assume continuity in the definition of cross-ratio, one can still try to define a geodesic current, but then has to come up with a different way of proving countable subadditivity. For example, defining the current in some dense subset where one has some control on the pre-measure; we do this in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intersections}.
    \item In the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:currentfromcrossratio}, even if we don't assume the cross-ratio is continuous, we will always get a finitely additive pre-measure. If $B$ is continuous but not positive, we obtain a signed-current (see \cite[Page~103]{Ham99:Cocycles}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}
